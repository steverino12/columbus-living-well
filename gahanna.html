Gahanna: a renting and living guide to the city northeast of Columbus

What landlords and tenants should know about Gahanna, the separate city along Big Walnut Creek next to the airport, including its 2.5% income tax and filing rule, the city’s own rental registration and inspection program, short-term rental rules, the single city utility bill and the coming change in trash haulers, Gahanna-Jefferson schools, COTA bus service, and voucher figures for ZIP 43230, without inventing market rents.

Last updated November 2, 2026. Facts below come from the City of Gahanna and its Codified Ordinances, the Regional Income Tax Agency, Gahanna-Jefferson Public Schools, COTA, CMHA, the Ohio Revised Code, local government-meeting coverage, and Wikipedia’s summary of the city, all cited in Sources. Asking-rent medians are not invented here; see Rent Pulse and Fair Market Rents for verified figures.

Where Gahanna is, and why the city line matters

Gahanna is a suburb in northeastern Franklin County, just east of John Glenn Columbus International Airport. Its population was 35,726 at the 2020 census, in 14,165 households, and the census counted 14,734 housing units with a 6.6% rental vacancy rate. The city covers about 12.6 square miles. Columbus borders it on the north, west, and south, New Albany touches it on the north, Mifflin Township lies to the west, and Jefferson Township lies to the east, with several small islands of Jefferson Township entirely inside the city. Big Walnut Creek and its tributaries, Rocky Fork Creek and Sycamore Run, shape much of its geography.

Like Westerville, Hilliard, Worthington, and Bexley, Gahanna is its own city, not a Columbus neighborhood. Gahanna’s ordinances, income tax, police, zoning, and rental registration apply inside its limits, and Columbus-only rules such as Renter’s Choice deposit installments and the new Columbus rental registry do not. Because Columbus wraps around three sides of the city and township islands sit inside it, a Gahanna mailing address doesn’t by itself mean a property is in the city. Confirm the jurisdiction before you sign a lease, register a rental, or file taxes.

A short history

Gahanna was founded on Big Walnut Creek in 1849 by John Clark, on 800 acres his father had bought from Governor Worthington in 1814. Clark called his property the Gahanna Plantation, after a Native American name for the creek, said to mean three creeks joining into one, and the city seal still reads “Three In One.” A rival village, Bridgeport, was founded across Granville Street in 1853. The two eventually merged under the Gahanna name, since Ohio already had a Bridgeport, and the village was incorporated in 1881.

The city’s historic downtown is known as Olde Gahanna. Starting in 2004, the Creekside Gahanna redevelopment and park extension brought restaurants, residences, offices, public space, and a 389-space public parking garage to that area along the creek. Gahanna also turned the former Bedford I landfill, a 200-acre site whose closure was completed in late 2007, into the Central Park of Gahanna development campus.

Income tax: 2.5%, full credit, and residents file

Gahanna’s municipal income tax is 2.5%, and the Regional Income Tax Agency (RITA), which collects it, has listed that rate since at least January 1, 2020. It applies to residents’ earned income, to nonresidents who work in the city, and to business net profits in the city. Residents receive a 100% credit for municipal tax paid where they work, applied to the lesser of that tax or Gahanna’s 2.5%. So a resident who works in Columbus (also 2.5%) typically owes Gahanna nothing more, while someone working in a 2.0% city owes Gahanna the remaining 0.5%.

Gahanna requires every resident with earned income to file a return each year, even if the credit means no tax is due, and residents with no earned income or another exemption must file the appropriate exemption form. Returns go through RITA, and the closest in-person RITA office for Gahanna residents is in Worthington.

Rental registration and inspection: required every two years

This is the biggest difference between Gahanna and many nearby suburbs. Ordinance 0053-2018, adopted July 2, 2018, created Chapter 780 of the Codified Ordinances, Rental Property Registration and Maintenance. It requires every owner of residential rental property in the city to register biennially, between January 1 and March 15, and every registered property is inspected for compliance with the city’s property maintenance code. Any violations must be fixed before a rental permit is issued. Permits are valid for two years and expire on December 31 of the second year.

The city defines a rental dwelling unit as any structure or part of one rented or leased for residential purposes by someone other than the owner, and that includes renting to friends or letting someone live there in exchange for repairs. The program requires a designated contact person for each property. When ownership or the contact changes, the new owner or contact must register within 30 days. If a property is owned by an LLC, a member, manager, or officer files; for a trust, the trustee files.

The city’s posted fee schedule lists a $50 registration fee plus a permit fee of $100 for a single-family home or duplex. For multifamily properties, the permit fee is $200 for fewer than 10 units, $400 for 10 to 59 units, $600 for 60 to 99 units, and $800 for 100 or more. A single-family home occupied by its owner for more than half the year can request an exemption, as can a home occupied by a family member of the owner, but exemptions are decided case by case. Duplexes and multi-unit buildings must register every rental unit even when the owner lives in one of them. Since 2024, applications are accepted only online through the city’s OpenGov portal, and owners who ignore registration notices can receive a citation and a mandatory court date. Code Enforcement handles questions at 614-342-4022.

Short-term rentals

Gahanna defines a short-term rental as any dwelling rented wholly or partly, for a fee, for less than 30 consecutive days by someone other than the permanent occupant or owner. The city says a property used for Airbnb or similar hosting is a short-term rental, whether it’s a whole home or a single room, and that it must be registered under the rental registration program. The same owner-occupied exemption applies: a single-family home that the owner lives in for more than half the year may request one, with no guarantee of approval. Anyone thinking about listing a Gahanna unit should talk to Code Enforcement and the Zoning Division first, and tenants should check their lease, since subletting for short stays without the owner’s consent and city registration can put both parties at risk.

Zoning and design review

Gahanna adopted a new zoning code in April 2024 through Ordinance 0007-2024, which repealed the former Chapters 1101 through 1197, including the old certificate-of-appropriateness chapter. Design standards now sit in Chapter 1107, which includes city-wide standards and separate standards for the Creekside mixed-use and Creekside residential areas. There’s no separate architectural review board. Minor items such as fences, sheds, signs, and tree removal are zoning permits that staff can usually approve, while development plans, variances, conditional uses, and rezonings go to the Planning Commission and, where required, City Council through a public hearing.

The Planning Commission has seven members appointed to three-year terms. It holds regular meetings at 6:30 p.m. on the second Wednesday of each month in City Council Chambers, with workshops as needed. Since May 2024, zoning permits and planning applications are filed through OpenGov. For a landlord, this means a new fence, sign, or addition should be checked with the Zoning Division before work starts, and larger projects need to be planned around the Commission’s calendar.

Leases and deposits: Ohio law applies

Because Columbus rules such as Renter’s Choice don’t reach Gahanna, the core lease rules come from Ohio’s landlord-tenant law, Ohio Revised Code Chapter 5321. It sets landlord duties to keep the unit fit and habitable and make repairs, tenant duties to keep the unit safe and sanitary, the rule that a landlord must return a deposit with an itemized list of deductions within 30 days after the tenancy ends, and the 24-hour notice expected before a landlord enters, except in emergencies. Our Ohio landlord-tenant law guide covers these rules in more detail.

Gahanna’s city registration is in addition to the state requirement. Ohio Revised Code 5323.02 requires owners of residential rental property to register it with the county auditor, and Gahanna is in Franklin County, so that filing goes through the Franklin County Auditor. A Gahanna landlord typically keeps both registrations current.

Utilities and trash: one city bill, and a new hauler in 2027

Gahanna bills water, sanitary sewer, stormwater, and trash collection together on one monthly utility bill. Drinking water is supplied by the City of Columbus and wastewater is sent to Columbus for treatment, but Gahanna owns and maintains the local water, sewer, and stormwater systems. Bills are mailed by the 13th of each month, and the Utility Billing Division is at 614-342-4440 and the Gahanna Civic Center, 825 Tech Center Drive. Tenants should ask before signing whether the utility account stays in the owner’s name or moves to the tenant, since trash is part of the same bill.

Trash, recycling, and yard waste are collected under a city contract by Local Waste Services, on Monday or Tuesday depending on location. North of Granville Street, the dividing line is U.S. 62/Johnstown Road, with addresses to the west on Monday and those to the east, including addresses on U.S. 62, on Tuesday. South of Granville Street, the line is Big Walnut Creek. Each household gets a 95-gallon trash cart and a recycling cart, and yard waste goes out in paper bags or marked rigid containers, never plastic bags. Heads of household aged 60 and older can sign up for a discount on the trash portion of the bill.

That contract is changing. In July 2026, City Council adopted Ordinance 0021-2026, approving a five-year residential collection contract with Rumpke of Ohio for 2027 through 2031 after a consortium bid through the Solid Waste Authority of Central Ohio. City staff estimated the change at about $3.68 more per household per month. Council separately approved a Rumpke contract for recycling processing in September 2026. Watch for collection details from the city before the switch.

Getting around: COTA Lines 24, 25, and 46

Line 24 (Hamilton). Line 24 runs the length of Hamilton Road through Gahanna, between the Easton Transit Center and the Mobility Center at Rickenbacker, with stops near Granville Street, Johnstown Road, Rocky Fork Boulevard, and the Meijer on Hamilton Road. COTA classifies it as a standard line, which means service Monday through Saturday from about 5 a.m. to midnight and Sunday until about 10 p.m., with departures 16 to 60 minutes apart.

Line 25 (Brice). Line 25 is also a standard line, connecting the Easton Transit Center with the Gahanna Park and Ride, Mount Carmel East Hospital, the Reynoldsburg Park and Ride, and the Canal Winchester Park and Ride.

Line 46 (Gahanna). Line 46 is a rush-hour line, running weekday mornings and afternoons between the Royal Plaza Gahanna Park and Ride at 376-452 Agler Road and Downtown Columbus.

The fare is $2, with a $4.50 daily cap and a $62 monthly cap when paying digitally. Outside these lines, Gahanna is car-oriented, with quick access to the airport, I-270, and I-670. For bigger-picture transit plans, see Mobility & the Future.

Schools, safety, and daily life

Most of Gahanna is served by Gahanna-Jefferson Public Schools, which lists 8,385 students on its demographics page. The district runs seven elementary schools (Blacklick, Chapelfield, Goshen Lane, High Point, Jefferson, Lincoln, and Royal Manor), three middle schools (East, South, and West), and one high school, Gahanna Lincoln, and it is planning a redistricting for the 2027-28 school year. Because district and city lines don’t match exactly, confirm the assigned school for any address with the district.

Gahanna has its own police department. Fire and EMS service comes from Mifflin Township and Jefferson Township, depending on the area. Creekside and Olde Gahanna, with restaurants, residences, and public space along Big Walnut Creek, form the city’s most walkable area.

What landlords and tenants should check before signing

  • City or not. Confirm the address is actually inside Gahanna, since Columbus borders three sides and township islands sit inside the city.
  • Rental permit. Owners: register between January 1 and March 15 of the registration year, pass the inspection, and re-register within 30 days of an ownership or contact change. Tenants: it’s fair to ask whether the unit has a current Gahanna rental permit.
  • County registration too. Owners: keep the Franklin County Auditor registration current as well.
  • Short stays. Short-term rentals must be registered, and the owner-occupied exemption is not automatic.
  • File the return. Residents with earned income file a Gahanna return through RITA each year, even when the work-city credit means nothing is owed.
  • Utility account. Water, sewer, stormwater, and trash are one city bill, so settle in the lease who holds it, and expect a hauler change to Rumpke in 2027.
  • Transit. Lines 24 and 25 run all week, but Line 46 is rush-hour only, so check how you’ll commute.

Frequently asked questions

Is Gahanna part of Columbus?

No. Gahanna is a separate city in northeastern Franklin County with a 2020 census population of 35,726. It has its own ordinances, income tax, police department, zoning, and rental registration program, so Columbus City Code rules such as Renter’s Choice and the Columbus rental registry do not apply inside Gahanna. Columbus borders Gahanna on the north, west, and south, and several small islands of Jefferson Township sit inside the city, so confirm the jurisdiction for the exact address.

What is the Gahanna income tax rate?

Gahanna’s municipal income tax is 2.5%, the rate RITA has listed since at least January 1, 2020. Residents get a 100% credit for tax paid to the city where they work, applied to the lesser of that tax or Gahanna’s, so someone working in Columbus (2.5%) typically owes Gahanna nothing more. Every resident with earned income must still file a Gahanna return each year through the Regional Income Tax Agency (RITA), even when no tax is due.

Do Gahanna landlords have to register rental properties?

Yes. Under Chapter 780 of the Codified Ordinances, enacted by Ordinance 0053-2018, owners of residential rental property in Gahanna must register every two years between January 1 and March 15, and each property is inspected for compliance with the property maintenance code before a rental permit is issued. Permits are valid for two years and expire December 31 of the second year. The city lists a $50 registration fee plus a permit fee of $100 for a single-family home or duplex, and $200 to $800 for multifamily properties depending on the number of units. Applications go through the city’s OpenGov portal.

Can I run an Airbnb or short-term rental in Gahanna?

Gahanna treats any dwelling rented for less than 30 consecutive days as a short-term rental, whether it is a whole home or a room, and says these properties must go through rental registration. A single-family home occupied by the owner for more than half the year may request an exemption, but exemptions are case by case and not guaranteed. Contact Gahanna Code Enforcement at 614-342-4022 before listing a unit.

Who provides water and trash service in Gahanna?

The City of Gahanna bills water, sanitary sewer, stormwater, and trash together on one monthly utility bill; call Utility Billing at 614-342-4440. Drinking water is supplied by the City of Columbus and wastewater goes to Columbus for treatment, but Gahanna owns the local system. Local Waste Services currently collects trash, recycling, and yard waste on Monday or Tuesday depending on the address. In July 2026 City Council approved a five-year residential collection contract with Rumpke of Ohio for 2027 through 2031.

Which COTA buses serve Gahanna?

COTA Line 24 runs along Hamilton Road through Gahanna between the Easton Transit Center and the Mobility Center at Rickenbacker, and Line 25 links Easton with the Gahanna, Reynoldsburg, and Canal Winchester park-and-rides. Both are standard lines with departures 16 to 60 minutes apart. Line 46 is a weekday rush-hour line between the Royal Plaza Gahanna Park and Ride at 376-452 Agler Road and Downtown Columbus.

What is the voucher payment standard in Gahanna?

For ZIP 43230, CMHA’s FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents at 100%, effective October 1, 2026, are $1,540 for a studio, $1,650 for one bedroom, $1,960 for two, $2,340 for three, and $2,660 for four bedrooms. These are payment-standard benchmarks for Housing Choice Voucher holders, not asking rents.
