Hilliard: a renting and living guide to the city west of Columbus

What landlords and tenants should know about Hilliard, the separate city on Columbus’s west side, including its 2.5% income tax and mandatory filing, the tight limits on short-term rentals, how Old Hilliard design review works, who handles water and trash, Hilliard City Schools, COTA bus service, and voucher figures for ZIP 43026, without inventing market rents.

Last updated November 1, 2026. Facts below come from the City of Hilliard and its Codified Ordinances, the Regional Income Tax Agency, Hilliard City Schools, COTA, CMHA, the Ohio Revised Code, and Wikipedia’s summary of the city, all cited in Sources. Asking-rent medians are not invented here; see Rent Pulse and Fair Market Rents for verified figures.

Where Hilliard is, and why the city line matters

Hilliard is a suburb of Columbus in western Franklin County, part of Norwich Township. Its population was 37,114 at the 2020 census, and the Census Bureau estimated 37,262 in 2023. The city is bordered by Columbus and Upper Arlington on the east, Columbus and Dublin on the north, Galloway and Columbus on the south, and open farmland on the west. Interstate 270 runs along its eastern side, and Cemetery Road and Main Street/Hilliard Rome Road are its main streets.

Like Dublin, Upper Arlington, Worthington, and Westerville, Hilliard is its own city, not a Columbus neighborhood. Hilliard’s ordinances, income tax, police, and zoning apply inside its limits, and Columbus-only rules such as Renter’s Choice deposit installments and the new Columbus rental registry do not. Be careful with addresses, though. Hilliard is surrounded by Columbus on several sides, and the Hilliard City School District covers a portion of Columbus about the same size as the part inside the city, plus parts of Dublin and Galloway. A “Hilliard” mailing address or a Hilliard schools assignment doesn’t by itself mean an address is in the city, so confirm the jurisdiction before you sign or file taxes.

A short history

In 1852 John Reed Hilliard bought farmland in western Franklin County, and the town grew up as Hilliard’s Station around the Piqua and Indiana Railroad line that crossed it. He platted the original town on September 1, 1853, a post office opened in 1854 and the word “Station” was dropped, and the Village of Hilliard incorporated on July 13, 1869, with 280 residents. Rail service ended in 1962, and the original depot was restored and now sits in Weaver Park.

Growth came in two waves. Three large subdivisions in the 1950s and a connection to the Columbus regional water and sewer systems in the 1960s pushed the population to 5,633, and Hilliard became a city on December 12, 1960. The completion of the I-270 outerbelt in the early 1970s set off a second wave, and the population roughly tripled between 1990 and 2020. The historic district along Norwich Street, in what is now called Old Hilliard, keeps much of the original town’s mix of homes and storefronts.

Income tax: 2.5%, full credit, and everyone files

Hilliard’s municipal income tax is 2.5%. Voters approved an increase from 2.0%, and Ordinance No. 21-38, adopted November 22, 2021, dedicated the extra half percent to recreation and parks capital and operating costs; the Regional Income Tax Agency (RITA) shows the 2.5% rate in effect since January 1, 2022. Residents who are taxed by the city where they work receive full credit for that tax, up to the entire 2.5%. In practice, a Hilliard resident who works in Columbus (also 2.5%) typically owes Hilliard nothing extra, while someone who works in a city with a 2.0% rate owes Hilliard the remaining 0.5%.

Hilliard also has mandatory filing. Every resident 18 and older must file a Hilliard return by April 15 each year, or the following Monday when April 15 falls on a weekend, whether or not they work in the city or owe anything. Returns are filed through RITA.

Short-term rentals: allowed only in Old Hilliard, and effectively full

Hilliard defines a short-term rental as any room or dwelling rented for fewer than 30 consecutive days by someone other than the permanent occupant or owner. Before 2022, short-term rentals were not a permitted use anywhere in the city. Ordinance No. 22-18, adopted July 11, 2022, and effective August 11, 2022, allowed them as a trial in two Old Hilliard zoning districts, OH-MD (Old Hilliard Mixed Use) and OH-RD (Old Hilliard Residential), under Section 1121.06(i) of the Codified Ordinances.

Each short-term rental needs its own zoning certificate, with a separate certificate for each unit in a two-family or multi-family building, and a copy must be kept on site. The certificate is void if the owner stops operating, sells the property, changes the floor plan or site, or loses it for a violation. The ordinance also requires a 1,000-foot buffer between short-term rentals. When Planning and Zoning Commission staff reviewed the ordinance in August 2023, they reported that two short-term rentals had been approved, at 5222 Norwich Street and 3933 Columbia Street, and that under the buffer limits no additional short-term rentals were permitted in the city. The same report noted that some apartment communities were advertising short stays without certificates. Anyone thinking about listing a Hilliard unit on a platform should ask the Planning Division whether a certificate is even available before signing a lease or buying.

Old Hilliard design review and the Planning and Zoning Commission

Hilliard doesn’t have a separate architectural review board. In the Old Hilliard districts, exterior changes and new construction go to the Planning and Zoning Commission as Old Hilliard District Plan applications, and the Commission reviews them against the design criteria in Section 1115.05. Those criteria ask whether a project fits the historic character of the district, relates to neighboring buildings in scale, materials, and setbacks, supports pedestrian activity, and creates people-oriented facades along Main and Norwich streets, and they cover architecture, materials, color, roof forms, windows, mechanicals, awnings, landscaping, screening, parking, utilities, maintenance, and signs.

The Commission has seven residents serving four-year terms, one from each of the city’s five wards and two at large. It meets at 6 p.m. on the second Thursday of each month at the Municipal Building, 3800 Municipal Way, and its November 2026 meeting is November 12. The Planning Division encourages pre-submittal meetings and can be reached at 614-334-2444. For owners of Old Hilliard rentals, this means a facade, window, awning, or sign project needs to be planned around the Commission’s calendar.

Leases, deposits, and registration: Ohio law applies

Columbus rules such as Renter’s Choice don’t reach Hilliard, so the core rules for leases here come from Ohio’s landlord-tenant law, Ohio Revised Code Chapter 5321. That chapter sets landlord duties to keep the unit fit and habitable and make repairs, tenant duties to keep the unit safe and sanitary, the rule that a landlord must return a deposit with an itemized list of deductions within 30 days after the tenancy ends, and the 24-hour notice expected before a landlord enters, except in emergencies. Our Ohio landlord-tenant law guide covers these rules in more detail.

Ohio Revised Code 5323.02 requires owners of residential rental property to register it with the county auditor, and Hilliard is entirely in Franklin County, so registration goes through the Franklin County Auditor. Owners should keep that registration current when contact information or ownership changes.

Utilities and trash

Hilliard residents get water and sewer service from the City of Columbus Division of Water, a legacy of the 1960s connection to the regional systems. Billing, starting or stopping service, and problems are handled by Columbus at 614-645-8276, so tenants should set up water with Columbus rather than with Hilliard.

Trash, recycling, and yard waste are collected every Tuesday by Local Waste Services under a city contract, except on certain holidays. Since July 2025, residents have been billed directly by Local Waste Services, quarterly. New service is set up at 614-409-9375, each address gets a blue recycling bin and a trash bin (65 gallons standard), and carts go at the end of the driveway by 7 a.m. Bulk items such as furniture and mattresses go out with regular trash, with upholstered items wrapped in plastic. Tenants moving in should confirm with the landlord whether trash service is in the tenant’s name or the owner’s.

Getting around: COTA Lines 30 and 71

Line 30. COTA launched Line 30 in September 2026 as one of the first routes shaped by its Short-Range Transit Plan. It runs daily, connecting Hilliard, Upper Arlington, Ohio State, and Downtown Columbus with no transfer, and its stops include the Hilliard United Methodist Church Park and Ride, Kingsdale Shopping Center, the John Herrick Transit Center at Ohio State, and the Arena District. COTA’s September 2026 schedule shows weekday departures from Hilliard roughly every half hour through the morning.

Line 71. Line 71 is a weekday rush-hour line between the Hilliard UMC and Renner Road park-and-rides and Downtown Columbus. COTA added trips in January 2026, citing the state’s return-to-office order, so it now runs two morning trips downtown and two afternoon trips back.

Outside those lines, Hilliard is car-oriented, and there’s no rail service. For bigger-picture transit plans, see Mobility & the Future.

Schools, parks, and daily life

Hilliard City Schools reported 16,194 students on October 1, 2026, including 74 in its online academy, down from 16,365 a year earlier. The district runs fourteen elementary schools, two sixth-grade schools, three middle schools, and three high schools, Darby, Davidson, and Bradley. In November 2024, voters approved a $142 million bond issue and a 6.9-mill operating levy, which the district said would fund a new sixth-grade school, three new elementary buildings, and more preschool classrooms. Because the district’s lines don’t match the city’s, confirm the assigned school for any address with the district.

The city’s Recreation and Parks Department oversees more than 25 parks, and the Heritage Rail Trail begins in Old Hilliard on Center Street. Hilliard also hosts the Franklin County Fair, the Old Hilliardfest Art & Street Fair, a Fourth of July parade and fireworks, and the summer Celebration at the Station concerts. Hilliard has its own Division of Police, and fire and EMS service comes from Norwich Township.

What landlords and tenants should check before signing

  • City or not. Confirm the address is actually inside Hilliard, since a Hilliard mailing address or school assignment can be in Columbus or a township.
  • File the return. Every adult resident files a Hilliard return through RITA by April 15, even when the work-city credit means nothing is owed.
  • No short-term rental assumption. Short stays are limited to Old Hilliard with a certificate and a 1,000-foot buffer, and the city reported in 2023 that no more could be approved.
  • Old Hilliard exteriors. Owners: plan facade, window, awning, and sign work around the Planning and Zoning Commission’s monthly meetings.
  • Water with Columbus. Tenants: start water and sewer service with the City of Columbus, and ask who holds the trash account with Local Waste Services.
  • Registration. Owners: keep the Franklin County Auditor rental registration current.
  • Transit. Line 30 runs daily, but Line 71 is only a few weekday rush-hour trips, so check how you’ll commute.

Frequently asked questions

Is Hilliard part of Columbus?

No. Hilliard is a separate city in Franklin County, west of Columbus, with a 2020 census population of 37,114. It has its own codified ordinances, income tax, police division, and zoning, so Columbus City Code rules such as Renter’s Choice and the new Columbus rental registry do not apply inside Hilliard city limits. Hilliard is bordered by Columbus on several sides, and the Hilliard City School District also covers a portion of Columbus, so confirm the city of residence for the exact address.

What is the Hilliard income tax rate?

Hilliard’s municipal income tax is 2.5%. Voters raised it from 2.0% effective January 1, 2022, with the extra 0.5% dedicated to recreation and parks. Residents who pay tax to the city where they work get full credit up to the entire 2.5%, so someone working in Columbus (2.5%) typically owes Hilliard nothing more. Filing is mandatory: every resident 18 and older must file a return by April 15 through the Regional Income Tax Agency (RITA), even if no tax is due.

Can I run an Airbnb or short-term rental in Hilliard?

Only in limited cases. Hilliard defines a short-term rental as a stay of fewer than 30 consecutive days, and its 2022 ordinance allows them only in the Old Hilliard Mixed Use (OH-MD) and Old Hilliard Residential (OH-RD) zoning districts, with a zoning certificate for each dwelling unit and a 1,000-foot buffer between short-term rentals. In its August 2023 review, city staff reported that two had been approved and that, under the buffer rule, no additional short-term rentals were permitted in the city. Check with the Planning Division before listing a unit.

Who reviews exterior changes in Old Hilliard?

Hilliard does not have a separate architectural review board. Exterior changes and new construction in the Old Hilliard districts go to the seven-member Planning and Zoning Commission, which reviews them against the design criteria in Section 1115.05 of the Codified Ordinances, covering architecture, materials, color, roofs, windows, awnings, signage, landscaping, and parking. The Commission meets at 6 p.m. on the second Thursday of each month at 3800 Municipal Way.

Who provides water and trash service in Hilliard?

Water and sewer service comes from the City of Columbus Division of Water, which handles billing and service changes at 614-645-8276. Trash, recycling, and yard waste are collected every Tuesday by Local Waste Services, which has billed residents directly since July 2025; new service is set up at 614-409-9375.

Which COTA buses serve Hilliard?

COTA’s Line 30, launched in September 2026, runs daily from Hilliard through Upper Arlington and Ohio State to Downtown Columbus, with stops including the Hilliard United Methodist Church Park and Ride and Kingsdale Shopping Center. Line 71 is a weekday rush-hour line between the Hilliard UMC and Renner Road park-and-rides and Downtown, with two morning trips downtown and two afternoon trips back since January 2026.

What is the voucher payment standard in Hilliard?

For ZIP 43026, CMHA’s FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents at 100%, effective October 1, 2026, are $1,510 for a studio, $1,610 for one bedroom, $1,920 for two, $2,290 for three, and $2,600 for four bedrooms. These are payment-standard benchmarks for Housing Choice Voucher holders, not asking rents.
