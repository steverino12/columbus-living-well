Clintonville: a Columbus renting and living guide

What landlords and tenants should know about Clintonville—how it grew along High Street, its older housing stock, the Clintonville Area Commission’s role in zoning, the Zone In changes on the High Street and Indianola corridors, COTA Line 2, Whetstone Park and the Park of Roses, and ZIP-level voucher figures—without inventing market rents.

Last updated October 21, 2026. Neighborhood facts below come from the Clintonville Area Commission, local history sources, the City of Columbus, Columbus Recreation and Parks, COTA, and CMHA tables cited in Sources. Asking-rent medians are not invented here; see Rent Pulse and Fair Market Rents for verified figures.

Where Clintonville sits

Clintonville is a neighborhood in north-central Columbus, inside the City of Columbus and Franklin County, centered on North High Street. It is not a separate city, so Columbus city services, the Columbus income tax, and Columbus City Code rules for landlords and tenants apply. Common descriptions put the Olentangy River on the west, the railroad tracks just east of Indianola Avenue on the east, the Glen Echo ravine area on the south, and the City of Worthington on the north. Some sources treat the area north of Henderson Road as Beechwold, but the Clintonville Area Commission’s nine districts run all the way to the Worthington city limits, and most people use the Clintonville name for the whole stretch.

That geography matters when you write a listing or read one. A “Clintonville” address can be a ravine-side side street near Glen Echo, a block off High Street near Weber Road, or a house on the far north end near the Worthington line. Pair the neighborhood name with the cross streets, and check the ZIP (below) before quoting any voucher figure. How Clintonville got here

The area was part of Clinton Township, which the Franklin County Historical Society notes was organized as a township of Franklin County in 1811. The township’s land was part of the United States Military Lands, according to the 1858 History of Franklin County. Alanson Bull, described by the Franklin County Historical Society as a prosperous farmer on the Columbus and Worthington plank road and by the Clintonville Historical Society as the son of Thomas Bull, sold several small lots along that road—now High Street—to tradesmen for their shops, so neighboring farms had wagon makers and other craftsmen close by.

The 1858 History of Franklin County records that the hamlet was never formally platted, that it “assumed the name of ‘Clintonville,’” and that a post office opened there in October 1847, with James Ferguson as postmaster. The Clintonville Historical Society’s history notes that the post office sat at the northwest corner of High Street and Orchard Lane and that High Street was then a dirt-and-plank turnpike linking Columbus with Worthington and Sandusky. Today the whole area lies inside the City of Columbus, with houses filling the side streets off High and Indianola.

The result is a neighborhood whose commercial life still runs along one long corridor, with residential blocks behind it. That pattern—shops on High, homes on the side streets—shapes almost every renting decision here, from parking to noise to what new construction may appear nearby.

Housing stock: older homes, doubles, and small buildings

Most Clintonville rentals are older single-family houses, side-by-side doubles, and small apartment buildings on tree-lined residential streets, with some larger and newer buildings along High Street. Ravines such as Glen Echo cut through parts of the neighborhood, so some lots sit on or near wooded slopes. Those traits are a big part of the appeal, and they also set the maintenance agenda: mature trees, leaf cleanup, gutters, sloped yards, and basement water management.

For landlords, that means writing clear lease terms for snow, leaves, and lawn care, and budgeting for the systems typical of older homes—roofs, windows, furnaces, and sewer laterals. For tenants, it means asking about basement dampness, sump pumps, heating costs, laundry hookups, and whether a garage or driveway is included. Ohio law requires landlords to keep the premises fit and habitable and to make repairs; tenants must keep their part of the unit safe and sanitary. Our Ohio landlord-tenant law guide covers those duties and the Columbus rules on top of them.

The Clintonville Area Commission and zoning

The Clintonville Area Commission (CAC) is the neighborhood’s elected advisory body. It has nine districts, each with a commissioner, and its bylaws set regular public meetings on the first Thursday of each month. The Commission’s Zoning & Variance Committee “shall regularly receive, review and make recommendations on all applications for rezoning, variances, graphics and other zoning adjustment appeals and special permits located wholly or partially in the Clintonville area.” Other standing committees cover planning and development, historic buildings (cataloguing buildings along High Street and Indianola Avenue), technology and community relations, a Green Team, and elections.

The CAC advises; City of Columbus boards and City Council decide. Still, an owner who wants to convert a building, add a unit, change a sign, or seek a variance should expect to present to the Zoning & Variance Committee and the full Commission first, and to hear neighbors’ comments there. As of October 2026, the Commission has announced a special election on December 19, 2026, for its District 3 and District 9 seats, so check its website for current commissioners and meeting details.

Zone In: what changed on High Street and Indianola

Columbus’s Zone In initiative is the City’s first comprehensive zoning update in about 70 years. Columbus City Council adopted Phase One—Title 34, the 2024 Zoning Code—on July 29, 2024. It rezoned parcels along roughly 140 miles of major corridors from the older Title 33 districts into six new mixed-use districts. Local coverage in the Clintonville Spotlight lists North High Street and Indianola Avenue in Clintonville among the corridors in Phase One, and a 2026 Spotlight report says Phase One took effect in summer 2025.

A key piece of Phase One is parking. The adopting legislation eliminated minimum vehicle parking requirements in the new mixed-use districts (bicycle parking is still required) and added a parking impact review for new development to address concerns about spillover onto neighborhood streets. In a 2026 update to the CAC, a City planning administrator said Phase Two focuses mostly on older industrial and business areas and that Clintonville would not be affected much by it.

What this means in practice: more mixed-use and multifamily construction is possible along the corridors, which can add rental supply and also change parking and traffic on nearby side streets. Tenants on a block near High Street should ask whether any nearby redevelopment is planned; owners of corridor parcels should read their new district rules on the City’s Zone In map before planning changes.

Getting around: COTA Line 2 and the Olentangy Trail

COTA Line 2 (East Main/North High) runs the length of North High Street through Clintonville and on through the University District, the Short North, and Downtown before heading east on Main Street. Its northern end is at N. High and Fenway. COTA labels it a frequent line, meaning departures 15 minutes or less apart during frequent-service hours (Monday through Saturday 5 a.m. to midnight, Sunday 5 a.m. to 10 p.m.). The standard fare is $2, with a $4.50 day pass and a $62 31-day pass, and fares cap at $4.50 a day or $62 a month when you pay with a digital payment method. COTA observes Sunday schedules on major holidays.

COTA’s proposed 2027–2031 short-range transit plan, reported by the Columbus Dispatch in May 2026, would run Line 2 north service every 15 minutes on weekends and add 24-hour service on several lines including Line 2; check COTA’s service-change page for what is final. For a non-bus option, Whetstone Park is a trailhead for the Olentangy Trail, which Columbus Recreation and Parks says provides access to Northmoor Park. Landmarks: Whetstone Park, the Park of Roses, and the library

Whetstone Park, at 3923 N. High St. (ZIP 43214), is a 149-acre Columbus Recreation and Parks regional park with a community center, athletic fields, ball diamonds, a stocked pond, a lighted tennis court, pickleball overlays, a community garden, a playground, and about 1.6 miles of paved path; posted hours are 7 a.m. to 11 p.m. The department says the land was a family farm in the early 1800s, was bought by Columbus mayor James A. Rhodes in 1944 for World War II Victory Gardens—more than 500 were planted—and was christened Whetstone Park on Memorial Day 1950. A 5.1-acre native prairie with vernal pools was planted in May 2004.

Inside Whetstone Park, the Columbus Park of Roses is a 13-acre rose garden that the department says showcases more than 350 varieties and 11,000 total roses across five gardens, in existence since 1953. Peak bloom runs from mid-June through mid-September. Nearby, Columbus Metropolitan Library’s Whetstone Branch is at 3909 N. High St.

Vouchers, ZIPs, and what the SAFMR tables say

Clintonville includes parts of two ZIP codes: 43202, generally the southern part of the neighborhood, and 43214, generally the northern part, including Whetstone Park. ZIP lines do not follow neighborhood lines exactly, and 43214 also extends west of the Olentangy. Confirm the unit’s exact ZIP, because CMHA prices vouchers by ZIP, not by neighborhood name.

CMHA’s FY2027 SAFMR payment standards effective October 1, 2026, published at 100% of SAFMR, list these figures:

So the same two-bedroom can carry a $140 higher payment standard on the 43214 side than on the 43202 side. These are payment-standard benchmarks, not asking rents and not automatic approvals; contract rent still faces rent-reasonableness review, and utility allowances affect the gross-rent comparison. Because Clintonville is inside Columbus, Columbus City Code 4551.03 restricts refusing to rent based on lawful source of income, including vouchers—covered in our landlord-tenant law guide.

What landlords and tenants should check before signing

  • Exact location. Cross streets, distance to High Street, and whether the lot backs onto a ravine.
  • Water and drainage. Basement history, sump pump, gutters, and grading on sloped lots.
  • Outdoor duties. Who handles snow, leaves, lawn, and tree debris, in writing.
  • Parking. Driveway or garage included? How busy is street parking near the corridor?
  • Nearby development. Any Zone In-era projects planned on the corridor close to the unit?
  • ZIP and voucher math. Use CMHA’s current SAFMR schedule for 43202 or 43214 before an RFTA.
  • City rules. Security deposits, entry notice, late fees, and source-of-income rules are in the landlord-tenant law page.
  • Application package. Pair this read with our Applications guide and Tenant Playbook.

Clintonville suits renters who want a quieter, leafy residential setting with a frequent bus line and a long commercial street a short walk away. Owners should plan for the upkeep that comes with older homes and mature trees, and keep an eye on corridor development. Clear leases, honest listings, and ZIP-accurate pricing keep both sides out of avoidable conflict.

Frequently asked questions

Where is Clintonville in Columbus?

Clintonville is a neighborhood in north-central Columbus, inside the City of Columbus, centered on North High Street. Common descriptions put the Olentangy River on the west, the railroad tracks just east of Indianola Avenue on the east, the Glen Echo area on the south, and Worthington on the north; the Clintonville Area Commission’s nine districts reach the Worthington city limits.

What is the Clintonville Area Commission?

It is the neighborhood’s elected advisory body, with nine districts. Its Zoning & Variance Committee reviews and makes recommendations on rezonings, variances, graphics (sign) requests, and special permits in the Clintonville area before City bodies decide. Regular meetings are on the first Thursday of each month and are open to the public.

How did Zone In change zoning on North High Street?

Columbus City Council adopted the 2024 Zoning Code (Title 34), Phase One of the Zone In initiative, on July 29, 2024. It rezoned parcels along about 140 miles of major corridors, including North High Street and Indianola Avenue in Clintonville, into new mixed-use districts and eliminated minimum vehicle parking requirements in those districts, with a parking impact review for new development. Interior residential streets are not part of Phase One.

Which COTA line serves Clintonville?

COTA Line 2 (East Main/North High) runs the length of North High Street through Clintonville, starting at N. High and Fenway, and is a frequent line with departures 15 minutes or less apart during its frequent-service hours. The standard fare is $2, with daily fare capping at $4.50 when you pay digitally.

What is the voucher payment standard in Clintonville?

Clintonville includes parts of ZIPs 43202 and 43214. CMHA’s FY2027 SAFMR figures effective October 1, 2026 (at 100% of SAFMR) list $1,270 for a one-bedroom and $1,510 for a two-bedroom in 43202, and $1,390 for a one-bedroom and $1,650 for a two-bedroom in 43214. Always confirm the unit’s ZIP on CMHA’s current table.

What should landlords and tenants check before renting in Clintonville?

Expect older single-family homes, doubles, and small apartment buildings on tree-lined side streets, with newer mixed-use buildings possible along High Street. Check basement and drainage history near the ravines, who handles snow and leaves, off-street parking, the exact ZIP for voucher math, and Columbus city rules on deposits and source of income.
