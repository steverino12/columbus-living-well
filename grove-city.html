Grove City: a renting and living guide to the city on Columbus’s southwest side

What landlords and tenants should know about Grove City, the separate city along Broadway, Stringtown Road, and I-71 southwest of Downtown Columbus, including its 2% income tax, who bills water, sewer, and trash, how short stays and fair housing are handled, South-Western City Schools, COTA bus service, the Beulah Park neighborhood, and voucher figures for ZIP 43123, without inventing market rents.

Last updated November 4, 2026. Facts below come from the City of Grove City and its Codified Ordinances, the Regional Income Tax Agency, the City of Columbus, South-Western City Schools, COTA, CMHA, the Ohio Revised Code, local news coverage, and Wikipedia’s summary of the city, all cited in Sources. Asking-rent medians are not invented here; see Rent Pulse and Fair Market Rents for verified figures.

A separate city, not a Columbus neighborhood

Grove City is its own city in southern Franklin County, not part of Columbus. Settler Hugh Grant bought the land in 1803, and William F. Breck platted the village on the east side of Broadway in 1852 along the Harrisburg Turnpike to Columbus. The village was incorporated in 1866 and became a city in 1959. The 2020 census counted 41,252 residents, and the Census Bureau’s 2025 estimate is 45,180. At the 2020 census, the city had 17,308 housing units and a rental vacancy rate of 8.5%.

The city runs under a charter with an elected mayor and City Council and an appointed City Administrator. It has its own Division of Police on Park Street, while the Jackson Township Fire Department covers both the city and the township. Because Grove City borders parts of Columbus and unincorporated Jackson Township, a “Grove City” mailing address doesn’t prove an address is inside the city, so check the parcel before applying any of the city rules below. Columbus-only programs, such as the Columbus rental registry and Renter’s Choice, don’t apply inside Grove City.

Income tax: 2% with a full credit for your work city

Grove City’s municipal income tax is 2%, and the Regional Income Tax Agency (RITA), which has administered it since July 1, 2004, lists that rate from January 1, 2020 on. Residents get a 100% credit for municipal tax paid to the city where they work, capped at 2%. In practice, a resident who works in Columbus and has 2.5% withheld there usually owes Grove City nothing more, while someone working in a lower-tax city or for an employer that withholds nothing may owe the difference.

The city says residents must file if they lived in Grove City for any part of the year and earned income during that time, and it lists income from rental property you own as earned income. Owning rental property in the city also counts as doing business there, so out-of-town landlords may need to file too. Retirees whose only income is Social Security, pensions, interest, and dividends don’t need to file but should send the city’s exemption form once. Unpaid tax carries a 15% penalty plus interest, and late returns can be charged a late-filing fee. Questions go to RITA at 800-860-7482 or the city’s tax administrator at 614-277-3030.

Rental registration and property upkeep

Unlike Reynoldsburg or Gahanna, Grove City doesn’t appear to run its own rental registry. We didn’t find a city rental registration requirement on its website or in the code sections we reviewed. Instead, the city’s Code Compliance division enforces local ordinances and the International Code Council’s property maintenance code, and its staff visually inspect homes and businesses around the city to find properties that aren’t in compliance. Tenants with repair problems that a landlord won’t fix can also contact the city.

The state requirement still applies. Ohio Revised Code 5323.02 requires owners of residential rental property to register it with the county auditor, which for Grove City is the Franklin County Auditor. Owners must update that information within 60 days of any change, and the auditor says each noncompliant property can be fined $150 per tax year. Out-of-state owners must name an Ohio contact.

Fair housing: Chapter 550

Grove City’s Chapter 550 bans unlawful discrimination in employment, housing, and public accommodations. Its protected classes are race, color, religion, sex, national origin, age, disability, military status, veteran or special disabled veteran status, ancestry, familial status, pregnancy, sexual orientation, gender identity, and gender expression. Complaints go to the city’s Law Director, and a city hearing officer can mediate or hold a hearing.

Source of income isn’t on that list, and Ohio has no statewide source-of-income protection, so the city code we reviewed doesn’t require landlords to accept Housing Choice Vouchers. That’s different from Reynoldsburg, which added source-of-income protection in 2021. Landlords who do screen applicants should apply the same written criteria to everyone, and federal and state fair housing laws still apply.

Short stays and lodging tax

We didn’t find a dedicated short-term rental permit chapter in Grove City’s code, unlike Reynoldsburg’s new permit that starts in 2027. The zoning code does define a bed and breakfast inn as an owner-occupied single-family home where transient guests stay no more than 30 consecutive days, with no more than five guests at a time. A whole-home rental may not fit that definition, so get a written answer on the parcel’s zoning from the Grove City Building Division at 614-277-3075 before listing.

Lodging is taxed under Chapter 192. It defines a transient accommodation as a place with four or fewer rooms for guests staying fewer than 30 consecutive days, and it levies two separate 3% taxes on what guests pay, one for the city’s general fund and one that also supports convention and visitors bureaus. Tenants should also check their lease, since subletting for short stays without the owner’s consent is a separate problem from the city’s rules.

Leases and deposits: Ohio law applies

The core lease rules come from Ohio’s landlord-tenant law, Ohio Revised Code Chapter 5321. It sets landlord duties to keep the unit fit and habitable and make repairs, tenant duties to keep the unit safe and sanitary, the rule that a landlord must return a deposit with an itemized list of deductions within 30 days after the tenancy ends, and the 24-hour notice expected before a landlord enters, except in emergencies. Our Ohio landlord-tenant law guide covers these rules in more detail. For help, Franklin County tenants can contact the Franklin County Municipal Court Self Help Resource Center at 614-645-7381 or the Legal Aid Society of Columbus at 614-241-2001.

Utilities: Columbus water and sewer, Local Waste Services trash

Water and sewer. Grove City buys water and sewer service from the City of Columbus. Columbus’s Department of Public Utilities has been upgrading or replacing most water meters in Grove City and Urbancrest through its Enhanced Meter Project, which is mandatory under Columbus City Code 1105.038 and free to the owner. The city says that once all meters are converted, billing will move from quarterly to monthly. Appointments are booked at ColumbusEMP.org or 614-526-0917. Tenants should ask before signing whether the water account stays in the owner’s name.

Trash, recycling, and yard waste. The city contracts with Local Waste Services for weekly curbside collection of trash, recycling, and yard waste on the same day, and residents of Grove City and Jackson Township must use it. Local Waste Services bills customers directly every quarter, and accounts are set up at 614-409-9375. Heads of household 62 or older can get a 10% discount. Carts go out by 7 a.m. on collection day but not before 5 p.m. the day before, and must be put back within 24 hours. Apartments and condos are served by a private hauler of the owner’s choice, so ask the property manager.

In July 2026, City Council approved a new franchise with Local Waste Services, bid through the Solid Waste Authority of Central Ohio, that runs from January 1, 2027 through December 31, 2031. According to coverage of the vote, the typical household charge rises from $19.99 to $23.99 a month. If trash is the owner’s bill under the lease, expect the higher rate in 2027.

Getting around: COTA Lines 3 and 61

Line 3 (Northwest/Harrisburg). Line 3 is a standard COTA line, which COTA defines as buses 16 to 60 minutes apart. It runs from Grove City along Stringtown Road, Columbus Street, and Broadway, up Harrisburg Pike through the Hilltop and Downtown Columbus, and out to Northwest Boulevard.

Line 61 (Grove City). Line 61 is a rush-hour line, running weekday mornings and afternoons between the Grove City Park and Ride at 2321 Old Stringtown Road and Downtown Columbus.

COTA//Plus Grove City. COTA’s on-demand service covers trips within its Grove City zone and serves both the Grove City Park and Ride and the Great Southern Park and Ride on South High Street.

The fare is $2, with a $4.50 daily cap and a $62 monthly cap when paying digitally. Away from these routes, Grove City is car-oriented, with I-71 and I-270 close by. For bigger-picture transit plans, see Mobility & the Future.

Schools, Beulah Park, and daily life

All of Grove City is in the South-Western City School District, which the district calls the fifth-largest public district in Ohio and the second-largest in Franklin County. It covers 119 square miles, serves more than 21,000 students, and also includes parts of Columbus and four townships. Inside the city it runs Grove City and Central Crossing high schools, Beulah Park, Jackson, and Pleasant View middle schools, intermediate and elementary schools, and the South-Western Career Academy. District lines are wider than city lines, so a South-Western address isn’t automatically a Grove City address.

The former Beulah Park horse track next to the historic Town Center has become one of the city’s largest neighborhoods. A February 2025 recap from the developer said it was nearing 1,000 homes, including the 384-unit Beulah Place apartments and 82 rental townhomes at The Strand, along with single-family homes and senior living. The last piece, Beulah Crossing at the Columbus Street entrance, adds apartments above ground-floor shops and restaurants, with delivery targeted for late 2026. The city’s Park at Beulah has seven lighted pickleball courts, trails, and a pavilion. Town Center hosts events such as the Wine & Arts Festival and the Chamber’s Arts in the Alley.

What landlords and tenants should check before signing

  • City or township. Confirm the address is inside Grove City and not Jackson Township or Columbus.
  • County registration. Owners: keep the Franklin County Auditor rental registration current.
  • Income tax. Owners of Grove City rentals and residents with income there may need to file with RITA.
  • Vouchers. Source of income isn’t a protected class in the city code, but apply the same written screening criteria to every applicant.
  • Short stays. Get a written zoning answer before listing, and plan to collect the city’s 3% plus 3% lodging tax.
  • Utility accounts. Water and sewer come from Columbus and trash from Local Waste Services, so settle in the lease who holds each bill.
  • Transit. Line 3 runs all week, but Line 61 is rush-hour only, so check how you’ll commute.

Frequently asked questions

Is Grove City part of Columbus?

No. Grove City is a separate city in southern Franklin County, founded in 1852 and incorporated as a city in 1959, with a 2020 census population of 41,252. It has its own charter, ordinances, income tax, and police division, so Columbus-only rules such as Renter’s Choice and the Columbus rental registry do not apply inside Grove City. Parts of Columbus and Jackson Township border the city, so confirm the jurisdiction for the exact address.

What is the Grove City income tax rate?

Grove City’s municipal income tax is 2%, the rate RITA lists since at least January 1, 2020. Residents get a 100% credit, capped at 2%, for municipal tax paid to the city where they work, so someone working in Columbus (2.5%) typically owes Grove City nothing more. RITA administers the tax, and the city says residents who earned income while living there, including rental income, must file.

Does Grove City have a rental registry?

We did not find a city rental registration program on Grove City’s website or in the sections of its code we reviewed; the city enforces property upkeep through its Code Compliance division instead. Ohio law still applies: Ohio Revised Code 5323.02 requires owners of residential rental property to register it with the Franklin County Auditor and update it within 60 days of a change. Ask the city before leasing in case local rules change.

Can Grove City landlords refuse housing vouchers?

Grove City’s fair housing chapter, Chapter 550, protects race, color, religion, sex, national origin, age, disability, military and veteran status, ancestry, familial status, pregnancy, sexual orientation, gender identity, and gender expression. Source of income is not on that list, and Ohio has no statewide source-of-income protection, so the city code we reviewed does not require landlords to accept Housing Choice Vouchers. Screening rules must still be applied the same way to every applicant.

Can I run an Airbnb in Grove City?

We did not find a dedicated short-term rental permit chapter in Grove City’s code. The zoning code defines a bed and breakfast inn as an owner-occupied single-family home with stays of no more than 30 consecutive days and no more than five guests at a time, and Chapter 192 levies two 3% lodging taxes on transient accommodations with four or fewer rooms. Check the parcel’s zoning with the Grove City Building Division at 614-277-3075 before listing, and get the owner’s written consent if you rent.

Who handles trash and water in Grove City?

Local Waste Services collects trash, recycling, and yard waste for single-family homes under a city contract and bills customers quarterly; apartments and condos use a private hauler chosen by the owner. Council approved a new 2027–2031 franchise with Local Waste Services that, according to coverage of the July 2026 vote, raises the typical household charge from $19.99 to $23.99 a month. Water and sewer come from the City of Columbus, which is replacing meters so it can move Grove City from quarterly to monthly billing.

Which COTA buses serve Grove City?

COTA Line 3 (Northwest/Harrisburg), a standard line, runs from Grove City up Harrisburg Pike through Downtown Columbus to Northwest Boulevard. Line 61 (Grove City) is a rush-hour line between the Grove City Park and Ride at 2321 Old Stringtown Road and Downtown Columbus. COTA//Plus Grove City provides on-demand trips within its zone.

What is the voucher payment standard in Grove City?

For ZIP 43123, CMHA’s FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents at 100%, effective October 1, 2026, are $1,270 for a studio, $1,360 for one bedroom, $1,620 for two, $1,930 for three, and $2,200 for four bedrooms. These are payment-standard benchmarks for voucher holders, not asking rents.
