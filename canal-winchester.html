Canal Winchester: a renting and living guide to the canal town southeast of Columbus

What landlords and tenants should know about Canal Winchester, the Franklin and Fairfield County city along U.S. Route 33 southeast of Columbus, including its 2% city income tax collected by RITA, the school district income taxes that apply on top of it, the absence of a city rental registry in the published code, city-billed water and sewer, Rumpke trash, how short stays and fair housing look on paper, COTA’s rush-hour Line 52, and HUD voucher figures for ZIP 43110, without inventing market rents.

Last updated November 9, 2026. Facts below come from the City of Canal Winchester and its Codified Ordinances, RITA, the Ohio Revised Code, COTA, NCES, HUD, AHACO, and Wikipedia’s summary of the city, all cited in Sources. Asking-rent medians are not invented here; see Rent Pulse and Fair Market Rents for verified figures.

A separate small city, not a Columbus neighborhood

Canal Winchester is its own city, not part of Columbus. It straddles Franklin and Fairfield counties, with territory in Madison, Violet, and Bloom townships, and covers about 7.85 square miles of land. Reuben Dove and John Colman recorded the first plat for “Winchester” on November 4, 1828, after the Ohio & Erie Canal was routed through Dove’s wheat field; the first canal boat came through in 1831, the town took the name Canal Winchester when its post office opened in 1841, and it was incorporated as a village in 1866. The railroad arrived in 1869.

The 2020 census counted 9,107 residents in 3,410 households, up from 7,101 in 2010 and 4,478 in 2000, and the Census Bureau’s 2025 estimate is 10,058. Of the 3,550 housing units counted in 2020, 77.9% of occupied units were owner-occupied and 22.1% were renter-occupied, with a rental vacancy rate of 6.4%. That makes Canal Winchester a mostly owner-occupied town where rental supply is thinner than in larger suburbs, which is worth knowing whether you are searching for a unit or pricing one.

The city warns that a Canal Winchester mailing address does not always mean an address is inside city limits; ZIP 43110 also reaches parts of Columbus and surrounding townships. The city’s utility billing page points residents to its interactive map, or to the “City/Village” line under Tax Status on the county auditor’s site, to confirm. Columbus-only programs such as the Columbus rental registry and Renter’s Choice do not apply inside the city. Nearby suburbs with their own guides here include Pickerington to the northeast, Reynoldsburg farther north, and Grove City across the south side of the county.

Income tax: 2% city tax with a full work-city credit

Canal Winchester’s municipal income tax rate is 2%, according to the city’s Tax Information page, and it applies both to people who work in the city and to residents who work elsewhere. The Regional Income Tax Agency (RITA) collects it; RITA’s municipality page for Canal Winchester (a member since January 1, 2019) lists a 2.000% rate from January 1, 2020 to the present, with a 100% credit factor and a 2.000% credit limit. In practice, a resident who works in Columbus (2.5%) gets credit for tax paid there up to Canal Winchester’s 2% and typically owes the city nothing more on those wages. That is a meaningful difference from Pickerington, which credits only half a percent.

Filing is mandatory: the city says all residents and taxpayers must file an annual return with RITA even if no tax is due, and online filing is available through RITA. For tax year 2026, the city’s posted interest rate on unpaid tax is 10% per year, set under Ohio Revised Code 718.27 at the federal short-term rate plus five percentage points.

School district income tax: 0.75% or 1.25% on top

This is the line item most renters miss. The city’s tax page lists two school districts inside city limits that levy their own income tax: Canal Winchester Local School District at 0.75% and Bloom-Carroll Local School District at 1.25%. School district income tax is filed with the Ohio Department of Taxation, not RITA, and it is owed by residents of the district no matter where they work, so the work-city credit above does not offset it. The city’s new-residents list also names Pickerington Local Schools as serving part of the area. Before signing a lease, confirm which district serves the exact address, because the same city can carry different total income tax bills depending on the street.

Rental registration: county auditor rule, no city chapter found

We did not find a citywide rental registration, rental permit, or landlord-license chapter in Canal Winchester’s published Codified Ordinances. Part Thirteen of the code covers the electrical, building, and residential codes and the Canal Winchester Property Maintenance Code, not landlord registration. That is a real difference from nearby suburbs such as Reynoldsburg, which has a city rental registry, and Pickerington, which registers some single-family rentals.

Ohio Revised Code 5323.02 still requires owners of residential rental property to file owner and contact information with the county auditor where the property sits, which is the Franklin County Auditor or the Fairfield County Auditor depending on the parcel. Out-of-state owners must designate an in-state contact. Day-to-day habitability rests on Ohio Revised Code Chapter 5321 and the city’s property maintenance code, which applies to rentals the same way it applies to owner-occupied homes.

Fair housing: state and federal rules, no local SOI found

We did not find a Canal Winchester ordinance adding source of income—Housing Choice Vouchers, disability payments, child support, and similar lawful income—to local fair-housing protections. AHACO’s list of central Ohio communities with source-of-income protections includes Bexley, Columbus, Gahanna, Grandview Heights, Pickerington, Reynoldsburg, Upper Arlington, Westerville, Whitehall, and Worthington; Canal Winchester is not on it. Ohio and federal fair housing laws still prohibit discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, disability, and familial status, and complaints can go to the Ohio Civil Rights Commission or HUD.

Because the address could sit near a city line, it matters which city’s rules apply: a 43110 address that is actually inside Columbus is covered by Columbus’s source-of-income protection. Landlords should apply the same written screening criteria to every applicant and document decisions. Tenants who are denied because of a voucher can ask for the reason in writing and contact AHACO or the Ohio Civil Rights Commission.

Short stays and the 6% transient occupancy tax

We did not find a dedicated short-term rental permit chapter, occupancy-limit ordinance, or platform-registration rule aimed at whole-home Airbnbs in Canal Winchester’s published code. What the code does have is Chapter 185, Transient Occupancy Tax. As quoted in Ordinance 23-038, Section 185.02 levies two separate 3% taxes on transactions by which lodging by a hotel is furnished to transient guests, for a combined 6%. The city’s Finance Department describes it as a 6% bed tax imposed when lodging is furnished to transient guests by a hotel, motel, or similar business, with half going to the general fund and half to a bed tax fund that supports Destination: Canal Winchester, the city’s visitors bureau, and a bed tax grant program.

Whether a single whole-home listing counts as a “hotel” under the chapter is a question for the city, not something to assume. Before listing, get a written zoning answer for the parcel, check the lease and any homeowners association rules, and ask the Finance Department (614-837-6937) whether you need to register and remit the 6% tax.

Leases and deposits: Ohio law applies

The core lease rules come from Ohio’s landlord-tenant law, Ohio Revised Code Chapter 5321. It sets landlord duties to keep the unit fit and habitable and make repairs, tenant duties to keep the unit safe and sanitary, the rule that a landlord must return a deposit with an itemized list of deductions within 30 days after the tenancy ends, and the notice expected before a landlord enters, except in emergencies. Our Ohio landlord-tenant law guide covers these rules in more detail, and our Tenant Playbook lists practical steps for tenants.

Utilities: city water, sewer, and stormwater; Rumpke trash

Water, sewer, and stormwater. Unlike suburbs that rely on Columbus for water billing, the City of Canal Winchester itself provides water, sanitary sewer, and stormwater service to addresses inside city limits. Bills are mailed every two months and are due in February, April, June, August, October, and December. To put service in your name, the city says to come to City Hall at 45 E. Waterloo Street during business hours with a photo ID and a closing statement or lease; questions go to the Utility Billing Clerk at 614-837-7716. Rates set by ordinance and effective January 1, 2025 are:

The city also lists a separate water rate of $8.27 per 1,000 gallons for the Canal Pointe water system and a sewer rate of $4.77 for Lithopolis. We did not find a published renter deposit for city utilities, so we do not state one. Because the city bills by the account holder, the lease should say plainly whether the tenant or owner holds the account.

Trash and recycling. Rumpke provides residential trash and recycling, according to the city’s new-residents list (1-800-828-8171). When Rumpke took over service on October 1, 2020, the city’s mailer said residents would get weekly trash and recycling with a 95-gallon trash cart and a 65-gallon recycling cart, Thursday collection, quarterly invoices from Rumpke, and a monthly price of $19.50 at that time. We did not find a current published rate, so confirm today’s price with Rumpke rather than relying on the 2020 figure.

Other providers. The city’s new-residents page lists Columbia Gas of Ohio (800-344-4077) for natural gas and South Central Power (800-282-5064) for electric. Confirm the electric provider for the exact address, and settle in the lease who holds each account.

Getting around: COTA Line 52 at rush hour

Canal Winchester has commuter-only fixed-route service. COTA Line 52 (Canal Winchester) is a rush-hour line between the Canal Winchester Park and Ride and Downtown Columbus, ending at the Spring Street Terminal. COTA’s published schedule data shows three weekday morning trips toward downtown and three afternoon trips back to the Park and Ride, with no weekend service. COTA’s timetable lists a $2 adult fare with free transfers valid for two hours, a $4.50 day pass, and a $62 31-day pass, with half-price fares for seniors, veterans, and qualifying riders.

Outside those commute windows, a car is the practical default; U.S. Route 33 connects the city to Interstate 270 and downtown. For bigger-picture transit plans in the region, see Mobility & the Future.

Schools and daily life

Most of the city is served by Canal Winchester Local School District. NCES lists the district (ID 3904694) as serving grades PK–12 with 5 schools and reports 3,706 students for the 2024–2025 school year, with about 268 classroom teachers (FTE) and a student–teacher ratio of 13.83. The district office is at 100 Washington Street. Parts of the city fall in Bloom-Carroll Local Schools, and the city also lists Pickerington Local Schools, so confirm the assignment for the exact address with the district.

Daily life centers on the historic downtown along High and Waterloo streets, with antique shops, the National Barber Museum, and the Columbus Metropolitan Library’s Canal Winchester branch. The city hosts the Blues and Ribs Fest in early August, a Labor Day Festival with a Monday parade, and Christmas in the Village in early December. The Bergstresser Bridge, the only standing wooden covered bridge in Franklin County, is here, as is the U.S. headquarters of BrewDog. Diley Ridge Medical Center provides emergency care nearby.

What landlords and tenants should check before signing

  • City or not. Confirm the address is inside Canal Winchester and not a Columbus or township parcel with a 43110 mailing address; taxes, utilities, and fair-housing rules differ.
  • School district tax. Find out whether the address is in Canal Winchester Local (0.75%) or Bloom-Carroll Local (1.25%), and budget for that tax separately from the city’s 2%.
  • Registration. No city rental registry found; keep the county auditor filing current under Ohio Revised Code 5323.02 for Franklin or Fairfield County.
  • Vouchers. No local source-of-income protection found; apply consistent written screening either way.
  • Short stays. No dedicated permit chapter found; ask the city about zoning and the 6% transient occupancy tax before listing.
  • Utility accounts. The city bills water, sewer, and stormwater every two months and needs a lease to open an account; Rumpke bills trash directly.
  • Transit. COTA Line 52 is rush-hour only from the Park and Ride; plan on a car for most trips.

Frequently asked questions

Is Canal Winchester part of Columbus?

No. Canal Winchester is a separate city in Franklin and Fairfield counties southeast of downtown Columbus, with a 2020 census population of 9,107. It has its own mayor and council and collects its own income tax through RITA, so Columbus-only rules such as Renter’s Choice and the Columbus rental registry do not apply. A Canal Winchester mailing address (ZIP 43110) can sit outside city limits, so confirm the jurisdiction for the exact address with the city’s map or the county auditor.

What is the Canal Winchester income tax rate?

The city’s income tax rate is 2.0%, collected by the Regional Income Tax Agency (RITA). RITA lists a 100% credit for tax paid to another municipality, capped at 2.0%, so a resident who pays Columbus’s 2.5% on wages typically owes Canal Winchester nothing more on those wages. All residents must file an annual return with RITA even if no tax is due.

Do Canal Winchester residents pay a school district income tax?

Yes, depending on the address. The city’s tax page lists two school districts inside city limits that levy an income tax: Canal Winchester Local School District at 0.75% and Bloom-Carroll Local School District at 1.25%. School district income tax is filed with the Ohio Department of Taxation, separate from the city tax, and it applies to residents of the district regardless of where they work, so check which district serves a rental before signing.

Does Canal Winchester have a rental registry?

We did not find a citywide rental registration or rental permit chapter in Canal Winchester’s published Codified Ordinances; Part Thirteen covers building, residential, and property maintenance codes rather than landlord registration. Ohio Revised Code 5323.02 still requires owners of residential rental property to register with the county auditor, which is Franklin County or Fairfield County depending on the parcel.

Can Canal Winchester landlords refuse housing vouchers?

Canal Winchester is not on AHACO’s list of central Ohio cities that have adopted source-of-income protections, and we did not find a local ordinance adding source of income as a protected class. Ohio and federal fair housing laws still prohibit discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, disability, and familial status. Landlords should apply the same written screening standards to every applicant.

Can I run an Airbnb in Canal Winchester?

We did not find a dedicated short-term rental permit chapter. Chapter 185 of the city code levies a transient occupancy tax of 3% plus an additional 3%, a combined 6%, on lodging furnished to transient guests, and the city describes it as a 6% bed tax on lodging furnished by a hotel, motel, or similar business. Silence on whole-home listings is not permission, so get a written zoning answer from the city and ask the Finance Department whether the tax applies before listing.

Who handles water and trash in Canal Winchester?

The City of Canal Winchester bills water, sanitary sewer, and stormwater for addresses inside city limits, on a bimonthly schedule; starting service requires a photo ID and a lease or closing statement at City Hall. Rates effective January 1, 2025 are $7.13 per 1,000 gallons for water, $6.62 per 1,000 gallons for sanitary sewer, and $3.50 per month for residential stormwater. Rumpke collects residential trash and recycling.

Does COTA serve Canal Winchester?

Yes, but only at rush hour. COTA Line 52 runs weekdays between the Canal Winchester Park and Ride and the Spring Street Terminal downtown, with three morning trips toward downtown and three afternoon trips back and no weekend service. The adult fare is $2 with free transfers valid for two hours. For most daily trips a car is the practical default.

What is the voucher payment standard in Canal Winchester?

For ZIP 43110, HUD’s FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents, effective October 1, 2026, are $1,260 for a studio, $1,340 for one bedroom, $1,600 for two, $1,910 for three, and $2,170 for four bedrooms. These are payment-standard benchmarks for voucher holders, not asking rents. ZIP 43110 also covers parts of Columbus and nearby townships, and the housing authority that administers a voucher sets the actual payment standard.
