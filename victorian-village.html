Victorian Village: a Columbus renting and living guide

What landlords and tenants should know about Victorian Village—how it grew along Neil Avenue between downtown and Ohio State, its Queen Anne, Italianate, and Second Empire homes and converted apartments, the Victorian Village Commission’s exterior review, Short North permit parking, COTA service on High Street and Neil Avenue, Goodale Park, and ZIP-level voucher figures—without inventing market rents.

Last updated October 23, 2026. Neighborhood facts below come from the City of Columbus, the Victorian Village Commission, Columbus Recreation and Parks, COTA timetables, Columbus City Schools, local history sources, and CMHA tables cited in Sources. Asking-rent medians are not invented here; see Rent Pulse and Fair Market Rents for verified figures.

Where Victorian Village sits

Victorian Village is a neighborhood inside the City of Columbus, north and slightly west of downtown. It is commonly described as bounded by Goodale Street on the south, West Fifth Avenue on the north, North High Street on the east, and Harrison Avenue on the west. Goodale Street puts the southern edge a block from the North Market and the Greater Columbus Convention Center, and West Fifth Avenue is about half a mile south of Ohio State’s campus. Because it is part of Columbus itself, Columbus city services, the Columbus income tax, and Columbus City Code rules for landlords and tenants apply.

Neil Avenue is the main north–south street through the middle of the neighborhood and continues north through the Ohio State campus. The High Street frontage is shared with the Short North Arts District, so a “Short North” listing can sit inside Victorian Village, and Italian Village is directly across High Street to the east. For the commercial strip, see our Short North guide; for the neighborhood across High, see our Italian Village guide. Other spotlights start at the Neighborhoods hub, including German Village, Olde Towne East, Clintonville, and Dublin.

How Victorian Village got here

In 1827 Columbus businessman William Neil bought farmland just north of downtown, and by 1853 he owned the land from west of North High Street to the Olentangy River. The road he built to reach his farm became Neil Avenue. After his death the farm was subdivided, and its southern portion became one of Columbus’s first suburbs. Streetcar lines reached Neil Avenue in 1879, electric streetcars ran on High Street in 1888 and on Neil Avenue in 1891, and the part of the Neil farm west of Neil Avenue and south of West Fifth Avenue was platted between 1888 and 1902, largely for large homes. By 1920 most parcels held Victorian-era houses in Italianate, Queen Anne, Second Empire, Stick, and Foursquare styles.

Goodale Park anchored the south end from the start. Columbus Recreation and Parks says Dr. Lincoln Goodale, Franklin County’s first physician, donated 44 acres to the city in 1851 to create the park, with a deed requiring that it be kept forever as a public park. The neighborhood declined after the 1920s as cars let residents and businesses move to the suburbs, and the nearby Flytown, Short North, and Italian Village areas declined too. Urban renewal in the late 1950s cleared nearly 300 houses south of St. Francis of Assisi Church, according to the parish history summarized by Wikipedia.

Renewed interest came in the 1970s, following German Village’s revival. The City created the Victorian Village Historic District and its Commission in 1973, and the area is part of the Near North Side Historic District listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1980. Landmarks include the 1895 Sells house, built for circus owner Peter Sells and designed by Frank Packard, and St. Francis of Assisi Church, built in 1896.

What the housing looks like

Most of the housing is late-19th- and early-20th-century brick and frame houses on narrow lots with alleys behind them, many with front porches facing tree-lined streets. Some of the large houses have been split into apartments, so a rental can be a full single-family house, one unit in a converted house, half of a double, or a unit in a newer building near High Street or Goodale Street. Ask which of these you are looking at, which walls, stairs, and basements are shared, and how utilities are metered. Older buildings can also mean older wiring, windows, and plumbing; Ohio’s landlord duties to keep the unit fit and habitable are covered in our landlord-tenant law guide.

The Victorian Village Commission and exterior work

The City of Columbus says the Victorian Village Commission and the Victorian Village Historic District were established by Ordinance 1788-73 in 1973, and the Commission’s guidelines were adopted in 1989. The Commission has seven mayoral appointees who serve without pay. It reviews applications and issues Certificates of Appropriateness (COAs) for exterior alterations at properties inside the district under Columbus City Code chapters 3116 and 3119 and the Victorian Village guidelines. The High Street corridor within Victorian Village and Italian Village is reviewed under the Short North Design Guidelines.

For landlords this matters for routine work as well as big projects. Replacing windows, doors, roofing, siding, porches, or fences, or adding to a building, can need a COA before a building permit. COA applications are filed through the City’s Citizens Access Portal. Regular hearings are generally held on the second Wednesday of the month at 4 p.m. at 111 N. Front St., with a business meeting about two weeks earlier, and hearings may be live-streamed on the City’s YouTube channel. The City’s 2026 schedule lists a November 9 hearing (moved because of the holiday) with an October 15 application deadline, and a December 9 hearing with a November 12 deadline; the first 2027 hearing is January 13, 2027, with a December 17 deadline. Dates can change, so confirm with the Historic Preservation Office before you plan work around them.

Parking: the Short North plan

Streets near High Street are managed under the City of Columbus Short North parking plan, whose parking benefit district committee includes the Victorian Village Commission chair. The City’s plan detail sheet sets meters and permit zones at a three-hour limit from 8 a.m. to 10 p.m. and permit-only parking from 10 p.m. to 8 a.m. in permit zones. Residential permits are limited to one per licensed driver and two per address, at $25 per permit plus an additional $25 per address for guest privileges. Rates vary by zone and time of day, and the Short North Alliance notes that Sundays and City-recognized holidays are free at on-street spaces.

Not every address on a permitted street is eligible, and blocks farther west toward Harrison Avenue may have different rules. Before signing a lease, check the exact address on the City’s permit eligibility map, ask whether the unit has an off-street or alley space, and read the posted signs on the block. Goodale Park has its own lots off Park Street and Dennison Avenue for park visitors. Landlords should say in the listing whether parking is included rather than assuming a permit will be available.

Getting around: COTA on High Street and Neil Avenue

COTA Line 2 runs on North High Street along the neighborhood’s eastern edge. Through the middle of Victorian Village, Line 8 (Karl/S. High/Parsons), which COTA lists as a frequent line, and Line 30 (Hilliard–OSU) both run on Neil Avenue, connecting the neighborhood with downtown to the south and Ohio State to the north; COTA introduced Line 30 to link Hilliard, Upper Arlington, the OSU campus, and downtown. Eligible Short North workers and residents may qualify for the Short North C-pass; see our Short North guide. Check COTA’s current timetables for frequency on the hours and days you actually travel.

Goodale Park, schools, and nearby landmarks

Columbus Recreation and Parks lists Goodale Park at 120 W. Goodale St. as a 32.56-acre regional park, open 7 a.m. to 11 p.m., with ball diamonds, basketball, pickleball and tennis courts, a playground, a pond, and a shelterhouse built in 1912. The park is known as the home of ComFest, a major annual festival. Victorian Village is in the Columbus City Schools district; Hubbard Elementary School is at 104 W. Hubbard Ave. School assignments follow the address, so families should confirm the assigned school with the district before signing.

Vouchers, ZIPs, and what the SAFMR tables say

Victorian Village spans ZIP codes 43201 and 43215, and the two carry very different voucher figures. CMHA’s FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents, effective October 1, 2026, at 100% of SAFMR, list $1,420 for a one-bedroom and $1,690 for a two-bedroom in 43201, and $1,970 for a one-bedroom and $2,350 for a two-bedroom in 43215. These are payment-standard benchmarks, not asking rents and not automatic rent approvals; contract rent still goes through rent-reasonableness review, and utility allowances affect the gross-rent comparison. Our Fair Market Rents explainer covers how they work.

Inside Columbus city limits, Columbus City Code 4551.03 restricts refusing to rent based on lawful source of income, including vouchers. That rule and Ohio’s deposit, notice, and repair rules are covered in our Ohio and Columbus landlord-tenant law guide.

What landlords and tenants should check before signing

  • Exterior work. Confirm whether planned repairs need a Certificate of Appropriateness and which hearing they would go to.
  • Unit type. Know whether it is a full house, a unit in a converted house, half of a double, or a newer building, and what is shared.
  • Parking. Check permit eligibility for the exact address, off-street or alley space, and guest options.
  • Utilities. Ask which utilities are separately metered and who pays for water, trash, and common-area lighting.
  • Noise and location. Units near High Street, Goodale Park events, and Neil Avenue see more activity than interior blocks.
  • ZIP for vouchers. Pull the ZIP from the address, not the neighborhood name, before doing voucher math.

Frequently asked questions

Where is Victorian Village in Columbus?

Victorian Village is a neighborhood inside the City of Columbus, north and slightly west of downtown. It is commonly described as bounded by Goodale Street on the south, West Fifth Avenue on the north, North High Street on the east, and Harrison Avenue on the west, with Neil Avenue as its main street. The Short North shares its High Street edge, and Italian Village sits across High Street to the east.

Is Victorian Village a historic district?

Yes. The City of Columbus says the Victorian Village Commission and the Victorian Village Historic District were established by Ordinance 1788-73 in 1973, and the Commission’s guidelines were adopted in 1989. The Commission has seven mayoral appointees and issues Certificates of Appropriateness for exterior alterations inside the district under Columbus City Code chapters 3116 and 3119.

Do I need approval to change the outside of a rental in Victorian Village?

Usually, yes. Exterior alterations at properties inside the Victorian Village Historic District need a Certificate of Appropriateness, applied for through the City’s Citizens Access Portal. Regular hearings are generally on the second Wednesday of the month at 4 p.m. at 111 N. Front St. The City’s 2026 schedule lists a November 9 hearing (moved for the holiday) and a December 9 hearing, with a November 12 application deadline for December.

How does parking work in Victorian Village?

The blocks near High Street are managed under the City’s Short North parking plan, which uses three-hour limits from 8 a.m. to 10 p.m. and permit-only parking overnight in permit zones. Residential permits are $25 each, one per licensed driver and up to two per address, with an additional $25 per address for guest privileges. Not every address is eligible, so check the exact address before you sign.

Which COTA lines serve Victorian Village?

COTA Line 2 runs on North High Street along the neighborhood’s eastern edge. Line 8 (Karl/S. High/Parsons), which COTA lists as a frequent line, and Line 30 (Hilliard–OSU) both run on Neil Avenue through the middle of the neighborhood toward downtown and Ohio State. Check COTA’s current timetables for the hours you travel.

What is the voucher payment standard in Victorian Village?

Victorian Village spans ZIPs 43201 and 43215. CMHA’s FY2027 SAFMR figures effective October 1, 2026, at 100% of SAFMR, list $1,420 for a one-bedroom and $1,690 for a two-bedroom in 43201, and $1,970 for a one-bedroom and $2,350 for a two-bedroom in 43215. Always confirm the unit’s ZIP on CMHA’s current table.
