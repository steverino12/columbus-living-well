Pickerington: a renting and living guide to the suburb east of Columbus

What landlords and tenants should know about Pickerington, the Fairfield and Franklin County city along Hill Road and Refugee Road east of Columbus, including its 1% city income tax, the single-family rental registration chapter, who bills water, sewer, and trash, how short stays and fair housing look in the code, Pickerington Local Schools, the LINK bus loop, and HUD voucher figures for ZIP 43147, without inventing market rents.

Last updated November 6, 2026. Facts below come from the City of Pickerington and its Codified Ordinances, the Ohio Revised Code, Fairfield County Transit, NCES, HUD, AHACO, and Wikipedia’s summary of the city, all cited in Sources. Asking-rent medians are not invented here; see Rent Pulse and Fair Market Rents for verified figures.

A separate suburb, not a Columbus neighborhood

Pickerington is its own city, not part of Columbus. It sits in Fairfield and Franklin counties, just east of Columbus, and was founded in 1815 as Jacksonville before being renamed in 1827 for its founder, Abraham Pickering. The Ohio Secretary of State certified it as a city in 1991, and the Ohio Legislature designated it the “Violet Capital of Ohio” in 1996. The 2020 census counted 23,094 residents in 7,914 households and 8,373 housing units, with a rental vacancy rate of 13.3%. The Census Bureau’s 2024 estimate is 25,814 residents, and the city grew from 9,792 people in 2000 to 18,291 in 2010.

The city covers about 10.2 square miles, making it the second-largest city in Fairfield County after Lancaster. It runs on a mayor–council–manager form of government with a seven-member council, a city manager, and its own police department. Hill Road (State Route 256) and Refugee Road are the main corridors, with Olde Pickerington Village as the historic center. Because the 43147 ZIP code also covers parts of Violet Township that are outside city limits, the city itself warns that not every 43147 address is inside Pickerington, and it publishes a street listing for checking. Verify the parcel before applying any of the city rules below. Columbus-only programs, such as the Columbus rental registry and Renter’s Choice, don’t apply here.

Hill Road (State Route 256) runs from Main Street in Reynoldsburg to Olde Pickerington Village, so Reynoldsburg is the closest neighbor with its own guide here; comparing it with Gahanna and Grove City helps show how tax and registration rules differ across the region. OhioHealth Pickerington Methodist Hospital, which opened as a full-service hospital in December 2023, is a major employer; the city’s 2024 financial report lists it with 1,694 employees, ahead of Pickerington Local School District at 1,332, Kroger at 371, and Roosters at 262.

Income tax: 1%, city-administered, with a half-percent credit

Pickerington’s municipal income tax rate is 1%, according to the city’s Income Tax FAQs, and Section 880.02 of the codified ordinances sets the rate. The city runs its own tax department at City Hall, 100 Lockville Road, and its FAQ tells filers to e-file through its own system or mail paper returns to that address. Some third-party tax sites describe the city as a member of the Regional Income Tax Agency, but the city’s own pages describe in-house administration, so we follow the city. The tax applies to earned income, and the city notes it uses the higher of Medicare wages (box 5) or local wages (box 18) on a W-2.

The credit for tax paid to another city is partial. The city says it gives a credit of up to one-half of one percent (.005) of the wages taxed elsewhere, and its example is $50,000 of Columbus-taxed wages producing a $250 credit. In practice, a Pickerington resident who commutes to Columbus pays Columbus first and still owes Pickerington about 0.5% on those wages, which is a real difference from suburbs such as Whitehall that fully credit the work city. A W-2 naming the city that taxed the wages is required, and a refund from the work city can reduce the credit.

All residents 18 or older must file annually, even with no income, unless fully retired or permanently disabled, and the return is due April 15 for calendar-year filers. Non-residents file only when they have income earned in the city that was not fully withheld on, or a business or rental property in the city, so owners of Pickerington rentals should plan to file. Social Security and pension income are not taxed by the city. Questions on school district tax go to the Ohio Department of Taxation at 1-800-282-1780, because the Pickerington Local School District income tax (shown as “Pick 2307” on W-2s) is not administered by the city and cannot be claimed as a credit on the city return. We did not find the school district tax rate in the sources we reviewed, so we do not state it.

Rental registration: Chapter 1492 for owners of more than five single-family homes

Pickerington does not run a citywide rental license for every landlord, but it does have a registration chapter aimed at larger single-family owners. Under Chapter 1492, effective January 1, 2023, every owner or agent in charge of more than five single-family residential dwelling units rented or held out for rent in the city must register them with the City Manager’s office by March 31 each year, paying a non-refundable $250 registration fee per unit. Units acquired during the year must be registered within 30 days of acquiring title. The definition of a single-family unit covers an entire home and expressly does not include apartments, condominiums, or duplexes.

The registration packet includes forms for each unit, proof that property taxes are fully paid or that the owner is on a county payment plan in good standing, and tenant information forms. Owners must also pay a $250 tenant rental license fee per unit when the tenant forms are returned, and that fee is due again when the tenant changes. Payment triggers a request for a general inspection covering safety, health, habitability, and compliance with city, county, and state codes; the owner may deny entry, in which case the city can pursue an administrative warrant for good cause. If a unit fails, the city mails a list of violations within 30 days. Registration fees are not transferable and must be reapplied for on a change of ownership. The chapter also lets an owner who does not live in Fairfield or Franklin County name a local agent in charge. A 2022 local news report described the proposal as also including a 3% excise tax on gross rents; in the sections we read (1492.01 and 1492.02) we saw no such tax, so we do not report one.

For landlords with five or fewer single-family homes, and for apartment and duplex owners, this chapter does not apply on its face, but other rules do. Ohio Revised Code 5323.02 requires owners of residential rental property to register it with the county auditor, which is the Fairfield County Auditor for most of the city and the Franklin County Auditor for the Franklin County portion. The city directs real estate tax questions to the Fairfield County Auditor at 740-652-7030. Day-to-day upkeep is governed by the city’s Property Maintenance Code in Chapter 1490, which applies to rentals and owner-occupied homes alike.

Fair housing: what the code shows and what we could not confirm

Pickerington’s civil rights provisions sit in Chapter 622 of the Codified Ordinances. As published, Section 622.02 makes it unlawful to refuse to sell, rent, lease, or finance housing, to discriminate in terms and conditions, to misrepresent availability, to advertise a preference, or to honor a restrictive covenant because of race, color, religion, ancestry, or national origin. Violations are punishable by a fine of up to $500, in addition to the civil remedy in Section 622.03. Chapter 622 cross-references the Ohio Civil Rights Commission and Ohio Revised Code Chapter 4112, which cover additional state and federal protected classes.

Source of income is where the sources disagree. AHACO, the Columbus-area fair housing group, lists Pickerington among central Ohio communities that adopted source-of-income protections in 2024, alongside Bexley, Columbus, Gahanna, Grandview Heights, Reynoldsburg, Upper Arlington, Westerville, Whitehall, and Worthington. But the Chapter 622 text we read did not contain source-of-income language, and we could not find the 2024 ordinance on the city’s legislation page. Until the current text is confirmed with the city clerk, treat the voucher rule here as unverified, not as settled either way. Landlords should apply the same written screening criteria to every applicant, and tenants who are denied because of a voucher should ask for the reason in writing and consider contacting AHACO or the Ohio Civil Rights Commission.

Short stays and lodging tax

We did not find a dedicated short-term rental permit chapter in Pickerington’s code. What we did find is Chapter 884, the hotel and motel tax. It defines a hotel as an establishment with five or more guest rooms and a transient accommodation as one with four or fewer, defines transient guests as people staying fewer than 30 consecutive days, and levies a 3% excise tax on lodging furnished to them under Ohio Revised Code 5739.02(C)(1). Revenue goes to the general fund, to promoting the city for conventions and events, to cultural services, and to emergency human services. Vendors collect the tax from guests and must file returns under Section 884.06.

The planning and zoning code regulates hotels and motels in Section 1286.24 as conditional uses in C2 and M1 districts, with requirements on size, height, and arterial access. We found no section that expressly addresses whole-home rentals in residential districts, so a host should not read the silence as permission. Get a written answer from the city’s planning and zoning staff on the parcel, check the lease and any homeowners association rules, and plan to collect the 3% tax if you host stays under 30 days.

Leases and deposits: Ohio law applies

The core lease rules come from Ohio’s landlord-tenant law, Ohio Revised Code Chapter 5321. It sets landlord duties to keep the unit fit and habitable and make repairs, tenant duties to keep the unit safe and sanitary, the rule that a landlord must return a deposit with an itemized list of deductions within 30 days after the tenancy ends, and the notice expected before a landlord enters, except in emergencies. Our Ohio landlord-tenant law guide covers these rules in more detail. Our Tenant Playbook lists practical steps for tenants.

Utilities: city water and sewer, Rumpke trash

Water, sewer, and storm water. The City of Pickerington bills water and sewer monthly for the previous month’s use. Bills go out on the first of the month and are due on the 15th, with a 10% penalty after that. The city’s posted water rates are a $4.50 monthly service charge plus $5.07 per thousand gallons through 12,000 gallons, and $7.30 per thousand gallons above that. Sewer is $24.66 minimum per month for the first 3,000 gallons and $8.22 per thousand gallons over that, calculated on all water through the meter, including lawn watering and pools, unless the owner installs a $455.60 deduct meter. The storm water fee is $4.50 per month for a single-family residence. The city’s billing page says rates normally rise 3% each April 1, but a 2026 city resolution (2026-07R) suspended the April 1, 2026 increase, so confirm current rates with the utility office at 614-833-2289.

Opening an account. The city’s utility application says renters must provide a copy of the lease and the landlord’s name, address, and phone number, and all renters are required to pay a $100 deposit to establish service. Applicants bring photo ID to City Hall. The form also lists other providers for the area: Columbia Gas, South Central Power, and for Springcreek addresses in Violet Township, Fairfield County Water/Sewer at 614-575-5556. Tenants should ask before signing which provider serves the address and whether the water account stays in the owner’s name.

Trash and recycling. The city’s Service Department and new-resident page identify Rumpke as the trash and recycling provider; residents call Rumpke at 1-800-828-8171 or use its request-service page to start service. The city’s utility application notes that Rumpke offers a senior discount and recycling options. We did not find a published Rumpke rate for Pickerington, so we do not state one. Hazardous waste questions go to the Coshocton-Fairfield-Licking-Perry Solid Waste District at 740-349-6308.

Getting around: the LINK, not COTA

Pickerington is outside COTA’s fixed-route network as far as we could find, and COTA’s on-demand COTA//Plus zones, as we understand them, do not include Pickerington. Public transit here is Fairfield County Transit’s LINK. Route 6 (Pickerington Loop) is a flex route in Pickerington and Violet Township running an hourly loop Monday through Friday from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Stops include City Hall at 100 Lockville Road, the Pickerington Senior Center, Giant Eagle, Walmart, Kroger, the Pickerington Library, the Fairfield County Building, OhioHealth, Grandhaven, Highland Park, and Turnberry; several are served on request, by calling 740-681-5086 in advance.

Route 7 (Lancaster–Pickerington Connector) runs at 8:00 a.m., noon, 1:00 p.m., and 5:00 p.m. on weekdays. LINK Line fares are $0.50 for the general public, with $0.25 for the mobility program and $0.10 transfers. LINK Direct offers reserved curb-to-curb rides: $2 for advance-day general public trips within Fairfield County, $5 same-day, reserved at 740-681-5086 Monday through Friday from 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. The LINK lists COTA as a connector service. For most commuters, Interstate 70 on the city’s north edge and Hill Road toward Reynoldsburg are the practical routes, so car access matters more here than in the core Columbus neighborhoods. Schools and daily life

Pickerington is served by the Pickerington Local School District. NCES lists the district as an open regular local district serving grades PK–12 with 16 schools and reports 11,729 students for the 2024–2025 school year; the 2025–2026 NCES figure was still preliminary. The district’s own demographic presentation projects 11,962 K–12 students for 2025–2026, or 12,456 including preschool and electronic-school enrollment. Wikipedia’s summary of the district lists two high schools, Pickerington High School Central and Pickerington High School North, plus an alternative school, two junior highs, three middle schools, and seven elementary schools. District lines extend beyond city limits and the city does not administer school boundaries, so confirm the assignment for the exact address with the district.

Daily life centers on Olde Pickerington Village, Hill Road retail around Walmart and Kroger, and the city’s parks, including the community pool, trails, and an arboretum; Wikipedia lists more than 158 acres of parkland. The Motorcycle Hall of Fame museum sits near Interstate 70, and Pickerington Ponds Metro Park is nearby. Top employers per the city’s financial reports include OhioHealth, the school district, Kroger, Roosters, Cracker Barrel, and JPMorgan Chase.

Vouchers and what the SAFMR table says

Pickerington’s ZIP code is 43147. HUD’s FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents, effective October 1, 2026, list the following for 43147 in the Columbus, OH HUD Metro FMR Area, which includes Fairfield and Franklin counties. These are payment-standard benchmarks, not asking rents and not automatic approvals; contract rent still goes through rent-reasonableness review, and utility allowances affect the gross-rent comparison. Because 43147 also covers areas outside city limits, these figures apply to the whole ZIP. The ZIP is not on the short list CMHA published for its own payment-standard table, so we use HUD’s table directly, and the housing authority that administers a particular voucher sets the actual payment standard.

What landlords and tenants should check before signing

  • City or township. Confirm the address is inside Pickerington and not a Violet Township parcel with a 43147 mailing address; utilities and taxes differ.
  • Registration. Owners of more than five single-family rentals: file the Chapter 1492 packet by March 31 and budget $250 per unit plus $250 per tenant change. All owners: keep the county auditor registration current.
  • Income tax. Residents 18 and older file with the city’s tax department; the work-city credit is only 0.5%. Owners of city rentals may need to file as non-residents.
  • Vouchers. Source-of-income protection is reported by AHACO but not confirmed in the code text we read; get the current ordinance from the city.
  • Short stays. No dedicated permit chapter found; get a written zoning answer and collect the 3% lodging tax on stays under 30 days.
  • Utility accounts. City water and sewer need a $100 renter deposit; trash is Rumpke. Settle in the lease who holds each bill.
  • Transit. Plan on a car; the LINK Route 6 loop runs weekdays 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. only.

Frequently asked questions

Is Pickerington part of Columbus?

No. Pickerington is a separate city in Fairfield and Franklin counties, just east of Columbus, with a 2020 census population of 23,094. It has its own city council, city manager, police department, income tax office, and utility department, so Columbus-only rules such as Renter’s Choice and the Columbus rental registry do not apply. Not every 43147 mailing address is inside city limits, because parts of Violet Township share the ZIP, so confirm the jurisdiction for the exact address.

What is the Pickerington income tax rate?

The city’s income tax rate is 1%, according to the city’s Income Tax FAQs, and the city’s tax department collects it directly. Residents who work in another taxing city get a partial credit of up to one-half of one percent (0.5%) of the wages taxed there, so a Columbus commuter still owes Pickerington roughly 0.5% on those wages. Residents 18 and older must file annually unless fully retired or permanently disabled. The Pickerington Local School District has a separate income tax that is filed with the Ohio Department of Taxation, not the city.

Does Pickerington have a rental registry?

Only for larger single-family landlords. Chapter 1492 requires an owner or agent in charge of more than five single-family rental homes to register them by March 31 each year for $250 per unit, plus a $250 tenant rental license fee per unit that triggers an inspection and is due again when the tenant changes. It does not cover apartments, condominiums, or duplexes. Ohio Revised Code 5323.02 separately requires owners of residential rentals to register with the county auditor.

Can Pickerington landlords refuse housing vouchers?

We could not verify this from the city code. A fair housing group, AHACO, lists Pickerington among central Ohio cities that adopted source-of-income protection in 2024. However, Chapter 622, Section 622.02 in the city’s published code lists race, color, religion, ancestry, and national origin and did not show source-of-income language when we read it. Treat the rule as unconfirmed, ask the city clerk for the current ordinance text, and apply the same written screening standards to every applicant either way.

Can I run an Airbnb in Pickerington?

We did not find a dedicated short-term rental permit chapter in the city code. Chapter 884 levies a 3% excise tax on lodging furnished to transient guests staying fewer than 30 consecutive days, and it defines a transient accommodation as an establishment with four or fewer guest rooms. The zoning code regulates hotels and motels as conditional uses in C2 and M1 districts, but we found no rule that expressly addresses whole-home rentals, so get a written zoning answer from the city and the owner’s consent before listing.

Who handles water and trash in Pickerington?

The City of Pickerington bills water, sewer, and storm water monthly, with bills sent on the first and due on the 15th; renters must pay a $100 deposit to open an account. Trash and recycling come from Rumpke under the city’s arrangement, and residents contact Rumpke directly to start service. Some Violet Township addresses, called Springcreek in the city’s application form, use Fairfield County water and sewer instead, so confirm which provider serves the address.

Does COTA serve Pickerington?

We did not find a COTA fixed route in Pickerington. Fairfield County Transit’s LINK runs Route 6, an hourly flex loop in Pickerington and Violet Township, Monday through Friday from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., with stops at Giant Eagle, Walmart, Kroger, the library, and City Hall. Route 7 connects Lancaster and Pickerington four times a day, and LINK Direct offers reserved curb-to-curb rides at 740-681-5086. LINK Line fares are $0.50 for the general public.

What is the voucher payment standard in Pickerington?

For ZIP 43147, HUD’s FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents, effective October 1, 2026, are $1,600 for a studio, $1,710 for one bedroom, $2,040 for two, $2,430 for three, and $2,770 for four bedrooms. These are payment-standard benchmarks for voucher holders, not asking rents. The ZIP also covers areas outside city limits, and the figures sit in HUD’s Columbus metro table rather than in CMHA’s own published ZIP list.
