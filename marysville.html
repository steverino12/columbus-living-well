Marysville: a renting and living guide to the Union County seat northwest of Columbus

What landlords and tenants should know about the City of Marysville, the Union County seat on U.S. Route 33, including its 1.5% city income tax with no credit for tax paid where you work, the zero school district income tax in Marysville Exempted Village Schools, why Ohio’s county-auditor rental filing does not reach Union County, the city’s vacant building registration and landlord report, the county lodging tax that now covers short-term rentals, the single city bill for water, sewer, stormwater, and trash, the $150 tenant utility deposit, the lack of COTA service, and HUD voucher figures for ZIP 43040, without inventing market rents.

Last updated November 13, 2026. Facts below come from the City of Marysville, the Ohio Department of Taxation, the Ohio Revised Code, Union County, Union County Tourism, the Union County Health Department, the Marysville Journal-Tribune, COTA, NCES, HUD, the Marion Metropolitan Housing Authority, AHACO, and Wikipedia’s summaries of the city and county, all cited in Sources. Asking-rent medians are not invented here; see Rent Pulse and Fair Market Rents for verified figures.

A county seat with its own economy

Marysville is a separate city and the county seat of Union County, about 27 miles northwest of Columbus. Wikipedia’s summary says Samuel W. Culbertson founded the town in 1819 along Mill Creek and named it after his daughter Mary, that it was incorporated as a village in 1840, and that its tree-lined streets earned it the nickname “the Shaded City” by 1888. The Union County Courthouse, completed in 1883, still stands in Uptown, the city’s historic center.

The 2020 census counted 25,571 residents, 8,783 households, and 9,327 housing units on about 16.57 square miles, according to Wikipedia’s summary of the census. Of those housing units, 5.8% were vacant; the homeowner vacancy rate was 1.1% and the rental vacancy rate was 7.8%. The median age was 35.5, and 37.1% of households had children under 18. The city has grown quickly, from 9,656 residents in 1990 to 15,942 in 2000 and 22,094 in 2010, and Union County as a whole grew 20% between 2010 and 2020, to 62,784 people.

Marysville is a job center in its own right rather than a pure bedroom suburb. Scotts Miracle-Gro, founded in Marysville in 1868, keeps its global headquarters and research operations on a corporate campus east of the city. Honda’s Marysville Auto Plant, which opened in 1982 about six miles northwest of town, has built the Accord since then, and Nestlé runs a product technology center on the west side. Memorial Health operates a 107-bed hospital in the city. For renters, that mix means demand from plant and office workers who may never commute to Columbus at all, which matters for the income tax discussion below.

Because Marysville is a separate city, Columbus-only rules such as the Columbus rental registry, Renter’s Choice, and Columbus source-of-income protections do not apply. The Marysville mailing area (ZIP 43040) also reaches into surrounding townships. The city’s fair housing page, for example, sends residents who live outside the corporation limits but within Union County to a separate county number, and the city bills county residents who tap into city water and sewer lines at different rates. Confirm that a parcel is inside city limits before relying on anything below.

Income tax: 1.5% with no credit for work-city tax

The City of Marysville levies a 1.5% income tax, a rate its Income Tax Division says has applied since July 1, 2010. Like Delaware, and unlike many suburbs that use the Regional Income Tax Agency (RITA), Marysville collects its own tax through the Income Tax Division at City Hall, 209 S. Main Street (937-645-7350).

The detail that matters most is the credit, because there isn’t one. The city’s tax page says plainly that Marysville “does not give tax credits for taxes paid to another city,” and its FAQ and return instructions say the credit for tax paid to an employment city was eliminated in 2005. A resident who works in another taxing city pays that city’s tax through withholding and owes Marysville the full 1.5% as well. Using the published rates, a resident earning $60,000 in wages in Columbus pays Columbus $1,500 at 2.5% and Marysville another $900, for $2,400 in municipal income tax. The city’s FAQ notes that it receives nothing from a resident who works elsewhere unless the employer agrees to a “courtesy withholding” for Marysville, so many commuters will owe the whole amount when they file.

Marysville has mandatory filing. Every resident 18 or older must file a city return by April 15 whether or not tax is owed, including college students whose permanent residence is in Marysville. Residents under 18 are exempt, and retirees whose only income is Social Security, pensions, interest, or dividends can file a Declaration of Exemption to come off the active filing list. Anyone who expects to owe $200 or more beyond withholding must make estimated payments. For tax years 2016 and later, the city charges a 15% penalty on late payment, a $25-per-month late filing penalty capped at $150 per tax year, and interest at 9% a year for calendar 2026.

Two points for landlords. First, the Income Tax Division treats renting out real property you own as a taxable business activity that must be reported, even if the rental shows no profit. Second, the division posts a “Semi-Annual Landlord Report” form that asks owners to list each rental address, the first and last names of the tenants, and whether the unit is vacant for each reporting period. We did not find the ordinance section that sets the deadline or penalty for that report, so ask the Income Tax Division whether and when it applies to your property.

School district income tax: none in Marysville schools

Wikipedia’s summary says almost all of Marysville lies in the Marysville Exempted Village School District, with a few parcels in Fairbanks Local School District. The Ohio Department of Taxation’s school district income tax table lists Marysville EVSD (district 8002) at 0.00% and Fairbanks LSD (8001) at 1.00% on a traditional base, with the 1.00% rate scheduled to continue from 2027 on. Another Union County district in the table, North Union LSD (8003), is also listed at 1.00%. For a Marysville address, check the school district on the county auditor’s record; the Fairbanks portion adds 1% on top of the city tax.

NCES lists Marysville Exempted Village (district ID 3904547) as a PK–12 district with 10 schools, 5,781 students, and 261.3 classroom teachers (FTE) in the 2024–2025 school year, a student–teacher ratio of 22.12. Its reported total revenue was $80.3 million in fiscal 2022–2023, about $14,459 per student, with 56% from local sources. Marysville High School is the district’s comprehensive high school.

Rental registration: none for occupied rentals, but vacant buildings must register

We did not find a City of Marysville rental registration, rental license, or proactive rental inspection program for occupied long-term rentals. Ohio’s statewide rental filing does not help here either. Ohio Revised Code 5323.02 requires owners of residential rental property to register with the county auditor, but Section 5323.01 limits “residential rental property” to property in a county with more than 200,000 residents at the most recent census, and Union County had 62,784. Unlike Delaware or Franklin County owners, Marysville landlords therefore have no county auditor rental filing under that chapter.

The city does regulate empty buildings. Its Vacant Building Registration form says all vacant buildings must register with the Engineering Department under Chapter 1320 of the codified ordinances, with a vacant building plan (secure, rehabilitate, re-occupy, sell, or demolish) and a required exterior inspection by a code enforcement officer within ten business days. Residential fees are $600 for the first year of vacancy, $900 for the second, $1,200 for the third, and $1,500 for the fourth and later years. The form lists temporary exemptions that may be approved on written request, including properties listed with a licensed Ohio real estate agent and vacant buildings being marketed for rent, so an owner between tenants should ask for the exemption in writing rather than assume it applies.

Rentals are also subject to the city’s general code enforcement. The city’s Code Enforcement FAQ says it seeks voluntary compliance with property maintenance, health and safety, and quality-of-life rules, grants extensions case by case when an owner shows good faith, and may pursue criminal prosecution or civil abatement when owners do not comply.

Fair housing: federal and state law, no local voucher protection found

Marysville is not on AHACO’s list of ten central Ohio communities that have adopted source-of-income protections: Bexley, Columbus, Gahanna, Grandview Heights, Pickerington, Reynoldsburg, Upper Arlington, Westerville, Whitehall, and Worthington. We did not find a local ordinance requiring landlords to accept housing vouchers.

The city keeps a Fair Housing page that names the City Planner as the local contact at (937) 645-7350, lists the HUD discrimination hotline at (800) 669-9777, and links to “Know Your Rights,” Ohio tenant-landlord law, and “What is Fair Housing?” materials; residents outside city limits but within Union County are directed to (937) 645-3012. Ohio Revised Code Chapter 4112 and the federal Fair Housing Act bar housing discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, ancestry, disability, familial status, and, under Ohio law, military status. Without a local source-of-income rule, a Marysville landlord is not required by city law to accept a voucher, but every applicant should be screened against the same written standards.

Short-term rentals: no permit chapter found

We did not find a short-term rental permit, registration, or licensing chapter for Marysville. The zoning code PDF the city posts, revised as of November 18, 2020, has no definition of a short-term rental. It defines a bed and breakfast as “an owner-occupied residential single-family, detached structure wherein lodging and breakfast only are provided to transient guests for compensation,” and lists bed and breakfasts among the uses in some districts, such as the Uptown Transitional and Hospital Mixed-Use districts. It defines a hotel or motel as a place of transient occupancy with more than five guest rooms. The city’s current code is hosted on Municode, which we could not open directly, so later amendments may not be reflected here.

Because the code is silent on whole-home rentals, ask the city’s Planning and Zoning staff (937-645-7350) how an Airbnb-style rental would be classified in your zoning district before you list it. The city’s zoning FAQ notes that a change of use requires a zoning permit and that home occupations in residential districts need a conditional use permit from the Planning Commission with a $250 application fee; we did not confirm whether either applies to a short-term rental.

Lodging tax: the county now reaches short-term rentals

Union County has levied a 3% lodging excise tax since December 1, 1991. Under prior law it applied only to establishments with more than five guest rooms. In Resolution No. 25-503 the county commissioners restated the tax under Ohio Revised Code 5739.091 so that, effective January 1, 2026, a “hotel” includes establishments with fewer than five rooms, a change the Marysville Journal-Tribune reported in November 2025 under the headline “County can now tax Airbnb, Vrbo.”

Union County Tourism’s guidance for hosts says the 3% county lodging tax applies to short-term rental stays beginning on or after July 1, 2026, covering homes, apartments, individual rooms, and other overnight accommodations. Reports are filed monthly, even for months with no rentals; the first payment was due August 20, 2026; checks go to the Union County Treasurer, mailed to the Union County Auditor’s Office at 233 W. Sixth Street; and Airbnb, VRBO, and other platforms do not collect or remit the county tax on a host’s behalf.

Marysville also has its own lodging tax: the Ohio Department of Taxation’s Table S-4 for calendar year 2018, the most recent we could open, lists the City of Marysville at 3.00% alongside Union County’s 3.00%. We did not find a current city rate table or confirm whether the city’s tax reaches a whole-home rental with fewer than five rooms, so confirm with the city’s finance office before pricing a stay.

Leases and deposits: Ohio law applies

We did not find a local landlord-tenant code in Marysville, so Ohio Revised Code Chapter 5321 governs security deposits, repairs, entry notice, and eviction notices. Under that chapter, a landlord generally must return the deposit, with an itemized list of any deductions, within 30 days after the tenant gives up the unit and leaves a forwarding address. Ohio Revised Code 1901.02 gives the Marysville Municipal Court jurisdiction within Union County, so eviction cases for Marysville addresses are filed there rather than in Franklin County. Our Ohio landlord-tenant basics page walks through the statewide rules.

Utilities: one city bill, and a $150 tenant deposit

The City of Marysville runs its own water and sewer systems. Its water FAQ says drinking water comes from Mill Creek, by way of the reservoir on Raymond Road, and from high-capacity wells about 200 feet deep, with fluoride added at the treatment plant. Wastewater from the city and other parts of Union County is treated at the Marysville Water Reclamation Facility on Beecher Gamble Road and discharged into Mill Creek.

The utility FAQ says a typical city bill includes water, sewer, refuse, yard waste, and stormwater, but that tenants are not charged for stormwater. Bills are due on the last business day of each month. The city’s 2026 rate sheet, effective January 1, 2026, sets these charges:

Higher-volume tiers drop to $4.00 (water) and $6.95 (sewer) per 100 cubic feet for the next 46,000 cubic feet. County customers pay the same water rates but sewer rates 10% higher. As an illustration using our own arithmetic from that table, a household using 600 cubic feet in a month (about 4,500 gallons) would pay about $50.00 for water and $49.86 for sewer, plus $36.25 for refuse and yard waste, or roughly $136 before any stormwater charge. Minimum water and sewer charges apply even with no usage.

A few billing rules are worth putting in the lease:

  • Deposit and contract. Deposits are required for any tenant-occupied residential property. A Contract for City Utility Services must be signed by both the property owner and all residents on the lease, and a $150 deposit must be paid by cash, money order, or credit card before service starts; personal and business checks are not accepted.
  • Late payment. Payments not received by the due date are assessed a 5% late fee, and bills not paid by the delinquent due date are subject to disconnection. Reconnection costs $25, payable in guaranteed funds. The city says it does not offer payment plans and refers customers to local assistance agencies.
  • Outdoor watering. Customers without a secondary meter can apply for a sewer credit for outdoor water use for up to three consecutive months a year, or buy a secondary meter for outside use.
  • Repairs. The city maintains water lines up to the curb stop; the line from the curb stop to the building is the owner’s responsibility. Shut-offs for plumbing repairs need 24 hours’ notice and are free if the water is off for less than 72 hours.

Trash is a flat-rate, unlimited, automated program. The city contracts with Republic Services for refuse and recycling and provides one green trash toter and one blue recycling toter free; residents may have up to three recycling toters at no extra cost. Carts go to the curb by 6 a.m. on collection day, and bulk items are collected on the regular day in piles up to 10 feet long, 4 feet wide, and 4 feet high, which helps at turnover. The city’s own Sanitation Division collects bagged yard waste, brush, and leaves weekly. Wikipedia’s summary says electricity in the city comes from Dayton Power and Light, with Union Rural Electric and AEP Ohio serving some surrounding areas; we did not confirm natural gas providers.

Getting around: no COTA, limited county rides

Marysville does not appear on COTA’s current system map, and we found no general public fixed-route bus service in the city. Most residents drive; U.S. Route 33, a four-lane controlled-access highway, runs through Marysville, connecting it with the I-270 outerbelt to the southeast and Interstate 75 to the northwest. CSX serves the city with freight rail, and there is no passenger rail. The Union County Airport sits about a mile southeast of downtown.

Union County Agency Transportation Service (UCATS), at 940 London Avenue, offers free rides to eligible county residents: people 60 and older living independently, Medicaid recipients for approved medical trips, clients of the county developmental disabilities board, and individuals with disabilities as determined by UCATS. Trips need at least 24 hours’ notice in the county and three working days for out-of-county trips, and the dispatch line (937-642-5100) is staffed weekdays from 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. The Union County Health Department’s UCMOVES page also points to GOhio Commute carpools and vanpools and GoBus intercity service. The Journal-Tribune reported in 2021 that county commissioners decided not to expand UCATS into public transportation for all residents, and we found no newer plan to do so. A tenant without a car should map the commute before signing.

Parks and daily life

Wikipedia’s summary counts more than 300 acres of city parks, with walking and bike trails along Mill Creek, fishing at several parks, a nature preserve at MacIvor Woods, lighted tennis courts at Eljer and Lewis parks, and a skate park and disc golf course at Eljer Park. The Union County Fair runs in late July and early August, and the Marysville Public Library is on Plum Street near Uptown. The Marysville Journal-Tribune, published since 1849, remains the county’s daily newspaper and a good place to watch for council action on rental and tax issues.

Vouchers and what the SAFMR table says

Union County is its own HUD Metro FMR Area, separate from the Columbus HUD Metro FMR Area that covers Franklin, Delaware, and five other counties. HUD’s FY2027 figures for the Union County area, effective October 1, 2026, are:

The county-wide two-bedroom FMR rose from $1,433 in FY2026 to $1,607 in FY2027, about 12%. The ZIP 43040 figures sit just below the county-wide FMR and below the $1,610 two-bedroom figure for Delaware’s ZIP 43015. They are benchmarks that housing authorities use to set voucher payment standards, not asking rents, and ZIP 43040 includes township areas outside the city. The Marion Metropolitan Housing Authority publishes Section 8 utility allowance schedules for Union County effective July 1, 2026, so a voucher holder should confirm the payment standard and utility allowance with the housing authority that issued the voucher. What landlords and tenants should check before signing

  • Jurisdiction. Confirm the parcel is inside the City of Marysville rather than a surrounding township; taxes, zoning, and utility rates differ.
  • Income tax. Budget for the full 1.5% even if you work in Columbus or another taxing city; with no credit, it is usually due at filing time, not withheld.
  • School district. Marysville EVSD adds no school income tax, but the few Fairbanks parcels add 1%.
  • Registration. No city rental registry and no county auditor rental filing in Union County; register vacant buildings under Chapter 1320 or request the for-rent exemption, and ask the Income Tax Division about the semi-annual landlord report.
  • Short stays. No short-term rental permit chapter found; collect and remit Union County’s 3% lodging tax monthly and confirm zoning and the city lodging tax with City Hall.
  • Vouchers. No local source-of-income protection found; apply consistent written screening either way.
  • Utility account. One city bill covers water, sewer, $26 refuse, and $10.25 yard waste; plan for the $150 tenant deposit and a contract signed by owner and tenants.
  • Transit. No COTA service; UCATS rides are limited to eligible residents and need advance booking.

Frequently asked questions

Is Marysville part of Columbus?

No. Marysville is a separate city and the county seat of Union County, about 27 miles northwest of Columbus on U.S. Route 33, with a 2020 census population of 25,571. It has its own council, income tax, and utilities, so Columbus-only rules such as Renter’s Choice, the Columbus rental registry, and Columbus source-of-income protections do not apply. Not every Marysville mailing address (ZIP 43040) is inside the city, so confirm the parcel is within city limits.

What is the Marysville, Ohio income tax rate?

The City of Marysville’s income tax is 1.5%, collected by the city’s own Income Tax Division rather than RITA. Marysville gives residents no credit for income tax paid to the city where they work; the credit was eliminated in 2005. A resident earning $60,000 in Columbus would typically pay Columbus $1,500 and Marysville another $900. All residents 18 and older must file by April 15, even if no tax is due.

Is there a school district income tax in Marysville?

Not in Marysville Exempted Village Schools, which the Ohio Department of Taxation lists at 0.00%. A few parcels fall in Fairbanks Local Schools, which carries a 1.00% school district income tax. NCES reports Marysville Exempted Village had 5,781 students in 10 schools in 2024–2025.

Does Marysville have a rental registry?

We did not find a City of Marysville rental registration, rental license, or proactive rental inspection program. Ohio’s county-auditor rental filing in Revised Code 5323.02 applies only in counties with more than 200,000 residents, and Union County had 62,784 at the 2020 census. Marysville does require registration of vacant buildings under Chapter 1320, with residential fees starting at $600 a year, and its Income Tax Division posts a semi-annual landlord report listing rental addresses and tenant names.

Can I run an Airbnb in Marysville, Ohio?

We found no short-term rental permit or registration chapter in Marysville’s code. The city’s zoning code defines a bed and breakfast as an owner-occupied single-family detached home where lodging and breakfast only are provided to transient guests and lists it only in certain districts. Confirm with the city’s Planning and Zoning staff (937-645-7350) how a whole-home short-term rental would be classified before listing.

Is there a lodging tax on short-term rentals in Marysville?

Yes. Union County levies a 3% lodging tax and, under Resolution 25-503, now defines a hotel to include places with fewer than five rooms; Union County Tourism says short-term rental owners must collect and remit the 3% on stays beginning on or after July 1, 2026, with monthly reports. The Ohio Department of Taxation’s 2018 lodging tax table also lists a 3.00% City of Marysville lodging tax. Booking platforms do not remit the county tax for hosts.

Can Marysville landlords refuse housing vouchers?

Marysville is not on AHACO’s list of ten central Ohio communities that have adopted source-of-income protections, and we found no local rule requiring landlords to accept vouchers. Federal and Ohio fair housing laws still apply, and the city names its City Planner as the local fair housing contact at 937-645-7350.

Who provides water, sewer, and trash in Marysville?

The City of Marysville. One monthly city bill covers water, sewer, stormwater, refuse, and yard waste; for 2026 refuse is $26 a month ($19 for customers over 65), yard waste $10.25, and stormwater $8.25 per ERU, though the city says tenants are not charged for stormwater. Tenants must pay a $150 deposit, and the contract for service must be signed by the owner and everyone on the lease. Late bills carry a 5% fee.

Does COTA serve Marysville, Ohio?

No. Marysville does not appear on COTA’s system map, and we found no general public bus service in the city. Union County Agency Transportation Service (UCATS) provides free rides for eligible county residents, mainly people 60 and older, Medicaid recipients for medical trips, and riders with disabilities, booked at least 24 hours ahead at 937-642-5100.

What is the voucher payment standard in Marysville, Ohio?

Union County is its own HUD Metro FMR Area. For ZIP 43040, HUD’s FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents are $1,110 for a studio, $1,220 for one bedroom, $1,590 for two, $1,890 for three, and $2,090 for four bedrooms. The county-wide FY2027 FMRs are $1,119, $1,228, $1,607, $1,915, and $2,112. These are benchmarks, not asking rents; the housing authority that administers a voucher sets the actual payment standard.
