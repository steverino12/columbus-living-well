Upper Arlington: a renting and living guide

What landlords and tenants should know about Upper Arlington—a separate city on the Scioto River northwest of downtown Columbus with its own income tax, schools, and zoning board—including its ban on short-term rentals, how code complaints work, COTA’s new Line 30, the November 2026 school ballot issue, and ZIP-level voucher figures, without inventing market rents.

Last updated October 26, 2026. Facts below come from the City of Upper Arlington, the Upper Arlington Historical Society, Upper Arlington Schools, the Regional Income Tax Agency, COTA, the U.S. Census Bureau (via Wikipedia’s summary of the 2020 count), and CMHA tables cited in Sources. Asking-rent medians are not invented here; see Rent Pulse and Fair Market Rents for verified figures.

A separate city, not a Columbus neighborhood

Upper Arlington is its own municipality in Franklin County, surrounded largely by Columbus, with Marble Cliff and Grandview Heights to the south. Locally it is often shortened to “Arlington” or “UA.” The 2020 Census counted 36,800 residents on about 9.8 square miles. Mailing addresses use ZIPs 43221 and 43220, and Wikipedia also lists 43212, so the city name on an envelope may read Columbus even when the property is in Upper Arlington.

That independence matters for a rental. Columbus City Code does not apply in Upper Arlington, so Columbus-only rules such as the source-of-income protection in Columbus City Code 4551.03 do not reach UA properties. Upper Arlington’s own codified ordinances, its Unified Development Ordinance (UDO), and Ohio’s statewide landlord-tenant law in Ohio Revised Code Chapter 5321 do. The City runs a council-manager government, adopted by charter amendment in 1956, in which the elected council appoints a city manager and chooses a mayor from among its members. Confirm the taxing city with the Franklin County Auditor before you sign a lease or quote a rule.

For other neighborhood spotlights, start at the Neighborhoods hub, including neighboring Grandview Heights to the south, Dublin to the northwest, Bexley on the east side, Clintonville across the Olentangy, and in-town Columbus guides for Victorian Village, the Short North, Italian Village, German Village, and Olde Towne East.

How Upper Arlington grew

According to the Upper Arlington Historical Society and the City’s history pages, brothers King and Ben Thompson bought 840 acres of James T. Miller’s farm on Christmas Eve 1913. The Miller farm ran roughly between today’s Fifth Avenue, Riverside Drive, Lane Avenue, and Northwest Boulevard, and it is now the city’s Historic District. The Thompsons hired landscape architect William Pitkin Jr. of Rochester, New York, whose plan let streets follow the land’s contours with open park space and deep setbacks, and they first marketed the project as the “Country Club District,” after the development of that name in Kansas City.

Work stopped in 1916, when the land was taken for Camp Willis, a short-lived Ohio National Guard training camp during the Pancho Villa expedition; the Thompsons restarted in the fall of 1916 after the camp closed. By 1917 the community was called Upper Arlington because it sat just north of the area then known as Arlington (renamed Marble Cliff in 1901). The village incorporated on March 20, 1918, with about 200 residents and Miller as its first mayor. Its 1919 charter granted women the vote 17 months before the Nineteenth Amendment. The population grew from 620 in 1920 to 5,370 in 1940, and Upper Arlington became a city in 1941. Wikipedia’s summary notes that most of its later growth came through annexations from Perry Township in the late 1940s through 1960, and that from 1920 to 1945 deeds in the city carried racially restrictive covenants, a history worth knowing when you read older title documents.

Short-term rentals are not allowed

Upper Arlington is one of the strictest places in central Ohio for short-term rentals. Ordinance 38-2018, passed April 23, 2018, amended UDO Section 5.02(B) to prohibit renting a residence, or any portion of one, to any tenant for less than 30 days in residential districts, and it also barred bed-and-breakfasts, apartment hotels, and hotels and motels from single-family districts. That ordinance had a one-year sunset, but City Council voted 5–2 on April 8, 2019, to pass Ordinance 22-2019, removing the sunset and making the ban permanent. There is no permit path: a whole-home or room-share Airbnb or Vrbo listing in a residential district violates the UDO. Leases of 30 days or more are ordinary residential tenancies. Investors comparing suburbs should note the contrast with Dublin, which caps STR stays, and Grandview Heights, which allows only owner-occupied or on-site-managed STRs.

Zoning, code compliance, and who decides

The City’s Planning and Zoning Division and the Board of Zoning and Planning (BZAP) handle variances, conditional uses, site and development plans, and subdivisions, and BZAP recommends rezonings and UDO changes to City Council. BZAP typically meets on the first and third Wednesdays of each month: the first is an informal work session where commercial projects are usually discussed, and the board takes formal action at the third-Wednesday meeting. Agendas, minutes, and audio are posted on the City’s portals, and Community Development is at the Municipal Services Center, 3600 Tremont Road.

Upper Arlington does not run a Columbus-style rental inspection program. The City’s Property Maintenance (Code Compliance) Division says enforcement is primarily complaint-driven, though officers also note violations on neighborhood patrols. After a complaint, an officer inspects; if a violation exists, the owner gets a written notice with a deadline and a re-inspection date. Most violations carry a maximum of $150 per offense per day, and a few are fourth-degree misdemeanors with up to a $250 fine and 30 days in jail. Grass must stay under 10 inches, with five days to fix it after notice, and rubbish must be cleared within seven days of notice or the City’s contractor removes it and the cost becomes a lien on the property taxes. Landlords should make clear in the lease who handles mowing, snow, and trash, since the notice goes to the owner.

Getting around: COTA Line 30 and more

Transit in Upper Arlington changed on September 7, 2026, when COTA launched Line 30 Hilliard-OSU. COTA says it runs every 30 minutes, seven days a week, linking Hilliard, Upper Arlington, Ohio State, and downtown on one bus, and its timetable lists it as standard service. Through UA it travels via West Lane Avenue, Tremont Road, and Fishinger Road, serving the Shops on Lane Avenue, Kingsdale Shopping Center, the Bob Crane Community Center, and Upper Arlington High School, then continues to the Wexner Medical Center and OSU’s Carmenton Innovation District. At the same time, Line 32 was cut back to run only between Kingsdale and the Easton Transit Center, every 60 minutes. Line 3 Northwest/Harrisburg also reaches Kingsdale and Tremont Road by way of Grandview Yard and downtown, and Line 75 is a rush-hour line serving Kingsdale, Tremont Road, and the Griggs Dam Park and Ride. Check current timetables for the hours you travel; COTA adjusts service each January, May, and September.

Schools and the Issue 7 ballot question

Upper Arlington is served by Upper Arlington Schools, a separate district from Columbus City Schools. Its buildings are Barrington, Greensview, Tremont, Windermere, and Wickliffe Progressive elementary schools, Hastings and Jones middle schools, Upper Arlington High School on Zollinger Road, the Burbank Early Childhood School, and the UA Online Academy. Students are assigned to the school serving their attendance area, and a different building requires an intra-district enrollment request. Under a new state law, a child must be 5 by the first day of school to start kindergarten.

On the November 3, 2026 ballot, the district has placed Issue 7, a combined 4.9-mill operating levy and $273.5 million bond issue. The district estimates the bond at a 2.25-mill increase over its current 5.7-mill bond rate. It would fund phase two of the facilities master plan: rebuilding Burbank Early Childhood School with more classrooms, rebuilding Hastings Middle School on its current athletic field, and keeping the original 1923 three-story Jones Middle School structure while rebuilding around it, with Jones students in temporary space during construction. Property-tax levies affect owners directly and often reach tenants through rent, so both sides may want to follow the result. This guide takes no position on the issue.

Parks and community life

The City’s Bob Crane Community Center and its pool sit at 3200 Tremont Road, and the City has installed solar panels on the building to offset its energy use. The Amelita Mirolo Barn at 4395 Carriage Hill Lane is a reservable event space. The City has been a Tree City USA community every year since 1990. Lane Avenue and Kingsdale are the main shopping areas, and the City lists ongoing redevelopment projects for the Golden Bear site, the National Church Residences corporate campus, and Westmont Boulevard.

Taxes for tenants who move here

Upper Arlington levies a 2.5% municipal income tax, collected by the Regional Income Tax Agency (RITA), which lists that rate as in effect since at least 2020. Residents get a credit up to 2.5% for municipal tax paid to another city, so someone who works in a 2.5% city such as Columbus with correct withholding generally owes nothing extra. Two points catch newcomers: every resident aged 18 or older must file an annual return or a tax exemption form with the City even if no tax is due, and estimated quarterly payments are required when local tax isn’t withheld and the yearly bill is $200 or more. Tenants should update their address with their employer and file each April, typically by April 15.

What landlords and tenants should check before signing

  • Which city. Confirm the parcel is in Upper Arlington, not Columbus; taxes, codes, and schools differ even within the same ZIP.
  • No short-term rentals. Don’t buy or sublease expecting Airbnb income; stays under 30 days are banned in residential districts.
  • Upkeep duties. Spell out mowing, snow, and trash responsibilities, since code notices go to the owner.
  • Income tax filing. Budget for the 2.5% city tax and remember the annual filing requirement for every adult resident.
  • Bus access. If you don’t drive, check distance to Line 30 stops on Lane, Tremont, or Fishinger.
  • ZIP for vouchers. Use the property’s ZIP, 43221 or 43220, before doing voucher math.

Frequently asked questions

Is Upper Arlington part of Columbus?

No. Upper Arlington is its own city in Franklin County. It incorporated as a village on March 20, 1918, became a city in 1941, and has used a council-manager government since 1956. It has its own income tax, zoning code (the Unified Development Ordinance), police, fire, and school district, though many addresses carry a Columbus mailing name. Check the parcel’s city on the Franklin County Auditor’s site before applying Columbus rules.

What is the Upper Arlington income tax rate?

Upper Arlington levies a 2.5% municipal income tax, administered by the Regional Income Tax Agency (RITA). Residents get a credit for municipal tax paid to another city, up to 2.5%. Every resident aged 18 or older must file an annual return or a tax exemption form with the City, even if no tax is due.

Can I run an Airbnb in Upper Arlington?

No. Ordinance 38-2018 amended Unified Development Ordinance Section 5.02(B) to prohibit renting a residence, or any part of one, for less than 30 days in residential districts, and City Council made the ban permanent with Ordinance 22-2019 on April 8, 2019. Bed-and-breakfasts and apartment hotels are also prohibited in single-family districts. Leases of 30 days or more are ordinary residential rentals.

Which COTA lines serve Upper Arlington?

Line 30 Hilliard-OSU, launched September 7, 2026, runs daily through Upper Arlington via West Lane Avenue, Tremont Road, Kingsdale, and Fishinger Road to Ohio State and downtown. Line 3 Northwest/Harrisburg reaches Kingsdale and Tremont Road, and Line 75 is a rush-hour line serving Kingsdale and Tremont Road. Line 32 now runs only east from Kingsdale to Easton.

What is on the November 3, 2026 ballot for Upper Arlington Schools?

Issue 7 combines a 4.9-mill operating levy with a $273.5 million bond issue, which the district estimates at a 2.25-mill increase over its current 5.7-mill bond rate. The bond would fund phase two of the facilities master plan: rebuilding Burbank Early Childhood School and Hastings Middle School and renovating and rebuilding around the 1923 Jones Middle School building.

What is the voucher payment standard in Upper Arlington?

Most of Upper Arlington uses ZIP 43221, with the north end in 43220. CMHA’s FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents, effective October 1, 2026, at 100% of SAFMR, list $1,550 studio, $1,660 one-bedroom, $1,970 two-bedroom, and $2,350 three-bedroom for 43221, and $1,300, $1,390, $1,650, and $1,970 for 43220. These are payment-standard benchmarks, not asking rents.
