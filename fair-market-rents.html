Fair Market Rents · CMHA / HUD

Columbus Fair Market Rents and CMHA payment standards, explained

A plain-language guide to HUD Fair Market Rents, Small Area FMRs by ZIP, and how the Columbus Metropolitan Housing Authority sets Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher payment standards in central Ohio.

Last updated October 14, 2026. Figures below are from HUD and CMHA publications cited in Sources; always verify the current table before a lease or Request for Tenancy Approval.

What is a HUD Fair Market Rent?

The U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development publishes Fair Market Rents (FMRs) each year. Under federal rules (24 CFR 888.113), FMRs are estimates of the 40th percentile of gross rents for standard-quality rental units in a market area. In everyday terms, they describe what a typical mid-to-lower market rent looks like for a given bedroom size, including the idea of tenant-paid utilities in the gross-rent concept.

HUD uses FMRs for several programs. The one most landlords and tenants in Columbus hear about is the Housing Choice Voucher (HCV) program—often called Section 8. Public housing agencies such as the Columbus Metropolitan Housing Authority (CMHA) use FMRs, or ZIP-level Small Area FMRs, when they set payment standards. Those standards help determine how much subsidy a voucher can support for a unit of a given size in a given location.

FMRs are published annually and generally become effective on October 1, the start of the federal fiscal year. HUD’s dataset page is the authoritative place to look up current and prior-year figures for any metro.

Columbus, OH HUD Metro FMR Area

Columbus sits inside a multi-county HUD Metro FMR Area. The counties included are Delaware, Fairfield, Franklin, Licking, Madison, Morrow, and Pickaway. When HUD publishes a single “metro” FMR table for Columbus, those figures apply across that geography unless a program uses Small Area Fair Market Rents (SAFMRs) by ZIP instead.

That distinction matters. A two-bedroom unit in a higher-rent downtown ZIP can face a very different voucher payment standard than a two-bedroom in a lower-rent ZIP, even though both are “in Columbus” on a map. Metro FMRs average the market; SAFMRs try to reflect neighborhood-level rent levels.

FMR vs payment standard vs contract rent vs rent reasonableness

Four terms get mixed together in voucher conversations. Keeping them separate avoids expensive surprises at lease-up.

Fair Market Rent (FMR or SAFMR) is HUD’s estimate of gross rent for a standard unit—metro-wide or by ZIP. It is a benchmark, not a lease amount.

Payment standard is the maximum subsidy basis the housing authority uses for a voucher household. PHAs may set payment standards within a HUD-allowed band around FMR (historically often 90–110% of FMR, subject to HUD rules and funding). CMHA’s 2026 adjustment moved HCV payment standards from 110% of FMR to 100% of FMR (and, where SAFMR applies, to 100% of the ZIP SAFMR). The payment standard is not a rent ceiling the landlord can automatically charge.

Contract rent is the rent the landlord and tenant (and, for vouchers, the housing assistance payment contract) agree to. It must be supportable by market comps and program rules.

Rent reasonableness is CMHA’s review that the proposed rent is reasonable compared with similar unassisted units. Even if a proposed rent sits under the payment standard, it can still be reduced or denied if it fails reasonableness. Landlords should price from ZIP-level comps and expect documentation, not from the payment-standard number alone.

Gross rent for voucher purposes generally includes an allowance for tenant-paid utilities. When tenants pay utilities separately, CMHA applies a utility allowance so that the combination of contract rent and the allowance is compared against the payment standard. Say this carefully in lease talks: a higher “rent” with owner-paid utilities is not the same deal as a lower rent plus tenant-paid gas and electric. Confirm how CMHA will treat utilities for the specific unit before you lock numbers on a Request for Tenancy Approval (RFTA).

CMHA’s 2026 payment standard adjustment

On July 1, 2026, CMHA issued a public statement explaining that it was reducing the Housing Choice Voucher payment standard from 110% of Fair Market Rent to 100% of FMR. The reason given was a projected federal HCV funding shortfall—not a claim of local mismanagement. According to CMHA, HUD directed public housing agencies trending toward shortfall to reduce payment standards.

CMHA also noted that at initial lease-up, a family generally may pay up to 40% of adjusted monthly income toward housing. Existing households can face different timing rules when standards change; voucher holders should confirm their situation with CMHA rather than assuming a new lease-up rule applies automatically.

Contact information published with that statement: 880 E. 11th Ave., Columbus, OH 43211-2771; phone 614-421-6000. Operational details (waitlist status, inspection scheduling, and RFTA forms) live on CMHA’s site and can change; use the statement and current payment-standard PDFs as starting points, then verify before you act.

In short: mid-2026 brought a percentage cut (110% → 100%), and October 1, 2026 brought higher FY2027 dollar FMRs and SAFMRs. The net effect on any one ZIP and bedroom size depends on both moves. Do not interpolate undocumented transitions; use the current CMHA table for the unit’s ZIP.

What landlords and voucher holders should check before an RFTA

A Request for Tenancy Approval is where theory meets a specific address. Before you submit:

  • Confirm the unit’s ZIP and bedroom size on CMHA’s current payment-standard schedule (SAFMR vs metro, as applicable).
  • Clarify which utilities the tenant pays and how the utility allowance will affect the gross-rent comparison.
  • Price the contract rent from recent comps in the same ZIP; expect rent-reasonableness review.
  • Remember the payment standard is a subsidy basis, not an automatic rent approval.
  • At initial lease-up, families generally may pay up to 40% of adjusted monthly income toward housing—plan affordability with that rule in mind.
  • Allow time for inspection and CMHA processing; do not assume same-week turnaround.

Applicants who hold vouchers should also see our Applications guide for screening and document basics, and the Tenant Playbook for how to present a complete package. Inside Columbus city limits, landlords also may not refuse voucher income; see Ohio and Columbus landlord-tenant law basics. Landlords who accept vouchers are a meaningful part of the Columbus market; clear ZIP-level math reduces failed RFTAs for everyone.

Timing recap: summer 2026 cut, then October 1 FY2027 levels

Two calendar events shaped 2026 for CMHA voucher payment standards. First, CMHA reduced standards from 110% of FMR to 100% of FMR in response to projected federal funding shortfalls (CMHA’s July 1, 2026 statement; Rent Pulse also notes the Aug. 1 operational framing of that cut). Second, HUD’s FY2027 Fair Market Rents and Small Area FMRs took effect October 1, 2026, generally raising the underlying dollar benchmarks even while the percentage remained at 100%.

For day-to-day decisions after October 1, 2026, rely on CMHA’s current FY2027 SAFMR and FMR tables and on HUD’s FY2027 documentation—not on reconstructed mid-year interpolations. Frequently asked questions

What is a HUD Fair Market Rent in Columbus?

It is HUD’s estimate of the 40th percentile gross rent for standard-quality units in the Columbus, OH HUD Metro FMR Area (or, for SAFMRs, in a specific ZIP). FMRs help set voucher payment standards and support other HUD programs. They update annually, typically effective October 1.

Which counties does the Columbus metro FMR cover?

Delaware, Fairfield, Franklin, Licking, Madison, Morrow, and Pickaway.

Why did CMHA lower payment standards in 2026?

CMHA cited a projected federal Housing Choice Voucher funding shortfall and stated that HUD directed PHAs trending toward shortfall to reduce standards. The agency moved from 110% of FMR to 100% of FMR. That is separate from the later October 1, 2026 effective date of higher FY2027 FMR/SAFMR dollar amounts.

Is the payment standard the rent I can charge?

No. It is the maximum subsidy basis for the voucher calculation (including the utility-allowance concept for tenant-paid utilities). Contract rent must still pass rent reasonableness. Asking rents in the private market can be higher or lower than FMR; see Rent Pulse for tracker medians.

Why do ZIP payment standards differ so much?

Because SAFMRs are calculated at the ZIP level. Higher-rent ZIPs receive higher Small Area FMRs; lower-rent ZIPs receive lower ones. CMHA’s October 1, 2026 two-bedroom examples range from $1,340 in 43211 to $2,350 in 43215 at 100% of SAFMR.

Where do I verify the latest numbers?

HUD’s FMR dataset and ZIP calculator, plus CMHA’s published payment-standard PDFs and statements. Links are listed below. Program rules and tables change; treat this page as an explainer tied to the dated sources, not a substitute for CMHA’s current instructions.
