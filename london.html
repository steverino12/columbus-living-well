London: a renting and living guide to the Madison County seat west of Columbus

What landlords and tenants should know about the City of London, the Madison County seat about 25 miles southwest of Columbus, including its 1.5% city income tax with no credit at all for tax paid where you work, the 1.00% London City Schools income tax on a traditional base that reaches pensions and investment income, the October 1 tenant list landlords owe the tax office, why there is no rental registry, the rooming house permit, water and sewer accounts through the Mid-Ohio Water & Sewer District, Rumpke trash billing since August 2026, how zoning treats tourist homes and bed and breakfasts, the 3% Madison County lodging tax, Fayette Madison Transit’s call-ahead buses, and HUD voucher figures for ZIP 43140, without inventing market rents.

Last updated November 18, 2026. Facts below come from the City of London, its codified ordinances (as published by American Legal Publishing, local legislation current through July 17, 2025), the Central Collection Agency’s municipal rate table, the Ohio Department of Taxation, the Ohio Revised Code, the Mid-Ohio Water & Sewer District, Fayette Madison Transit and the United Way of Clark, Champaign and Madison Counties, the London Metropolitan Housing Authority, NCES, HUD, AHACO, and Wikipedia’s summaries of the city and county, all cited in Sources. Asking-rent medians are not invented here; see Rent Pulse and Fair Market Rents for verified figures.

A small county seat on the western edge of the metro

London is a separate city and the county seat of Madison County. Wikipedia’s summary places it about 25 miles southwest of Columbus and says it was established in 1811 by Patrick McLene specifically to serve as the seat of the new county. The 2020 census counted 10,279 residents on 8.45 square miles, in 4,049 households. Of the city’s 4,390 housing units, 7.8% were vacant, and the census put the rental vacancy rate at 4.7% and the homeowner vacancy rate at 1.9%. The median age was 39.2, and 17.9% of residents were 65 or older.

Madison County as a whole is small: Wikipedia’s county summary gives a 2020 census population of 43,824 and notes the county is part of the Columbus metropolitan statistical area. That size matters for renters and owners, as explained below, because several Ohio rental rules only switch on in counties above 200,000 people. The city’s own utilities page notes that the sewer system also serves the London and Madison correctional institutions outside the city limits.

Because London is its own municipality, Columbus-only rules such as the Columbus rental registry, Renter’s Choice, and Columbus’s source-of-income protections do not apply here. The ZIP code 43140 also reaches into surrounding townships, so before you sign, confirm the parcel is inside the city. The city’s Building & Zoning page explains how to check a parcel’s zoning on the Madison County Auditor’s map, which is also a quick way to see whether an address is in the city at all.

Income tax: 1.5%, and no credit for tax paid elsewhere

London’s income tax is 1.5%. Section 881.012 of the city’s income tax ordinance splits it into a 1% levy for general city operations and capital improvements and a 0.5% levy restricted to the Fire Department’s operating and capital costs (Ord. 162-15, passed October 1, 2015). The city’s 2025 individual return multiplies taxable income by 1.5% (.015).

The credit is what sets London apart from most of the suburbs we cover. The same 2025 return says, in bold type next to the estimated-tax lines, that beginning January 1, 2021 the City of London no longer offers a credit for taxes paid to other cities. The Central Collection Agency’s rate table for London shows the same history: 1.50% with a 100% credit limited to 0.50% from 2014 through 2020, and 1.50% with a 0% credit since January 1, 2021. In practice, a London resident who works in a city with its own tax pays both. If you earn $60,000 in a city that charges 2.5%, you pay that city $1,500 and still owe London $900, for $2,400 in municipal income tax. Lines 5 and 9 of the London return allow credit only for London tax withheld, London estimated payments, and prior-year overpayments. If your employer does not withhold London tax, the return says you must file a declaration and pay quarterly estimates once the tax due reaches $200, with a 15% penalty for nonpayment.

London requires nearly everyone to file. The Income Tax Department page says all residents over 18 must file an annual return regardless of income, and that anyone with self-employment or rental property income must file even if the business or rental showed a loss. The return instructions say interest, dividends, and retirement income are not taxable, and the city lets retirees who receive only that kind of income (Social Security, pensions, dividends, or interest) file an exemption certificate instead of a return. The certificate is void once the retiree or spouse has any taxable income, including from owning rental property. Returns are due April 15 or the IRS due date, and the tax office is in City Hall, Suite 103, 20 S. Walnut Street (740-852-0693). The department page notes that the city does not collect or file school district tax; that goes to the state.

The landlord tenant list, due October 1

London also asks landlords for something most suburbs do not. Section 881.21 of the tax ordinance, titled Mandatory registration with Director; rental reporting, says that on October 1, 2016 and every year after, all landlords who rent property within the city must submit a current list of their tenants, with addresses, to the Director of Taxation. A late list draws a $75 penalty per year, and the Director is supposed to notify landlords in writing at least 30 days before the due date. Unlike Newark’s version, London’s rule is not tied to who pays the water bill; it covers all landlords with property in the city.

The same section requires each new resident to register with the Director of Taxation within 30 days of moving in, and the department links an online New Residence Form for that purpose. For tenants, the practical result is that the tax office will usually learn about a new household from the landlord’s list even if the tenant forgets to register, so it is simpler to register and file from the first year.

School district income tax: 1.00% on a traditional base

London City Schools adds a school district income tax on top of the city tax. The Ohio Department of Taxation’s school district income tax table lists London CSD (district number 4903) at 1.00% on a traditional base since January 1, 2006, with no scheduled end, and the city’s tax page confirms district number 4903 and the 1% rate. The state’s guide to the school district income tax explains that the traditional base starts from Ohio adjusted gross income, less personal exemptions, so it reaches interest, dividends, capital gains, and pensions, not just wages. That cuts the other way from the city tax: a retiree who can file London’s exemption certificate may still owe the school tax on a pension. On $60,000 in wages alone, the school tax is roughly $600 before exemptions. It is filed with the state on Form SD 100 and gets no credit for tax paid elsewhere.

The school district on the parcel’s auditor record decides the rate, and Madison County districts in the same state table differ sharply. Jonathan Alder Local (district 4902) is at 1.25% on earned income through December 31, 2026, and the table shows it dropping to 0.50% from January 1, 2027 through 2031. Madison-Plains Local (4904) is at 1.25% on earned income through 2033, and Jefferson Local (4901), the West Jefferson district, is at 1.00% on earned income through 2033. The earned-income base taxes wages and self-employment income but not pensions or investment income.

NCES lists London City (district ID 3904425) as a PK–12 district with 4 schools, 2,139 students, and 116.13 classroom teachers (FTE) in the 2024–2025 school year, a student–teacher ratio of 18.42. Its reported total revenue was $29.5 million in fiscal 2022–2023, about $13,934 per student, with 54% from local sources, 35% from the state, and 11% federal.

Rental registration: none found, but rooming houses need a permit

We did not find a City of London rental registration, rental license, or proactive rental inspection program. The housing and building chapters of the London Codified Ordinances cover building codes, contractor registration, dangerous buildings, the historic district, a housing code, and property maintenance, but no rental registry. Ohio’s county-auditor rental filing does not reach London either: Ohio Revised Code 5323.01 limits the political subdivisions that may use that system to counties with more than 200,000 residents and the cities and townships in them, and Madison County had 43,824 at the 2020 census. The October 1 tenant list for the tax office is the closest thing London has to a rental registry.

Rooming houses are the exception. Chapter 1488 of the housing code, Rooming Houses, says no one may operate a rooming house in the city without a valid permit issued by the Health Commissioner in the operator’s name for the specific dwelling. The permit must be displayed, cannot be transferred, and expires at the end of each calendar year; an operator who sells or transfers the property must notify the Commissioner in writing within 24 hours. Inspectors can suspend a permit if violations are not fixed within 30 days. The chapter as published does not state a permit fee, so ask the health department. The zoning code defines a rooming house as lodging for unrelated individuals for compensation in a residential-type structure, and Section 1254.06 caps occupancy at two persons per sleeping room.

Ordinary rentals still have to meet the property maintenance code. Chapter 1496 adopts the International Property Maintenance Code (originally the 1998 edition, with later editions adopted automatically unless council rejects them) and amends it for London: heat must be supplied from October 31 to May 31, insect screens are required from April 1 to October 30, plant growth over six inches is a violation, and a first offense carries a fine of $25 to $100, with a second offense within 12 months a fourth-degree misdemeanor. Each day a violation continues after notice is a separate offense. The older housing code still splits some duties: under Chapter 1486, the occupant of a single-family house is responsible for exterminating pests unless the infestation comes from the owner’s failure to keep the building reasonably rodent- and insect-proof, and owners must supply garbage containers in buildings with more than four units.

We found no vacant building registration chapter. Chapter 1460, Dangerous, Unsafe and Substandard Buildings, sets out nuisance findings, notice and order, standards for repair, vacation, or demolition, and city abatement, but it is not a registry or fee program for empty buildings.

Fair housing: a local ordinance, but no voucher protection

London has its own fair housing law. Chapter 626 of the General Offenses Code declares a policy of equal housing opportunity regardless of race, color, creed, sex, marital status, religious belief, national origin, familial status, age, or handicap, and makes it unlawful for a person or real estate agent to discriminate on those grounds in renting, leasing, or subleasing, to misrepresent availability, or to put discriminatory terms in a lease. The chapter also provides for a Fair Housing Board. Source of income is not on the list, and London is not among the ten central Ohio communities on AHACO’s list of places that have adopted source-of-income protections (Bexley, Columbus, Gahanna, Grandview Heights, Pickerington, Reynoldsburg, Upper Arlington, Westerville, Whitehall, and Worthington). Federal and Ohio fair housing law still applies, including disability accommodation rules. Landlords who accept vouchers, and those who do not, should use the same written screening criteria for every applicant.

The zoning code’s definition of family is also worth knowing for shared rentals. Section 1240.11 of the zoning definitions treats a household as a family if everyone is related by blood, marriage, adoption, guardianship, or custodianship, or if there are five or fewer people when some are unrelated. A house rented to six or more unrelated roommates may no longer count as a single family under zoning, so ask Building & Zoning whether it would be treated as a rooming house.

Water and sewer: the Mid-Ohio Water & Sewer District

London’s water and sewer system is now run by a regional district. Chapters 1042 (Water) and 1046 (Sewer Usage) were amended on July 18, 2024 (Ords. 161-24 and 163-24) to say that all water services, tapping fees, and maintenance, and the collection and treatment of wastewater, are managed by the Mid-Ohio Water and Sewer District under the district’s rules. The city’s utilities page says every new city resident must complete an application for water and sewer service. Meters are read monthly and sewer charges are based on water use. Residential bills are mailed on the 1st and due on the 20th, a 10% late fee is added on the 21st, unpaid accounts can be shut off after the 21st, and reconnection costs $50. Crews will not knock before disconnecting.

The district’s 2025 application for service is the same form for owners and renters. It asks for the service address, whether the applicant is an owner or renter, driver’s license numbers, employer, contact details, and any previous district service addresses, and it is emailed to the district’s billing office. Two lines matter for rentals. First, if the applicant, a spouse, anyone on the lease, or anyone in the household owes the district past-due bills, those must be paid in full before service starts, and service can be cut later if an old debt turns up. Second, an owner who signs the form agrees that tenants are authorized to receive water and sewer bills as the owner’s agent and that tenants are required to apply for service themselves, and agrees to meet the owner responsibilities in the district’s regulations. The form does not list a deposit, and we did not find a published deposit amount, so ask the district (740-845-1707) whether one applies.

The last rate schedule in the city code, Section 1042.02 (effective July 1, 2023), charged $17.63 for the first 2,000 gallons and $9.67 per 1,000 gallons from 2,001 to 15,000 gallons, with 3% increases every January 1 starting in 2024. The utilities page says the January 2024 changes added an $8.62 base-rate increase, $5.99 for sewer service, 3% on water and sanitation, and $1.50 for storm sewer. Because those increases compound and the district now sets charges, we have not calculated a current monthly bill; ask the district for its current schedule. Property owners remain responsible for the sewer lateral from the house to the district’s line.

Trash, gas, and electric

Trash changed in 2026. The city’s utilities page says Rumpke became London’s sanitation provider on August 1, 2026, at $20.75 a month for weekly residential pickup, billed directly by Rumpke every three months rather than on the water bill. Pickup days and the existing city carts stayed the same, yard waste can go in the trash cart, bulk pickup must be requested at least 24 hours ahead, upholstered items must be wrapped in plastic, extra carts beyond one trash and one recycling cart cost $3.50 a month, and the city transfer drop-off site closed on August 1. That replaces the city-billed rate still shown in Section 1060.09 of the code ($20.15 a month), which the published code had not yet caught up with. Leases written before August should say who now holds the Rumpke account.

The code’s editor’s notes say the city contracts with Columbia Gas of Ohio for natural gas and with Ohio Edison for electric service, and that council adopted a municipal electric aggregation plan in 2001. Tenants typically open gas and electric accounts in their own names with those utilities; confirm the provider for the specific address before move-in.

Short-term rentals: no separate chapter, so zoning controls

We did not find a short-term rental chapter, permit, or registration in London’s code. The zoning code instead regulates traditional lodging uses. Section 1240.11 defines a tourist home as a dwelling where the residents provide lodging, but not meals, for transients, and a bed and breakfast facility as lodging and breakfast for compensation to unrelated individuals. The Official Schedule of District Regulations in Section 1248.07 lists both as conditional uses in the R-1, R-2, R-3, and R-4 residential districts, the B-1 and B-3 business districts, and planned unit developments, and as permitted uses in the B-2 district. Hotels are permitted in B-2 and conditional in B-3 and planned unit developments.

A conditional use needs approval from the Board of Zoning Appeals. Under Section 1254.06, a tourist home may have no more than two adults per sleeping room (with children, no more than five people in a room), guests may not stay longer than two continuous weeks, and the home should have no more than six guest rooms; bed and breakfasts follow similar rules with up to three adults per room. Every applicant must submit a parking and circulation plan, and the general application rules in Section 1254.03 require a site plan, a narrative addressing the review standards, a fee set by council, and the names and mailing addresses of all owners within 200 feet. Because the tourist home definition assumes the residents are the hosts, a whole-house rental by an owner who lives elsewhere is not clearly covered. Ask Building & Zoning (City Hall, Suite 105, 740-852-7045) before listing a London property, and expect the rental income to be taxable on the city return.

Lodging tax: Madison County’s 3%

We did not find a city lodging tax: the taxation title of London’s code contains only the income tax, real estate tax, and motor vehicle license tax chapters. Madison County does levy one. The Ohio Department of Taxation’s Table S-4 of local lodging taxes, the most recent edition we could retrieve (calendar year 2018), lists Madison County at 3.00%, Deercreek Township at 6.00%, and Plain City, Pleasant Township, and Range Township at 3.00%, with no separate entry for the City of London. Ohio Revised Code 5739.09 allows a county to levy up to 3% on lodging furnished to transient guests by a hotel, and requires the county to return part of the revenue collected in cities and townships that do not levy their own tax. We could not confirm whether Madison County applies its tax to rentals with fewer than five guest rooms, which a 2001 state law change lets counties choose to do, so short-term hosts should ask the Madison County Auditor before their first stay.

Leases and deposits: Ohio law applies

We did not find a local landlord-tenant code in London, so Ohio Revised Code Chapter 5321 governs security deposits, repairs, entry notice, and eviction notices. Under Section 5321.16, a landlord must return the deposit, with an itemized list of any deductions, within 30 days after the lease ends and the tenant gives up possession, and the part of a deposit above $50 or one month’s rent, whichever is greater, earns 5% interest each year if the tenant stays six months or more. Ohio Revised Code 1901.02 establishes the Madison County Municipal Court in London, so eviction cases for London addresses are filed there. Our Ohio landlord-tenant basics page walks through the statewide rules.

Getting around: Fayette Madison Transit, by reservation

London has no fixed-route city bus. Fayette Madison Transit, run by the Community Action Commission of Fayette County, describes itself as the only public transit service available to all residents of Fayette and Madison counties; in Madison County it runs demand-response trips that you book by calling 740-335-9628, in accessible vehicles. According to the United Way of Clark, Champaign and Madison Counties, new hours and fares took effect January 1, 2026: service runs Monday through Friday from 7:00 to 10:00 a.m. and from 2:00 to 5:00 p.m., and one-way fares are $2.00 within the City of London, $2.50 elsewhere in Madison County, $5.00 to Fayette County, and $3.00 per mile for trips outside the service area. Advance reservations are requested, and same-day rides depend on availability. The county commissioners announced a financial contribution to keep the service running in October 2025.

That schedule leaves midday, evening, and weekend trips uncovered, and we found no COTA or other fixed-route bus serving London, so a tenant without a car should test the trips they need before signing. Commuters should also remember that London taxes their wages in full no matter where they work.

Vouchers and what the SAFMR table says

Madison County is part of the Columbus, OH HUD Metro FMR Area, along with Delaware, Fairfield, Franklin, Licking, Morrow, and Pickaway counties, and HUD sets Small Area Fair Market Rents by ZIP code in that area. HUD’s FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents for London’s ZIP 43140 are:

For comparison, the same table lists a two-bedroom at $1,250 in ZIP 43162 (West Jefferson), $1,620 in ZIP 43123 (Grove City), $1,880 in ZIP 43064 (Plain City), and $1,920 in ZIP 43026 (Hilliard). These are payment-standard benchmarks, not asking rents and not automatic approvals; contract rent still goes through rent-reasonableness review, utility allowances affect the gross-rent comparison, and ZIP 43140 includes rural areas outside the city.

The local public housing agency is the London Metropolitan Housing Authority at 179 South Main Street (740-852-1888), open weekdays from 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. with an hour closed for lunch. Its website did not post a voucher payment standard schedule or say which programs it currently runs when we checked, so landlords considering a voucher tenant, and voucher holders moving from another county, should call to confirm the payment standard for the unit size and whether a voucher can be used or transferred to a London address.

What landlords and tenants should check before signing

  • Jurisdiction. Confirm the parcel is inside the City of London rather than a surrounding township; ZIP 43140 covers both, and taxes, zoning, and utilities differ.
  • Income tax. Budget for the full 1.5% to London on top of any work-city tax, because London gives no credit. Register within 30 days of moving in and file every year from age 18; retirees with only nontaxable income can file the exemption certificate.
  • School tax. Add the 1.00% London City Schools tax on a traditional base, which includes pension and investment income, filed with the state on Form SD 100. Check the district on the auditor’s record, since nearby Madison County districts are at 1.00% to 1.25% in 2026.
  • Tenant list. Landlords owe the Director of Taxation a list of tenants and addresses every October 1, or a $75 late penalty applies.
  • Registration. No rental registry or county-auditor filing, but a rooming house needs an annual permit from the Health Commissioner, and more than five unrelated occupants fall outside the zoning definition of a family.
  • Water and sewer. Tenants apply to the Mid-Ohio Water & Sewer District themselves; any old district debt in the household must be paid first, and bills are due on the 20th with a 10% late fee.
  • Trash. Rumpke bills quarterly since August 1, 2026; say in the lease who holds the account.
  • Short stays. Tourist homes and bed and breakfasts need a conditional use permit in residential districts; ask Building & Zoning about whole-house rentals and the county auditor about the 3% lodging tax.
  • Vouchers. No local source-of-income protection; call the London Metropolitan Housing Authority about payment standards.
  • Transit. No fixed-route bus; Fayette Madison Transit runs by reservation on weekdays in two three-hour windows.

Frequently asked questions

Is London, Ohio part of Columbus?

No. London is a separate city and the county seat of Madison County, about 25 miles southwest of Columbus, with a 2020 census population of 10,279. It has its own council, income tax, zoning code, and fair housing ordinance, so Columbus-only rules such as the Columbus rental registry, Renter’s Choice, and Columbus source-of-income protections do not apply. ZIP 43140 also covers areas outside the city, so confirm the parcel is inside city limits.

What is the London, Ohio income tax rate?

The City of London’s income tax is 1.5%, made up of a 1% general levy and a 0.5% levy for the Fire Department. Since January 1, 2021, London has given no credit for income tax paid to another city, so a resident who earns $60,000 in a city with a 2.5% tax pays $1,500 there and still owes London $900. All residents over 18 must file a London return each year, and new residents must register with the tax office within 30 days.

Is there a school district income tax in London, Ohio?

Yes. The Ohio Department of Taxation lists London City School District (district number 4903) at 1.00% on a traditional base, which starts from Ohio adjusted gross income and so reaches pensions, interest, and dividends as well as wages. It is filed with the state on Form SD 100, not with the city. NCES reports London City Schools had 2,139 students in 4 schools in 2024–2025.

Do London landlords have to report their tenants?

Yes. Section 881.21 of London’s income tax ordinance requires all landlords who rent property in the city to give the Director of Taxation a current list of their tenants, with addresses, every October 1. A late list carries a $75 penalty per year, and the Director is to send written notice of the requirement at least 30 days before the due date.

Does London, Ohio have a rental registry?

We did not find a City of London rental registration, rental license, or rental inspection program. Ohio’s county-auditor rental filing in Revised Code 5323 applies only in counties with more than 200,000 residents, and Madison County had 43,824 at the 2020 census. Rooming houses do need an annual permit from the Health Commissioner under Chapter 1488.

Who provides water and sewer service in London, Ohio?

The Mid-Ohio Water and Sewer District has managed London’s water and sewer service since city ordinances passed July 18, 2024. Every new resident, owner or renter, completes the district’s application for service, and any past-due district balance owed by the applicant or household must be paid first. Bills are due on the 20th, with a 10% late fee on the 21st and a $50 reconnection fee after a shutoff.

Can I run an Airbnb in London, Ohio?

London has no separate short-term rental ordinance that we could find, so zoning controls. The zoning code treats a tourist home, where the residents provide lodging to transients, and a bed and breakfast as conditional uses in the residential districts and permitted uses in the B-2 district, with guest stays limited to two continuous weeks and no more than six guest rooms. A whole-house rental by an owner who lives elsewhere is not clearly addressed, so ask Building & Zoning at 740-852-7045 first.

Is there a lodging tax on short-term rentals in London, Ohio?

We found no City of London lodging tax. The Ohio Department of Taxation’s most recent lodging tax table we could retrieve, for 2018, lists a 3.00% Madison County lodging tax. We could not confirm whether the county applies it to rentals with fewer than five guest rooms, so hosts should ask the Madison County Auditor.

Can London landlords refuse housing vouchers?

London’s fair housing ordinance, Chapter 626, protects race, color, creed, sex, marital status, religious belief, national origin, familial status, age, and handicap, but not source of income, and London is not on AHACO’s list of central Ohio communities with source-of-income protections. Federal and Ohio fair housing law still applies, so landlords should screen every applicant with the same written criteria.

Does COTA serve London, Ohio?

We found no COTA or other fixed-route bus service in London. Fayette Madison Transit, which says it is the only public transit available to all Madison County residents, runs demand-response trips booked at 740-335-9628, Monday through Friday from 7 to 10 a.m. and 2 to 5 p.m., for $2.00 one way within London and $2.50 elsewhere in the county as of January 1, 2026.

What is the voucher payment standard in London, Ohio?

HUD’s FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents for ZIP 43140 are $990 for a studio, $1,060 for one bedroom, $1,260 for two, $1,500 for three, and $1,710 for four. Payment standards are set by the housing agency, and the London Metropolitan Housing Authority (740-852-1888) did not post a schedule online, so call to confirm the figure for your unit size.
