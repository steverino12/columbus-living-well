New Albany: a renting and living guide to the suburb northeast of Columbus

What landlords and tenants should know about New Albany, the Franklin and Licking County city along State Route 161 and U.S. Route 62 northeast of Columbus, including its 2% city income tax collected by RITA, the absence of a city rental registry in the published code, who bills water and trash, how short stays and fair housing look on paper, New Albany–Plain Local Schools, COTA service to the Park and Ride, and HUD voucher figures for ZIP 43054, without inventing market rents.

Last updated November 7, 2026. Facts below come from the City of New Albany and its Codified Ordinances, RITA, the Ohio Revised Code, COTA, NCES, HUD, AHACO, the U.S. Census Bureau, and Wikipedia’s summary of the city, all cited in Sources. Asking-rent medians are not invented here; see Rent Pulse and Fair Market Rents for verified figures.

A separate suburb, not a Columbus neighborhood

New Albany is its own city, not part of Columbus. It sits mostly in Franklin County with a smaller portion in Licking County, about 15 miles northeast of downtown Columbus. Nobel Landon and William Yantis founded the settlement in the center of Plain Township in 1837; it was incorporated in 1856 with a population of about 50. The 2020 census counted 10,825 residents. The Census Bureau’s July 1, 2025 population estimate is 11,803, up from 7,724 in 2010 and 3,711 in 2000. Census QuickFacts lists about 16.56 square miles of land area as of 2020.

Rapid growth since the 1990s reshaped a once-small village into a planned suburb with Market Square, large-lot residential neighborhoods, and the New Albany International Business Park along the State Route 161 corridor. Major employers cited in public reporting include Abercrombie & Fitch, Bob Evans Farms, Aetna, American Electric Power, Mount Carmel New Albany Surgical Hospital, and large data-center and semiconductor projects planned or underway in and around the business park. Columbus-only programs, such as the Columbus rental registry and Renter’s Choice, do not apply here.

The city’s own tax page warns that a New Albany mailing address is not always inside city limits: some 43054 addresses sit in Plain Township or even Columbus and are not subject to New Albany income tax. Use the Ohio Department of Taxation’s municipal address lookup, or confirm the parcel with the Franklin or Licking County auditor, before assuming which city’s rules apply. Nearby suburbs with their own guides here include Gahanna to the south and west, Reynoldsburg farther south along the east side, Whitehall, and Westerville to the northwest; comparing them shows how tax credits, registries, and transit differ across the region.

Income tax: 2%, RITA-administered, with a full work-city credit

New Albany’s municipal income tax rate is 2.0% on individual earned income and corporate net profits, according to the city’s Taxes page. The tax does not apply to investment earnings or retirement income. The Regional Income Tax Agency (RITA) collects it; RITA’s municipality page for New Albany (member since June 1, 1993) lists a 2.000% rate from January 1, 2020 to the present, with a 100% credit factor and a 2.000% credit limit. In practice, a New Albany resident who works in Columbus (2.5%) gets credit for tax paid to Columbus up to New Albany’s 2.0% rate and typically owes New Albany nothing more on those wages—unlike suburbs such as Pickerington, which credit only half a percent.

Filing is mandatory for all individuals who live within city limits and for businesses operating inside corporate boundaries. Calendar-year individual returns are due April 15. RITA’s notes for New Albany say individuals under 18 are exempt from paying the municipal income tax, amounts under $10.01 are not collected or refunded (except employer withholdings), and quarterly estimated payments are required when the anticipated amount due is $200 or more. Questions go to RITA at 1-800-860-7482 or ritaohio.com. RITA also lists Jersey–New Albany JEDD I, II, and III as related taxing arrangements for some business-park territory; confirm the exact jurisdiction for a work address inside those districts.

Property taxes are separate from the income tax. The city’s Taxes page cites the Franklin County Auditor’s 2025 figure of $2,421.88 per $100,000 of fair market value for Tax District 222 (City of New Albany, Franklin County, New Albany–Plain Local Schools, Plain Township Fire). Property-tax questions go to the county auditor for the parcel’s county.

Rental registration: county auditor rule, no city chapter found

We did not find a citywide rental registration, rental permit, or landlord-license chapter in New Albany’s published Codified Ordinances (Municode, codified through Ordinance O-21-2026). That is a real difference from nearby suburbs such as Gahanna, which runs a biennial registration and inspection program, and Reynoldsburg, which has a city rental registry. Absence of a city chapter does not mean rentals are unregulated.

Ohio Revised Code 5323.02 still requires owners of residential rental property to file owner and contact information with the county auditor where the property sits. For most New Albany addresses that is the Franklin County Auditor; for the Licking County portion it is the Licking County Auditor. Out-of-state owners must designate an in-state management contact or provide an Ohio statutory-agent filing. Failure to file or update can draw a fine. Day-to-day habitability still rests on Ohio Revised Code Chapter 5321 and any applicable property-maintenance rules the city enforces through its code-enforcement staff.

Fair housing: state and federal rules, no local SOI found

We did not find a New Albany ordinance that adds source of income—Housing Choice Vouchers, disability payments, child support, and similar lawful income—to local fair-housing protections. AHACO’s central Ohio source-of-income list includes Bexley, Columbus, Gahanna, Grandview Heights, Pickerington, Reynoldsburg, Upper Arlington, Westerville, Whitehall, and Worthington; New Albany is not on that list. Ohio and federal fair housing laws still prohibit discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, disability, and familial status, and complaints can go to the Ohio Civil Rights Commission or HUD.

Because local SOI protection is not established here, a landlord’s written screening criteria matter even more. Apply the same standards to every applicant, document decisions, and do not invent a city voucher ban that the code does not state. Tenants who are denied because of a voucher should ask for the reason in writing and consider contacting AHACO or the Ohio Civil Rights Commission. For comparison, see how Whitehall and Reynoldsburg handle source-of-income rules in their guides.

Short stays and the 6% transient occupancy tax

We did not find a dedicated short-term rental permit chapter, occupancy-limit ordinance, or platform-registration rule aimed at whole-home Airbnbs in New Albany’s published code. What we did find is Chapter 184, Transient Occupancy Tax. Section 184.01 defines a hotel as an establishment with five or more guest rooms and a transient accommodation as one with four or fewer, defines transient guests as people staying fewer than 30 consecutive days, and Section 184.03 levies a combined 6% excise tax—3% under Ohio Revised Code 5739.02(C)(1) plus an additional 3%—on lodging furnished to transient guests. Operators must register with the Finance Director within 30 days of commencing business and collect the tax from guests.

In 2026, Ordinance O-11-2026 confirmed that half of the additional 3% continues to fund tourism-related purposes, now administered by the city’s Strategic Initiatives Department after the city assumed Convention and Visitors Bureau functions. The ordinance did not change the tax rate. Because the code defines small transient accommodations but does not spell out a residential STR permit path, a host should not treat silence as approval. Get a written zoning answer for the parcel, check the lease and any homeowners association rules, register if you are collecting lodging for stays under 30 days, and plan to remit the 6% tax.

Leases and deposits: Ohio law applies

The core lease rules come from Ohio’s landlord-tenant law, Ohio Revised Code Chapter 5321. It sets landlord duties to keep the unit fit and habitable and make repairs, tenant duties to keep the unit safe and sanitary, the rule that a landlord must return a deposit with an itemized list of deductions within 30 days after the tenancy ends, and the notice expected before a landlord enters, except in emergencies. Our Ohio landlord-tenant law guide covers these rules in more detail. Our Tenant Playbook lists practical steps for tenants.

Utilities: Columbus water billing, New Albany sewers, Rumpke trash

Water. New Albany owns the water mains inside city limits but contracts with the City of Columbus Division of Water for service, maintenance of public mains, billing, and water quality. The city’s utilities page directs billing and quality questions to Columbus at 614-645-8276, rusty-water reports to 614-645-7691, and water-break reports to 614-645-7788. Service lines from the curb to the home are the property owner’s responsibility. We did not find a New Albany-specific published renter deposit for water, so we do not invent one; ask Columbus Water & Power and the landlord whose name will be on the account.

Sewer and stormwater. The New Albany Public Service Department maintains more than 60 miles of sanitary sewer lines serving roughly 3,600 customers and a roughly 62-mile storm sewer system; wastewater ultimately goes to Columbus for treatment. Sewer and stormwater issues during business hours go to 614-855-0076, and after hours to 614-855-1234. Confirm before signing whether sanitary sewer charges appear on the Columbus water bill, on a separate city bill, or stay in the owner’s name—the city’s public pages emphasize Columbus for water billing and New Albany for sewer maintenance without publishing a single combined residential rate card we could cite here.

Trash and recycling. Rumpke collects trash, recycling, and yard waste and bills residents directly. The city’s recycling and waste page says Thursday is the normal collection day, that materials should be at the curb before 7 a.m., and that missed-collection questions go to Rumpke at 1-800-828-8171 (the utilities contact list also shows 1-888-786-7531). We did not find a published Rumpke residential rate for New Albany, so we do not state one.

Other providers. The city’s utilities roster lists electric service through AEP Ohio (1-800-277-2177) and, for some customers, The Energy Cooperative (1-800-255-6815); natural gas through Columbia Gas (1-800-344-4077); and fiber options including New Albany Net. Call 811 / OUPS at 1-800-362-2764 before digging. Settle in the lease who holds each account.

Getting around: COTA Lines 35 and 45, plus SmartRide

Unlike farther-east suburbs that sit outside COTA’s fixed-route map, New Albany has direct COTA service centered on the New Albany Park and Ride. Line 35 (Dublin–Granville) is a standard line that COTA and local business reporting describe as running between the north Columbus area and the New Albany Park and Ride seven days a week, with timetable frequency in the standard 16-to-60-minute range. Line 45 (New Albany) is a weekday rush-hour express between the New Albany Park and Ride and Downtown Columbus. SmartRide New Albany shuttles, operated in partnership with COTA, run hourly between the Park and Ride and employers in the New Albany International Business Park.

COTA’s published adult fare for standard, frequent, and rush-hour service is $2, with free transfers valid for two hours; day passes are $4.50 and 31-day passes are $62, with half-price options for qualifying riders. For most household trips inside the residential neighborhoods, a car remains the practical default, but the Park and Ride makes downtown and business-park commuting more workable than in suburbs without fixed-route service. For bigger-picture transit plans, see Mobility & the Future.

Schools and daily life

New Albany is served by the New Albany–Plain Local School District. NCES lists the district (ID 3904699) as a regular local school district serving grades PK–12 with 5 schools and reports 4,991 students for the 2024–2025 school year, with about 297 classroom teachers (FTE) and a student–teacher ratio of 16.79. The district’s mailing address is 55 N High Street, New Albany, OH 43054. District lines can extend beyond city limits, and the city does not administer school boundaries, so confirm the assignment for the exact address with the district at napls.us or 614-855-2040.

Daily life centers on Market Square and the Village Center, the Jeanne B. McCoy Community Center for the Arts, the New Albany branch of the Columbus Metropolitan Library, Founders Day each May, and the city’s parks and trail network along Rocky Fork and related greenways. The business park, data centers, and planned semiconductor investment shape employment and traffic along State Route 161 more than a typical bedroom suburb.

What landlords and tenants should check before signing

  • City, township, or Columbus. Confirm the address is inside New Albany and not a Plain Township or Columbus parcel with a 43054 mailing address; income tax and services differ.
  • Registration. No city rental registry found; keep the county auditor filing current under Ohio Revised Code 5323.02 for Franklin or Licking County as applicable.
  • Income tax. Residents file through RITA at 2.0% with a full credit up to 2.0% for tax paid to the work city. Confirm JEDD status for business-park work addresses.
  • Vouchers. No local source-of-income protection found on AHACO’s list or in the code sections we reviewed; apply consistent written screening either way.
  • Short stays. No dedicated permit chapter found; register and collect the 6% transient occupancy tax on stays under 30 days, and get a written zoning answer first.
  • Utility accounts. Columbus bills water; New Albany maintains sewers; Rumpke bills trash directly. Settle in the lease who holds each bill.
  • Transit. COTA Lines 35 and 45 serve the New Albany Park and Ride; SmartRide links the Park and Ride to the business park. Plan on a car for most neighborhood trips.

Frequently asked questions

Is New Albany part of Columbus?

No. New Albany is a separate city in Franklin and Licking counties, about 15 miles northeast of downtown Columbus, with a 2020 census population of 10,825. It has its own city council, police department, and income tax collected through RITA, so Columbus-only rules such as Renter’s Choice and the Columbus rental registry do not apply. A New Albany mailing address can sit in Plain Township or even Columbus, so confirm the jurisdiction for the exact address.

What is the New Albany income tax rate?

The city’s municipal income tax rate is 2.0% on earned income and corporate net profits, administered by the Regional Income Tax Agency (RITA). Residents get a 100% credit for tax paid to another municipality, capped at 2.0% of the wages taxed elsewhere, so a Columbus commuter typically owes New Albany nothing more on those wages. Filing is mandatory for individuals who live in the city and for businesses operating inside city limits. Investment earnings and retirement income are not taxed by the city.

Does New Albany have a rental registry?

We did not find a citywide rental registration or landlord-permit chapter in New Albany’s published Codified Ordinances. Ohio Revised Code 5323.02 still requires owners of residential rental property to register with the county auditor—Franklin County for most of the city and Licking County for the eastern portion—so keep that filing current. Columbus’s rental registry does not apply inside New Albany.

Can New Albany landlords refuse housing vouchers?

New Albany is not on AHACO’s list of central Ohio cities that have adopted source-of-income protections, and we did not find a local fair-housing ordinance adding source of income as a protected class. Ohio and federal fair housing laws still cover race, color, religion, sex, national origin, disability, and familial status. Landlords should apply the same written screening standards to every applicant, and tenants denied because of a voucher can ask for the reason in writing and contact AHACO or the Ohio Civil Rights Commission.

Can I run an Airbnb in New Albany?

We did not find a dedicated short-term rental permit chapter in the city code. Chapter 184 levies a combined 6% transient occupancy tax on lodging furnished to guests staying fewer than 30 consecutive days, defines a hotel as five or more guest rooms and a transient accommodation as four or fewer, and requires operators to register with the Finance Director within 30 days of starting business. Silence on whole-home listings is not permission, so get a written zoning answer from the city and the owner’s consent before listing.

Who handles water and trash in New Albany?

The City of Columbus Division of Water handles water billing and quality for New Albany addresses; New Albany owns the water mains and maintains sanitary and storm sewer lines, with wastewater treated by Columbus. Trash, recycling, and yard waste are collected by Rumpke, which bills residents directly, with Thursday as the city’s normal collection day. Electric is typically AEP Ohio, and natural gas is Columbia Gas; confirm providers and whose name is on each account before signing a lease.

Does COTA serve New Albany?

Yes. COTA Line 35 (Dublin–Granville) is a standard line that serves the New Albany Park and Ride seven days a week, and Line 45 is a weekday rush-hour express between the New Albany Park and Ride and Downtown Columbus. SmartRide New Albany shuttles run hourly between the Park and Ride and employers in the New Albany International Business Park. Standard adult fares are $2 with free transfers valid for two hours.

What is the voucher payment standard in New Albany?

For ZIP 43054, HUD’s FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents, effective October 1, 2026, are $1,810 for a studio, $1,930 for one bedroom, $2,300 for two, $2,740 for three, and $3,120 for four bedrooms. These are payment-standard benchmarks for voucher holders, not asking rents. The ZIP also covers areas outside city limits, and the housing authority that administers a particular voucher sets the actual payment standard.
