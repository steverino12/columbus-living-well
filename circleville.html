Circleville: a renting and living guide to the Pickaway County seat south of Columbus

What landlords and tenants should know about the City of Circleville, the Pickaway County seat on the Scioto River, including its 2.5% city income tax with a full credit for tax paid where you work, the 0.75% Circleville City Schools earned-income tax, why Ohio’s county-auditor rental filing does not reach Pickaway County, the vacant building registration, the 2026 change that puts every new water and sewer account in the owner’s name, short-term rental zoning and the 6% city lodging tax, PICCA Transit’s free Flex Bus and Rickenbacker Shuttle, and HUD voucher figures for ZIP 43113, without inventing market rents.

Last updated November 15, 2026. Facts below come from the City of Circleville, its zoning code and council legislation, the Ohio Department of Taxation, the Ohio Revised Code, PICCA Transit, the Pickaway Metropolitan Housing Authority, NCES, HUD, AHACO, Rumpke, local news coverage of council meetings, and Wikipedia’s summaries of the city and county, all cited in Sources. Asking-rent medians are not invented here; see Rent Pulse and Fair Market Rents for verified figures.

Roundtown: a county seat on the Scioto

Circleville is a separate city and the county seat of Pickaway County, on the eastern bank of the Scioto River about 25 miles south of Columbus and 22 miles north of Chillicothe. Wikipedia’s summary explains the name: the town was laid out in 1810 inside a circular Hopewell earthwork about 1,100 feet across, with the county courthouse at the center of the innermost circle. Residents later found the round lots inconvenient, and between 1838 and 1856 a “Circleville Squaring Company” converted the plan to a standard grid. The town was incorporated in 1814 and became a city on March 25, 1853. Its nickname is still Roundtown, and its motto is “Home of the Pumpkin Show,” after the Circleville Pumpkin Show held every October.

The 2020 census counted 13,927 residents, 5,625 households, and 6,130 housing units, according to Wikipedia’s summary, with a 2023 Census Bureau estimate of 14,452. Of the housing units, 8.2% were vacant. Among occupied homes, 55.2% were owner-occupied and 44.8% renter-occupied, a much larger rental share than most suburbs we cover. The homeowner vacancy rate was 2.6% and the rental vacancy rate was 6.7%. The median age was 39.3, and 27.6% of households had children under 18.

Circleville is a manufacturing town with its own job base. Wikipedia lists DuPont’s largest Ohio chemical plant, which makes Mylar and Tedlar films; a long-running PACCAR truck factory; a Sofidel tissue plant on the south side that produced its first roll in 2018 and is expected to employ about 700 people at full capacity; and PPG’s polymer resin plant. OhioHealth, the local school districts, the State of Ohio, and Walmart are other major employers, and Ohio Christian University has operated in the city since 1948. Many tenants work in town or commute north toward Rickenbacker and Columbus, which matters for the income tax and transit sections below.

Because Circleville is a separate city, Columbus-only rules such as the Columbus rental registry, Renter’s Choice, and Columbus source-of-income protections do not apply. The 43113 ZIP code also covers surrounding Pickaway County townships, and the city charges higher water and sewer rates outside its limits. Confirm that a parcel is inside city limits before relying on anything below.

Income tax: 2.5% with a full credit

The City of Circleville’s income tax page lists a 2.5% rate effective January 1, 2020, with a 100% credit, up to 2.5%, for municipal income tax paid elsewhere. The page also shows the history: 2.0% with a full credit from 2015 through 2019, and 1.5% with only a 50% credit from 1988 through 2014. The city calls income tax its largest single source of revenue. It is a member of the Regional Income Tax Agency (RITA), which collects the tax, and it keeps a local income tax office open two days a week.

The full credit is good news for commuters. A Circleville resident who earns $60,000 in Columbus pays Columbus $1,500 at 2.5%, and the credit wipes out the Circleville tax on those wages, so the city return shows nothing more due on that income. A resident who works in a city with a lower rate owes Circleville the difference, and a resident who works in a township with no municipal income tax owes the full 2.5% to Circleville.

Circleville has mandatory filing. The city says every resident 18 or older must file a Circleville return whether or not tax is due, partial-year residents must file too, and the deadline is April 15. Anyone who expects to owe $200 or more must make quarterly estimated payments, due April 15, June 15, September 15, and December 15. RITA offers free e-filing, e-payment, registration, and walk-in help.

School district income tax: 0.75% on earned income

Circleville City Schools adds a school district income tax. The Ohio Department of Taxation’s school district income tax table lists Circleville City School District (district number 6501) at 0.75% on an earned-income base, a rate the table shows in effect since 2006 with no scheduled end. The state’s guide to the school district income tax explains that an earned-income base covers only wages, salaries, tips, other compensation, and net self-employment earnings, not interest, dividends, capital gains, or pensions. On $60,000 in wages, that is $450 a year. Unlike the city tax, there is no credit for tax paid where you work, and it is filed with the state on Form SD 100.

Check the school district for the exact parcel on the Pickaway County Auditor’s record, because neighboring Pickaway County districts in the same state table carry different rates: Logan Elm Local at 1.00% (scheduled through 2030), Teays Valley Local at 1.50%, and Westfall Local at 0.00%.

NCES lists Circleville City (district ID 3904376) as a PK–12 district with 3 schools, 2,114 students, and 112 classroom teachers (FTE) in the 2024–2025 school year, a student–teacher ratio of 18.88. Its reported total revenue was $41.3 million in fiscal 2022–2023, about $19,034 per student, with 46% from local sources and 19% from federal sources. Wikipedia notes that the district runs one elementary school, one middle school, and Circleville High School.

Rental registration: none found, but vacant buildings must register

We did not find a City of Circleville rental registration, rental license, or proactive rental inspection program for long-term rentals. Ohio’s statewide rental filing does not apply either. Ohio Revised Code 5323.01 limits “residential rental property” for the county-auditor registration in Section 5323.02 to property in a county with more than 200,000 residents at the most recent census, and Pickaway County had 58,539 in 2020, according to Wikipedia’s county summary.

Rentals are still subject to code enforcement. Chapter 1335 of Circleville’s codified ordinances adopts the International Property Maintenance Code, 2018 edition as amended or updated, to keep structures safe, sanitary, and fit for occupancy. The city’s Code Enforcement office in the Service Department, 104 E. Franklin Street (740-477-8224), enforces the zoning code, the property maintenance code, and the vacant building program, and takes violation reports online.

The Vacant Building Enforcement Program, created by Ordinance 11-56-2017, requires owners of vacant, abandoned, or foreclosure-stage buildings to register them under Chapter 1337. The city’s registration form says a registration lasts 12 months and must be renewed each year, and the fees double annually:

Year vacantResidentialCommercial
1st year$200$400
2nd year$400$800
3rd year$800$1,600
4th year$1,600$3,200
5th year and beyond$3,200$6,400

Owners must submit a plan with the registration: a demolition timeline, a plan to keep the building secured and maintained along with the reason it will stay vacant, or a rehabilitation plan that generally may not run more than 12 months after permits are issued. The city lists four grounds for exemption: damage from fire, vandalism, or a natural disaster; a building listed for sale; a building listed for rent or lease; and a building under construction or being remodeled. A landlord between tenants who is actively marketing the unit should therefore file the exemption form rather than pay the fee. City council has levied unpaid vacant building fines as assessments on property, as in Ordinances 12-84-2023 and 10-72-2024.

Circleville also has a Historic Review Board, which meets in the council chambers at 127 S. Court Street. Check with the city before changing the exterior of an older rental downtown.

Fair housing: federal and state law, no local voucher protection found

Circleville is not on AHACO’s list of ten central Ohio communities that have adopted source-of-income protections: Bexley, Columbus, Gahanna, Grandview Heights, Pickerington, Reynoldsburg, Upper Arlington, Westerville, Whitehall, and Worthington. We did not find a local ordinance requiring landlords to accept housing vouchers. Federal and Ohio fair housing laws still apply, so any income or credit standards should be written down and applied the same way to every applicant.

Water and sewer: new accounts go in the owner’s name

Circleville’s biggest 2026 change for landlords is in utility billing. The city’s Department of Public Utilities provides water and sanitary sewer service, and its overview page says that, effective June 3, 2026, it no longer opens new utility accounts in a tenant’s name. New service for a rental property must be set up in the name of the deeded owner. The owner may have bills mailed to the tenant and let the tenant make payments, but the account stays in the owner’s name, and the city no longer collects utility deposits for new rental accounts. Owners and tenants can still make their own private arrangements about who pays.

The rule comes from Ordinance 06-25-2026, passed by council and approved by the mayor on June 2, 2026. It directs the utilities department to sign all new water and sewer contracts with property owners only and to convert existing tenant accounts into the owner’s name within twelve months. After that period, the ordinance says, the city will automatically convert any remaining tenant accounts and add the deposit amount to the next billing cycle. The ordinance cites House Bill 92, which it says passed the Ohio House on March 18, 2026. The Scioto Post reported that the bill would bar cities from placing liens on a landlord’s property for a tenant’s unpaid water bill, and that the utilities director told a council committee that about a third of the city’s roughly 5,300 utility accounts were in tenants’ names. The Circleville Herald reported that council lengthened the conversion period from six months to twelve. We did not confirm HB 92’s status in the Ohio Senate.

Two practical effects follow. A landlord should decide whether water and sewer are included in the rent or billed back to the tenant under the lease, and should budget for unpaid bills now that the account is in the owner’s name; in September 2026 council passed Ordinance 09-57-2026, levying assessments for outstanding delinquent water and sewer bills on specific utility accounts. Voucher holders should ask how the change affects their utility allowance, since the Scioto Post reported that a council member raised that concern.

The city’s water and sewer rates page lists these charges:

ServiceInside the cityOutside the city
Water$8.50 monthly service charge plus $6.66 per 100 cu. ft.$8.50 plus $9.99 per 100 cu. ft.
Sewer$9.50 monthly service charge plus $10.84 per 100 cu. ft.$9.50 plus $16.25 per 100 cu. ft.

As an illustration using our own arithmetic from that page, a household inside the city using 500 cubic feet of water in a month (about 3,740 gallons) would pay about $41.80 for water and $63.70 for sewer, or roughly $105.50. Council adopted new water rates in Ordinance 01-01-2026 and new sewer rates in Ordinance 12-84-2025; confirm current charges with the Utilities Office at 108 E. Franklin Street (740-477-8255). Owners sign up for service with the city’s Property Owner Contract for Water and/or Sewer, and an optional autopay form is available.

Trash is not a city utility as far as we could find. The city’s stormwater page points residents to Rumpke for trash and recycling information, and Rumpke says it serves Circleville homes with weekly pickup, pricing by quote, and bulk pickup with 48 hours’ notice. The Circleville Herald reported in 2025 that Rumpke’s voluntary curbside recycling add-on for single-family homes costs $6 a month for existing Rumpke trash customers and $8 for others. Leases should say who arranges and pays for trash. We did not confirm the gas and electric providers for Circleville addresses.

Short-term rentals: allowed by zoning, with conditions

Circleville regulates Airbnb-style rentals through its zoning code rather than a separate permit chapter. The official zoning code, as amended June 3, 2025, defines a short-term rental as a dwelling unit, or part of one, offered to a guest for a fee for fewer than 30 consecutive nights, including whole houses, apartments, condominiums, and individual rooms, and naming bed-and-breakfasts, Airbnbs, VRBOs, and vacation rentals as examples.

Under the code’s use tables, a short-term rental is a conditional use in every residential district (AG, LDR, MDR, HSU, TUR, MUR, and MHR). That means the owner must apply to the Board of Zoning Appeals under Section 1113.06 with a site plan, a narrative on the effect on neighbors, the names and addresses of nearby property owners, and the review fee, and the board holds a public hearing and may approve, add conditions, or deny. In the business districts, short-term rentals are permitted with site plan design approval in the Neighborhood Business and General Business districts and permitted outright in the Downtown Business district. We did not find a separate short-term rental license, registration, or fee schedule; ask the Zoning Inspector (740-477-8224) before listing a unit. A tenant who wants to sublet on a booking platform needs both the zoning approval and the landlord’s written consent.

Lodging tax: 6% in the city

Circleville taxes short stays. Ordinance 07-38-2026, passed in July 2026, restates the city’s transient occupancy tax: a 3% excise tax on lodging furnished to transient guests, levied since May 6, 1988, plus an additional 3% excise tax, with both expressly covering short-term rentals. A transient guest is anyone who stays fewer than 30 consecutive days, and an operator includes anyone running a hotel, motel, bed and breakfast, or short-term vacation rental. Ordinance 07-39-2026 then reallocated the additional 3% so that half goes to the Pickaway County Visitor’s Center for tax year 2026 and all of it from 2027 on. The Ohio Department of Taxation’s 2018 Table S-4, the latest we could open, also lists Circleville at a 6.00% lodging rate and shows no countywide Pickaway County rate. Ask the city how to register and file. Rental profits are also subject to the 2.5% city income tax.

Leases and deposits: Ohio law applies

We did not find a local landlord-tenant code in Circleville, so Ohio Revised Code Chapter 5321 governs security deposits, repairs, entry notice, and eviction notices. Under Section 5321.16, a landlord generally must return the deposit, with an itemized list of any deductions, within 30 days after the lease ends and the tenant gives up possession. Ohio Revised Code 1901.02 gives the Circleville Municipal Court jurisdiction within Pickaway County, so eviction cases for Circleville addresses are filed there. Our Ohio landlord-tenant basics page walks through the statewide rules.

Getting around: PICCA Transit, not COTA

We found no COTA service in Circleville. Public transit in Pickaway County is run by PICCA Transit, based at 469 E. Ohio Street, and it offers three services:

  • Flex Bus. A free loop around Circleville, Monday through Friday from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., according to PICCA’s Flex Bus page, with stops including Walmart, Jobs One Stop, PICCA, Ohio Heights Apartments, Kroger, Apple Ridge Apartments, the Pickaway County Family YMCA, OhioHealth Berger Hospital, the Pickaway County Library, the courthouse, and Twin Oaks Apartments. Riders can request a deviation within the Flex Bus map, as long as the trip starts or ends at a regular stop.
  • Rickenbacker Shuttle. A free weekday commuter route between Circleville and the Rickenbacker job area, per PICCA’s shuttle schedule. The morning trip leaves PICCA at 5:50 a.m., stops at the library at 5:58 and the Ashville Neighborhood Center at 6:11, and reaches the Rickenbacker Marathon Station on London-Groveport Road at 6:30; the afternoon trip leaves Rickenbacker at 4:20 p.m. and returns by 5:00. No reservation is needed.
  • Demand Response. Door-to-door rides anywhere in Pickaway County, Monday through Friday from 7 a.m. to 4 p.m., booked up to four weeks ahead at 740-474-8835. PICCA’s fare page lists $1 with 24 hours’ notice or $3 with one hour’s notice for trips inside city limits, with a $1-per-mile charge on trips that start or end outside the city, and a 50% discount for riders approved through PICCA’s E/D application. PICCA proposed a flat in-county fare in 2025; confirm the current fare when booking.

There is no weekend or evening fixed-route service, so a tenant without a car who works outside these hours should map the commute before signing.

Vouchers and what the SAFMR table says

Pickaway County is part of the Columbus, OH HUD Metro FMR Area, along with Delaware, Fairfield, Franklin, Licking, Madison, and Morrow counties, and HUD sets Small Area Fair Market Rents by ZIP code in that area. HUD’s FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents for Circleville’s ZIP 43113 are:

Unit sizeFY2027 SAFMR, ZIP 43113
Studio$970
1 bedroom$1,030
2 bedrooms$1,230
3 bedrooms$1,470
4 bedrooms$1,670

For comparison, the same table lists $1,320 for a two-bedroom in ZIP 43103 (Ashville), $1,330 in ZIP 43130 (Lancaster), and $1,620 in ZIP 43123 (Grove City), so Circleville’s benchmark is among the lowest in the metro area. These are payment-standard benchmarks, not asking rents and not automatic approvals; contract rent still goes through rent-reasonableness review, utility allowances affect the gross-rent comparison, and ZIP 43113 includes township areas outside the city.

The Pickaway Metropolitan Housing Authority, at 176 Rustic Drive in Circleville (740-477-2514), runs the local Housing Choice Voucher program. Its landlord page says it sets payment standards between 90% and 110% of HUD’s Fair Market Rents, pays the housing assistance payment by direct deposit on the third working day of each month, and updates payment standards and utility allowances annually. Confirm the current payment standard and utility allowance with the housing authority that issued the voucher, especially now that new water and sewer accounts must be in the owner’s name. For how these numbers are built, see our Fair Market Rents explainer.

What landlords and tenants should check before signing

  • Jurisdiction. Confirm the parcel is inside the City of Circleville rather than a surrounding township; taxes, zoning, and utility rates differ.
  • Income tax. Budget for 2.5% to Circleville, with a full credit, up to 2.5%, for tax paid to a work city; a Columbus commuter typically owes nothing more to Circleville on those wages.
  • School tax. Add the 0.75% Circleville City Schools earned-income tax, filed with the state on Form SD 100, or the rate for whichever district the parcel is in.
  • Registration. No rental registry or county auditor filing, but a vacant building must be registered unless it qualifies for an exemption, such as being listed for rent.
  • Water and sewer. New accounts must be in the owner’s name since June 3, 2026, and existing tenant accounts are being converted; spell out in the lease whether water and sewer are included or billed back.
  • Trash. Arrange private trash service, such as Rumpke, and say in the lease who pays.
  • Short stays. Get conditional use approval from the Board of Zoning Appeals in a residential district, and collect and remit the 6% city lodging tax.
  • Vouchers. No local source-of-income protection found; apply consistent written screening either way.
  • Transit. No COTA; PICCA’s free Flex Bus and Rickenbacker Shuttle run on weekdays only.

Frequently asked questions

Is Circleville, Ohio part of Columbus?

No. Circleville is a separate city and the county seat of Pickaway County, on the Scioto River about 25 miles south of Columbus, with a 2020 census population of 13,927. It has its own council, income tax, utilities, and zoning code, so Columbus-only rules such as Renter’s Choice, the Columbus rental registry, and Columbus source-of-income protections do not apply. The 43113 ZIP code reaches beyond city limits, so confirm the parcel is inside the city.

What is the Circleville, Ohio income tax rate?

The City of Circleville’s income tax is 2.5%, in effect since January 1, 2020, and collected through RITA. Residents get a 100% credit, up to 2.5%, for municipal income tax paid where they work, so a resident earning $60,000 in Columbus would typically pay Columbus $1,500 and owe nothing more to Circleville on those wages. Every resident 18 or older must file a Circleville return by April 15, even if no tax is due.

Is there a school district income tax in Circleville?

Yes. The Ohio Department of Taxation lists Circleville City School District (district number 6501) at 0.75% on an earned-income base, which taxes wages and self-employment income but not pensions, interest, or dividends. It is filed with the state on Form SD 100 and gets no credit for tax paid elsewhere. NCES reports Circleville City Schools had 2,114 students in 3 schools in 2024–2025. Nearby Logan Elm (1.00%) and Teays Valley (1.50%) carry higher rates.

Does Circleville have a rental registry?

We did not find a City of Circleville rental registration, rental license, or proactive rental inspection program. Ohio’s county-auditor rental filing in Revised Code 5323 applies only in counties with more than 200,000 residents, and Pickaway County had 58,539 at the 2020 census. Circleville does require owners of vacant buildings to register under Chapter 1337, with fees from $200 to $3,200 a year for residential buildings, but a building listed for rent qualifies for an exemption.

Can tenants put water and sewer in their own name in Circleville?

Not for new accounts. Effective June 3, 2026, the City of Circleville no longer opens water or sewer accounts in a tenant’s name; new service for rental property must be in the deeded owner’s name under Ordinance 06-25-2026, and existing tenant accounts are being converted to the owner within twelve months. The owner can have bills mailed to the tenant and let the tenant pay, and the city no longer collects deposits for new rental accounts. Inside the city, water is $8.50 a month plus $6.66 per 100 cubic feet and sewer is $9.50 plus $10.84 per 100 cubic feet.

Who picks up trash in Circleville?

We did not find a city trash utility. The city’s stormwater page points residents to Rumpke for trash and recycling information, and Rumpke offers weekly pickup in Circleville with pricing by quote. A voluntary Rumpke curbside recycling add-on for single-family homes was reported at $6 a month for Rumpke trash customers and $8 for others. Leases should say who arranges and pays for trash.

Can I run an Airbnb in Circleville, Ohio?

Yes, with zoning approval. Circleville’s zoning code defines a short-term rental as a dwelling or part of one rented for fewer than 30 consecutive nights. In every residential district it is a conditional use, which requires an application and public hearing before the Board of Zoning Appeals under Section 1113.06. Short-term rentals are permitted with site plan approval in the Neighborhood and General Business districts and permitted outright in the Downtown Business district. We found no separate short-term rental license.

Is there a lodging tax on short-term rentals in Circleville?

Yes. Circleville levies a 3% transient occupancy tax, in place since May 6, 1988, plus an additional 3%, for 6% in total, and Ordinance 07-38-2026 states that both apply to short-term rentals as well as hotels. Stays of 30 consecutive days or more are not transient. The additional 3% goes partly to the Pickaway County Visitor’s Center in 2026 and entirely from 2027.

Can Circleville landlords refuse housing vouchers?

Circleville is not on AHACO’s list of ten central Ohio communities that have adopted source-of-income protections, and we found no local rule requiring landlords to accept vouchers. Federal and Ohio fair housing laws still apply. The Pickaway Metropolitan Housing Authority at 176 Rustic Drive (740-477-2514) runs the local Housing Choice Voucher program.

Does COTA serve Circleville, Ohio?

No. We found no COTA service in Circleville. PICCA Transit runs a free Flex Bus loop around the city on weekdays from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., a free weekday Rickenbacker Shuttle for commuters, and door-to-door Demand Response rides in Pickaway County on weekdays from 7 a.m. to 4 p.m., booked at 740-474-8835.

What is the voucher payment standard in Circleville, Ohio?

Pickaway County is part of the Columbus, OH HUD Metro FMR Area, where HUD sets Small Area Fair Market Rents by ZIP code. For ZIP 43113, HUD’s FY2027 SAFMRs are $970 for a studio, $1,030 for one bedroom, $1,230 for two, $1,470 for three, and $1,670 for four bedrooms. The Pickaway Metropolitan Housing Authority says it sets payment standards between 90% and 110% of HUD’s Fair Market Rents, so confirm the current figure with the agency that issued the voucher.
