Franklinton: a renting and living guide to Columbus’s oldest neighborhood

What landlords and tenants should know about Franklinton, the neighborhood just west of Downtown across the Scioto River, including who reviews zoning and design, what the 2004 floodwall does and doesn’t change, the new citywide rental registry with its December 31, 2026 deadline, Renter’s Choice deposit installments, short-term rental permits, COTA bus lines, and voucher figures for ZIPs 43215, 43222, and 43223, without inventing market rents.

Last updated October 30, 2026. Facts below come from the Columbus City Codes and Columbus City Council legislation, the City of Columbus, the Franklinton Area Commission, COTA, CMHA, FEMA, the Franklin County Auditor, Columbus Monthly, and Wikipedia’s summary of the neighborhood, all cited in Sources. Asking-rent medians are not invented here; see Rent Pulse and Fair Market Rents for verified figures.

Where Franklinton is

Franklinton sits immediately west of Downtown Columbus. Wikipedia’s summary describes it as bounded by the Scioto River on the north and east, Harmon Avenue on the east, Stimmel Road and Greenlawn Avenue on the south, and Interstate 70 on the west, with West Broad Street, part of the old National Road and U.S. Route 40, as its main street. West of I-70 are the neighborhoods commonly called the Hilltop. The Scioto Peninsula, home to COSI, the National Veterans Memorial and Museum, and Genoa Park, was historically part of Franklinton, though the City has counted it as part of Downtown since 1998.

Within Franklinton, East Franklinton is the area between State Route 315 and the river, closest to Downtown, where most new mixed-use buildings, galleries, and breweries have gone up. The rest of the neighborhood west of 315 is mostly older houses, small apartment buildings, industrial sites along McKinley and Harmon Avenues, and commercial frontage on West Broad Street.

A short history

Surveyor Lucas Sullivant laid out Franklinton in 1797 and named it for Benjamin Franklin, making it the first American settlement in what became Franklin County. It was the county seat from 1803 until 1824, served as a staging point for General William Henry Harrison’s Army of the Northwest in the War of 1812, and was annexed by Columbus in 1859. Railroads brought industry in the second half of the 1800s.

Low-lying land along the river earned part of the neighborhood the nickname “The Bottoms.” The Great Flood of March 1913 broke the levees and, according to Wikipedia’s account, swept 93 people to their deaths and left 20,000 homeless. After FEMA mapped almost all of Franklinton into the 1% annual-chance floodplain in 1983, new construction was restricted for two decades. Population peaked at 26,500 in the 1950 census and had fallen to an estimated 8,132 by 2017. Interstate construction in the 1960s further cut East Franklinton off from the rest of the neighborhood.

The floodwall and flood insurance

The Franklinton Floodwall, which the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers calls the Columbus Local Protection Project, was dedicated in 2004. Columbus Monthly reports that it is 7.14 miles of earth levees and concrete and sheet-pile walls, with 14 gate openings for road and rail traffic, that it was designed to withstand a 500-year flood, and that the City’s Department of Public Utilities maintains and operates it. Its completion lifted most of the old floodplain building restrictions and opened the way for redevelopment in East Franklinton.

That doesn’t make flood risk zero. FEMA notes that levees can deteriorate, fail, or be overtopped, and that flood insurance is available to owners, renters, and businesses even outside high-risk zones. A renter in a community that participates in the National Flood Insurance Program can buy coverage for the contents of their home. Standard renters and homeowners policies typically exclude flood, so before signing a lease or buying a rental here, look up the address on FEMA’s Flood Map Service Center and get a quote from an insurance agent.

Who reviews zoning and design

Franklinton Area Commission. The Franklinton Area Commission (FAC) is the neighborhood’s advisory body to the City. Its Zoning Committee reviews and makes recommendations on rezonings, variances, and special permits, and it also reviews demolition permits in the Franklinton area. Cases must reach the Zoning Chair at least 14 calendar days before the FAC meeting, and the chair tries to notify property owners within 125 feet. The commission also has Planning, Housing, Finance, and Community Service committees. Franklinton’s community resource guide lists full FAC meetings on the second Tuesday of each month except August at 6 p.m. at the Mount Carmel Healthy Living Center, 777 W. State Street, Building 2.

East Franklinton Review Board. City Council adopted the East Franklinton Creative Community District Plan on November 12, 2012, and created the East Franklinton District zoning (Columbus City Code Chapter 3323) in 2013. The district runs from State Route 315 east to the Scioto River and the edge of the Downtown District, with I-70 to the south. It mixes residential, retail, office, institutional, and some industrial uses, and it is split into the Arts and Innovation Sub-District, the Dodge Park Sub-District, and an East Franklinton Overlay. The seven-member East Franklinton Review Board, appointed by the Mayor and approved by City Council, reviews site plans, architecture, signs, and demolitions there. At least four members must live in or own a business or property in Franklinton, and the board includes a seat recommended by the FAC and one by the Franklinton Board of Trade.

Applications for a Certificate of Approval go through the City’s Citizens Access Portal. The board generally meets at 3 p.m. at the Michael B. Coleman Government Center, 111 N. Front Street. The City’s 2026 calendar lists remaining meetings on November 16 (application deadline October 20) and December 14 (deadline November 17); several 2026 meetings were canceled, so confirm with the Planning Division at efrb@columbus.gov. The chapter also bans billboards, pole signs, roof signs, flashing signs, and automatic changeable-copy signs in the district.

Columbus rental registry: register by December 31, 2026

Ordinance 0923-2026, passed by Columbus City Council in April 2026, created Chapter 4515 of the Columbus City Codes, a citywide registry of residential rental properties. Initial registration runs October 1 through December 31, 2026, for the 2027 registry year. The fee is $15 per unit with a $1,500 cap per complex, registered properties get a preventative education inspection of the exterior, common areas, and shared systems every three years, and an owner who ignores a notice of violation can face a civil penalty of $2 per unit per day. A new buyer must re-register within 30 days of purchase. The city registry is separate from the Franklin County Auditor’s rental registration under Ohio Revised Code 5323.02, which still applies. Our Ohio landlord-tenant law guide covers both.

Deposits, leases, and tenant protections

Because Franklinton is inside Columbus, Columbus City Code 4551.04, known as Renter’s Choice, applies to operators with five or more rental units: if they require a security deposit, they must give written notice that the tenant may pay it in full, over at least three monthly installments, or over at least six monthly installments, each due with rent. Landlords also can’t refuse an applicant because of a lawful source of income such as a housing voucher (4551.03), and monthly late fees are limited to the larger of $50 or 10% of rent (4551.07). Ohio’s statewide rules in Revised Code Chapter 5321, including the 30-day deposit return, apply too.

Much of Franklinton’s housing is older, and Wikipedia’s summary notes that the 2000 census counted only 29.2% of housing units as owner-occupied, a higher rental share than most Columbus neighborhoods. For an older house or small building, check the age of the furnace, water heater, and electrical service, and ask whether the unit has been through a City code inspection.

Short-term rentals

Columbus requires a short-term rental permit under Chapter 598 for stays of fewer than 30 nights. The City’s 2026 information sheet lists a $20 application fee, a $75 license for a primary residence or $150 for a non-primary residence, a $32 BCI background check for each listed party, a letter of good standing from the Columbus Income Tax Division, and a 24-hour local contact. A 5.1% lodging excise tax applies to stays, and listed occupancy may not exceed three times the number of bedrooms shown on the Franklin County Auditor’s website. A tenant applying needs a lease that explicitly allows short-term rental use.

Getting around: COTA and the West Broad corridor

COTA’s Franklinton guide lists Lines 3, 6, 9, 10, and 12, with stops such as West Rich at Lucas and McDowell, West Broad at Rodgers, Hawkes, and Belle (near COSI and the Veterans Museum), and McKinley Avenue near COTA’s McKinley facility. Line 10 (East Broad/West Broad) is a frequent line on Broad Street, and Line 6 (Sullivant) became a frequent line on May 4, 2026, moving from every 30 minutes to every 15 minutes on weekdays between Downtown and West Columbus with LinkUS funding. Line 9 runs on West Mound Street and Line 12 serves McKinley Avenue.

COTA and the City of Columbus are also running a West Broad Street Transit-Oriented Development study, with public meetings held from April through June 2026, looking at how land along the corridor could develop around transit. It is a study, not a funded project, so treat it as long-range planning rather than a reason to pay more today. For broader context on transit and car-free living, see Mobility & the Future.

Parks, schools, and daily life

Neighborhood parks include Dodge Park, McKinley Park, and Cody Park, and Genoa Park and the Scioto Mile trail are on the riverfront. Franklinton is served by Columbus City Schools; Wikipedia’s summary lists Starling K-8, Sullivant Elementary, and Avondale Elementary for younger students and West High School in the Hilltop as the high school, but confirm the assigned school for any address with the district. The Columbus Metropolitan Library has a Franklinton branch on West Town Street. Wikipedia also notes that the neighborhood has long lacked a full commercial grocery store and has had no hospital since Mount Carmel West closed in 2019, though Mount Carmel built a new emergency department here around 2020; renters without a car should map their grocery trip before signing.

Taxes for tenants who move here

Columbus residents pay the city’s 2.5% municipal income tax, and the City gives a credit for tax paid to the city where you work, up to Columbus’s rate. Wikipedia’s summary notes that Franklinton is a City of Columbus Community Reinvestment Area offering 15-year, 100% property tax abatements on qualifying projects, which mostly matters to owners and developers, though property taxes often reach tenants indirectly through rent.

Vouchers, ZIPs, and what the SAFMR tables say

Franklinton spans three ZIP codes: 43222 for much of the central neighborhood, 43223 for South Franklinton, and 43215, which East Franklinton shares with Downtown. Because CMHA uses Small Area Fair Market Rents, the payment standard can change a lot from one side of SR 315 to the other. CMHA’s FY2027 SAFMRs, effective October 1, 2026, at 100% of SAFMR, list the following. These are payment-standard benchmarks, not asking rents and not automatic approvals; contract rent still goes through CMHA’s rent-reasonableness review, and utility allowances affect the gross-rent comparison.

What landlords and tenants should check before signing

  • Flood coverage. Look up the address on FEMA’s flood map and price contents or building flood coverage.
  • Registry. Owners: register each Columbus rental with the City by December 31, 2026, and keep the Franklin County Auditor registration current.
  • Design review. In East Franklinton, exterior work, new signs, and demolitions need a Certificate of Approval from the East Franklinton Review Board.
  • Deposit options. Operators with five or more units must give written Renter’s Choice notice before the lease is signed.
  • Which ZIP. Confirm whether the unit is in 43215, 43222, or 43223, because the voucher payment standard differs by ZIP.
  • Short stays. Anything under 30 nights needs a City short-term rental permit.
  • Bus and errands. Check the walk to Broad Street, Sullivant Avenue, or Rich Street stops and to the nearest full grocery store.

Frequently asked questions

Is Franklinton part of the City of Columbus?

Yes. Franklinton was settled in 1797 as its own town and was annexed by Columbus in 1859, so today Columbus city services, the 2.5% Columbus income tax, and Columbus City Code rules for landlords and tenants apply, including source-of-income protection (4551.03), Renter’s Choice deposit installments (4551.04), the late-fee limit (4551.07), and the new rental registry (Chapter 4515). The neighborhood is generally described as bounded by the Scioto River on the north and east, Harmon Avenue on the east, Stimmel Road and Greenlawn Avenue on the south, and Interstate 70 on the west.

Who reviews building projects in East Franklinton?

The East Franklinton Review Board, created by Columbus City Code Chapter 3323 in 2013. It has seven members appointed by the Mayor and approved by City Council, and it reviews site plans, architecture, graphics, and demolitions inside the East Franklinton District, which lies between State Route 315, I-70, the Scioto River, and the Downtown District. Applications for a Certificate of Approval go through the City’s Citizens Access Portal. Elsewhere in Franklinton, the Franklinton Area Commission’s Zoning Committee reviews rezonings, variances, special permits, and demolition permits and makes recommendations.

Does the floodwall mean I don’t need flood insurance in Franklinton?

Not necessarily. The Franklinton Floodwall, dedicated in 2004, is a 7.14-mile system that Columbus Monthly reports was designed for a 500-year flood. FEMA warns that levees reduce risk but do not eliminate it, and that flood insurance is available to owners and renters even outside high-risk zones. Renters in a community that participates in the National Flood Insurance Program can buy contents coverage. Look up the exact address on FEMA’s Flood Map Service Center and ask an insurance agent.

Do Franklinton landlords have to join the new Columbus rental registry?

Yes, for rentals inside Columbus city limits. Ordinance 0923-2026 created Chapter 4515 of the Columbus City Codes. Initial registration runs October 1 through December 31, 2026, for the 2027 registry year, at $15 per unit with a $1,500 cap per complex. This is separate from the Franklin County Auditor rental registration required by Ohio Revised Code 5323.02.

Which COTA buses serve Franklinton?

COTA lists Lines 3, 6, 9, 10, and 12 for Franklinton. Line 10 (East Broad/West Broad) is a frequent line on Broad Street, and Line 6 (Sullivant) became a frequent line on May 4, 2026, moving from every 30 minutes to every 15 minutes on weekdays between Downtown and West Columbus.

What is the voucher payment standard in Franklinton?

Franklinton spans ZIPs 43215, 43222, and 43223. CMHA’s FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents at 100%, effective October 1, 2026, list $990 for a studio, $1,060 for one bedroom, $1,260 for two, $1,500 for three, and $1,710 for four bedrooms in 43222; $1,130, $1,210, $1,440, $1,720, and $1,950 in 43223; and $1,850, $1,970, $2,350, $2,800, and $3,190 in 43215. These are payment-standard benchmarks, not asking rents.
