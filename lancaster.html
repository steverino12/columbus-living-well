Lancaster: a renting and living guide to the Fairfield County seat southeast of Columbus

What landlords and tenants should know about the City of Lancaster, the Fairfield County seat on the Hocking River, including its 2.3% city income tax with only a 1% credit for tax paid where you work, the 1.5% Lancaster City Schools earned-income tax, why Ohio’s county-auditor rental filing does not reach Fairfield County, the annual tenant report landlords owe the tax office, the new short-term rental permit and 3% lodging tax, the single city bill for gas, water, sewer, stormwater, and trash, Fairfield County Transit’s LINK buses, and HUD voucher figures for ZIP 43130, without inventing market rents.

Last updated November 14, 2026. Facts below come from the City of Lancaster and its Utilities Collection Office, Lancaster’s codified ordinances, the Ohio Department of Taxation, the Ohio Revised Code, Fairfield County Transit, the Fairfield Metropolitan Housing Authority, COTA, NCES, HUD, AHACO, and Wikipedia’s summaries of the city and county, all cited in Sources. Asking-rent medians are not invented here; see Rent Pulse and Fair Market Rents for verified figures.

A county seat with its own economy

Lancaster is a separate city and the county seat of Fairfield County, near the Hocking River about 33 miles southeast of Columbus and part of the Columbus metropolitan area. Wikipedia’s summary traces the town to Ebenezer Zane, who was awarded square-mile tracts where his road, Zane’s Trace, crossed the Hocking, Muskingum, and Scioto rivers. The village was founded on the Hocking tract in 1800 as New Lancaster, shortened its name to Lancaster in 1805, and was incorporated in 1831. Many early settlers were German immigrants and their descendants from Lancaster, Pennsylvania, and the Lancaster Lateral Canal opened to commerce in 1834.

The 2020 census counted 40,552 residents, 16,988 households, and 18,302 housing units on about 18.9 square miles, according to Wikipedia’s summary, making Lancaster Ohio’s 30th-largest city. Of those housing units, 7.2% were vacant; the homeowner vacancy rate was 1.4% and the rental vacancy rate was 6.9%. The median age was 39.8, and 27.2% of households had children under 18.

Lancaster is a job center rather than a pure bedroom community. The city’s 2021 annual financial report, as summarized by Wikipedia, listed Fairfield Medical Center (2,314 employees), glassmaker Anchor Hocking (1,782), Fairfield County government (1,387), Lancaster City Schools (1,121), and Kroger (936) as the largest employers. Ohio University-Lancaster runs a branch campus in the city. For renters, that means many tenants work in town, which matters for the income tax math below.

Because Lancaster is a separate city, Columbus-only rules such as the Columbus rental registry, Renter’s Choice, and Columbus source-of-income protections do not apply. The 43130 mailing area and the Lancaster school district also extend into surrounding townships, and the city bills customers outside the corporation limits at different utility rates. Confirm that a parcel is inside city limits before relying on anything below.

Income tax: 2.3% with only a 1% credit

The City of Lancaster’s Income Tax Department states that the current rate is 2.3%. The Central Collection Agency, run by Cleveland’s Division of Taxation, lists Lancaster among its member municipalities, and its rate table for Lancaster shows the history: 1.75% from July 1, 1989 through 2020, 2.2% for 2021 and 2022, and 2.3% from January 1, 2023. The city’s instructions direct returns and payments to its own Income Tax Department at City Hall, 104 E. Main Street, second floor (740-687-6606),.

The credit is what Columbus commuters need to budget for. Section 182.081 of Lancaster’s codified ordinances, and the city’s 2025 individual return instructions, give residents a credit for tax paid to the city where they work equal to the lesser of the tax actually paid or 1% of the income taxed there. The instructions use a Columbus example: a resident earning $20,000 in Columbus has $500 withheld for Columbus at 2.5% but receives only a $200 credit. Scaling that same math to $60,000 in Columbus wages, the resident pays Columbus $1,500, owes Lancaster $1,380 at 2.3% less a $600 credit, or $780, and pays $2,280 in municipal income tax in total. A resident who works inside Lancaster simply pays the 2.3% through withholding.

Lancaster has mandatory filing. Beginning with tax year 2024, every resident 18 or older with taxable income must file a city return by April 15 whether or not more tax is owed, and residents under 18 are exempt from the tax. Interest, dividends, Social Security, and retirement income are not taxable to the city, according to its new-resident form. Anyone who expects to owe $200 or more beyond withholding must make quarterly estimated payments. The instructions set a $25 late-filing penalty per return, a one-time 15% penalty on tax not paid on time, and interest at the federal short-term rate plus 6% a year.

Two points for landlords. First, the instructions say “any owner of Lancaster rental property” must file a city return, even if the rental shows a loss. Second, the same chapter of the code requires an annual tenant report, covered in the registration section below.

School district income tax: 1.5% on earned income

Unlike most of the suburbs we have covered, Lancaster carries a sizable school district income tax. The Ohio Department of Taxation’s school district income tax table lists Lancaster City School District (district number 2305) at 1.50% on an earned-income base, a rate the table shows in place since 2007 and continuing with no scheduled end. The state’s guide to the school district income tax explains that an earned-income base covers only wages, salaries, tips, other compensation, and net self-employment earnings, and excludes interest, dividends, capital gains, and pensions. That makes the tax light on retirees and heavy on workers: on $60,000 in wages, it comes to $900 a year, on top of the city tax.

The school tax is filed with the state on Form SD 100, not with the city. Lancaster’s new-resident form notes that employers must withhold it and that the district’s boundaries reach beyond the city limits, so some township residents pay it too. Nearby Fairfield County districts in the state table carry rates from 1.25% (Bloom-Carroll) to 2.00% (Amanda-Clearcreek, Berne Union, Fairfield Union), so check the school district on the Fairfield County Auditor’s record for the exact parcel.

NCES lists Lancaster City (district ID 3904420) as a PK–12 district with 10 schools, 6,248 students, and 322.59 classroom teachers (FTE) in the 2024–2025 school year, a student–teacher ratio of 19.37. Its reported total revenue was $114.5 million in fiscal 2022–2023, about $18,275 per student, with 51% from local sources. Lancaster High School is the district’s high school.

Rental registration: no registry, but an annual tenant report

We did not find a City of Lancaster rental registration, rental license, or proactive rental inspection program for long-term rentals. The question has come up: minutes of council’s Law Committee from August 2, 2023, record a resident’s proposal for “some type of rental property owner registration program” under the heading “Vacant Buildings/Absentee Landlords,” and the Law Director’s office was asked to research it. We found no ordinance adopting such a program.

Ohio’s statewide rental filing does not apply either. Ohio Revised Code 5323.01 limits “residential rental property” for the county-auditor registration in Section 5323.02 to property in a county with more than 200,000 residents at the most recent census, and Fairfield County had 158,921 in 2020. Unlike Franklin County owners, Lancaster landlords therefore have no county auditor rental filing under that chapter.

What Lancaster does have is a tenant report tied to the income tax. Section 182.21 requires every owner of rental property in the city, by October 1 each year, to file with the Tax Administrator each current tenant’s full name, address, last four digits of the Social Security number, and phone number if available, along with move-out dates and forwarding addresses for tenants who left. Tenants who are responsible for their own city water bill are excepted. The penalty for not filing is $5 per tenant per month, up to $1,000. The city’s Occupancy Report form lists a September 30 due date, and a separate Occupancy Change Form must be filed within 30 days of a change of tenant.

Rentals are still subject to code enforcement. Lancaster’s building code adopts the 2021 International Property Maintenance Code by reference (Section 1303.01(d), Ordinance 13-21). The city’s Code Enforcement Department works from complaints: it inspects, decides whether there is a violation, mails a notice giving 3 to 30 days to repair depending on the violation, reinspects, and files charges or closes the case. We did not find a vacant building registration chapter in Lancaster’s code.

Owners in the historic core should also know that the city has a Historic Commission that issues Certificates of Appropriateness under the Historic Lancaster District rules in Chapter 1151, with a $25 application fee on the current planning and zoning fee schedule. Wikipedia notes the city has three national historic districts. Check the city’s historic district map before changing windows, siding, porches, or signs on an older rental.

Fair housing: federal and state law, no local voucher protection found

Lancaster is not on AHACO’s list of ten central Ohio communities that have adopted source-of-income protections: Bexley, Columbus, Gahanna, Grandview Heights, Pickerington, Reynoldsburg, Upper Arlington, Westerville, Whitehall, and Worthington. We did not find a local ordinance requiring landlords to accept housing vouchers.

The city’s Fair Housing page names the Community Development Department, 104 E. Main Street, first floor (740-687-6663), as the local fair housing agency. It can help a resident file a complaint with HUD or the Ohio Civil Rights Commission or attempt to mediate, and it notes that complaints to HUD or OCRC must be filed within one year. The city’s fair housing brochure adds that a landlord may require a certain income, credit references, and a security deposit, as long as the requirements apply equally to all applicants.

Short-term rentals: a new permit chapter

Lancaster now regulates Airbnb-style rentals directly. Chapter 1131 of the codified ordinances, enacted by Ordinance 58-25 and passed November 17, 2025, prohibits operating a short-term rental without an approved Zoning Clearance Permit. Section 1161.01 defines a short-term rental as a dwelling, or space in a dwelling, with five or fewer guestrooms rented for less than 30 consecutive days.

Key rules in the chapter:

  • Permit term. A separate permit is needed for each short-term rental, though several units on the same premises can share an application. The code says a permit runs until December 31 of the calendar year after it is issued and must be renewed every two years, with renewal applications due by the first Monday in November of the year it expires. A change in ownership or leasehold voids the permit.
  • Fees. The city’s Planning and Zoning fee schedule, signed by the mayor with an August 4, 2025 date, lists $150 for a short-term rental permit and $75 for a renewal.
  • Application. Applicants must name a local host who is available at all times and can respond within one hour to an on-site emergency, state a maximum occupancy no higher than the occupancy permit, show parking on a site plan or aerial photo, and submit a simple floor plan. BCI and FBI background checks are required for the applicant, the local host, and any property manager, and must be redone at renewal.
  • Standards. The rental needs an occupancy permit from the Building Department showing compliance with fire and building codes, including fire extinguishers and smoke and carbon monoxide detectors. Exterior signs are banned, cameras are allowed only outside or in common areas such as a living room or kitchen, guests must be 18 or older, and hourly rentals are prohibited.
  • Denial. The City Planner can deny a permit for an incomplete application, pending code violations, inadequate fire protection, a documented nuisance or criminal history at the property, or if the applicant, local host, or manager is a felon, a sex offender, or has committed an offense of violence.

The city’s short-term rental guide adds an initial occupancy and compliance inspection and a tax ID and letter of good standing from the Income Tax Department to the checklist; its wording on how long a first permit lasts differs from the code’s, so confirm the expiration date with Planning and Zoning (740-681-5017). In May 2026 a council committee reviewed proposed zoning changes, including detached accessory dwelling units in some districts and short-term rental use of additional dwellings on lots that allow more than one; we did not confirm whether they were adopted. A tenant who wants to sublet on a booking platform needs both the permit and the landlord’s written consent.

Lodging tax: 3% to the city, filed quarterly

According to the city’s excise lodging tax FAQ for short-term rental vendors, Lancaster has levied a 3.0% lodging excise tax since July 1, 1994. The Income Tax Department administers it, and the money goes to the Fairfield County Visitors and Convention Bureau. The guest owes the tax, but the host or the host platform must collect and remit it, and it applies to the total transaction, including cleaning, service, and extra-guest fees. Stays longer than 30 days and lodging billed directly to a government entity are exempt.

Returns are due quarterly, on the 15th of the month after each quarter ends, and hosts must file even in quarters with no guests. The city’s Lodging Tax page says it does not accept electronic remittance for this tax, so a paper return must accompany each payment. Late tax carries 1.5% interest per month, and a vendor who fails to collect, file, or remit faces a $500 penalty. Rental profits are also subject to the 2.3% city income tax.

The Ohio Department of Taxation’s 2018 Table S-4, the latest we could open, also lists a 4.50% Fairfield County lodging rate; we did not confirm whether it reaches stays inside Lancaster, so ask the Fairfield County Auditor.

Leases and deposits: Ohio law applies

We did not find a local landlord-tenant code in Lancaster, so Ohio Revised Code Chapter 5321 governs security deposits, repairs, entry notice, and eviction notices. Under Section 5321.16, a landlord generally must return the deposit, with an itemized list of any deductions, within 30 days after the lease ends and the tenant gives up possession. Ohio Revised Code 1901.02 gives the Fairfield County Municipal Court, established in Lancaster, jurisdiction within Fairfield County, so eviction cases for Lancaster addresses are filed there. Our Ohio landlord-tenant basics page walks through the statewide rules.

Utilities: the city runs gas, water, sewer, stormwater, and trash

Lancaster is unusual in central Ohio because the city owns the gas utility too. The Utilities Collection Office says the city owns and operates five utility systems: Lancaster Municipal Gas, the Division of Water, the Division of Water Pollution Control (sewer), Sanitation, and Stormwater. The Division of Water says the current system began in 1925 with the Miller Park wellfield, water plant, and reservoir, and today supplies about 17 square miles from two treatment plants. Sewage is treated at the Lawrence Street and Upper Hocking water pollution control facilities.

The city’s current rate sheet lists these residential charges inside the city:

Customers outside the corporation limits pay higher water and gas rates. As an illustration using our own arithmetic from that sheet, a single-family home with a 3/4-inch meter using 500 cubic feet of water in a month (about 3,740 gallons) would pay about $44.55 for water, $60.47 for sewer, $9.25 for stormwater, and $17.50 for trash, or roughly $132 before gas; 25 hundred cubic feet of gas would add about $30.75. Residential sewer bills for May through October are based on the household’s winter average water use under the city’s summer sanitary sewer average rule, so lawn watering doesn’t inflate the sewer charge.

The office’s general rules and regulations, effective March 2, 2026, set the billing rules that belong in a lease:

  • Owner signs first. Before a tenant can apply, every owner listed on the deed with the Fairfield County Auditor must sign the Contract for Utility Service, and an owner with past-due balances on any property must pay them before a tenant gets new service. The tenant brings government photo ID and a copy of a lease signed by both the landlord and the tenant.
  • Deposit. New customers, meaning anyone without city service in their name in the last three years, pay a residential deposit of $100 for all services ($75 gas, $50 each for water, sewer, or sanitation alone). The deposit is held six months and then applied to the account. A $5 account set-up fee applies at each location.
  • Late payment and shutoff. Bills are due 15 days after the bill date, and unpaid bills get a 5% late fee on current charges and a notice of disconnection. Reconnection costs $50, and an illegal reconnection fee is $250.
  • Move-outs and liens. When a tenant closes an account, service reverts to the owner’s name, and the city says it will not get involved in landlord-tenant disputes; the landlord must recover costs directly from the tenant. Where the owner is responsible for unpaid water, sewer, sanitation, or stormwater charges, the city may certify them as a lien to the County Auditor.

The city’s Sanitation Department collects trash weekly; carts must be out by 7:30 a.m. on pickup day, and trash in the city cart comes first before extra cans. Under the rates and fees page, a second cart costs a one-time $25 plus $3 a month, up to one cubic yard of trash outside the cart adds a $10 charge to the bill, and larger piles are picked up and billed at bulk rates, which is worth knowing at turnover. Curbside yard waste runs from March 30 to December 18, 2026. We did not confirm the electric provider for Lancaster addresses.

Getting around: LINK buses, not COTA

Lancaster does not appear on COTA’s current system map. Instead, Fairfield County Transit’s LINK runs local service. Its printable schedule, effective March 2026, describes five LINK Lines in Lancaster (Memorial, Main, West, Sheridan, and North), each a continuous hourly loop Monday through Friday from 7 a.m. to 9 p.m., with stops such as Kroger, Walmart, River Valley Mall, Ohio University Lancaster, City Hall on West Main Street, and the library on Broad Street. Buses will deviate up to three-quarters of a mile with 24 hours’ notice. Fares are $0.50, or $0.25 for qualified mobility-program riders, with $0.10 transfers and a $30 monthly unlimited pass; exact change is required.

A weekday Lancaster–Pickerington Connector makes two departures to Pickerington and two return trips to Lancaster, with stops including Benson Park in Carroll and Diley Medical Center in Canal Winchester; see our Pickerington guide for its loop there. LINK Direct offers curb-to-curb rides anywhere in the county for $2 booked in advance or $5 same-day, weekdays from 6 a.m. to 6 p.m. and Saturdays from 7 a.m. to 4 p.m., reserved up to 14 days ahead at 740-681-5086. LINK lists COTA, GoBus, and Logan Public Transit as connector services. A tenant without a car who commutes to Columbus should map the trip before signing.

Vouchers and what the SAFMR table says

Fairfield County is part of the Columbus, OH HUD Metro FMR Area, along with Delaware, Franklin, Licking, Madison, Morrow, and Pickaway counties, and HUD says all Housing Choice Voucher programs operated in that area use Small Area FMRs set by ZIP code. HUD’s FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents for Lancaster’s ZIP 43130 are:

For comparison, the same table lists $2,040 for a two-bedroom in ZIP 43147 (Pickerington) and $1,600 in ZIP 43110 (Canal Winchester), so Lancaster’s benchmark is well below its Fairfield County neighbors to the northwest. These are payment-standard benchmarks, not asking rents and not automatic approvals; contract rent still goes through rent-reasonableness review, utility allowances affect the gross-rent comparison, and ZIP 43130 includes township areas outside the city. The Fairfield Metropolitan Housing Authority, at 315 N. Columbus Street in Lancaster (740-653-6618), administers vouchers locally; the newest fair market rent document on its site when we checked was labeled FY2026, so confirm the current payment standard and utility allowance with the housing authority that issued the voucher. For how these numbers are built, see our Fair Market Rents explainer.

What landlords and tenants should check before signing

  • Jurisdiction. Confirm the parcel is inside the City of Lancaster rather than a surrounding township; taxes, zoning, and utility rates differ.
  • Income tax. Budget for 2.3% to Lancaster, less a credit capped at 1% of wages taxed by a work city; a Columbus commuter still owes about 1.3% to Lancaster.
  • School tax. Add the 1.5% Lancaster City Schools earned-income tax, filed with the state on Form SD 100.
  • Tenant report. No rental registry or county auditor filing, but landlords must file the annual occupancy report with the Income Tax Department (due by September 30 on the city form) and an occupancy change form within 30 days of a new tenant, unless the tenant pays the city water bill.
  • Short stays. Get a Chapter 1131 Zoning Clearance Permit ($150, $75 renewal) and an occupancy permit, and file the 3% lodging tax quarterly on paper.
  • Vouchers. No local source-of-income protection found; apply consistent written screening either way.
  • Utility account. The owner signs the city’s utility contract first; the tenant brings ID, a signed lease, and a $100 deposit, and one city bill covers gas, water, sewer, stormwater, and trash.
  • Transit. No COTA; LINK Lines run hourly on weekdays only, with LINK Direct for other trips.

Frequently asked questions

Is Lancaster, Ohio part of Columbus?

No. Lancaster is a separate city and the county seat of Fairfield County, about 33 miles southeast of Columbus, with a 2020 census population of 40,552. It has its own council, income tax, utilities, and zoning code, so Columbus-only rules such as Renter’s Choice, the Columbus rental registry, and Columbus source-of-income protections do not apply. The 43130 ZIP code and the Lancaster school district both reach beyond city limits, so confirm the parcel is inside the city.

What is the Lancaster, Ohio income tax rate?

The City of Lancaster’s income tax is 2.3%, collected by the city’s own Income Tax Department at City Hall. Residents who pay tax to the city where they work get a credit of no more than 1% of the income taxed there. A resident earning $60,000 in Columbus would typically pay Columbus $1,500 and Lancaster another $780 (2.3% minus the 1% credit). Residents 18 and older with taxable income, and every owner of Lancaster rental property, must file by April 15.

Is there a school district income tax in Lancaster?

Yes. The Ohio Department of Taxation lists Lancaster City School District (district number 2305) at 1.50% on an earned-income base, which taxes wages and self-employment income but not pensions, interest, or dividends. It is filed with the state on Form SD 100, separately from the city return. NCES reports Lancaster City Schools had 6,248 students in 10 schools in 2024–2025.

Does Lancaster have a rental registry?

We did not find a City of Lancaster rental registration, rental license, or proactive rental inspection program; a 2023 council Law Committee discussed one but we found no adopted ordinance. Ohio’s county-auditor rental filing in Revised Code 5323 applies only in counties with more than 200,000 residents, and Fairfield County had 158,921 at the 2020 census. Lancaster does require landlords to file an annual tenant occupancy report with the Income Tax Department under Section 182.21, unless the tenant pays the city water bill, with a penalty of $5 per tenant per month up to $1,000.

Can I run an Airbnb in Lancaster, Ohio?

Yes, with a permit. Chapter 1131, passed November 17, 2025, requires a Zoning Clearance Permit for every short-term rental, defined as a dwelling or space with five or fewer guestrooms rented for less than 30 consecutive days. The city’s fee schedule sets $150 for a new permit and $75 for a renewal, renewals are due every two years by the first Monday in November, and applicants need a site plan, floor plan, a local host who can respond within one hour, BCI and FBI background checks, an occupancy permit, and a letter of good standing from the tax office.

Is there a lodging tax on short-term rentals in Lancaster?

Yes. The City of Lancaster has levied a 3.0% lodging excise tax since July 1, 1994, administered by the Income Tax Department and paid to the Fairfield County Visitors and Convention Bureau. Hosts or the booking platform must collect it on the guest’s total transaction, including cleaning fees, and file a paper return quarterly, even in quarters with no guests. Stays longer than 30 days are exempt.

Can Lancaster landlords refuse housing vouchers?

Lancaster is not on AHACO’s list of ten central Ohio communities that have adopted source-of-income protections, and we found no local rule requiring landlords to accept vouchers. Federal and Ohio fair housing laws still apply, the city’s fair housing brochure says landlord requirements must apply equally to all applicants, and the Community Development Department (740-687-6663) takes discrimination complaints.

Who provides gas, water, sewer, and trash in Lancaster?

The City of Lancaster runs five utility systems: Lancaster Municipal Gas, the Division of Water, Water Pollution Control (sewer), Sanitation, and Stormwater, all billed by the Utilities Collection Office. A new residential customer pays a $100 deposit for all services, held six months and then applied to the account. The property owner must sign the Contract for Utility Service before a tenant can open an account, and late bills carry a 5% fee. Trash with one 96-gallon cart is $17.50 a month.

Does COTA serve Lancaster, Ohio?

No. Lancaster does not appear on COTA’s system map. Fairfield County Transit’s LINK runs five hourly LINK Lines in Lancaster, Monday through Friday from 7 a.m. to 9 p.m., for a $0.50 fare, plus a weekday Lancaster–Pickerington Connector with two round trips and LINK Direct curb-to-curb rides booked at 740-681-5086.

What is the voucher payment standard in Lancaster, Ohio?

Fairfield County is part of the Columbus, OH HUD Metro FMR Area, where voucher programs use Small Area Fair Market Rents. For ZIP 43130, HUD’s FY2027 SAFMRs are $1,040 for a studio, $1,120 for one bedroom, $1,330 for two, $1,580 for three, and $1,800 for four bedrooms. These are benchmarks, not asking rents; the Fairfield Metropolitan Housing Authority or other agency administering a voucher sets the actual payment standard.
