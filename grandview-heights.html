Grandview Heights: a renting and living guide

What landlords and tenants should know about Grandview Heights—a small, separate city west of the Short North with its own income tax, schools, and zoning boards—including Grandview Yard, the City’s short-term rental and permit-parking rules, COTA service on Northwest Boulevard, Fifth Avenue, and Grandview Avenue, parks, and ZIP-level voucher figures, without inventing market rents.

Last updated October 25, 2026. Facts below come from the City of Grandview Heights, Grandview Heights Schools, the Regional Income Tax Agency, the U.S. Census Bureau (via Wikipedia’s summary of the 2020 count), COTA timetables, and CMHA tables cited in Sources. Asking-rent medians are not invented here; see Rent Pulse and Fair Market Rents for verified figures.

A separate city, not a Columbus neighborhood

Grandview Heights is its own municipality in Franklin County, bordered by Columbus on most sides and by the village of Marble Cliff. The City’s government page says it was incorporated in 1906 and became a charter city in 1931, when residents voted to adopt their first charter; the current charter was adopted by voters in November 2018. It runs on a strong-mayor system with a seven-member City Council, and its 2023 annual financial report describes an area of about 1.24 square miles. The 2020 Census counted 8,085 residents, up 23.7% from 6,536 in 2010.

That independence changes the rules that apply to a rental. Columbus City Code does not reach Grandview Heights, so Columbus-only protections such as the source-of-income rule in Columbus City Code 4551.03 do not apply there; Grandview’s own codified ordinances and Ohio’s statewide landlord-tenant law (Ohio Revised Code Chapter 5321) do. The City provides its own police and fire protection, refuse collection, parks, planning, and zoning, and it contracts with Franklin County Public Health for health services such as plumbing and food inspections. Because nearby Columbus addresses share the 43212 ZIP, confirm the parcel’s taxing city with the Franklin County Auditor rather than relying on the mailing address.

For other neighborhood spotlights, start at the Neighborhoods hub, including Victorian Village and the Short North to the east, Dublin (another separate city), Clintonville, German Village, Italian Village, and Olde Towne East.

How Grandview Heights grew

Wikipedia’s history notes that the area was originally part of Marble Cliff, one of Columbus’s first suburbs, which incorporated as a hamlet in 1901 before Grandview Heights split off as a separate village in 1906. The Census shows the population jumping from 1,185 in 1920 to 6,358 in 1930, its fastest decade of growth. The Bank Block on Grandview Avenue, built in 1927, was one of the first strip shopping centers to include a parking lot. The City’s parks page credits developer George Urlin with coining the name “Grand view” for the view from his mansion on Goodale Boulevard.

According to the City’s planning history, the closure of the Big Bear warehouse and headquarters complex in the early 2000s set off a new round of planning. Between 2004 and 2022, the Grandview Yard and Grandview Crossing developments turned former industrial land into offices, shops, and apartments, and the City says that growth paid to rebuild its parks, municipal pool, and other facilities and covered most of the cost of the schools’ recent reconstruction.

Grandview Yard and the rental stock

Much of the City’s newer apartment, condo, and townhome construction is in Grandview Yard, the former industrial area along Goodale Boulevard. Grandview Heights Schools reports that owners of multi-family housing there qualify for a 50% property tax abatement for 15 years as an incentive for density, while single-family homeowners in the Yard pay full property taxes; the district says Grandview Yard revenue makes up about 20% of its annual operating revenue. Outside the Yard, the housing is older, so expect rentals in houses, doubles, and small apartment buildings rather than large complexes. The 2020 Census counted 4,018 housing units with a rental vacancy rate of 3.7%, which points to a tight market, but it is a 2020 snapshot, not a current figure.

Zoning, permits, and short-term rentals

Land-use decisions go through two citizen boards. The Planning Commission reviews commercial development proposals, conditional uses, lot splits and consolidations, rezonings, and commercial demolition permits, and meets on the third Wednesday of each month at 7:00 p.m. at 1525 Goodale Boulevard. The Board of Zoning Appeals hears variance requests and appeals and meets on the second Wednesday at 7:00 p.m. at the same address. On that regular schedule, the remaining 2026 meetings fall on November 11 and December 9 for the BZA and November 18 and December 16 for the Planning Commission; check the City calendar before relying on a date. Building and zoning permits are handled by the Building & Zoning Department at 1260 McKinley Avenue.

Short-term rentals are tightly limited. Grandview’s STR ordinance (Ordinance 2020-08) defines a short-term rental as occupancy of a dwelling in a residential district for less than 30 consecutive days. The City allows one only where the owner lives in a single-family home while renting rooms, or where one unit of a multi-family building is permanently occupied by the person managing the other unit as the rental. Approval requires a pre-application meeting, a Planning Commission application with a $100 fee and notice to surrounding parcels, a public hearing, and then a City STR permit and a joint building and electrical inspection. A non-owner-occupied whole-house Airbnb is not one of the allowed arrangements. Leases of 30 days or more are ordinary residential rentals.

Parking: resident permit zones

Parking pressure from businesses along West Third Avenue in Columbus led the City to study the streets of its northeast neighborhood, generally bounded by Northwest Boulevard, Edgehill Road, and the City’s northern boundary. City Council passed Ordinance 2025-01 on February 24, 2025, and a resident-only permit zone took effect June 9, 2025. Permits are free and valid for five years: one per vehicle registered at the address, plus two guest permits, applied for online. Replacements cost $10 at the Service Center, 1260 McKinley Avenue. A permit does not exempt a car from no-parking, street-sweeping, or snow restrictions. If a unit sits on a posted permit street, tenants should register their vehicles at the new address quickly, and landlords should say in the listing whether off-street parking is included.

Getting around: COTA lines 3, 5, and 31

Three COTA bus lines serve the City, all listed by COTA as standard service with departures 16 to 60 minutes apart, on timetables effective May 4, 2026. Line 3 (Northwest/Harrisburg) runs on Northwest Boulevard through the northern part of the City and via Grandview Yard to downtown and the Arena District. Line 5 (W 5th Ave/Refugee) has a timepoint at West Fifth Avenue and Grandview Avenue on its route between the west side and downtown. Line 31 (Hudson) is a crosstown line from Grandview Yard and Grandview Avenue at West Fifth Avenue to King Avenue, Ohio State, and Easton. Check current timetables for the hours you travel. Downtown Columbus is a short drive east via Goodale Boulevard or Route 670.

Schools

Grandview Heights and Marble Cliff are served by Grandview Heights City Schools, a separate district from Columbus City Schools, with Stevenson Elementary, Larson Middle School, and Grandview Heights High School. After voters passed Issue 37, a $69.5 million bond issue, on November 5, 2024, the district began replacing the 1926 Stevenson Elementary building. During the 2026–27 school year, Stevenson students attend modular classrooms on the grounds of Larson Middle School, and the new building is planned to open for the 2027–28 school year. Families should confirm current school assignments and arrival procedures with the district.

Parks and community life

The City counts 21 parks and facilities, with the Parks Department maintaining about 45 acres of parks, green space, and trees. Pierce Field has two tennis courts and four pickleball courts, the Urlin courts have four lit tennis courts, Wyman Woods and C. Ray Buck Park have StoryWalks with the Grandview Heights Public Library, and the skate park sits in the Municipal Pool lot at 1350 Goodale Boulevard. Wallace Gardens, at Grandview Avenue and Goodale Boulevard, offers community garden plots to Grandview Heights and Marble Cliff residents through a waitlist. Dogs are not allowed in Pierce Field, McKinley Field, C. Ray Buck Sports Park, or Wyman Woods except on its perimeter path. The City is updating its Parks and Recreation Master Plan in 2026. Grandview Avenue is the City’s main commercial street.

Taxes for tenants who move here

Grandview Heights levies a 2.5% municipal income tax, collected by the Regional Income Tax Agency (RITA), at that rate since at least 2020 according to RITA’s records. Residents get a 100% credit for municipal tax paid to another city, up to 2.5%, so a resident who works in a city with a 2.5% rate and has correct withholding generally owes nothing extra to Grandview. A resident who works from home or in a lower-rate city can owe the difference. Tenants moving from Columbus should update their address with their employer so withholding follows them.

Vouchers, ZIPs, and what the SAFMR tables say

Grandview Heights uses ZIP 43212, which also covers neighboring parts of Columbus. CMHA’s FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents, effective October 1, 2026, at 100% of SAFMR, list $1,380 for a studio, $1,480 for a one-bedroom, $1,760 for a two-bedroom, and $2,100 for a three-bedroom in 43212. These are payment-standard benchmarks, not asking rents and not automatic approvals; contract rent still goes through CMHA’s rent-reasonableness review, and utility allowances affect the gross-rent comparison. What landlords and tenants should check before signing

  • Which city. Confirm the parcel is in Grandview Heights and not Columbus or Marble Cliff; taxes, codes, and schools differ.
  • Short-term plans. Don’t sign a lease, or buy, expecting to run a whole-unit Airbnb; Grandview allows only owner-occupied or on-site-managed STRs with Planning Commission approval.
  • Parking. Check whether the street is a posted permit zone and how many off-street spaces come with the unit.
  • Income tax. Budget for the 2.5% city income tax and update withholding.
  • School logistics. Families should ask the district about the Stevenson Elementary construction schedule.
  • ZIP for vouchers. Pull the ZIP from the address before doing voucher math.

Frequently asked questions

Is Grandview Heights part of Columbus?

No. Grandview Heights is its own city in Franklin County, incorporated as a village in 1906 and a charter city since 1931. It has its own mayor, seven-member council, police and fire departments, income tax, zoning code, and school district, even though Columbus borders it on most sides. Check the parcel’s city on the Franklin County Auditor’s site before applying Columbus rules.

What is the Grandview Heights income tax rate?

The City of Grandview Heights levies a 2.5% municipal income tax, administered by the Regional Income Tax Agency (RITA). Residents get a 100% credit for municipal tax paid to another city, up to 2.5%.

Can I run a short-term rental in Grandview Heights?

Only in limited cases. Under Ordinance 2020-08, a short-term rental is a stay of less than 30 consecutive days. The owner must live in a single-family home while renting rooms in it, or, in a multi-family building, one unit must be permanently occupied by the person managing the other unit as the rental. Approval goes through the Planning Commission ($100 application fee and a required pre-application meeting), followed by a city STR permit and a building and electrical inspection.

Do I need a parking permit in Grandview Heights?

Only on streets posted as a residential permit zone. A resident-only permit zone in the northeast neighborhood near West Third Avenue took effect June 9, 2025, under Ordinance 2025-01. Permits are free, valid for five years, issued one per vehicle registered at the address plus two guest permits, and applied for through the City’s online portal.

Which COTA lines serve Grandview Heights?

COTA Line 3 (Northwest/Harrisburg) runs on Northwest Boulevard and through Grandview Yard to downtown; Line 5 (W 5th Ave/Refugee) has a timepoint at West Fifth Avenue and Grandview Avenue; and Line 31 (Hudson) runs crosstown from Grandview Yard and Grandview Avenue to Ohio State and Easton. COTA lists all three as standard lines, meaning departures 16 to 60 minutes apart, on timetables effective May 4, 2026.

What is the voucher payment standard in Grandview Heights?

Grandview Heights uses ZIP 43212. CMHA’s FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents, effective October 1, 2026, at 100% of SAFMR, list $1,380 for a studio, $1,480 for a one-bedroom, $1,760 for a two-bedroom, and $2,100 for a three-bedroom in 43212. These are payment-standard benchmarks, not asking rents.
