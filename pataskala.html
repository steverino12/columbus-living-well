Pataskala: a renting and living guide to the Licking County city east of Columbus

What landlords and tenants should know about the City of Pataskala, about 19 miles east of Columbus in southwestern Licking County, including its 1% city income tax with no credit at all for tax paid where you work, the January 31 tenant report landlords owe the tax administrator, why the school district on the parcel changes your tax bill, the absence of a rental registry, the two different water and sewer utilities and how tenants get service from each, contracted trash billing, bed and breakfast and extended-stay hotel zoning, the 3% city hotel tax, transit options without COTA, and what HUD’s Small Area Fair Market Rents say about ZIP 43062.

Last updated November 20, 2026. Facts below come from the City of Pataskala, its income tax ordinance as posted by the Regional Income Tax Agency (RITA), the 2021 replacement edition of its codified ordinances (local legislation current through August 16, 2021, as archived by the Internet Archive), city council reports, the Ohio Department of Taxation, the Ohio Revised Code, the Licking Regional Water District, Local Waste Services, Licking County Transit, The Reporting Project, HUD, and the Licking Metropolitan Housing Authority. Each is linked where it is used and listed under Sources. Where we could not confirm something, we say so. The city does not post a current online code, so later amendments may not be reflected; check anything zoning-related with City Hall.

A fast-growing bedroom community on the Licking County line

Pataskala is a separate city in Licking County. Wikipedia’s summary places it about 19 miles east of Columbus along the South Fork of the Licking River and notes that it was a small community until 1996, when the village of Pataskala merged with Lima Township, vastly increasing its population and land area. The city now covers about 29 square miles. The 2020 census counted 17,886 residents, up from 14,962 in 2010 and 10,249 in 2000, living in 6,554 households. Of 6,852 housing units, 4.3% were vacant, and the census put the rental vacancy rate at 4.6%.

Licking County is mid-sized by Ohio standards: Wikipedia’s county summary gives a 2020 census population of 178,519. That number matters for landlords, as explained below, because Ohio’s county rental property registration law only applies in counties above 200,000 people.

Because Pataskala is its own municipality, Columbus-only rules such as the Columbus rental registry and Columbus’s source-of-income protections do not apply. The ZIP code 43062 also covers land outside the city, so confirm on the Licking County Auditor’s parcel record that a rental is inside city limits before you rely on anything below. The city describes itself on its tax information page as a “bedroom community” without the business base of its neighbors, and that shapes the single biggest money question for tenants: the income tax.

Income tax: 1%, and no credit for tax paid elsewhere

Pataskala’s income tax is 1.0%. The city’s tax information page says voters approved the tax in May 2010 to fund the Police and Public Service departments, and that the city cancelled earlier voter-approved property tax levies for city operations in exchange. Section 171.02 of the income tax ordinance posted by RITA sets the annual rate at 1.0% (Ord. 2015-4241, passed November 16, 2015). The city has been a member of RITA since July 1, 2010, and RITA collects and enforces the tax.

The credit is what sets Pataskala apart from most suburbs. RITA’s Pataskala page lists a 1.000% tax rate with a 0.000% credit factor and 0.000% credit limit from January 1, 2020 to the present, and Section 171.06(A) of the ordinance says plainly that no credit is provided to residents for tax paid to other municipalities. The city’s own FAQ answers the question “Do I get credit for municipal income taxes paid to that city?” with a one-word “No,” explaining that Pataskala lacks the business base and property tax revenue of neighboring cities. In practice, a resident who earns $60,000 in a city with a 2.5% income tax pays $1,500 to the work city through withholding and still owes Pataskala $600.

The tax reaches earned income only. The city’s FAQ says taxable earned income includes wages, tips, bonuses, rental income, business profits or losses, and partnership distributions, while interest, dividends, Social Security, pensions, IRA and 401(k) distributions, active military pay, unemployment, and child support are exempt. Income of residents under 18 is exempt. Every resident 18 or older with earned income must file a RITA return even if an employer courtesy-withholds the full 1%, and RITA requires quarterly estimated payments when the expected tax due is $200 or more. Retirees with only pension or Social Security income generally owe nothing, but the city encourages them to notify RITA so estimate bills and non-filing notices stop.

Landlords should note that rental income is on the taxable list, so an owner of Pataskala rental property reports the net rental result to RITA as well.

The landlord tenant report, due January 31

Pataskala asks landlords for something most suburbs do not. Section 171.23 of the income tax ordinance, titled Rental and Leased Property, says every owner who rents residential property in the city, including apartments and rooms, must file a written report with the Tax Administrator on or before January 31 following each calendar year. The report lists the name, address, and telephone number (if available) of each tenant known to have occupied the unit on December 31 of that year.

The section also lets the Tax Administrator order anyone believed to know a tenant’s name and address to appear, testify under oath, and produce records. Failing to file on time, filing an incomplete or intentionally false report, or ignoring such an order is made subject to Section 171.99, the chapter’s violations and penalties section (Ord. 2015-4241). For tenants, the practical result is that RITA will learn of a new household even when no employer withholds Pataskala tax, so budget for the 1% and file from the first year.

School district income tax: depends on which district

Two school districts serve Pataskala, and they tax income very differently. According to Wikipedia, the city is home to Southwest Licking Local Schools (Watkins Memorial High School) and Licking Heights Local Schools, and the Licking Heights district also serves parts of Reynoldsburg, Jefferson Township, and Blacklick.

The Ohio Department of Taxation’s school district income tax rate table lists Southwest Licking Local (district number 4510) at 0.75% on a traditional base, in effect since 1991. A traditional-base tax starts from Ohio adjusted gross income, so as the department’s guide to school district income tax explains, it reaches pensions, interest, and dividends as well as wages, and it is filed with the state on Form SD 100 rather than with RITA. The same table lists Licking Heights Local (district 4505) at 0.00%. The district on the parcel’s auditor record, not the mailing address, decides which applies, so a two-bedroom on one side of town can cost a working household noticeably more in tax than an identical one on the other.

Rental registration: none found

We did not find a City of Pataskala rental registration, rental license, or proactive rental inspection program. We searched the 2021 codified ordinances and found no rental registration, landlord licensing, rooming house licensing, or vacant building registration chapter; the only landlord-specific local duty we found is the tax report described above. Ohio’s county-level rental registration law does not reach Licking County either: under Revised Code 5323.01, it applies only to counties with more than 200,000 people at the most recent census and to townships and cities inside them, and Licking County had 178,519.

The zoning code does shape who can share a rental. The definitions in Chapter 1203 say a family may not contain more than three persons unless all members are related by blood, adoption, or marriage, and define a rooming house as a dwelling where lodging is provided for compensation to three or more unrelated persons without cooking facilities in the rooms. Chapter 1269, Rental Units, allows an accessory rental unit inside an existing house only if the house is at least five years old, keeps at least 1,400 square feet for the primary unit, is converted internally (not from a garage), stays owner-occupied, and houses no more people in both units than the family definition allows.

Neglected properties fall under Chapter 1223, Distressed Properties (Ord. 2020-4358), which applies to parcels in platted subdivisions, multifamily, commercial, and industrial zones, and lots of 1.5 acres or less. It lets the city declare an occupied or vacant building with a nuisance condition, or one that fails exterior maintenance and security standards, a distressed property, and it makes everyone in charge of the property jointly responsible for abatement. Building permits run through the Licking County Building Code Department, as reflected in the Director of Planning’s reports on the city’s Agendas & Minutes page, while the city handles zoning permits.

Fair housing and vouchers

We did not find a Pataskala fair housing chapter; the 2021 codified ordinances contain no fair housing or source-of-income chapter, and the index’s only “discrimination” entry points to the ethnic intimidation offense. Pataskala is also not among the ten central Ohio communities that the Affordable Housing Alliance of Central Ohio lists as having adopted source-of-income protections (Bexley, Columbus, Gahanna, Grandview Heights, Pickerington, Reynoldsburg, Upper Arlington, Westerville, Whitehall, and Worthington). State and federal fair housing laws still apply, but a Pataskala landlord is not barred by local law from declining Housing Choice Voucher holders, as a landlord in nearby Reynoldsburg or Pickerington would be.

Water and sewer: two different utilities

Who supplies water depends on where the home is. The city’s Set Up Services page lists two water providers “depending on which part of the City”: the City of Pataskala and the Licking Regional Water District, formerly the Southwest Licking Community Water and Sewer District. Ask the landlord which one serves the unit before you sign, and whether any part of the property is on a private well or septic system.

City of Pataskala Utility Department. The Utilities page says the department, at 430 S. Main Street (the old town hall, 740-964-6275, open 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. weekdays), provides water and/or sewer billing and service for 1,800 residents within the city limits and 1,200 outside them. Its billing page says bills are mailed by the last working day of each month and due on the 15th of the next month, with a 10% charge on current charges not paid by the due date; failure to receive a bill does not excuse penalties. Payment options include in person, the after-hours drop box, InvoiceCloud online, phone at 1-877-342-1361, and automatic deduction on the 10th. A May 2025 Utility Department report to council, posted on the city’s Agendas & Minutes page, said Ordinance 2025-4494, a water rate increase starting in 2026, was up for second reading on May 19, 2025; we did not confirm the final 2026 rates, so ask the department for the current schedule.

Licking Regional Water District. The district’s Application for Service page says its rules require proof of ownership and photo identification when an account is opened, at 8675 York Road or by fax or email, and that property owners are ultimately responsible for payment. Tenants can still be billed directly: the owner and tenant both sign the district’s Direct Billing Agreement, and the tenant completes the Application for Service. The district asks for 48 hours’ notice for routine work such as final meter readings. Landlords should set this up before move-in so the tenant’s account is ready.

Trash, gas, and electric

The city lists Local Waste Services as Pataskala’s trash provider. Local Waste Services’ City of Pataskala service page shows Friday collection, with materials out by 6 a.m., a free 95-gallon trash cart and 65-gallon recycling cart for each residence, weekly recycling at no extra charge, and a rate of $21.75 a month plus current fuel charges, billed quarterly. Payment is due 30 days from the statement date, with a $3.00 late fee, and a senior rate of $19.58 a month applies to heads of household 62 or older. Move-in and move-out debris and large bulk items are on its list of items it will not collect, which matters at turnover. Say in the lease who holds the trash account.

The same city page lists Columbia Gas for natural gas and AEP or The Energy Cooperative for electricity, depending on location.

Short-term rentals: no separate chapter found

We did not find a short-term rental chapter, permit, or registration in Pataskala’s code. The zoning code instead regulates bed and breakfasts and hotels. Chapter 1273 defines a bed and breakfast as a private residence where lodging and breakfast are provided by a resident family for compensation, and Section 1273.04 requires that the operator live in the house as a primary residence, caps the use at 5 guest bedrooms and 10 guests a night, and limits each paying guest to 30 consecutive nights per visit and 45 nights a year. Section 1273.05 requires one off-street parking space per guest room, and Section 1273.06 requires a conditional use review. A whole-house rental with no resident host does not fit that model, and the zoning definitions exclude transient accommodations from the definition of a dwelling, so talk to Planning and Zoning before listing.

Pataskala has been tightening the hotel side. The Director of Planning’s July 2025 report to council explains that, as state law and building codes changed to allow long-term residential stays in buildings built and licensed as hotels, many communities saw hotels used as apartments in commercial districts. Ordinance 2025-4500 would amend Chapter 1203 (Definitions), Chapter 1239 (Multi-Family Residential), Chapter 1249 (General Business), Chapter 1277 (Hotels and Motels), and Chapter 1291 (Parking and Loading) to address extended-stay hotels; the Planning and Zoning Commission recommended approval and a council public hearing was set for August 18, 2025. We did not confirm the final vote; the Director of Planning’s reports are posted on the city’s Agendas & Minutes page.

Lodging tax: a 3% city hotel tax

Pataskala has its own hotel tax. Section 191.02 of the codified ordinances levies a tax of three percent on all transactions by which lodging by a hotel is furnished to transient guests (Ord. 2008-3876, passed October 20, 2008). Section 191.01 defines a hotel as an establishment offering sleeping accommodations to guests in which five or more rooms are used for guests, and a transient guest as someone staying fewer than 30 consecutive days. The operator collects the tax, states it separately, and files a monthly return with the Finance Director by the 20th; lodging furnished to the State of Ohio or its political subdivisions is exempt.

The Ohio Department of Taxation’s lodging tax table for 2018, the latest we could retrieve, lists a 3.00% Licking County lodging tax and 3.00% rates in Newark, Heath, and Buckeye Lake, but does not list Pataskala. A single house with fewer than five guest rooms falls outside the city’s hotel definition, but hosts should confirm with the Finance Director and the Licking County Auditor whether any lodging tax applies to their rental.

Leases and deposits: Ohio law applies

We did not find a local landlord-tenant code in Pataskala, so Ohio Revised Code Chapter 5321 governs leases. Under Section 5321.16, a landlord who keeps any part of a security deposit must give the tenant an itemized written notice of deductions, with any amount due, within 30 days after the lease ends and possession is returned, and the tenant must provide a forwarding address in writing. See our Ohio landlord-tenant law basics for notices, repairs, and evictions.

Getting around: no COTA, and the county route plan stalled

We found no COTA or other fixed-route bus serving Pataskala. Licking County Transit runs free fixed routes Monday through Friday from 5 a.m. to 7 p.m., but its current routes, according to The Reporting Project, are Main St., 21st St, Granville, the Earthworks Circulator, and Buckeye Lake/Hebron, none of which reach Pataskala. Countywide, Licking County Transit offers curb-to-curb demand-response service Monday through Friday from 5 a.m. to 8 p.m. and Saturdays from 6 a.m. to 6 p.m., booked at 740-670-5185. The fare is $4 for the general public and $2 for elderly and disabled riders, and riders 60 and older ride free under the Senior Citizens Levy Program.

The agency’s plans for Pataskala depended on new money. Before the May 2026 primary, its executive director told The Reporting Project that a proposed 10-year, 0.15% countywide sales tax would fund routes “to/through Pataskala to Etna” and mobility hubs connecting to COTA. Voters rejected it on May 5, 2026, with 57.52% opposed, according to The Reporting Project’s results story, and the agency said it had made no new funding decision. A tenant without a car should plan on demand-response trips or rides, and remember that Pataskala taxes wages in full no matter where they work.

Vouchers and what the SAFMR table says

Licking County is part of the Columbus, OH HUD Metro FMR Area, along with Delaware, Fairfield, Franklin, Madison, Morrow, and Pickaway counties, and HUD sets Small Area Fair Market Rents by ZIP code there. HUD’s FY2027 Small Area FMR table lists these figures for ZIP 43062:

For comparison, the same table lists a two-bedroom at $1,770 in ZIP 43018 (Etna), $1,680 in ZIP 43068 (Reynoldsburg), $1,850 in ZIP 43004 (Blacklick), $2,040 in ZIP 43147 (Pickerington), and $1,250 in ZIP 43055 (Newark). These are payment-standard benchmarks, not asking rents; see our Fair Market Rents explainer for how they work.

The local public housing agency is the Licking Metropolitan Housing Authority at 144 West Main Street in Newark (740-349-8069). Its guide to the Housing Choice Voucher program says payment standards are based on HUD’s Fair Market Rent and that current payment standards are in each family’s briefing packet; it did not post the schedule as text. The guide says families may pay between 30% and 40% of adjusted monthly income depending on the rent, with a minimum tenant rent of $50.

What landlords and tenants should check before signing

  • Jurisdiction. Confirm the parcel is inside the City of Pataskala rather than a surrounding township; ZIP 43062 covers both.
  • Income tax. Budget for the full 1% to Pataskala on top of any work-city tax, because Pataskala gives no credit. File a RITA return every year from age 18 if you have earned income, and make quarterly estimates if you expect to owe $200 or more.
  • School tax. Check the district on the auditor’s record: Southwest Licking adds 0.75% on a traditional base (including pensions and investment income), filed on Form SD 100; Licking Heights adds nothing.
  • Tenant report. Landlords file a list of December 31 tenants with the Tax Administrator by January 31 each year.
  • Registration and occupancy. No rental registry or county-auditor filing; no more than three unrelated people count as a family, and an accessory rental unit requires an owner-occupied house (Chapter 1269).
  • Water and sewer. Find out whether the city or the Licking Regional Water District serves the unit. City bills are due on the 15th with a 10% late charge; the district needs a Direct Billing Agreement signed by both owner and tenant to bill a tenant.
  • Trash. Local Waste Services bills quarterly at $21.75 a month plus fuel; move-out debris is not collected.
  • Short stays. No short-term rental chapter found; bed and breakfasts need a resident operator and conditional use review, and hotels with five or more guest rooms owe the 3% city hotel tax.
  • Vouchers. No local source-of-income protection; call the Licking Metropolitan Housing Authority about payment standards.
  • Transit. No fixed-route bus; Licking County Transit demand-response trips cost $4 for most riders.

Frequently asked questions

Is Pataskala part of Columbus?

No. Pataskala is a separate city in southwestern Licking County, about 19 miles east of Columbus, with a 2020 census population of 17,886. It grew from a small village after merging with Lima Township in 1996, and today it covers about 29 square miles. Columbus-only rules such as the Columbus rental registry and Columbus source-of-income protections do not apply, and ZIP 43062 also reaches into surrounding townships, so confirm the parcel is inside city limits.

What is the Pataskala income tax rate?

Pataskala’s municipal income tax is 1.0%, approved by voters in May 2010 and collected by the Regional Income Tax Agency (RITA). The city gives no credit for income tax paid to another city: RITA lists a 0.000% credit factor, and Section 171.06 of the tax ordinance says no credit is provided to residents for tax paid to other municipalities. A resident earning $60,000 in a city with a 2.5% tax would pay $1,500 there and still owe Pataskala $600. Residents 18 and older must file a RITA return every year.

Is there a school district income tax in Pataskala?

It depends on the school district. The Ohio Department of Taxation lists Southwest Licking Local (district 4510) at 0.75% on a traditional base, which starts from Ohio adjusted gross income and so includes pension and investment income. Licking Heights Local (district 4505) is listed at 0.00%. Both districts serve parts of Pataskala, so check the district on the county auditor’s parcel record.

Do Pataskala landlords have to report their tenants?

Yes. Section 171.23 of Pataskala’s income tax ordinance requires owners who rent residential property in the city to file a written report with the Tax Administrator by January 31 each year, listing the name, address, and telephone number (if available) of each tenant living in the unit on December 31 of the prior year. Failing to file, or filing an incomplete or false report, is a violation subject to the chapter’s penalty section.

Does Pataskala have a rental registry?

We did not find a Pataskala rental registration, rental license, or rental inspection program. Ohio’s county-auditor rental filing in Revised Code Chapter 5323 applies only in counties with more than 200,000 residents, and Licking County had 178,519 at the 2020 census. The January 31 tenant report to the tax administrator is the main local filing for landlords. The zoning code limits a family to three people unless all are related by blood, adoption, or marriage, and Chapter 1223 lets the city declare neglected occupied or vacant property a distressed property.

Who provides water and sewer in Pataskala?

It depends on where the home is. The city’s own Utility Department serves part of Pataskala and bills monthly, with bills due on the 15th and a 10% late charge. Other areas are served by the Licking Regional Water District, formerly the Southwest Licking Community Water and Sewer District, which requires proof of ownership and photo ID to open an account but lets tenants be billed directly if the owner and tenant both sign its Direct Billing Agreement. Some outlying homes may use private wells or septic, so ask the landlord.

Who picks up trash in Pataskala?

The city lists Local Waste Services as its trash provider. Local Waste Services’ Pataskala page shows Friday collection at $21.75 a month plus current fuel charges, billed quarterly, with a $19.58 senior rate for heads of household 62 and older, and one 95-gallon trash cart and one 65-gallon recycling cart provided free.

Can I run an Airbnb in Pataskala?

We did not find a short-term rental ordinance in Pataskala’s code. The zoning code does regulate bed and breakfasts in Chapter 1273: the operator must live in the house as a primary residence, guests are capped at 5 bedrooms and 10 guests a night, each guest may stay no more than 30 consecutive nights or 45 nights a year, one off-street parking space is required per guest room, and a conditional use review is required. A whole-house rental with no resident host does not fit that definition, so ask the Planning and Zoning Department before listing one.

Is there a lodging tax in Pataskala?

Yes, for hotels. Chapter 191 of Pataskala’s code levies a 3% tax on lodging furnished to transient guests (stays of fewer than 30 consecutive days) by a hotel, defined as an establishment with five or more rooms for guests, with monthly returns due to the Finance Director by the 20th (Ord. 2008-3876). The Ohio Department of Taxation’s 2018 table also lists a 3.00% Licking County lodging tax. A single-home rental with fewer than five guest rooms falls outside the city’s hotel definition, but hosts should confirm with the Finance Director and the county.

Can Pataskala landlords refuse housing vouchers?

We found no Pataskala fair housing or source-of-income ordinance, and Pataskala is not on the Affordable Housing Alliance of Central Ohio’s list of ten central Ohio communities with source-of-income protections. State and federal fair housing laws still apply, but they do not protect voucher holders as a class.

Does COTA serve Pataskala?

We found no COTA or Licking County Transit fixed route serving Pataskala. Licking County Transit’s demand-response service is available countywide on weekdays from 5 a.m. to 8 p.m. and Saturdays from 6 a.m. to 6 p.m., with a $4 fare for the general public. A planned Pataskala and Etna route depended on a 0.15% county sales tax that voters rejected on May 5, 2026.

What is the voucher payment standard in Pataskala?

HUD’s FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents for ZIP 43062 are $1,390 for a studio, $1,490 for one bedroom, $1,770 for two, $2,110 for three, and $2,400 for four. Payment standards are set by the housing agency. The Licking Metropolitan Housing Authority says its current payment standards are in each family’s briefing packet and did not post them as text online.
