Westerville: a renting and living guide to the suburb northeast of Columbus

What landlords and tenants should know about Westerville, the separate city on Columbus’s northeast edge that straddles Franklin and Delaware counties, including its 2% income tax and the new school district income tax, the Uptown Review Board, city-owned electric service and renter deposits, the Ohio rules that govern leases here, COTA bus and on-demand service, and voucher figures for ZIP 43081, without inventing market rents.

Last updated October 31, 2026. Facts below come from the City of Westerville and its Codified Ordinances, the Westerville City School District and election coverage, COTA, CMHA, Visit Westerville, the Ohio Revised Code, and Wikipedia’s summary of the city, all cited in Sources. Asking-rent medians are not invented here; see Rent Pulse and Fair Market Rents for verified figures.

Where Westerville is, and why the city line matters

Westerville is a northeastern suburb of Columbus that lies in both Franklin and Delaware counties. Its population was 39,190 at the 2020 census. It is home to Otterbein University, and its historic core, Uptown Westerville, runs along State Street and Main Street near the campus. Alum Creek runs through the city, and the Polaris shopping and office area sits just to the west.

Like Dublin, Worthington, and Upper Arlington, Westerville is its own city, not a Columbus neighborhood. Westerville’s ordinances, income tax, police, utilities, and zoning apply inside its limits, and Columbus-only rules such as Renter’s Choice deposit installments and the new Columbus rental registry do not. The catch is that a “Westerville” mailing address doesn’t prove an address is in the city. The City’s own tax FAQ warns that postal addresses and school districts don’t follow city boundaries, and parts of the Westerville area are in Columbus or in Blendon, Genoa, or other townships. If you vote for Westerville City Council, you live in Westerville; otherwise the City will confirm your city of residence at (614) 901-6420.

A short history

European-American settlers arrived around 1810, and the Westervelt family, who came from New York in 1818, gave the settlement its name. The Blendon Young Men’s Seminary, chartered in 1839, became Otterbein College in 1847 and is today Otterbein University. Westerville was incorporated in August 1858 with 275 residents, and several homes, including the Hanby House near the college, were stops on the Underground Railroad.

An 1859 ordinance banned alcohol sales, and in 1909 the Anti-Saloon League moved its national headquarters here, earning Westerville the nickname “Dry Capital of the World.” The town stayed dry long after Prohibition ended. Voters approved the first Uptown liquor license in 2004, and later local-option votes approved more. Westerville also adopted a council-manager government in 1916 and still uses it: seven council members are elected at large, and an appointed city manager runs daily operations.

Income taxes: city and school district

City income tax. Westerville’s municipal income tax is 2.0% on wages and business net profits. The City says 0.25% of that is dedicated to Parks and Recreation, with the rest split between capital projects and the general fund. Residents who work elsewhere get a 100% credit for tax paid to the work city, capped at Westerville’s 2.0% rate. So a resident who works in Columbus, which taxes at 2.5%, usually owes Westerville nothing extra, while one who works in a city with a lower rate, such as Gahanna at 1.5%, owes the difference. Residents must file an annual Westerville return by April 15, and estimated payments may be required when the liability is over $100.

School district income tax. In November 2025, voters in the Westerville City School District approved Issue 39, a 0.75% continuing earned-income tax, with about 61% of the vote. It began January 1, 2026. This tax is separate from the City’s and follows school district boundaries, not city limits, so a renter who lives in the district pays it even if they work elsewhere. Ask your employer whether it is withheld, or plan to pay it with your Ohio school district return.

Uptown Westerville and the Uptown Review Board

Uptown is the City’s Architectural Review District, set out in Chapter 1149 of the Westerville Codified Ordinances, whose purpose is to protect the character of the period buildings in the Uptown District. The seven-member Uptown Review Board reviews new construction, building additions, facade renovations and exterior material changes, window and door replacements, signs, awnings, paint colors, demolitions, and landscaping elements such as fences, walls, and walkways. Those changes need a Certificate of Appropriateness, applied for through the City’s Permit Portal. Some minor changes can be approved administratively by the Zoning Officer.

The board meets at 6:30 p.m. on the first Thursday of each month, unless otherwise posted, in City Council Chambers at 21 S. State Street. Its review criteria favor repairing deteriorated architectural features rather than replacing them, matching original materials when replacement is necessary, and avoiding sandblasting and other damaging cleaning methods. For an owner, that can mean longer timelines and higher costs for windows, siding, and porches on an Uptown rental; for a tenant, it usually means older buildings with character and sometimes older systems. City Council adopted the Uptown Plan, the area’s long-range plan, on May 6, 2014.

Leases, deposits, and landlord rules

Because Westerville is outside Columbus, Ohio’s statewide landlord-tenant law in Revised Code Chapter 5321 is the main rulebook. Under ORC 5321.16, a landlord has 30 days after the lease ends and the tenant moves out to return the deposit balance with an itemized list of any deductions, and our Ohio landlord-tenant guide covers notice, repairs, and eviction steps in more detail. Columbus’s Renter’s Choice installment rule and its source-of-income protection are Columbus City Code provisions and don’t apply inside Westerville.

Owners of residential rentals must register with the county auditor under Ohio Revised Code 5323.02, which means the Franklin County Auditor or the Delaware County Auditor depending on which side of the county line the property sits. Westerville’s codified ordinances, current through July 7, 2026, also contain a Chapter 1335 titled Residential Rental Property Registration, and the City adopts the 2021 International Property Maintenance Code (Chapter 1327). We could not confirm the current Chapter 1335 fee or filing steps from the City’s public pages, so owners should ask the City’s Planning and Development staff directly before leasing a unit.

Short-term rentals

Unlike Upper Arlington, Worthington, and Bexley, Westerville does not have a stand-alone short-term rental chapter that we could find in its codified ordinances as of the July 7, 2026 update. That doesn’t mean anything goes: zoning, the property maintenance code, and county lodging taxes may still apply, and many leases and homeowners associations forbid subletting. A tenant should get written permission from the landlord, and an owner should confirm with the City’s zoning staff before listing a property for short stays.

Utilities: city electric and renter deposits

One thing that sets Westerville apart is that the City owns and operates its own electric utility, the Westerville Electric Division. Utility Billing handles starting, stopping, or transferring service in person at 21 S. State Street, by phone at (614) 901-6430, or through the City’s online Utility Customer Portal. The City says a deposit may be required for renters and lessees unless it is waived based on credit history, so tenants should budget for it and set up service a few days before move-in. Leases should spell out which utilities are in the tenant’s name.

Getting around: COTA, CMAX, and on-demand rides

COTA lists four fixed routes that connect with Westerville: Line 41 (Crosswoods-Polaris), Line 43 (Westerville), Line 102 (Polaris Parkway/North High), and CMAX, the bus rapid transit line that runs north and south on Cleveland Avenue between Downtown and Westerville. Line 43 is a weekday rush-hour line with no weekend trips, and it stops at the Westerville Park and Ride and in Uptown on State Street.

COTA//Plus fills the gaps with on-demand shared rides inside a zone that covers all of Westerville plus part of Columbus, including Otterbein, Uptown, Polaris Fashion Place, Mount Carmel St. Ann’s, and OhioHealth Westerville. Riders book through the COTA//Plus app or by calling 614-308-4400, weekdays from 5:30 a.m. to 8 p.m.; it does not run on weekends except for occasional special events. Westerville is still mostly a car-oriented suburb, so for broader context see Mobility & the Future.

Parking in Uptown. Visit Westerville says Uptown has four free public lots with about 360 spaces, plus free on-street parking. On-street spaces on State Street are generally limited to four hours, and the official map marks which lots allow 24-hour parking, which matters for tenants in Uptown apartments without assigned spaces.

Schools and daily life

Most of the city is served by the Westerville City School District, which had 14,589 students in the 2024–25 school year according to the National Center for Education Statistics. District lines don’t match city lines, so confirm the assigned school for any address with the district. Otterbein University brings student renters to the blocks around Uptown, and its academic calendar shapes turnover there. The Anti-Saloon League’s former headquarters is now a museum attached to the Westerville Public Library.

Vouchers, ZIPs, and what the SAFMR tables say

Westerville mostly uses two ZIP codes: 43081 on the Franklin County side and 43082, which is mostly in Delaware County. CMHA’s FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents, effective October 1, 2026, cover Franklin County only, so they list 43081 but not 43082; a voucher holder looking in Delaware County should check with the housing authority that issued the voucher. At 100% of SAFMR, CMHA lists the following for 43081. These are payment-standard benchmarks, not asking rents and not automatic approvals; contract rent still goes through rent-reasonableness review, and utility allowances affect the gross-rent comparison.

What landlords and tenants should check before signing

  • City or not. Confirm the address is actually inside Westerville, since the income tax, utilities, and rules change at the line.
  • Both income taxes. Budget for the City’s 2.0% tax (less any work-city credit) and the 0.75% school district earned-income tax that began in 2026.
  • Utility deposit. Tenants: call Westerville Utility Billing early, because a renter deposit may be required.
  • Uptown exteriors. Owners: windows, siding, paint, fences, and signs in Uptown need a Certificate of Appropriateness.
  • Registration. Owners: keep the Franklin or Delaware County Auditor rental registration current and ask the City about Chapter 1335.
  • Which ZIP. 43081 has a CMHA payment standard; 43082 does not appear in CMHA’s Franklin County table.
  • Transit hours. COTA//Plus and Line 43 are weekday services, so check how you’ll get around on weekends.

Frequently asked questions

Is Westerville part of Columbus?

No. Westerville is a separate city that lies in both Franklin and Delaware counties, with a 2020 census population of 39,190. It has run a council-manager government since 1916, and its own codified ordinances, income tax, utilities, police, and zoning apply. Columbus City Code rules such as Renter’s Choice and the new Columbus rental registry do not apply inside Westerville city limits, although some addresses with a Westerville mailing address are actually in Columbus or a township, so check the city of residence for the exact address.

What is the Westerville income tax rate?

Westerville’s municipal income tax is 2.0% on wages and net profits. The City allows residents full credit, up to its 2.0% rate, for tax paid to the city where they work, so someone working in Columbus (2.5%) typically owes Westerville nothing more, while someone working in a city with a lower rate owes the difference. Residents must file an annual Westerville return by April 15.

Is there a Westerville school district income tax?

Yes. Voters in the Westerville City School District approved Issue 39, a 0.75% continuing earned-income tax, in November 2025 with about 61% of the vote, and it took effect January 1, 2026. It is a school district tax, separate from the City’s 2.0% tax, and it applies to residents of the school district, whose boundaries do not match the city limits.

Who reviews exterior changes in Uptown Westerville?

The seven-member Uptown Review Board. Inside the Uptown Architectural Review District (Chapter 1149 of the Westerville Codified Ordinances), new construction, additions, facade and material changes, window and door replacements, signs, awnings, paint colors, demolitions, and landscaping elements such as fences need a Certificate of Appropriateness. Applications go through the City’s Permit Portal, some minor changes can be approved by staff, and the board meets at 6:30 p.m. on the first Thursday of each month at 21 S. State Street.

Do renters pay a utility deposit in Westerville?

Possibly. The City of Westerville runs its own electric utility, and Utility Billing says a deposit may be required for renters and lessees unless it is waived based on credit history. Service is started, stopped, or transferred through Utility Billing at 21 S. State Street, by phone at (614) 901-6430, or through the online Utility Customer Portal.

Which COTA buses serve Westerville?

COTA lists Lines 41 (Crosswoods-Polaris), 43 (Westerville, a weekday rush-hour line), 102 (Polaris Parkway/North High), and CMAX on Cleveland Avenue between Downtown and Westerville. COTA//Plus on-demand rides cover a zone that includes all of Westerville, Otterbein, Uptown, and Polaris, weekdays from 5:30 a.m. to 8 p.m.

What is the voucher payment standard in Westerville?

For ZIP 43081, the Franklin County part of Westerville, CMHA’s FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents at 100%, effective October 1, 2026, are $1,520 for a studio, $1,630 for one bedroom, $1,940 for two, $2,310 for three, and $2,630 for four bedrooms. CMHA’s table covers Franklin County only, so it does not list 43082, which is mostly in Delaware County. These are payment-standard benchmarks, not asking rents.
