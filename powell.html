Powell: a renting and living guide to the Delaware County suburb north of Columbus

What landlords and tenants should know about Powell, the fast-growing Delaware County city between the Scioto and Olentangy rivers, including its 2% city income tax collected by RITA, the zero school district income tax in Olentangy, the absence of a city rental registry, the new 2026 zoning code’s rules for bed and breakfasts and accessory dwelling units, Delaware County’s 6% lodging tax, Del-Co Water and county sewer bills, Rumpke trash, the lack of COTA fixed-route service, and HUD voucher figures for ZIP 43065, without inventing market rents.

Last updated November 11, 2026. Facts below come from the City of Powell and its Planning and Zoning Code, RITA, the Ohio Department of Taxation, the Ohio Revised Code, Delaware County, Del-Co Water, the Delaware County Regional Sewer District, Delaware County Transit, COTA, NCES, HUD, AHACO, and Wikipedia’s summary of the city, all cited in Sources. Asking-rent medians are not invented here; see Rent Pulse and Fair Market Rents for verified figures.

A separate Delaware County city, not a Columbus neighborhood

Powell is its own city in southern Delaware County, about 14 miles north of downtown Columbus, centered on the “Four Corners” where State Route 750 (Powell Road) meets Liberty Street. Wikipedia’s summary, drawing on census records, says the first settlers arrived in 1801, the village was incorporated in 1947, and the population stayed under 400 until the 1980s. It then grew quickly: 2,154 residents in 1990, 6,247 in 2000, 11,500 in 2010, and 14,163 at the 2020 census, on about 5.8 square miles of land.

The 2020 census counted 4,744 households and 4,965 housing units in Powell, with 47.7% of households including children under 18 and 74.6% made up of married couples. Housing units grew from 2,032 in 2000 to 4,965 in 2020, so more than half of today’s housing units were added after 2000. The same census reported a rental vacancy rate of 13.2%, a figure that can swing widely in a city with so few rental units, so treat it as background rather than a market signal.

Because Powell is a separate city, Columbus-only rules such as the Columbus rental registry and Renter’s Choice do not apply inside its limits. The city itself warns that not every Powell, Ohio address is within the City of Powell: Powell mailing addresses (ZIP 43065) also extend into surrounding unincorporated areas such as Liberty Township, where township and county rules apply instead. The city points residents to its interactive boundary map, or to the city offices at 614-885-5380, to confirm jurisdiction before relying on anything below.

Income tax: 2% city tax with a full work-city credit

Powell raised its income tax rate from 0.75% to 2.0% effective January 1, 2022, and the Regional Income Tax Agency (RITA), which has collected Powell’s tax since 1993, lists the current rate at 2.000% with a 100% credit for tax paid to another municipality, capped at 2.000%. That credit change matters as much as the rate: under the old 0.75% rate, RITA shows the credit was limited to 0.25%. Today a resident who pays Columbus’s 2.5% or Dublin’s 2% on wages generally owes Powell nothing more on those wages, while a resident who works in a lower-tax city, or in a township with no municipal income tax, owes the difference.

The city’s finance FAQ says gross income, including deferred income, and business net profits are generally taxable, and that Powell has mandatory filing for residents 18 and older, including college students living away from home; retirees whose only income is a pension, retirement pay, or Social Security can file an exemption form instead. RITA notes that beginning with tax year 2024, individuals under 18 are exempt, that quarterly estimated payments are required when the anticipated amount due is $200 or more, and that balances under $10.01 are neither collected nor refunded. Landlords who own rental property in the city should expect to report net rental profit to RITA.

School district income tax: none in Olentangy

Powell is served mainly by the Olentangy Local School District, which runs Olentangy Liberty High School and Tyler Run Elementary inside the city. The Ohio Department of Taxation’s school district rate table lists Olentangy LSD, district number 2104, at 0.0000%, so residents pay no school district income tax. Some addresses with a Powell mailing address fall in Dublin City Schools instead, and the same state table lists Dublin CSD, district 2513, at 0.0000% as well. Big Walnut (0.75%) and Buckeye Valley (1.00%) elsewhere in Delaware County do carry school income taxes, which is a reason to check the district for any address outside the city.

NCES lists Olentangy Local (district ID 3904676) as a large suburban district serving grades PK–12 in 29 schools, with 24,133 students and about 1,265 classroom teachers (FTE) for the 2024–2025 school year, a student–teacher ratio of 19.08. The district office is at 7840 Graphics Way in Lewis Center. The city’s finance FAQ says about 79% of a Powell property tax bill goes to the Olentangy and vocational schools, compared with roughly 4% to the city itself, which is worth knowing for owners pricing a rental.

Rental registration: county auditor only

We did not find a City of Powell rental registration, rental license, or rental inspection program on the city’s website or in its 2025 and 2026 legislation, and the city’s tax page says Powell does not require a special business registration. Ohio Revised Code 5323.02 still requires owners of residential rental property to register with the county auditor, which for every Powell parcel is the Delaware County Auditor at 145 N. Union Street in Delaware (740-833-2900). The auditor also processes property tax bills for the whole county.

Fair housing: state and federal law, no local voucher protection found

Powell is not on AHACO’s list of the ten central Ohio communities that have adopted source-of-income protections: Bexley, Columbus, Gahanna, Grandview Heights, Pickerington, Reynoldsburg, Upper Arlington, Westerville, Whitehall, and Worthington. We could not open the city’s online codified ordinances to confirm whether Powell has a local fair housing chapter, so check with the city before relying on any local rule.

Ohio Revised Code Chapter 4112 and the federal Fair Housing Act apply in Powell regardless. Together they bar housing discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, ancestry, disability, familial status, and, under Ohio law, military status. Without a local source-of-income rule, a Powell landlord is not required by city law to accept a housing voucher, but every applicant should be screened against the same written standards.

Short-term rentals and bed and breakfasts under the new 2026 zoning code

Powell replaced its zoning ordinance in 2026. Ordinance 2026-14, adopted August 4, 2026, repealed the old Part Eleven and adopted a new Planning and Zoning Code, effective October 6, 2026. As we read the new use table, it lists one- to four-unit dwellings, townhouses, cottage courts, bed and breakfasts, and hotels, but does not list short-term or vacation rentals as a separate use. Section 1104.01(b) says a use that is not listed is prohibited unless the Zoning Administrator determines it is substantially similar to a use the district allows. We did not find a city short-term rental permit or registration program, so anyone thinking about listing a Powell home on a booking platform should ask the Planning Department how the new code applies before taking reservations.

The code’s bed and breakfast standards in Section 1104.04(a) give a sense of how the city treats short stays in homes:

  • The owner or operator must live full-time on the lot and hold at least a 60% ownership interest, with no more than one nonresident employee.
  • No more than four guest rooms, no cooking facilities in guest rooms, and at least one full bathroom for every two guest rooms.
  • Rooms are rented by the day, never by the hour, and a guest may not stay more than 7 consecutive days or 45 days in a calendar year.
  • A guest register with each paying guest’s name, address, and phone number must be kept and shown to city officials on request.
  • Weddings, parties, and similar gatherings that go beyond paying guests require a Conditional Use Permit, and the operation must meet Liberty Township Fire Department and Delaware Public Health District requirements.

For hotels, Section 1104.04(c) caps a guest’s stay at 30 consecutive days and requires a guest register and daily housekeeping.

The new code also allows accessory dwelling units as an accessory use in the residential and downtown districts. Under Section 1104.08(a), one ADU is allowed per lot on lots larger than the district minimum that hold a one-, two-, or three-unit dwelling. It can be inside, attached to, or detached from the house, must be architecturally compatible, and is capped at 800 square feet on lots under 10,000 square feet, 1,000 square feet on lots under 20,000, and 1,200 square feet on larger lots, and never more than 40% of the main house’s floor area. That gives some owners a path to a small long-term rental unit.

Lodging tax: Delaware County’s 6%

Delaware County levies a lodging excise tax of 6% on rent paid by transient guests at establishments with five or more rooms for guests, outside the City of Columbus, where the county rate is 3%. The county says bed and breakfasts with at least five guest rooms must collect it, operators file a monthly return due by the 23rd of the following month, and new lodging businesses must register with the county within 30 days and post a Transient Occupancy Registration Certificate. Proceeds support the Delaware County Convention and Visitors Bureau and the county fairgrounds.

We did not find a separate City of Powell lodging tax; the city’s tax page covers income, property, business, and sales taxes (Delaware County’s sales tax is 7%) and does not mention one. Because the county tax as described applies to establishments with five or more guest rooms, a single home rented by the night may fall outside it; confirm with Delaware County’s Fiscal Services office before assuming either way.

Leases and deposits: Ohio law applies

Powell has no local landlord-tenant code that we found, so Ohio Revised Code Chapter 5321 governs security deposits, repairs, entry notice, and eviction notices. Under that chapter, a landlord generally must return the deposit, with an itemized list of any deductions, within 30 days after the tenant gives up the unit and leaves a forwarding address. Ohio Revised Code 1901.02 gives the Delaware Municipal Court jurisdiction within Delaware County, so eviction cases for Powell addresses are filed there rather than in Franklin County. Our Ohio landlord-tenant basics page walks through the statewide rules.

Utilities: Del-Co Water, county sewer, and Rumpke trash

The city lists Del-Co Water Company for water, the Delaware County Regional Sewer District for sewer, Ohio Power Company for electricity, and Columbia Gas for natural gas. None of these is billed by the city, so tenants and landlords should settle in the lease who opens and pays each account.

Del-Co Water bills monthly, and each bill shows the household’s usage history. Its rates effective May 1, 2026 for a standard 5/8-inch residential meter are a $15.00 monthly base charge plus $5.00 per 1,000 gallons for the first 4,000 gallons, $8.50 per 1,000 gallons from 4,000 to 10,000 gallons, and $12.00 per 1,000 gallons above 10,000. Del-Co’s own table puts a 4,000-gallon month, the median residential use, at $35.00, and a 5,500-gallon month, the average, at $47.75. The top tier is designed to discourage heavy lawn watering in summer.

The Delaware County Regional Sewer District does not bill by usage. Its fee schedule sets flat monthly user charges, billed quarterly, which rose on May 1, 2026 to $41.00 a month for a single-family home or condominium, $24.60 for an efficiency or one-bedroom apartment, and $30.75 for a two-bedroom apartment, with scheduled increases of $1.00 a month for a single-family home each May through 2029. The district’s main office is at 1610 State Route 521 in Delaware, phone 740-833-2240.

Powell residents sign up for trash service directly with Rumpke (1-800-828-8171), which bills quarterly. The city’s trash page lists a rate of $19.24 a month, noted as effective January 1, 2020, plus $3.69 a month for an optional 95-gallon trash cart and a 10% discount for heads of household 65 and older; confirm the current rate with Rumpke, since that figure may be out of date. Trash, recycling, and yard waste are collected every Tuesday, or Wednesday in a week with a Monday or Tuesday holiday, with containers at the curb by 7 a.m. In November 2024 Rumpke began delivering 65-gallon green recycling carts with lids to all Powell homes at no charge. Bulk items must be scheduled with Rumpke 24 hours ahead.

Getting around: plan on a car

Powell does not appear on COTA’s current system map, and we found no COTA fixed route serving the city. In practice, most Powell residents drive to work, school, and errands.

Delaware County Transit (DCT) runs a countywide Demand Response service that will carry riders anywhere within Delaware County on weekdays from 6 a.m. to 6 p.m. Trips must be booked by noon on the business day before, can be booked up to two weeks ahead, and are subject to availability. DCT’s fares are $8.00 for trips up to 10 miles, $17.00 for 10 to 20 miles, and $29.00 for more than 20 miles, and out-of-county trips are limited to medical destinations. DCT’s app-based FLEX service, with $2.00 fares, operates only within Delaware City and Sunbury, not Powell.

Parks and daily life

Wikipedia’s summary lists nine city parks, including Village Green Park, home of the annual Powell Festival, along with Adventure Park, Arbor Ridge Park, Library Park, Oak Park, Beechwood Park, Meadowview Park, Murphy Park, and Seldom Seen Park, and two branches of the Delaware County District Library serve the area. Downtown Powell around the Four Corners is the historic core; the new zoning code turns the old downtown overlay into a Historic Overlay District, where changes to contributing buildings need a Certificate of Appropriateness. The city keeps adding shared-use paths, including a Bennett Farms path project awarded in September 2026.

They are benchmarks that housing authorities use to set voucher payment standards, not asking rents, and ZIP 43065 includes parts of Liberty Township and other areas outside the city. The housing authority that administers a voucher sets the actual payment standard. For how these numbers are built, see our Fair Market Rents explainer.

What landlords and tenants should check before signing

  • Jurisdiction. Confirm the parcel is inside the City of Powell rather than Liberty Township with a 43065 address; taxes and rules differ.
  • Income tax. Budget for Powell’s 2% unless the work city already taxes at 2% or more; Olentangy and Dublin City Schools add no school district income tax.
  • Registration. No city rental registry found; keep the Delaware County Auditor filing current under Ohio Revised Code 5323.02.
  • Short stays. The new 2026 zoning code does not list short-term rentals as a use, and bed and breakfasts must be owner-occupied with a 7-day stay cap; ask the Planning Department first.
  • Vouchers. No local source-of-income protection found; apply consistent written screening either way.
  • Utility accounts. Del-Co Water bills monthly, the county sewer district bills a flat charge quarterly, and Rumpke bills trash quarterly; spell out who pays each.
  • Transit. No COTA fixed route in Powell; Delaware County Transit’s Demand Response needs a day’s notice.

Frequently asked questions

Is Powell part of Columbus?

No. Powell is a separate city in southern Delaware County about 14 miles north of downtown Columbus, with a 2020 census population of 14,163. It has its own council and income tax, so Columbus-only rules such as Renter’s Choice and the Columbus rental registry do not apply. Not every Powell mailing address (ZIP 43065) is inside the city, so confirm jurisdiction with the city’s boundary map or the city offices at 614-885-5380.

What is the Powell income tax rate?

Powell’s income tax rate has been 2.0% since January 1, 2022, up from 0.75%, and the tax is collected by the Regional Income Tax Agency (RITA). The city and RITA list a 100% credit for tax paid to another municipality, capped at 2.0%, so a resident who pays Columbus’s 2.5% or Dublin’s 2% on wages generally owes Powell nothing more on those wages. Residents 18 and older must file, though retirees with only retirement income can file an exemption form.

Is there a school district income tax in Powell?

Not for Olentangy Local Schools, which serves most of Powell. The Ohio Department of Taxation’s school district rate table lists Olentangy LSD (district 2104) at 0.00%, and Dublin City Schools, which serves some Powell mailing addresses, is also 0.00%. NCES reports Olentangy had 24,133 students in 29 schools in 2024–2025.

Does Powell have a rental registry?

We did not find a City of Powell rental registration, rental license, or rental inspection program. Ohio Revised Code 5323.02 still requires owners of residential rental property to register with the county auditor, which for Powell parcels is the Delaware County Auditor.

Can I run an Airbnb in Powell?

Check with the city first. Powell’s new Planning and Zoning Code, effective October 6, 2026, does not list short-term or vacation rentals as a use, and Section 1104.01(b) says unlisted uses are prohibited unless the Zoning Administrator finds them substantially similar to an allowed use. We found no city short-term rental permit. Bed and breakfasts are a listed use but must be owner-occupied, have no more than four guest rooms, and limit each guest to 7 consecutive days and 45 days a year.

Is there a lodging tax in Powell?

Delaware County levies a 6% lodging tax on transient guests at establishments with five or more guest rooms outside Columbus, filed monthly with the county. We did not find a separate City of Powell lodging tax. A single home rented by the night may fall outside the county tax as described, so confirm with Delaware County Fiscal Services.

Can Powell landlords refuse housing vouchers?

Powell is not on AHACO’s list of ten central Ohio communities that have adopted source-of-income protections, and we found no local rule requiring landlords to accept vouchers. Ohio and federal fair housing laws still apply, covering race, color, religion, sex, national origin, ancestry, disability, familial status, and military status. Landlords should apply the same written screening standards to every applicant.

Who provides water, sewer, and trash in Powell?

Del-Co Water Company supplies water and bills monthly; from May 1, 2026 a standard residential meter pays a $15.00 base charge plus $5.00 per 1,000 gallons for the first 4,000 gallons, $8.50 up to 10,000, and $12.00 above that. The Delaware County Regional Sewer District bills a flat $41.00 a month for a single-family home, billed quarterly. Residents sign up with Rumpke for trash, recycling, and yard waste, collected on Tuesdays and billed quarterly.

Does COTA serve Powell?

Powell does not appear on COTA’s system map, and we found no COTA fixed route in the city. Delaware County Transit offers countywide Demand Response rides on weekdays from 6 a.m. to 6 p.m., booked by noon the business day before, with fares of $8 for trips up to 10 miles, $17 for 10 to 20 miles, and $29 for longer trips.

What is the voucher payment standard in Powell?

For ZIP 43065, HUD’s FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents, effective October 1, 2026, are $1,710 for a studio, $1,830 for one bedroom, $2,180 for two, $2,600 for three, and $2,960 for four bedrooms. These are payment-standard benchmarks for voucher holders, not asking rents, and ZIP 43065 includes areas outside the city. The housing authority that administers a voucher sets the actual payment standard.
