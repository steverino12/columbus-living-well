The University District: a renting and living guide near Ohio State

What landlords and tenants should know about Columbus’s University District, the neighborhood around Ohio State’s main campus, including who reviews zoning, the new citywide rental registry with its December 31, 2026 deadline, Renter’s Choice deposit installments, short-term rental permits, permit parking, COTA and CABS buses, Ohio State’s second-year live-on rule, and voucher figures for ZIPs 43201 and 43210, without inventing market rents.

Last updated October 29, 2026. Facts below come from the Columbus City Codes and Columbus City Council legislation, the City of Columbus, the University Area Commission, Ohio State University offices, COTA, the Franklin County Auditor, Wikipedia’s summary of the district, and CMHA tables cited in Sources. Asking-rent medians are not invented here; see Rent Pulse and Fair Market Rents for verified figures.

Where the University District is

The University District, also called the University Area, is a Columbus neighborhood of about 2.8 square miles roughly two miles north of Downtown. It holds Ohio State’s main campus, the Wexner Medical Center, and the Battelle Institute, and Wikipedia’s summary calls it Columbus’s most densely populated area. The University Area Commission, which has represented residents, service organizations, and businesses since 1972, describes the boundary as Glen Echo Ravine on the north, the railroad east of North Fourth Street on the east, Fifth Avenue on the south, and the Olentangy River on the west. North of it is Clintonville; south of Fifth Avenue are the Short North, Victorian Village, and Italian Village.

It is not one uniform student zone. The densest student rental area sits east of High Street between roughly 11th and 17th Avenues, often called the off-campus neighborhood. Old North Columbus, platted in 1842 as the village of North Columbus, covers the area north of Woodruff Avenue and includes Iuka Ravine, Indianola Forest, the Oakland and Northwood Avenue area, Tuttle Park, and Glen Echo. The southern part of the district, including the Circles, Dennison Place, and NECKO west of High Street and Weinland Park east of High, has a higher share of homeowners.

Because it is part of Columbus itself, Columbus city services, the Columbus income tax, and Columbus City Code rules for landlords and tenants apply. For other neighborhood spotlights, start at the Neighborhoods hub.

Who reviews zoning and development

The University Area Commission meets at 6:30 p.m. on the third Wednesday of each month at the Northwood & High Building, 2231 N. High Street. Its Zoning Committee reviews rezonings, variances, graphics, and special permits in the area and makes recommendations to the full commission, and it votes on demolition requests, with its decision on demolitions final for the commission. The commission’s 2026 commissioner election is scheduled for December 5, with candidate petitions due November 17, according to its website.

The district also has its own section of the zoning code. Chapter 3325 of the Columbus City Codes, the University District Zoning Overlay, applies within the same boundary and carries out the University District Plan of 2015. Its stated goals include preserving and stabilizing neighborhoods, focusing the highest-density housing on the primary corridors closest to Ohio State, balancing parking needs with walkability, and promoting the reuse of original buildings. A smaller University Impact District near campus and High Street is subject to design review under the same chapter, so exterior changes and new buildings there take extra steps. Check the City’s online zoning map, which shows area commission and planning overlay layers, before planning work on a property.

New for landlords: the Columbus rental registry

Columbus City Council passed Ordinance 0923-2026 in April 2026, creating Chapter 4515 of the Columbus City Codes, the Registry of Residential Rental Properties. The registry starts with the 2027 calendar year, and the ordinance requires initial registration from October 1 through December 31, 2026. It covers rental property anywhere in city limits, so it reaches the University District’s houses, doubles, and apartment buildings alike. Dormitories, licensed short-term rentals, and buildings on the vacant building registry are excluded.

  • What to file. Parcel numbers, address, unit count, owner contact details (and, for an LLC or other entity, a member, manager, or officer), an emergency contact, details about shared building systems and common areas, and an affirmation that critical systems such as heat, water, and fire alarms work.
  • Local operator. Owners must name a local operator who lives within 100 miles of Columbus or holds an Ohio real estate license, unless they own fewer than ten units in the city and file a signed certification.
  • Fees. $15 per unit per year, capped at $1,500 per complex, per the ordinance. Late fees and conditional-status fees are set by the City’s fee schedule.
  • Inspections. Every three years a preventative education inspection covers the exterior, common areas, and shared mechanical systems. Individual units are inspected only if the tenant or owner asks, unless the property lands in conditional status after repeated code violations.
  • Penalties. An owner who doesn’t comply with a notice of violation can face a civil penalty of $2 per unit per day.
  • Updates. Changes must be filed within 30 days, and a buyer must re-register within 30 days of purchase.

The city registry is separate from the Franklin County Auditor’s rental registration under Ohio Revised Code 5323.02, which still applies. Our Ohio landlord-tenant law guide covers both.

Deposits, leases, and tenant protections

Columbus City Code 4551.04, known as Renter’s Choice, applies to operators with five or more rental units. If they require a security deposit, they must give written notice that the tenant may pay it in full, over at least three monthly installments, or over at least six monthly installments, each due with rent. Inside Columbus, landlords also can’t refuse an applicant because of a lawful source of income such as a housing voucher (4551.03), and monthly late fees are limited to the larger of $50 or 10% of rent (4551.07). Ohio’s statewide rules in Revised Code Chapter 5321, including the 30-day deposit return, apply too.

When several roommates share one lease, read whether the lease makes each roommate responsible for the whole rent, when the term starts and ends, and how the deposit will be paid.

Ohio State’s Off-Campus and Commuter Student Engagement office runs an Off-Campus Housing Network for property owners. Members commit to leases that follow city, state, and federal law, including Renter’s Choice, to meeting the Columbus Housing Code, to random safety inspections by City code enforcement officers with university staff, to registering properties with Franklin County, to front and rear exterior lighting and carbon monoxide detectors, and to an after-hours contact number. Network enrollment closes April 1 each year.

Short-term rentals

Columbus requires a short-term rental permit under Chapter 598 for stays of fewer than 30 nights. The City’s 2026 information sheet lists a $20 application fee, a $75 license for a primary residence or $150 for a non-primary residence, a $32 BCI background check for each listed party, a letter of good standing from the Columbus Income Tax Division, platform registration details, and a 24-hour local contact. A 5.1% lodging excise tax applies to stays, and listed occupancy may not exceed three times the number of bedrooms shown on the Franklin County Auditor’s website. A tenant applying as a primary resident needs a lease that explicitly allows short-term rental use, and a licensed short-term rental is excluded from the long-term rental registry.

Parking

The City’s University District Parking Plan divides permit parking into zones UDA, UDB, and UDC. According to the City’s FAQ for the plan, a permit lets a resident park on any street in their zone without paying or watching time limits, and parking is free and unrestricted from 10 p.m. to 8 a.m. Residential properties built before 2009, and newer ones with fewer than five units, are eligible; buildings of five or more units built after December 31, 2008 generally are not, unless each unit has its own address and private entrance, and Ohio State properties are not eligible. Addresses in UDB and UDC can get up to six permits and UDA up to four, one per licensed driver, and the FAQ listed tiered prices that rise with each additional permit. Check current fees and your address on the City’s permit eligibility map at parkcolumbus.com, because a new building’s tenants may have no street permit at all.

On home football Saturdays, check posted signs and CampusParc’s game-day parking information before leaving a car on the street near the stadium; Ohio State’s CABS buses also suspend service six hours before kickoff.

Getting around: COTA and CABS

COTA’s Ohio State guide lists Lines 1, 2, and 102 at High Street and Chittenden, Lines 1, 2, and 31 at High Street and 15th Avenue, Lines 8 and 22 on 11th Avenue at High, Lines 8, 22, and 31 on Neil Avenue and 12th Avenue, and Lines 22 and 31 at the medical center. Line 1 also runs west along Lane Avenue past the Schottenstein Center. Enrolled Columbus-campus students with the COTA fee on their account ride the whole COTA system by scanning the digital pass in the Ohio State app; swiping a physical BuckID will no longer work as of January 6, 2027.

Ohio State’s free Campus Area Bus Service (CABS) runs routes on and around campus, including East Residential, which serves Hudson Street via Fourth Street, 11th Avenue via Summit Street, and the east side of campus via High Street. CABS runs reduced service during academic breaks and none on university holidays. For broader context on transit and car-free living, see Mobility & the Future.

Who rents here: Ohio State’s live-on rule

Ohio State generally requires unmarried full-time students within two years of high school graduation to live on campus for their first two years. Exemptions include living with a parent or certain close relatives within 25 miles of campus and living in an approved fraternity or sorority house, and second-year exemption requests have set deadlines each spring. In practice, that means most off-campus student renters are juniors, seniors, and graduate or professional students, alongside the many non-student households in Old North Columbus, Weinland Park, and the district’s southern neighborhoods.

Taxes for tenants who move here

Columbus residents pay the city’s 2.5% municipal income tax, and the City gives a credit for tax paid to the city where you work, up to Columbus’s rate. Students and others who move here should check whether they owe a Columbus return. Property taxes often reach tenants indirectly through rent.

Vouchers, ZIPs, and what the SAFMR tables say

Most of the residential University District uses ZIP 43201, which it shares with the Short North and Victorian Village, while Ohio State’s main campus is ZIP 43210. CMHA’s FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents, effective October 1, 2026, at 100% of SAFMR, list the following. These are payment-standard benchmarks, not asking rents and not automatic approvals; contract rent still goes through CMHA’s rent-reasonableness review, and utility allowances affect the gross-rent comparison.

What landlords and tenants should check before signing

  • Registry. Owners: register each Columbus rental with the City by December 31, 2026, and keep the Franklin County Auditor registration current.
  • Deposit options. Operators with five or more units must give written Renter’s Choice notice before the lease is signed.
  • Roommate terms. Read how rent liability, move-in, and move-out work for every person on a group lease.
  • Parking. Confirm whether the address is eligible for a UD permit before bringing a car.
  • Short stays. Anything under 30 nights needs a City short-term rental permit.
  • Exterior work. In the University Impact District, budget time for design review.
  • Bus access. Check distance to High Street, Neil Avenue, and CABS stops, and set up the Ohio State app pass before January 6, 2027.

Frequently asked questions

Is the University District part of the City of Columbus?

Yes. The University District is a Columbus neighborhood, so Columbus city services, the 2.5% Columbus income tax, and Columbus City Code rules for landlords and tenants apply, including source-of-income protection (4551.03), Renter’s Choice deposit installments (4551.04), the late-fee limit (4551.07), and the new rental registry (Chapter 4515). The University Area Commission and the University District Zoning Overlay both use the same boundary: Glen Echo Ravine on the north, the first railroad east of North Fourth Street on the east, East Fifth Avenue on the south, and the Olentangy River on the west.

Do University District landlords have to join the new Columbus rental registry?

Yes, if the property is inside Columbus city limits. Ordinance 0923-2026, passed April 2026, created Chapter 4515 of the Columbus City Codes. Initial registration runs October 1 through December 31, 2026, for the 2027 registry year. The fee is $15 per unit with a $1,500 cap per complex. Registered properties get a preventative education inspection of the exterior, common areas, and shared systems every three years, and an owner who ignores a notice of violation can face a civil penalty of $2 per unit per day. This is separate from the Franklin County Auditor rental registration required by Ohio Revised Code 5323.02.

Can I pay my security deposit in installments near Ohio State?

Often, yes. Under Columbus City Code 4551.04, an operator with five or more rental units who requires a deposit must give written notice that the tenant may pay it in full, over at least three monthly installments, or over at least six monthly installments, due with rent. Operators with fewer than five units are exempt.

Can I run an Airbnb in the University District?

Only with a City of Columbus short-term rental permit for stays of fewer than 30 nights. The City’s 2026 application lists a $20 application fee, a $75 primary-residence or $150 non-primary-residence license, BCI background checks at $32 each, a letter of good standing from the Columbus Income Tax Division, a 24-hour local contact, and a 5.1% lodging excise tax on stays. Occupancy may not exceed three times the bedrooms listed on the Franklin County Auditor’s site. A tenant applying needs a lease that explicitly allows short-term rental use.

Which buses serve the University District?

COTA lists Lines 1, 2, and 102 on High Street by campus, Lines 8 and 22 on 11th and 12th Avenues and Neil Avenue, and Line 31 through campus and the medical center. Enrolled Columbus-campus students with the COTA fee ride free by scanning the Ohio State app; swiping a physical BuckID stops working January 6, 2027. Ohio State’s free CABS buses also circulate on and around campus.

Do Ohio State sophomores live off campus?

Usually not. Ohio State generally requires unmarried full-time students within two years of high school graduation to live on campus for their first two years, with exemptions such as living with a parent or close relative within 25 miles or in an approved fraternity or sorority house. Most off-campus student renters are juniors, seniors, and graduate students.

What is the voucher payment standard in the University District?

Most of the residential University District uses ZIP 43201, and the main campus is ZIP 43210. CMHA’s FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents at 100%, effective October 1, 2026, list $1,330 for a studio, $1,420 for one bedroom, $1,690 for two bedrooms, $2,010 for three, and $2,290 for four bedrooms in 43201, and $790, $840, $1,000, $1,200, and $1,360 in 43210. These are payment-standard benchmarks, not asking rents.
