Groveport: a renting and living guide to the canal town beside Rickenbacker

What landlords and tenants should know about Groveport, the Franklin County city southeast of Columbus that grew from an Ohio and Erie Canal village into a hub of warehouses around Rickenbacker International Airport, including its 2% city income tax collected by RITA, the zero school district income tax in Groveport Madison, the city’s license for short-term rentals and other group housing, the 4% hotel tax, its two water systems, Local Waste Services trash, COTA Lines 22 and 24 with the free GREAT shuttle, and HUD voucher figures for ZIP 43125, without inventing market rents.

Last updated November 10, 2026. Facts below come from the City of Groveport and its Codified Ordinances, RITA, the Ohio Department of Taxation, the Ohio Revised Code, COTA, Groveport Madison Schools, the U.S. Census Bureau, HUD, AHACO, and Wikipedia’s summary of the city, all cited in Sources. Asking-rent medians are not invented here; see Rent Pulse and Fair Market Rents for verified figures.

A separate small city, not a Columbus neighborhood

Groveport is its own city in southeastern Franklin County, with 6,009 residents at the 2020 census and a Census Bureau estimate of 5,818 for July 2025. The city says it was formed from two rival canal villages, Rarey’s Port and Wirt’s Grove, whose names were combined as a compromise, and that it was home to John Rarey, the nineteenth-century horse trainer known for his humane methods. Wikipedia lists a total area of about 8.9 square miles.

The city’s own history page credits Rickenbacker International Airport and its free trade zones for growth since the early 1980s, when Groveport annexed land for large industrial parks and new subdivisions. That mix matters to renters: much of the land inside city limits is distribution and logistics space, while homes cluster around the old Main Street core and newer neighborhoods to the north and west.

The Census Bureau’s 2020–2024 American Community Survey figures for the city show an owner-occupied housing rate of 70.7%, 2,259 households averaging 2.61 people, and a median gross rent of $1,082. That rent figure is a five-year survey average of what households reported paying, not a current asking rent, so treat it as background rather than a pricing guide.

Because Groveport is a separate city, Columbus-only programs such as the Columbus rental registry and Renter’s Choice do not apply inside its limits. The 43125 ZIP code also reaches into Columbus and Madison Township, so a “Groveport” mailing address does not prove the property is in the city. Check the parcel with the Franklin County Auditor before relying on anything below.

Income tax: 2% city tax with a full work-city credit

Groveport levies a 2.0% income tax under Chapter 181 of its code, and the Regional Income Tax Agency (RITA) has collected it since January 1, 2019. RITA’s member page lists the rate at 2.000% since 2020 with a 100% credit for tax paid to the municipality where a resident works, capped at 2.000%. In practice, a resident who pays Columbus’s 2.5% on wages usually owes Groveport nothing more on those wages, while a resident who works in a city with a lower rate, or in a township with none, owes the difference.

The city’s business tax page says the 2% applies to wages, salaries, and net profits of businesses and rentals, and that it does not apply to investment earnings or retirement income. Landlords who own rental property inside Groveport should expect to report net rental profit to RITA. RITA also notes that individuals under 18 are exempt from the municipal tax. The city lists April 15, 2026 as the filing deadline for tax year 2025 returns.

School district income tax: none in Groveport Madison

Many Ohio suburbs add a school district income tax on top of the city tax. Groveport Madison Local Schools does not. The Ohio Department of Taxation’s school district rate table lists Groveport Madison LSD, district number 2507, at a rate of 0.0000%. That is a real difference from Canal Winchester next door, where some addresses carry a 0.75% or 1.25% school tax.

Groveport Madison says it serves about 6,300 students from preschool through grade 12 in 11 schools across 40 square miles, covering the City of Groveport and Madison Township plus parts of Columbus, Obetz, Canal Winchester, and Reynoldsburg. Schools in Groveport itself include Groveport Elementary and Groveport Madison Middle School Central on Main Street, and Groveport Madison High School on South Hamilton Road.

Rental registration: county auditor rule, plus a license for group housing

We did not find a general rental registration or rental permit requirement for ordinary long-term rentals in Groveport’s published Codified Ordinances. Ohio Revised Code 5323.02 still requires owners of residential rental property to register with the county auditor, which for every Groveport parcel is Franklin County.

What Groveport does license is group housing. Chapter 1173, adopted by Ordinance 2024-032 in October 2024 and amended by Ordinance 2025-037 in December 2025, defines group housing to include boarding houses, recovery housing residences, supervised group living, and short-term rentals. Owners must register the property with the city, pay a $125 fee for an annual license in the owner’s name, display the license inside, and renew it each year because every license expires on January 31. The site plan, floor plan, and life safety plan must be approved before a license is issued, and licensed properties are inspected every year.

The chapter also sets hard limits: no more than five occupants, a bedroom for each occupant, one paved off-street parking space per permitted occupant located behind the rear building line, and spacing of at least 1,000 feet from other group housing and from schools and day cares. Applicants must carry commercial general liability insurance of at least $1,000,000 per occurrence, disclose any felony convictions of the owner, and prove title. Licenses are not transferable, so a buyer of a licensed property must apply for a new one within ten days.

Separately, Section 1321.11 of the building code says no building used as a private residence or apartment may be occupied until the city’s Building Official has issued a certificate of occupancy. That matters most for new construction and conversions.

Fair housing: a narrower local list, state and federal rules on top

Groveport’s fair housing section, 557.02, prohibits refusing to rent, discriminating in rental terms, and discriminatory advertising or application questions based on race, sex, color, religion, ancestry, or national origin. It does not list source of income, and Groveport is not on AHACO’s list of the ten central Ohio communities that have adopted source-of-income protections: Bexley, Columbus, Gahanna, Grandview Heights, Pickerington, Reynoldsburg, Upper Arlington, Westerville, Whitehall, and Worthington.

Ohio Revised Code Chapter 4112 and the federal Fair Housing Act still apply in Groveport and cover more classes than the local section, including disability and familial status, along with military status under Ohio law. Landlords should apply the same written screening standards to every applicant, whether or not a housing voucher is involved.

Short-term rentals: licensed, capped, and taxed at 4%

Groveport is one of the few central Ohio suburbs with a detailed short-term rental chapter. Chapter 1183 says short-term rentals must be licensed under Chapter 1173 and treats them as a limited, subordinate use of a single-family home rather than a lodging business. Section 1183.04(f) makes it unlawful to list or advertise a short-term rental before the license is issued or after it has been revoked.

The development standards in Section 1183.03 include:

  • No more than six guests at a time, including children, and no more than three guest rooms, each at least 100 square feet and on the first or second floor only.
  • A maximum stay of 14 consecutive nights per guest visit.
  • One full bathroom for every two guest rooms, none in the basement, and a single kitchen with no cooking in guest rooms.
  • One off-street parking space per bedroom, in the driveway or garage.
  • Quiet hours from 10:00 p.m. to 7:00 a.m., outdoor security cameras, and guest registration records kept for three years.
  • No guests sleeping in garages, detached buildings, tents, or campers, and no weddings, parties, or reunions that exceed the occupancy cap.

Section 1183.03(p) says the bed tax for short-term rentals is the same as for hotels and motels. Chapter 183 sets that tax at 4% of what a transient guest pays for lodging, defines a transient guest as anyone staying less than 30 consecutive days, and applies it both to hotels with five or more rooms and to transient accommodations with four or fewer. Vendors file monthly returns with the city’s Director of Finance, and violations of the short-term rental chapter carry fines of up to $100 per day.

Leases and deposits: Ohio law applies

Groveport has no local landlord-tenant code of its own, so Ohio Revised Code Chapter 5321 governs security deposits, repairs, entry notice, and eviction notices. Under that chapter, a landlord generally must return the deposit, with an itemized list of any deductions, within 30 days after the tenant gives up the unit and leaves a forwarding address. Our Ohio landlord-tenant basics page walks through the statewide rules, and the Franklin County Municipal Court handles eviction filings for Groveport addresses.

Utilities: two water systems and Local Waste Services trash

Water service in Groveport depends on the address. The city’s 2025 annual report says the Groveport Water System, which draws from two wells and stores water in a 200,000-gallon elevated tank, serves about 1,200 customers in the city core. Addresses outside that system get water and sewer through a supply contract with the City of Columbus and are billed directly by Columbus. Sewage treatment for the whole city is provided by Columbus under contract, while Groveport crews maintain the lines.

For Groveport Water System customers, the city bills water and sewer every quarter. Its fee schedule, revised July 29, 2026, lists inside-city rates for billing year 2026 of $11.51 per 1,000 gallons for water and $9.66 per 1,000 gallons for sewer, rising each year to $13.32 and $11.19 by 2029. The utilities page says a minimum bill applies whenever service is on, regardless of occupancy, and covers usage from 0 to 7,000 gallons. The annual report put the average quarterly water and sewer bill for city system customers at $316.81 in 2025, not counting the Columbus Clean River charge or Groveport’s stormwater fee.

Groveport bills stormwater separately at $2.50 per equivalent residential unit for billing year 2026, and customers on the Columbus water system pay that stormwater fee twice a year. Bills are due one month after the bill date, and a 10% penalty is added the day after an unpaid due date. New customers can reach the city’s Accounting Clerk at 614-830-2048.

The city contracts with Local Waste Services for trash, recycling, and yard waste, all collected on Mondays, with items at the curb by 7:00 a.m. and a 50-pound limit per item. For recurring collection problems the city lists Public Services at 614-836-3910. Leases should spell out who holds the water account and who pays for trash, since both can be billed to the owner.

Getting around: COTA Lines 22 and 24 and the GREAT shuttle

COTA extended Lines 22 and 24 in January 2026 so that both now continue past their old end point to the Mobility Center at Rickenbacker, at London-Groveport and Shook roads. COTA’s schedule data shows both lines running about every 30 minutes on weekdays, from roughly 5 a.m. to about 11:30 p.m. Line 22 runs north along Alum Creek Drive toward the Near East Side and Ohio State, and Line 24 runs north along Hamilton Road to Easton. The adult fare is $2.

At the Mobility Center, riders can transfer to GREAT, the Groveport Rickenbacker Employee Access Transit shuttle. The city describes GREAT as free, on-demand microtransit timed to shift changes that carries riders between COTA and employers inside Groveport; riders call for a pickup for the return trip. GREAT does not run on Christmas Day or Easter.

This network is built mainly around jobs. Service close to the older Main Street neighborhoods is limited, so check COTA’s trip planner for the exact address. For most errands, school runs, and trips downtown, a car is the practical default; the Census Bureau puts the average commute for Groveport workers at 23.8 minutes.

Parks and daily life

The city says it has the most park land per capita in Franklin County. Groveport Park, at 7370 Groveport Road, covers 82 acres and holds the Groveport Recreation and Senior Center, the aquatic center, eight pickleball courts, ball diamonds, soccer fields, ponds, and about 1.8 miles of paved paths. Blacklick Park follows the old canal towpath to Lock 22, the only lock in Groveport, and Heritage Park on Wirt Road holds the 1815 Groveport Log House, a stocked pond, and the October Apple Butter Day festival. Cruiser Park on Bixby Road adds 52 acres of soccer fields and wetland.

What landlords and tenants should check before signing

  • Jurisdiction. Confirm the parcel is inside Groveport rather than Columbus or Madison Township with a 43125 address; taxes, utilities, and rules differ.
  • Income tax. Budget for Groveport’s 2% unless the work city already taxes at 2% or more; Groveport Madison adds no school district income tax.
  • Registration. No general rental registry found; keep the Franklin County Auditor filing current under Ohio Revised Code 5323.02.
  • Group housing and short stays. Rooming houses, recovery housing, and short-term rentals need the $125 annual city license, insurance, an inspection, and spacing from other group housing, schools, and day cares.
  • Lodging tax. Short-term stays under 30 days owe Groveport’s 4% hotel tax.
  • Vouchers. No local source-of-income protection found; apply consistent written screening either way.
  • Water. Find out whether the address is on the Groveport Water System, billed quarterly by the city, or on Columbus water, and who holds the account.
  • Transit. COTA Lines 22 and 24 and the GREAT shuttle serve the Rickenbacker job centers; check the trip planner for the exact address.

Frequently asked questions

Is Groveport part of Columbus?

No. Groveport is a separate city in Franklin County southeast of downtown Columbus, with a 2020 census population of 6,009. It has its own council, police department, and income tax, so Columbus-only rules such as Renter’s Choice and the Columbus rental registry do not apply. A Groveport mailing address (ZIP 43125) can sit in Columbus, Madison Township, or another jurisdiction, so confirm the city limits for the exact address with the Franklin County Auditor.

What is the Groveport income tax rate?

Groveport’s income tax rate is 2.0%, collected by the Regional Income Tax Agency (RITA) since January 1, 2019. RITA lists a 100% credit for tax paid to another municipality, capped at 2.0%, so a resident who pays Columbus’s 2.5% on wages typically owes Groveport nothing more on those wages. The city says the tax applies to wages and to net profits of businesses and rentals, but not to investment earnings or retirement income.

Is there a school district income tax in Groveport?

Not for Groveport Madison Local Schools. The Ohio Department of Taxation’s school district rate table lists Groveport Madison LSD (district number 2507) at 0.00%, so residents of that district pay no school district income tax. The district says it serves about 6,300 students in 11 schools across 40 square miles, including the City of Groveport and Madison Township and parts of Columbus, Obetz, Canal Winchester, and Reynoldsburg.

Does Groveport have a rental registry?

We did not find a general rental registration or rental permit requirement for ordinary long-term rentals in Groveport’s published Codified Ordinances. Chapter 1173, adopted by Ordinance 2024-032, does require an annual city license for group housing, which the code defines to include boarding houses, recovery housing, supervised group living, and short-term rentals. Ohio Revised Code 5323.02 still requires owners of residential rental property to register with the Franklin County Auditor.

Can I run an Airbnb in Groveport?

Only with a city license. Chapter 1183 says short-term rentals must be licensed under Chapter 1173, which carries a $125 annual fee, expires each January 31, and requires an approved site plan, floor plan, and life safety plan, $1,000,000 in general liability insurance, and an annual inspection. Chapter 1183 then limits guests to six, stays to 14 consecutive nights, and guest rooms to three, requires one off-street parking space per bedroom, and bars advertising a listing before the license is issued. Chapter 1173 also requires group housing to be at least 1,000 feet from other group housing and from schools and day cares.

Is there a lodging tax in Groveport?

Yes. Chapter 183 of the city code levies a 4% excise tax on lodging furnished to transient guests, meaning stays of less than 30 consecutive days, by hotels and by smaller transient accommodations with four or fewer rooms. Section 1183.03(p) says the bed tax for short-term rentals is the same as for hotels and motels, and vendors file monthly returns with the city’s Director of Finance.

Can Groveport landlords refuse housing vouchers?

Groveport is not on AHACO’s list of ten central Ohio communities that have adopted source-of-income protections, and the city’s fair housing section, 557.02, lists race, sex, color, religion, ancestry, and national origin without source of income. Ohio and federal fair housing laws add disability and familial status, among other classes. Landlords should apply the same written screening standards to every applicant.

Who provides water, sewer, and trash in Groveport?

It depends on the address. The city’s own Groveport Water System serves about 1,200 customers in the city core and bills water and sewer quarterly; for billing year 2026 the inside-city rates are $11.51 per 1,000 gallons for water and $9.66 per 1,000 gallons for sewer, with a minimum bill covering the first 7,000 gallons. Other addresses get water and sewer through a supply contract with the City of Columbus and are billed by Columbus, while Groveport bills a stormwater fee of $2.50 per equivalent residential unit in 2026. Local Waste Services collects trash, recycling, and yard waste on Mondays.

Does COTA serve Groveport?

Yes, mainly around the Rickenbacker job centers. COTA Lines 22 and 24 now run to the Mobility Center at Rickenbacker at London-Groveport and Shook roads, roughly every 30 minutes on weekdays, and the adult fare is $2. From there the city’s free GREAT microtransit shuttle takes riders to employers inside Groveport. Check COTA’s trip planner for the exact address, since service in the older residential core is limited and many residents rely on a car.

What is the voucher payment standard in Groveport?

For ZIP 43125, HUD’s FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents, effective October 1, 2026, are $1,220 for a studio, $1,300 for one bedroom, $1,550 for two, $1,850 for three, and $2,100 for four bedrooms. These are payment-standard benchmarks for voucher holders, not asking rents. ZIP 43125 also covers parts of Columbus and Madison Township, and the housing authority that administers a voucher sets the actual payment standard.
