Whitehall: a renting and living guide to the city east of Columbus

What landlords and tenants should know about Whitehall, the separate city along Yearling Road, East Broad Street, and East Main Street east of Downtown Columbus, including its 2.5% city-run income tax, who bills water, sewer, and trash, how short stays and fair housing are handled, Whitehall City Schools, COTA bus service, and voucher figures for ZIP 43213, without inventing market rents.

Last updated November 5, 2026. Facts below come from the City of Whitehall and its Codified Ordinances, the City of Columbus, Whitehall City Schools, COTA, CMHA, the Ohio Revised Code, the U.S. Census Bureau, and Wikipedia’s summary of the city, all cited in Sources. Asking-rent medians are not invented here; see Rent Pulse and Fair Market Rents for verified figures.

A separate city, not a Columbus neighborhood

Whitehall is its own city in Franklin County, not part of Columbus. Residents filed for incorporation as a village in 1947, the borders expanded east toward Big Walnut Creek in 1952, and rapid postwar growth made it a city during the 1950s. The 2020 census counted 20,127 residents, with 8,479 housing units and a rental vacancy rate of 5.1%. Casto’s Town and Country, often called the first shopping center strip in the country, opened here in 1948 along the National Road corridor.

Like Bexley next door, Whitehall is an enclave wrapped by Columbus, with Yearling Road as its civic spine and John Glenn Columbus International Airport and the Defense Supply Center Columbus nearby. The city runs under a mayor–council form of government with its own police and fire divisions at City Hall on South Yearling Road. Because Columbus surrounds the city, a “Whitehall” or 43213 mailing address doesn’t prove an address is inside the city, so check the parcel before applying any of the city rules below. Columbus-only programs, such as the Columbus rental registry and Renter’s Choice, don’t apply inside Whitehall.

Income tax: 2.5%, city-administered, with a full credit for your work city

Whitehall’s municipal income tax is 2.5% on gross wages, salaries, compensation, and the net profits of businesses and rentals. Unlike Reynoldsburg, Gahanna, or Grove City, which use the Regional Income Tax Agency, Whitehall is not a RITA member. The City Auditor’s Income Tax Division at 360 South Yearling Road administers returns, withholding, and net-profit filings itself.

Residents receive a 100% credit, capped at 2.5%, for municipal tax withheld or paid to the city where they work. In practice, a resident who works in Columbus and has 2.5% withheld there usually owes Whitehall nothing more, while someone working in a lower-tax city or for an employer that withholds nothing may owe the difference. The city says every resident age 18 or older who earned income in the prior year must file, regardless of where the income was earned, and owning rental property in Whitehall counts as doing business there. Questions go to the Income Tax Division at 614-338-3100 or incometax@whitehall-oh.us; the 2025 tax-year return is due April 15, 2026, and unpaid balances carry interest the city publishes each year (9% per annum for liabilities incurred on or after January 1, 2026).

Rental registration: tenant lists for the tax office, plus the county filing

Whitehall does not run a housing-code rental license or periodic inspection registry of the kind Reynoldsburg and Gahanna require. What it does require is a tax-side tenant list. Codified Ordinances 184.21 says each owner of one or more rental units in the city must submit to the Tax Commissioner, twice a year on or before January 20 and July 20, a list of tenants then occupying the units and of units that are vacant, including each tenant’s name, address, and telephone number if available. The city’s tax-forms page also posts a Landlord Rental Property Registration Form and a Tenant List form for that filing. New residents must register with the Tax Commissioner within 60 days of moving in.

The state requirement still applies on top of that. Ohio Revised Code 5323.02 requires owners of residential rental property to register it with the county auditor, which for Whitehall is the Franklin County Auditor. Owners must update that information within 60 days of any change, and the auditor says each noncompliant property can be fined $150 per tax year. Out-of-state owners must name an Ohio contact.

Day-to-day upkeep is handled by Code Enforcement for issues such as high grass, junk, peeling paint, and deteriorated structures, and by the Building Department for permits, occupancy, and habitability questions in rental buildings. The city has adopted the International Property Maintenance Code. Tenants with repair problems a landlord won’t fix can call City Hall at 614-338-3100.

Fair housing: Chapter 512 includes source of income

Whitehall’s Chapter 512, Unlawful Discrimination, was updated by Ordinance 061-2025 (passed August 19, 2025). It bans discrimination in housing, employment, and public accommodations based on protected-class status, and it separately defines and protects source of income. Section 512.04 makes it unlawful to refuse to rent, serve a notice of termination, start an eviction on grounds not authorized by Ohio law, charge different deposits or fees, or misrepresent availability because of a tenant’s source of income—including Housing Choice Vouchers and other government or nonprofit rental assistance. Landlords may still require every applicant, at the applicant’s expense, to complete the same background screening, rental-history and credit review, security deposit, and market rent.

Complaints go to the Whitehall Clerk of Council within 180 days of the most recent alleged act. The City Attorney investigates; if probable cause is found, the Mayor appoints a hearing officer who can order cease-and-desist relief and civil penalties. Charges that also fall under state or federal protected classes are directed to the Ohio Civil Rights Commission. That source-of-income rule is a clear difference from suburbs such as Grove City, whose fair housing chapter does not list source of income, and it aligns Whitehall with Reynoldsburg on voucher acceptance.

Short stays and lodging tax

We didn’t find a dedicated short-term rental permit chapter in Whitehall’s code, unlike Reynoldsburg’s new permit that starts in 2027. Lodging is taxed under Chapter 182. It defines a hotel as five or more rooms and a transient accommodation as four or fewer rooms, and it levies a 6% excise tax on lodging furnished to transient guests—people staying fewer than 30 consecutive days. The vendor collects the tax from the guest and files a Hotel/Motel Tax Monthly Return with the City Auditor. Of the revenue, 25% goes to a Tourist Promotion Account and the rest to an Economic Improvement Account.

A whole-home Airbnb-style listing can fit the transient-accommodation definition, so plan to collect the 6% tax if you host stays under 30 days. Still get a written answer on the parcel’s zoning and occupancy rules from the Building Department at 614-338-3100 before listing, and check the lease, since subletting for short stays without the owner’s consent is a separate problem from the city’s tax rules.

Leases and deposits: Ohio law applies

The core lease rules come from Ohio’s landlord-tenant law, Ohio Revised Code Chapter 5321. It sets landlord duties to keep the unit fit and habitable and make repairs, tenant duties to keep the unit safe and sanitary, the rule that a landlord must return a deposit with an itemized list of deductions within 30 days after the tenancy ends, and the 24-hour notice expected before a landlord enters, except in emergencies. Our Ohio landlord-tenant law guide covers these rules in more detail. For help, Franklin County tenants can contact the Franklin County Municipal Court Self Help Resource Center at 614-645-7381 or the Legal Aid Society of Columbus at 614-241-2001.

Utilities: Columbus water and sewer, Local Waste Services trash

Water and sewer. Whitehall buys water and sewer service from the City of Columbus under long-term contract agreements; Columbus lists Whitehall among its water and sewer contract communities. On top of Columbus’s base rates, Whitehall Codified Ordinances 913.30 add meter-size surcharges for both sewer and water that Columbus Utilities bills with the account. Tenants should ask before signing whether the water account stays in the owner’s name.

Trash, recycling, and yard waste. The city contracts with Local Waste Services for weekly curbside collection of garbage, recycling, yard waste, and small brush for single-family homes. Residents pay Local Waste Services directly; the hauler bills quarterly. The city’s published 2026 cart schedule lists quarterly totals of $65.40 for a 35-gallon cart, $68.40 for a 65-gallon cart, and $71.40 for a 95-gallon cart, including a quarterly admin fee that covers cart maintenance. Heads of household 60 or older can get a 10% senior discount by calling Local Waste Services at 614-409-9375. Apartments and condominiums use a private hauler of the owner’s choice, so ask the property manager. Cart repairs go to the City Service Department at 614-338-3100, prompt 4; start/stop service and missed-pickup reports go to Local Waste.

Getting around: COTA Lines 2, 7, and 10

Line 10 (E Broad/W Broad). Line 10 is a standard COTA line, which COTA defines as buses 16 to 60 minutes apart. It runs the length of Broad Street from the Westwoods Park and Ride through Downtown Columbus, Bexley, and Whitehall toward Mount Carmel East Hospital, with continuing trips toward Reynoldsburg and Limited Brands. COTA’s Line 10 map marks a Whitehall Park and Ride and lists Whitehall among local destinations.

Line 2 (East Main/North High). Line 2 is a frequent line on parts of its route and serves East Main Street on Whitehall’s south side, including the Hamilton Road corridor, with service continuing downtown and up North High Street. Stops such as East Main and South Hamilton Road sit in Whitehall.

Line 7 (Mount Vernon). Line 7 reaches the city’s west side near Yearling Road and James Road, linking toward Fifth Avenue and the airport corridor. Check the current timetable for the stops closest to a specific address.

The fare is $2, with a $4.50 daily cap and a $62 monthly cap when paying digitally. I-70 and I-270 sit at the city’s edges for drivers. For bigger-picture transit plans, see Mobility & the Future.

Schools and daily life

Whitehall is served by Whitehall City Schools, a PK–12 district with five schools and a district office at 625 South Yearling Road. The district’s own about page says it serves approximately 3,400 students; NCES reports 3,472 students for the 2024–2025 school year, with a student–teacher ratio of about 17 to 1. Campuses include Whitehall-Yearling High School, Rosemore Middle School, and elementary schools such as Beechwood, Etna Road, and Kae Avenue. High school students can take Advanced Placement classes and college courses through Columbus State Community College, and the district is a charter member of the Eastland-Fairfield Career & Technical Centers. District lines track the city more closely than in many suburbs, but still confirm the assignment for the exact address.

Daily life centers on Yearling Road City Hall, Broad Street and Main Street retail, Norton Crossing’s mixed-use rebuild of mid-century apartments, the Whitehall Community Park YMCA, and employers such as Designer Brands / DSW near the airport, Rite Rug, and Wasserstrom. Big Walnut Creek forms part of the eastern edge.

What landlords and tenants should check before signing

  • City or Columbus. Confirm the address is inside Whitehall and not an adjoining Columbus parcel with a 43213 mailing address.
  • Tenant list and county registration. Owners: file the January 20 and July 20 tenant lists with the Tax Commissioner, and keep the Franklin County Auditor rental registration current.
  • Income tax. Residents 18 and older with earned income, and owners of Whitehall rentals, may need to file with the city’s Income Tax Division (not RITA).
  • Vouchers. Source of income is protected under Chapter 512; apply the same written screening criteria to every applicant.
  • Short stays. Get a written zoning answer before listing, and plan to collect the city’s 6% lodging tax on stays under 30 days.
  • Utility accounts. Water and sewer come from Columbus (with Whitehall surcharges) and trash from Local Waste Services, so settle in the lease who holds each bill.
  • Transit. Line 10 on Broad and Line 2 on Main are the main all-week corridors; Line 7 covers the Yearling/James edge.

Frequently asked questions

Is Whitehall part of Columbus?

No. Whitehall is a separate city in Franklin County about six miles east of Downtown Columbus, incorporated as a village in 1947 and grown into a city in the 1950s, with a 2020 census population of 20,127. It has its own charter, ordinances, income tax division, police and fire, so Columbus-only rules such as Renter’s Choice and the Columbus rental registry do not apply inside Whitehall. Columbus surrounds the city, so confirm the jurisdiction for the exact address.

What is the Whitehall income tax rate?

Whitehall’s municipal income tax is 2.5% on gross wages, salaries, compensation, and net profits of businesses and rentals. Unlike many suburbs that use RITA, Whitehall administers the tax itself through the City Auditor’s Income Tax Division. Residents get a 100% credit, capped at 2.5%, for tax withheld or paid to the city where they work, so someone working in Columbus (also 2.5%) typically owes Whitehall nothing more. Residents age 18 and older who earned income must file.

Does Whitehall have a rental registry?

Whitehall does not run a housing-code rental license or inspection registry like Reynoldsburg or Gahanna. Under Codified Ordinances 184.21, owners of rental property must submit a list of current tenants and vacant units to the Tax Commissioner twice a year, by January 20 and July 20. Ohio Revised Code 5323.02 still requires owners to register residential rentals with the Franklin County Auditor and update that filing within 60 days of a change.

Can Whitehall landlords refuse housing vouchers?

No. Whitehall’s Chapter 512 fair housing ordinance, updated by Ordinance 061-2025, makes it unlawful to refuse to rent, set different terms, or deny housing based on a tenant’s source of income, including Housing Choice Vouchers and other government or private rental assistance. Landlords may still apply the same written screening criteria—background checks, rental history, credit, and security deposits—to every applicant, and may still charge market rent.

Can I run an Airbnb in Whitehall?

We did not find a dedicated short-term rental permit chapter in Whitehall’s code. Chapter 182 does levy a 6% lodging tax on hotels with five or more rooms and on transient accommodations with four or fewer rooms when guests stay fewer than 30 consecutive days, so a whole-home short stay can fall under that tax. Confirm the parcel’s zoning with the Building Department at 614-338-3100 before listing, file the Hotel/Motel Tax Monthly Return if the tax applies, and get the owner’s written consent if you rent.

Who handles trash and water in Whitehall?

Local Waste Services collects trash, recycling, and yard waste for single-family homes under a city contract and bills customers quarterly; apartments and condos use a private hauler chosen by the owner. As of the city’s published 2026 cart rates, a typical quarterly bill ranges from $65.40 for a 35-gallon cart to $71.40 for a 95-gallon cart, with a 10% senior discount for heads of household 60 or older. Water and sewer come from the City of Columbus, with Whitehall adding meter-size surcharges that Columbus bills.

Which COTA buses serve Whitehall?

COTA Line 10 (E Broad/W Broad), a standard line, runs on East Broad Street through Whitehall toward Mount Carmel East Hospital and Reynoldsburg, with a Whitehall Park and Ride on the map. Line 2 (East Main/North High) serves East Main Street, including the Hamilton Road area on Whitehall’s south side. Line 7 (Mount Vernon) reaches stops near Yearling Road and James Road on the city’s west side.

What is the voucher payment standard in Whitehall?

For ZIP 43213, CMHA’s FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents at 100%, effective October 1, 2026, are $1,140 for a studio, $1,220 for one bedroom, $1,450 for two, $1,730 for three, and $1,970 for four bedrooms. These are payment-standard benchmarks for voucher holders, not asking rents. Because 43213 also covers parts of Columbus outside the city, the figures apply to the whole ZIP, not just Whitehall.
