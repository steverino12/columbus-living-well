Reynoldsburg: a renting and living guide to the city on Columbus’s east side

What landlords and tenants should know about Reynoldsburg, the separate city along East Main Street and I-70, including its 2.5% income tax, the city’s rental registry, source-of-income and Pay to Stay protections, the short-term rental permit that starts January 1, 2027, the city utility bill, Reynoldsburg City Schools, COTA bus service, and voucher figures for ZIP 43068, without inventing market rents.

Last updated November 3, 2026. Facts below come from the City of Reynoldsburg and its Codified Ordinances, the Regional Income Tax Agency, Reynoldsburg City Schools, COTA, CMHA, the Ohio Revised Code, local news coverage, and Wikipedia’s summary of the city, all cited in Sources. Asking-rent medians are not invented here; see Rent Pulse and Fair Market Rents for verified figures.

Where Reynoldsburg is, and why the city line matters

Reynoldsburg is a suburb east of Columbus that sits in three counties: Franklin, Licking, and Fairfield. Its population was 41,076 at the 2020 census, which made it the 30th most populous city in Ohio, and the Census Bureau estimated 43,350 residents in 2025. The census counted 15,439 households and 16,014 housing units, with a rental vacancy rate of 4.7%. The city covers about 11.4 square miles, and Blacklick Creek runs through it. Its ZIP codes are 43068 and 43069.

Like Gahanna, Westerville, Hilliard, and Bexley, Reynoldsburg is its own city, not a Columbus neighborhood. Reynoldsburg’s ordinances, income tax, police, zoning, and rental registry apply inside its limits, and Columbus-only rules such as Renter’s Choice deposit installments and the new Columbus rental registry do not. Columbus borders the city, and a Reynoldsburg mailing address doesn’t by itself mean a property is inside city limits, so confirm the jurisdiction before you sign a lease, register a rental, or file taxes. Because the city spans three counties, the county matters too: it decides which county auditor handles the state rental registration and which school district lines and housing-authority tables apply.

A short history

Reynoldsburg was first settled in 1802 by James and Margaret Crawford. It was platted in 1831 as Frenchtown, after its surveyor, John French, and later renamed for James C. Reynolds, a local merchant who helped found the village. A post office called Reynoldsburgh opened in 1833, and the spelling changed to Reynoldsburg in 1893. The village was incorporated on March 16, 1839, and became a city in 1960. Growth came fast after that: the census counted 724 residents in 1950 and 7,793 in 1960.

The city calls itself the Birthplace of the Tomato, based on its claim that the first commercial tomato variety was bred there in the 19th century, and it has held a Tomato Festival every year since 1965. The Ohio Department of Agriculture, the Ohio Fire Academy, and the Office of the State Fire Marshal are based in Reynoldsburg.

Income tax: 2.5%, full credit, and residents file

Reynoldsburg’s municipal income tax is 2.5%, and the Regional Income Tax Agency (RITA), which administers it, lists that rate from January 1, 2020 to the present. It applies to residents’ wages and other compensation, to nonresidents’ income earned in the city, and to business net profits. Residents get a full credit, up to 2.5%, for tax withheld or paid to the city where they work. So a resident who works in Columbus (also 2.5%) typically owes Reynoldsburg nothing more, while someone working in a 2.0% city owes Reynoldsburg the remaining 0.5%. RITA also notes that residents may take credit for county income tax paid on the same wages.

The city says all residents must file an annual Reynoldsburg return unless an exemption applies. Returns can be filed online with RITA, and the closest RITA office is in Worthington. The city’s Tax Department is at 614-322-6865.

Rental registration: register before you lease

Reynoldsburg has required rental registration since 2021. City Council created Chapter 1397 of the Codified Ordinances through Ordinance 28-2021, passed March 22, 2021, and the chapter says no one may lease, rent, or allow occupancy of a residential rental unit without first registering it. The application, on a city form, must list the owner (or a corporate officer or representative for an entity), a company or designated agent located in Ohio, the address of each rental unit, the number of detached structures and units per structure, and the number of parking spaces used for the dwelling. Post office boxes aren’t accepted as addresses, and registrations are public records.

Owners must file a new application when any required information changes. When a property is sold or transferred, the previous owner must give the city the new owner’s name and contact information so the new owner can register, and newly built or converted rental units need their own registration. When council created the registry, it set a one-time fee of $25 per dwelling unit, with multifamily buildings charged for the first five units in each structure and nothing for additional units in the same building. Unlike Gahanna’s biennial program, the ordinance sections we reviewed don’t describe a routine inspection tied to registration. The city’s posted application-forms page doesn’t list the rental form, so ask the city for the current form and fee before filing.

Tenant protections: source of income and Pay to Stay

Source of income. On the same night it created the registry, City Council voted 6-1 to adopt Ordinance 27-2021, adding lawful source of income to the city’s fair housing protections. That covers Housing Choice Vouchers, disability payments, veterans’ benefits, and other government or private sources of payment, so a landlord can’t refuse an applicant or treat a tenant differently because of how the rent is paid. Landlords can still use screening criteria such as rental history and credit, applied the same way to everyone, and can still charge security deposits.

Pay to Stay. Ordinance 26-2022 created Section 701.07 of the Codified Ordinances, known as Pay to Stay. The city’s tenant-rights summary explains that a tenant can pay the overdue rent and reasonable late fees to avoid an eviction filing. If an eviction case has already been filed but there’s no judgment yet, the tenant can still pay the overdue rent, late fees, and reasonable costs, and the landlord must then dismiss the case. If the landlord refuses a full payment before judgment, the tenant can raise that refusal as a defense. Late fees and costs only count if the lease provides for them, and landlords must state the exact amounts. Payments from third parties such as churches, nonprofits, or government agencies are also covered. For help, Franklin County tenants can contact the Franklin County Municipal Court Self Help Resource Center at 614-645-7381 or the Legal Aid Society of Columbus at 614-241-2001.

Short-term rentals: a city permit starting January 1, 2027

On July 13, 2026, Reynoldsburg City Council unanimously passed Ordinance 46-2026, which creates Chapter 705 of the Codified Ordinances to regulate short-term rentals such as Airbnb and Vrbo. City staff had counted roughly 40 short-term rental units operating in the city at a May 2026 work session, and council built in a six-month transition so staff could find and notify hosts before enforcement begins on January 1, 2027.

According to WOSU and Pique’s coverage of the ordinance, hosts will need an annual city permit costing $225, and the property must be owned by the host or be the host’s primary residence. Bookings must be at least three nights, occupancy is limited to two guests per bedroom, and parking can’t exceed the garage’s capacity plus two vehicles in the driveway and two on the street. The city can suspend or revoke a permit for noise violations, ignoring guest rules, delinquent city taxes, or guests entering neighboring private property within 500 feet uninvited. Tenants should also check their lease, since subletting for short stays without the owner’s consent is a separate problem from the city permit.

Leases and deposits: Ohio law applies

Outside the city protections above, the core lease rules come from Ohio’s landlord-tenant law, Ohio Revised Code Chapter 5321. It sets landlord duties to keep the unit fit and habitable and make repairs, tenant duties to keep the unit safe and sanitary, the rule that a landlord must return a deposit with an itemized list of deductions within 30 days after the tenancy ends, and the 24-hour notice expected before a landlord enters, except in emergencies. Our Ohio landlord-tenant law guide covers these rules in more detail.

The city registry is in addition to the state requirement. Ohio Revised Code 5323.02 requires owners of residential rental property to register it with the county auditor. For the Franklin County part of Reynoldsburg, that’s the Franklin County Auditor; properties in the Licking or Fairfield County parts file with that county’s auditor.

Utilities and trash: one city bill, billed quarterly

Reynoldsburg’s Water/Wastewater Department bills water, sewer, stormwater, and refuse collection on one city bill. The city buys water and sewer service from the City of Columbus but maintains its own system, which includes about 145.1 miles of water lines and 143.8 miles of sanitary sewer lines. Bills run on quarterly cycles, with due dates on the 21st of the billing month, and the city’s 2026 rate table lists a $62.91 quarterly residential refuse charge. Residents 60 and older can get a discounted refuse rate by showing ID at the Water Department in the Municipal Building, 7232 East Main Street, and the department’s number is 614-322-6811. A $50 reconnection fee applies if water is shut off for nonpayment. Tenants should ask before signing whether the account stays in the owner’s name or moves to them.

Trash, recycling, yard waste, and bulk items are collected by Local Waste Services under a city contract, on Wednesday or Thursday depending on the address. Bags or cans can’t exceed 50 pounds, recycling goes in the city’s blue cart, and yard waste goes in yard-waste bags or labeled containers, not plastic bags. Bulk pickups are scheduled with Local Waste Services at 614-409-9375 by 3 p.m. the day before trash day, and mattresses and upholstered items must be fully wrapped in plastic. In October 2026, City Council took up a proposed five-year contract with Local Waste Services for 2027 through 2031; a council agenda packet put the cost at $24.79 per household per month. Watch the city for any rate change that follows.

Getting around: COTA Lines 1, 25, and 51

Line 1 (Kenny/Livingston). Line 1 is one of COTA’s frequent lines. Its eastern end is the Reynoldsburg Park and Ride at 2100 Birchview Drive, and it connects the city with East Livingston Avenue, Downtown Columbus, and the Kenny Road corridor.

Line 25 (Brice). Line 25 is a standard crosstown line between the Easton Transit Center and the Canal Winchester Park and Ride, with Mount Carmel East Hospital, the Gahanna Park and Ride, and the Reynoldsburg Park and Ride among its listed destinations.

Line 51 (Reynoldsburg). Line 51 is a rush-hour line, running weekday mornings and afternoons between the Reynoldsburg Park and Ride and Downtown Columbus.

The fare is $2, with a $4.50 daily cap and a $62 monthly cap when paying digitally. Away from these corridors, Reynoldsburg is car-oriented, with I-70 and I-270 close by. For bigger-picture transit plans, see Mobility & the Future.

Schools, safety, and daily life

Most of Reynoldsburg is in Reynoldsburg City Schools, whose latest district Quality Profile lists 6,710 students. The district operates seven elementary schools (Herbert Mills, Rosehill, Slate Ridge, Taylor Road, Summit Road, French Run, and Waggoner Road), two junior high schools (Baldwin Road and Waggoner Road), and Reynoldsburg High School, which has two campuses, Livingston and Summit. Because the city spans three counties and district lines don’t match city lines exactly, confirm the assigned district and school for any address.

The Reynoldsburg Division of Police is next to City Hall. The city’s parks department manages about 275 acres of parkland, and Blacklick Woods, the first Franklin County Metro Park, opened in Reynoldsburg in 1948. It covers 643 acres with a golf course, trails, a nature center, and the Walter A. Tucker State Nature Preserve.

What landlords and tenants should check before signing

  • City, and county. Confirm the address is inside Reynoldsburg, and note which of the three counties it’s in.
  • Registry. Owners: register each unit before leasing it, name an Ohio-based agent, and update the registration after any change or sale. Tenants: it’s fair to ask whether the unit is registered.
  • County registration too. Owners: keep the county auditor registration current as well.
  • Vouchers. Source of income is protected, so apply the same screening criteria to every applicant.
  • Late rent. Pay to Stay lets a tenant cure overdue rent and lease-based fees before an eviction judgment, so state fee amounts clearly in the lease.
  • Short stays. From January 1, 2027, short-term rentals need a $225 annual permit, a three-night minimum, and owner or primary-residence status.
  • Utility account. Water, sewer, stormwater, and trash are one quarterly city bill, so settle in the lease who holds it.
  • Transit. Lines 1 and 25 run all week, but Line 51 is rush-hour only, so check how you’ll commute.

Frequently asked questions

Is Reynoldsburg part of Columbus?

No. Reynoldsburg is a separate city that sits in Franklin, Licking, and Fairfield counties, with a 2020 census population of 41,076. It has its own ordinances, income tax, police division, and rental registry, so Columbus-only rules such as Renter’s Choice and the Columbus rental registry do not apply inside Reynoldsburg. Columbus borders the city, so confirm the jurisdiction for the exact address.

What is the Reynoldsburg income tax rate?

Reynoldsburg’s municipal income tax is 2.5%, the rate RITA has listed since January 1, 2020. Residents get a 100% credit, up to 2.5%, for tax withheld or paid to the city where they work, so someone working in Columbus (2.5%) typically owes Reynoldsburg nothing more. All residents must file an annual return unless an exemption applies, and RITA administers the tax.

Do Reynoldsburg landlords have to register rental units?

Yes. Chapter 1397 of the Codified Ordinances, created by Ordinance 28-2021 in March 2021, says no one may lease or allow occupancy of a residential rental unit without first registering it with the city. The application lists the owner, an Ohio-based designated agent, each unit address, the number of structures and units, and parking spaces, and it must be updated when information changes or the property is sold. When council created the registry, it set a one-time fee of $25 per unit, capped at the first five units in each multifamily building.

Can Reynoldsburg landlords refuse housing vouchers?

No. Ordinance 27-2021 added lawful source of income, including Housing Choice Vouchers and other government or private assistance, to Reynoldsburg’s fair housing protections. Landlords can still apply screening criteria such as rental history and credit consistently to every applicant, and can still charge security deposits.

What is Reynoldsburg’s Pay to Stay rule?

Ordinance 26-2022 created Section 701.07, known as Pay to Stay. It lets a residential tenant pay overdue rent, plus reasonable late fees and costs the lease provides for, before an eviction judgment. If the tenant pays, the landlord must dismiss the eviction, and if the landlord refuses a full payment, the tenant can raise that refusal as a defense. Payments from third parties such as charities or agencies are also covered.

Can I run an Airbnb in Reynoldsburg?

Starting January 1, 2027, short-term rentals in Reynoldsburg need an annual city permit under Chapter 705, created by Ordinance 46-2026, which City Council passed unanimously on July 13, 2026. Reporting on the ordinance describes a $225 yearly permit, a three-night minimum stay, a limit of two guests per bedroom, and parking limited to garage capacity plus two vehicles in the driveway and two on the street. Permits can be suspended or revoked for noise, guest-rule violations, or delinquent city taxes.

Which COTA buses serve Reynoldsburg?

COTA Line 1 (Kenny/Livingston), a frequent line, and Line 51, a weekday rush-hour line to Downtown Columbus, both serve the Reynoldsburg Park and Ride at 2100 Birchview Drive. Line 25 (Brice), a standard crosstown line, links Easton Transit Center, Mount Carmel East Hospital, the Reynoldsburg area, and the Canal Winchester Park and Ride.

What is the voucher payment standard in Reynoldsburg?

For ZIP 43068, CMHA’s FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents at 100%, effective October 1, 2026, are $1,320 for a studio, $1,410 for one bedroom, $1,680 for two, $2,000 for three, and $2,280 for four bedrooms. CMHA’s table covers Franklin County, so check with the housing authority for units in the Licking or Fairfield County parts of the city. These are payment-standard benchmarks for voucher holders, not asking rents.
