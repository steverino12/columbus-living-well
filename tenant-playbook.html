Tenant Playbook

Find the right home. Become the applicant they choose.

Part I: Search smart

Choose a neighborhood first

Decide what matters most: walkability (Short North, German Village), schools and suburban space (Dublin), commute, and parking. See our Neighborhood Spotlights.

Time your move

Fall and winter are softer seasons in Columbus; current trackers show asking rents dipping. Concessions such as free weeks or waived fees are more common in new buildings now.

Touring checklist

  • Water pressure, hot water, and HVAC operation
  • Signs of leaks, pests, or moisture
  • Cell signal and internet options
  • Noise at different times of day
  • Laundry, storage, and package handling
  • Parking availability and security

Vet the management

Read recent online reviews for patterns in maintenance response and deposit returns. Ask current residents when you can.

Read the lease

Check the term, renewal and increase terms, early-termination fees, maintenance responsibilities, and the rules on guests, pets, and subletting.

Count hidden costs

Utilities (and who pays which), parking, pet rent and fees, amenity or trash fees, renters insurance, and move-in deposits. If you hold a Housing Choice Voucher, confirm the ZIP payment standard and utility allowance before you apply—see Fair Market Rents.

Spot scams

  • The rent is far below comparable listings
  • The "owner" can't show the unit in person
  • You're asked to wire money, pay in gift cards, or pay before seeing the unit or signing a lease
  • Verify ownership through the Franklin County Auditor's public records

Part II: Market yourself

Build an income proof package

Recent pay stubs, an offer letter, and bank statements in one tidy PDF. If you're self-employed, include two years of tax returns.

Line up references

Past landlords plus a professional reference, with permission and current contact details.

Prepare your credit

Pull your free reports at AnnualCreditReport.com, dispute errors early, and be ready to explain any blemish briefly and honestly.

Write a renter resume and cover note

One page: who you are, your move-in date, how long you plan to stay, your income, your rental history, and any pets. A short, courteous note sets you apart.

Be responsive

Answer within hours, have documents ready, and be ready to pay the application fee and sign promptly once you've decided.

What landlords look for

  • Stable, verifiable income
  • On-time payment history
  • Positive references
  • Complete, honest applications
  • Respectful communication
