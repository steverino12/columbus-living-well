Short North Arts District: a Columbus renting and living guide

What landlords and tenants should know about Columbus’s Short North Arts District—location on High Street, housing stock, walkability and transit, Gallery Hop and nearby landmarks, parking realities, and ZIP-level voucher notes—without inventing market rents.

Last updated October 16, 2026. Neighborhood facts below come from the Short North Alliance, City of Columbus Recreation and Parks, COTA, North Market, National Register documentation, and CMHA/HUD tables cited in Sources. Asking-rent medians for this corridor are not invented here; see Rent Pulse and Fair Market Rents for verified figures.

Where the Short North sits

The Short North Arts District is the commercial stretch of North High Street just north of downtown Columbus. National Register multiple-property documentation for the corridor describes it as the section of High Street between Goodale Street on the south and King Avenue on the north. Interstate 670 runs parallel with and under Goodale and marks a sharp edge between downtown and the Short North. To the north, King Avenue has traditionally divided the Short North from the University District along High Street; The Ohio State University area bounds the corridor in that direction.

Immediately west sits Victorian Village (part of the Near North Side National Register Historic District). Immediately east sits Italian Village, a local historic residential district. That sandwich of late-19th and early-20th-century neighborhoods around a streetcar- and automobile-era commercial strip is what gives the Short North its mix of loft-style apartments, renovated storefronts, and older houses a short walk off High.

This is an in-city neighborhood, not a suburb. Day-to-day life is oriented to High Street: galleries, restaurants, boutiques, bars, and public art within walking distance of most addresses on or near the corridor. For a broader map of Columbus neighborhood spotlights, start at our Neighborhoods hub, or compare with our Clintonville guide, our German Village guide and our Dublin guide.

Housing stock: apartments, rowhouses, and the villages next door

On and near High Street, the rental inventory is dominated by mid-rise and high-rise apartments—newer mixed-use buildings layered onto a historic commercial strip—plus smaller units above storefronts. A block or two off the corridor, Victorian Village and Italian Village add historic single-family homes, doubles, and rowhouse-style stock on narrower streets. Landlords marketing “Short North living” should be precise about whether the unit faces High Street nightlife or sits in a quieter residential pocket, because noise, parking, and guest traffic differ sharply over a few hundred feet.

Historic-district context matters for renovations and exterior work. The commercial corridor’s National Register documentation emphasizes streetcar-era and automotive-era development patterns; adjoining Victorian and Italian Village areas have their own preservation expectations. Tenants shopping older buildings should ask about insulation, windows, HVAC, and parking assignments; landlords should price and screen with that product type in mind rather than treating every Short North address as interchangeable.

We do not publish made-up asking rents for Short North on this page. Private-market medians move with season and inventory; use Rent Pulse for tracker context and comps from active listings. For Housing Choice Voucher math, use ZIP-level SAFMR payment standards—not a neighborhood nickname.

Walkability, COTA transit, and the Short North C-pass

The Short North Alliance describes the district as one of COTA’s most-served corridors: many of the Central Ohio Transit Authority’s 40-plus metro routes pass through or stop in the Short North. That density of bus service, plus a walkable High Street core, is a real amenity for tenants who want to reduce car dependence—and a leasing talking point landlords can support with route maps rather than vague “walkable” claims.

Eligible Short North Arts District workers and residents may qualify for the Short North C-pass, a partnership between COTA and the Short North Alliance (with MORPC also involved in the broader C-pass program). COTA states that qualifying Short North workers and residents can receive unlimited access to COTA fixed-route service at no cost to the employer, employee, or residence. Signup and eligibility details live on ShortNorth.org/cpass and COTA’s C-pass page; requirements include living or working in the eligible Short North area shown on the program map. Short North C-pass customers use the Transit app with a COTA account.

COTA also notes AirConnect service from John Glenn Columbus International Airport to downtown; from downtown it is a short bus or rideshare connection into the Short North. For landlords, transit access can widen the applicant pool. For tenants, confirming evening and weekend frequency on the routes you actually need matters more than a corridor-wide slogan.

Landmarks next door: Gallery Hop, North Market, Convention Center, Goodale Park

Gallery Hop is the district’s signature monthly event. According to the Short North Alliance, it has run since 1984 on the first Saturday of every month, free, typically 4–8 p.m. along High Street, with gallery exhibitions, street performances, and related business activity. Ohio Magazine has called it the best arts event in the state. For residents, Hop nights mean heavier foot traffic, louder evenings, and scarcer parking; for landlords, they are part of the lifestyle pitch and part of the noise conversation at lease signing.

Greater Columbus Convention Center sits at 400 N. High Street at the south end of the corridor. The Short North Alliance lists it among district businesses and notes its location across from the Arena District and Short North dining and entertainment. Event calendars at the Convention Center affect hotel demand, rideshare volume, and evening congestion on nearby blocks.

North Market downtown is at 59 Spruce Street—steps from the Convention Center and a short walk from the south end of the Short North. North Market’s history traces to 1876; the current downtown building opened in 1995. The market reports serving over one million guests per year and remains a grocery, prepared-food, and weekend destination for nearby renters.

Goodale Park, managed by Columbus Recreation and Parks, is a regional park of about 32.56 acres in Victorian Village, bordering the south/west edge of the Short North area. Dr. Lincoln Goodale donated land in 1851 for a public park; the Short North Alliance calls it the oldest park in Columbus and notes playgrounds, tennis, a shelter, and a pond-side gazebo. It is the soft-green counterweight to High Street denseness for many Victorian Village and Short North residents.

North of King Avenue, High Street continues into the University District. The Short North is not on campus, but the OSU area is a short trip up High for students, staff, and game-day visitors—another source of weekend demand that landlords and tenants feel in traffic and noise even when the unit itself is south of King.

Parking: metered streets, garages, and lease reality

Parking is one of the most practical Short North lease issues. On-street spaces are managed by the City of Columbus. The Short North Alliance’s parking guide points visitors to the ParkColumbus app or kiosks, notes progressive pricing by zone and time of day, and states that Sundays and city-recognized holidays are free. Time limits and overnight rules vary by zone; residents should not assume a visitor spot will be available late on a Gallery Hop Saturday.

Off-street options include numerous garages and surface lots listed by the Alliance (examples include Hubbard Garage, The Short North Garage, Joseph Garage, Goodale Garage, Vine Street Garage, and others). Some participating businesses offer garage validation—Alliance materials describe up to two hours of free parking at certain garages when entry is between 7:00 a.m. and 7:00 p.m., subject to current program rules. Event nights near the Convention Center and Arena District can push garage rates and fill lots quickly.

For landlords: spell out in the lease whether a space is assigned, first-come, or none; whether guest parking is available; and how street permits or HOA rules apply if any. For tenants: treat “parking included” as a must-verify line item, and budget for app-metered street parking or a garage contract if the unit does not include a space. Ambiguity here creates more move-in disputes than almost any other Short North amenity claim.

Vouchers, ZIPs, and what the SAFMR tables say

Much of the Short North corridor and its immediate edges fall in ZIP codes 43215 and 43201 (the Short North Alliance’s administrative office lists 875 N. High St., Columbus, OH 43215; its safety office lists an address in 43201). For Housing Choice Vouchers administered with Small Area Fair Market Rents, those ZIP codes are not interchangeable.

CMHA’s FY2027 SAFMR payment standards effective October 1, 2026—published at 100% of SAFMR—include a two-bedroom standard of $2,350 for ZIP 43215 and $1,690 for ZIP 43201. Those figures come from CMHA’s published PDF and are summarized on our Fair Market Rents explainer; they are payment-standard benchmarks, not asking rents and not automatic rent approvals. Contract rent still faces rent-reasonableness review, and utility allowances affect the gross-rent comparison.

Inside Columbus city limits, Columbus City Code 4551.03 restricts refusing to rent based on lawful source of income, including vouchers. That local rule is explained in our Ohio and Columbus landlord-tenant law guide. Practically: a voucher household shopping the Short North must match bedroom size and ZIP on the current CMHA table before spending application fees, and landlords who accept vouchers should pull the ZIP from the address—not from the neighborhood brand.

What landlords and tenants should check before signing

  • Noise and hours. Gallery Hop (first Saturday monthly), Convention Center events, and High Street nightlife are features for some renters and deal-breakers for others. Put quiet-hours expectations in writing.
  • Parking. Assigned space or not? Guest options? Street meter rules for your block?
  • Exact product. High Street mid-rise vs. Victorian Village house vs. Italian Village double—different maintenance, utilities, and guest patterns.
  • Transit use case. Confirm COTA routes and, if eligible, Short North C-pass signup steps.
  • ZIP and voucher math. Use CMHA’s current SAFMR schedule for the unit’s ZIP; see Fair Market Rents before an RFTA.
  • City rules. Security deposits, entry notice, late-fee caps, and source-of-income rules inside Columbus are covered in the landlord-tenant law page.
  • Application package. Pair this neighborhood read with our Applications guide and Tenant Playbook.

The Short North rewards people who want density, arts, and transit. It frustrates people who need easy free parking and quiet every night. Clear leases and accurate ZIP-level pricing keep both sides out of avoidable conflict.

Frequently asked questions

Where is the Short North Arts District in Columbus?

It is the North High Street corridor between Goodale Street on the south and King Avenue on the north, just north of downtown and south of the Ohio State University area, with Victorian Village to the west and Italian Village to the east.

What is Gallery Hop?

A free Short North tradition since 1984, held on the first Saturday of every month from 4–8 p.m. along High Street, with gallery exhibitions, street performances, and related events. Details: ShortNorth.org/gallery-hop.

Which ZIP codes matter for Short North vouchers?

Confirm the unit’s address ZIP. Corridor and edge addresses commonly fall in 43215 or 43201. CMHA’s FY2027 two-bedroom SAFMR payment standards effective October 1, 2026, are $2,350 (43215) and $1,690 (43201) at 100% of SAFMR—verify on CMHA’s current PDF before lease-up.

Is parking difficult in the Short North?

On-street parking is City-managed and metered (ParkColumbus or kiosks), free on Sundays and city holidays, with rules that vary by zone. Garages and lots are plentiful but fill on event nights; some businesses offer limited garage validation. Never assume parking is included with a lease unless it is written down.

Can Short North residents or workers ride COTA for free?

Eligible Short North Arts District workers and residents may qualify for the Short North C-pass (COTA + Short North Alliance), which provides unlimited fixed-route COTA access at no cost to the employer, employee, or residence. Check eligibility maps and signup on ShortNorth.org/cpass.

Where do I find rent and law context for this neighborhood?

Use Rent Pulse for private-market tracker context, Fair Market Rents for CMHA/HUD payment standards, and Ohio landlord-tenant law basics for ORC 5321 and Columbus City Code rules. This page is a neighborhood guide, not a rent quote sheet.
