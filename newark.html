Newark: a renting and living guide to the Licking County seat east of Columbus

What landlords and tenants should know about the City of Newark, the Licking County seat at the forks of the Licking River, including its 1.75% city income tax and the 1% cap on the credit for tax paid where you work, the 1.00% Newark City Schools income tax on a traditional base that reaches pensions and investment income, the October 1 tenant listing landlords file with the tax office, why Ohio’s county-auditor rental filing does not reach Licking County, the rooming house license, the Direct Billing Agreement tenants need for a water account, short-term rental zoning and the 3% hotel lodging tax, Licking County Transit’s free bus routes, and HUD voucher figures for ZIP 43055, without inventing market rents.

Last updated November 16, 2026. Facts below come from the City of Newark, its codified ordinances, zoning code, and income tax ordinance, the Ohio Department of Taxation, the Ohio Revised Code, Licking County Transit and Licking County government, the Licking Metropolitan Housing Authority, NCES, HUD, AHACO, The Reporting Project at Denison University, and Wikipedia’s summaries of the city and county, all cited in Sources. Asking-rent medians are not invented here; see Rent Pulse and Fair Market Rents for verified figures.

A county seat at the forks of the Licking River

Newark is a separate city and the county seat of Licking County, about 40 miles east of Columbus where the north and south forks of the Licking River meet. Wikipedia’s summary says the first European-American settlers arrived in 1802, led by Gen. William C. Schenck, who named the town after his hometown of Newark, New Jersey, and that it was incorporated in 1813. The Ohio and Erie Canal broke ground near Newark on July 4, 1825, and railroads reached the town in the 1850s. Much of the Newark Earthworks, the Hopewell culture’s mound complex built roughly 100 to 500 AD, lies in and around the city; the Octagon Earthworks are now part of the Hopewell Ceremonial Earthworks World Heritage Site, and the Great Circle sits just over the line in neighboring Heath.

The 2020 census counted 49,934 residents, making Newark the 18th-largest city in Ohio and, by population, the second-largest Newark in the country. Wikipedia’s summary of the census lists 20,817 households and 22,351 housing units, of which 6.9% were vacant. The homeowner vacancy rate was 1.7% and the rental vacancy rate was 6.3%. The median age was 38.6, and 27.8% of households had children under 18. Wikipedia also cites a median household income of $52,570 and a poverty rate of 16.8%. The city’s own Facts & Statistics page markets Newark on a low cost of living, with median home values under $130,000 and median rents under $800, citing the 2020 census; those are older census-based figures, not current asking rents, so check current listings before relying on them.

Newark has its own job base. Wikipedia lists the main Owens Corning fiberglass factory, Holophane (a lighting manufacturer founded in 1898), and the headquarters of Park National Bank downtown, plus industrial parks with employers such as Kaiser Aluminum, Dow, Boeing, and Anomatic. The former Longaberger headquarters, built in 1997 in the shape of a giant basket, is still a landmark. The Ohio State University at Newark, founded in 1957, shares its campus with Central Ohio Technical College, and Denison University is in nearby Granville, so student and staff housing is part of the rental market.

Because Newark is a separate city, Columbus-only rules such as the Columbus rental registry, Renter’s Choice, and Columbus source-of-income protections do not apply. Wikipedia lists four ZIP codes for the city (43055, 43056, 43058, and 43093), and they also reach into Heath and surrounding townships. Confirm that a parcel is inside Newark city limits before relying on anything below.

Income tax: 1.75%, with only a 1% credit

The City of Newark income tax page lists a 1.75% rate. Newark administers the tax itself rather than through RITA; the Income Tax Office moved on May 9, 2024 to the second floor of 15 N. 3rd Street downtown (740-670-7580), and staff will prepare a Newark return free of charge by appointment or on a walk-in basis. The city’s Chapter 890 income tax ordinance shows the rate history: 1% from July 1, 1967, 1.25% from April 1, 1975, and 1.75% since January 1, 2002.

The credit is where Newark differs from many suburbs we cover. Section 890.081 gives residents a credit of one percent of the income taxed by another municipality, and says the credit may not exceed one percent of that income. So a resident who works in Columbus and pays 2.5% there still owes Newark the difference between 1.75% and the 1% credit. On $60,000 in Columbus wages, that is $1,500 to Columbus, a Newark tax of $1,050, a $600 credit, and $450 more owed to Newark. The city’s employer withholding instructions say an employer of a Newark resident who pays tax to another city may choose to withhold that 0.75% for Newark; if the employer does not, the resident owes it with the annual return. A resident who works in a township with no municipal tax owes the full 1.75%.

Newark has mandatory registration and mandatory filing. Section 890.21 requires every resident to register with the Income Tax Department within 30 days of establishing a home in the city, and the tax page says every resident 18 or older must file an annual Newark return by April 15 regardless of income: Form EX for residents with no taxable income, Form SF for W-2 income only, and Form LF for everyone else. Anyone whose tax due after credits is $200 or more must make quarterly estimated payments. Section 890.99 sets fines for violations of the chapter starting at $250 for a first offense and rising to $1,000 for a fourth. Net rental income is also subject to the city tax.

The landlord tenant listing, due October 1

Newark asks landlords for something most suburbs do not. Under Section 890.22, Landlord Reporting, all owners of rental property, including houses, apartments, rooms, boarding houses, and other rental accommodations, must file an annual listing with the Tax Administrator of every tenant who is not responsible for their own municipal water bill. The listing must include each tenant’s name, address, telephone number, dates of occupancy, and forwarding address if available, and it is due on or before October 1 each year. In practice, a landlord who includes water in the rent, or keeps the water account in the owner’s name, should plan on filing this list each year; the ordinance ties the requirement to who pays the water bill.

School district income tax: 1.00% on a traditional base

Newark City Schools adds a school district income tax. The Ohio Department of Taxation’s school district income tax table lists Newark CSD (district number 4507) at 1.00% on a traditional base, continuing with no scheduled end. The state’s guide to the school district income tax explains that the traditional base starts from Ohio adjusted gross income, less personal exemptions, so it reaches interest, dividends, capital gains, and pensions, not just wages; Ohio deducts Social Security benefits. That matters for retired tenants, who pay the school tax on pension income even though they owe no city tax on it. On $60,000 in wages alone, the tax is roughly $600 before exemptions. It is filed with the state on Form SD 100, it gets no credit for tax paid elsewhere, and the city’s return instructions note it appears as SD or 4507 on a W-2.

Check the school district on the Licking County Auditor’s record for the exact parcel, because neighboring districts in the same state table differ: Heath City, Lakewood Local, and Licking Heights Local at 0.00%; Northridge Local at 0.50% on earned income; Granville Exempted Village at 0.75% (traditional, scheduled through 2028); Southwest Licking Local at 0.75% (traditional); North Fork Local at 1.00% on earned income; and Licking Valley Local at 1.00% (traditional).

NCES lists Newark City Schools (district ID 3904445) as a PK–12 district with 13 schools, 5,789 students, and 345.89 classroom teachers (FTE) in the 2024–2025 school year, a student–teacher ratio of 16.74. Its reported total revenue was $108.1 million in fiscal 2022–2023, about $18,294 per student, with 18% from federal sources, 40% from the state, and about 42% from local sources. Wikipedia notes that Newark High School enrolls about 1,400 students.

Rental registration: none found, but rooming houses need a license

We did not find a City of Newark rental registration, rental license, or proactive inspection program for ordinary long-term rentals. Ohio’s statewide rental filing does not apply either. Ohio Revised Code 5323.01 limits “residential rental property” for the county-auditor registration in Section 5323.02 to property in a county with more than 200,000 residents at the most recent census, and Licking County had 178,519 in 2020, according to Wikipedia’s county summary, with a 2025 estimate of 185,564.

Rooming houses are the exception. Newark’s Chapter 1446 Rooming House Code defines a rooming house as a residential building, other than a hotel, where sleeping accommodations are offered for hire for three or more people. Operating one requires an annual license from the Office of Code Administration, issued only for a dwelling that conforms to the zoning code and displayed in a conspicuous place. The fee is at least $50 for up to five rental rooms plus $5 for each additional room, and it doubles if the operator applies while already running without a license or with an expired one. If an inspection finds deficiencies and a reinspection shows they were not fixed, the license is denied and the fee is kept. The city’s Property Maintenance page posts a Rooming House Registration Form. Landlords renting rooms by the bedroom to three or more unrelated tenants should ask whether the house counts.

All rentals are subject to code enforcement. The Division of Property Maintenance, part of the Department of Public Safety at 40 W. Main Street, 4th floor (740-670-7585, propertymaintenance@newarkohio.net), enforces the city’s Property Maintenance Code, adopted as Ordinance 07-03 on January 17, 2007 and amended June 17, 2008, which is based on the International Property Maintenance Code with local changes. Ordinance 16-03 sets standard charges when the city has to abate a violation itself: $200 per mowing of grass or weeds, $250 per load of accumulated rubbish or garbage, and $50 for the first two tires plus $15 for each one after that. It also limits a property to four tires stored outside. Those costs, or a contractor’s actual invoice, become a lien sent to the county auditor for the next property tax bill. We did not find a vacant building registration chapter in Newark’s codified ordinances.

Older rentals downtown may also fall under Chapter 1450, the Historic Preservation Code (Ordinance 13-36-A, passed November 4, 2013), which requires a certificate of appropriateness for new construction, demolition, or exterior alteration of a designated landmark or property in a historic preservation district. Building permits within the city are handled by the Licking County Building Code Department (740-349-6671), according to the city’s Engineering & Zoning page.

Fair housing: a city program, but no local voucher protection found

Newark runs a Fair Housing and Landlord/Tenant Services program with federal Community Development Block Grant money from HUD. Its mission statement commits to housing choice regardless of race, religious creed, color, sexual orientation, national origin, sex, ancestry, familial status, veteran status, or physical or mental impairment. The program’s consultant is Southeastern Ohio Legal Services (740-670-7532, newark@oslsa.org), and the page posts guides on Ohio landlord and tenant rights, fair housing advertising, and how tenants should request repairs. Newark is not on AHACO’s list of ten central Ohio communities that have adopted source-of-income protections (Bexley, Columbus, Gahanna, Grandview Heights, Pickerington, Reynoldsburg, Upper Arlington, Westerville, Whitehall, and Worthington), and we did not find a local ordinance requiring landlords to accept housing vouchers. Federal and Ohio fair housing laws still apply, so write down income and credit standards and apply them the same way to every applicant.

Water, sewer, and stormwater: tenants need the owner’s Direct Billing Agreement

The city’s Division of Water and Wastewater serves more than 47,000 customers through over 18,300 connections, with Customer Service at 34 S. Fifth Street (740-670-7940). Its frequently asked questions explain how rentals work. A tenant can put water service in their own name only by bringing a Direct Billing Agreement completed by the owner or managing agent, with the tenant’s name on it, plus a photo ID. An activation fee and a deposit may be required; call Customer Service to find out whether a deposit applies. Even with the account in the tenant’s name, the owner still receives any disconnection notices. On the form, the owner appoints the tenant as an agent to receive the bills, agrees to the owner responsibilities in the division’s rules and regulations, and must return the form within 30 days. The FAQ also invites owners to ask the water office about a prospective tenant’s payment history.

A few other points from the FAQ matter for leases. A minimum charge applies whenever water service is on at the curb, even if nothing is used. The property owner is responsible for the service line from the curb box valve to the meter, while the city maintains the meter. The city does not shut off water on Fridays or the business day before a holiday, and it recommends a sewer backup rider on homeowners or renters insurance.

The water page’s public notices list these 2026 rates:

The sewer increase comes from Ordinance 23-02. Because the notice describes the charges only briefly, we have not built a sample bill; ask Customer Service for an estimate at a given address. The city is also offering free replacement of galvanized water service lines to customers it has notified, and owners of older rentals should check whether their property qualifies.

Trash is not a city utility as far as we could find. Licking County’s curbside recycling page, current as of October 2025, tells Newark residents to check with their own trash hauler about curbside recycling, which suggests households arrange their own service. Leases should say who hires and pays the hauler. The city’s stormwater page says leaves set out for city pickup must be in biodegradable paper or corn-starch bags, and placing loose leaves or yard waste in the street can bring a citation. We did not confirm the gas and electric providers for Newark addresses.

Short-term rentals: no separate chapter, so zoning controls

We did not find a Newark short-term rental license, registration, or ordinance. The zoning code the city posts (Ordinance 08-33, adopted May 4, 2009) does not define short-term rentals. Instead it defines a bed and breakfast as a dwelling occupied by the owner where lodging is provided for transient persons, and it treats any building with sleeping units for five or more transient guests as a hotel or motel. Under that code:

  • The single-family districts (RS, RL, RM, and RH), the zero-lot-line district (RZLL), the two-family district (TFR), and the agricultural district (AD) list a boarding house or bed and breakfast for up to two lodgers as a permitted use.
  • The conservation district (CD) allows a boarding house or bed and breakfast for up to two lodgers only as a conditional use approved by the board under Article 150.
  • The multi-family district (MFR) permits boarding houses, bed and breakfasts, and rooming houses.
  • The limited intensity business district (LB) permits a bed and breakfast as long as there is no on-street parking.

Read together, an owner-occupied home renting to one or two short-stay guests fits the code most easily, while a whole house rented by a non-resident owner is not clearly addressed. The posted code may have been amended since 2009, so ask the Division of Zoning (740-670-7729) before listing a unit, and remember that renting rooms to three or more people can also trigger the rooming house license. A tenant who wants to sublet on a booking platform needs both zoning clearance and the landlord’s written consent.

Lodging tax: 3% city hotel tax

Newark’s Chapter 892 Hotel Lodging Excise Tax (Ordinance 10-3, passed March 15, 2010) levies 3% on lodging furnished to transient guests, meaning people staying fewer than 30 consecutive days, by a hotel or a “transient accommodation,” collected from July 1, 2010 on to support local athletic and tourism facilities. The chapter defines a hotel as an establishment using five or more rooms for guests and a transient accommodation as one using four or more rooms, whether in one or several structures, and requires a new business to register with the City Treasurer within 30 days. By those definitions, a single home with fewer than four guest rooms may fall outside the city tax, but an operator with several units could be covered; ask the Treasurer before assuming either way. The Ohio Department of Taxation’s 2018 Table S-4, the latest we could open, lists both a 3.00% Licking County lodging tax and a 3.00% Newark rate; we did not confirm how the county tax applies to stays inside the city. Net rental profits are also subject to the 1.75% city income tax.

Leases and deposits: Ohio law applies

We did not find a local landlord-tenant code in Newark, so Ohio Revised Code Chapter 5321 governs security deposits, repairs, entry notice, and eviction notices. Under Section 5321.16, a landlord generally must return the deposit, with an itemized list of any deductions, within 30 days after the lease ends and the tenant gives up possession. Ohio Revised Code 1901.02 establishes the Licking County Municipal Court in Newark with jurisdiction within Licking County, so eviction cases for Newark addresses are filed there. Our Ohio landlord-tenant basics page walks through the statewide rules, and the city’s fair housing page links to its own guides on the same law.

Getting around: free Licking County Transit buses, not COTA

We found no COTA service in Newark. Licking County Transit (LCT), at 745 E. Main Street (740-670-5185), runs two services:

  • Route service. Buses on scheduled loops with designated stops, Monday through Friday from 5 a.m. to 7 p.m., with Routes 1, 2, and 4 also running Saturdays from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. There is no fare. The Reporting Project listed five routes in April 2026: Main St., 21st St., Granville, the Earthworks Circulator, and Buckeye Lake/Hebron, with the busiest lines running hourly. The first fixed route launched in summer 2023, and LCT reported ridership rising from about 62,000 trips in 2022 to about 136,000 in 2025.
  • Demand Response. Scheduled curb-to-curb rides, Monday through Friday from 5 a.m. to 8 p.m. and Saturdays from 6 a.m. to 6 p.m., booked at 740-670-5185 (option 1). The fare is $4 for the general public and $2 for elderly and disabled riders, and riders 60 and older ride free under the county’s Senior Citizens Levy Program. Exact change or an ePass is required.

Expansion is uncertain. LCT asked voters for a 10-year 0.15% countywide sales tax to add routes to Pataskala and Etna and to Johnstown and New Albany, raise the busiest lines to every 30 minutes, and study mobility hubs connecting to COTA. Voters rejected it on May 5, 2026, 15,984 (57.52%) to 11,805 (42.48%), according to preliminary results, and LCT said it would revisit its funding while continuing current service. Wikipedia notes that GoBus’s Columbus–Wooster intercity route serves Newark; we could not confirm a current schedule. LCT is closed on six major holidays and lists no Sunday service, so a tenant without a car should map the commute, especially a Columbus commute, before signing.

Vouchers and what the SAFMR table says

Licking County is part of the Columbus, OH HUD Metro FMR Area, along with Delaware, Fairfield, Franklin, Madison, Morrow, and Pickaway counties, and HUD sets Small Area Fair Market Rents by ZIP code in that area. HUD’s FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents for Newark’s main ZIP 43055 are:

1 bedroom$1,050

3 bedrooms$1,490
4 bedrooms$1,690

The other Newark ZIPs in the same table are higher: a two-bedroom is $1,360 in ZIP 43058 and $1,440 in ZIP 43056, which also covers Heath; ZIP 43093 does not appear. For comparison, the table lists $1,400 for a two-bedroom in ZIP 43023 (Granville), $1,770 in ZIP 43062 (Pataskala), $1,330 in ZIP 43130 (Lancaster), and $1,230 in ZIP 43113 (Circleville). These are payment-standard benchmarks, not asking rents and not automatic approvals; contract rent still goes through rent-reasonableness review, utility allowances affect the gross-rent comparison, and each ZIP includes areas outside city limits.

The Licking Metropolitan Housing Authority (LMHA), at 144 W. Main Street in Newark (740-349-8069), runs the Housing Choice Voucher program for Licking County. Its voucher guide says LMHA bases its payment standards on HUD’s Fair Market Rent, that the payment standard is the maximum monthly subsidy for the smaller of the voucher’s unit size or the unit’s actual bedrooms, and that current standards are in the participant briefing packet. Its owner page explains how landlords get vacant units onto LMHA’s list: first-time owners submit a W-9, direct deposit form, agent authorization form, voided check, and photo ID. LMHA’s 2027 Annual Plan is open for public comment through December 10, 2026. Confirm the current payment standard and utility allowance with the agency that issued the voucher. For how these numbers are built, see our Fair Market Rents explainer.

What landlords and tenants should check before signing

  • Jurisdiction. Confirm the parcel is inside the City of Newark rather than Heath or a surrounding township; taxes, zoning, and utilities differ.
  • Income tax. Budget for 1.75% to Newark with a credit of only 1% for tax paid to a work city; a Columbus commuter typically owes Newark another 0.75% of wages, often at filing time. Register within 30 days of moving in and file every year from age 18.
  • School tax. Add the 1.00% Newark City Schools tax on a traditional base, which includes pension and investment income, filed on Form SD 100.
  • Landlord listing. If the tenant does not pay their own water bill, list them with the Tax Administrator by October 1.
  • Registration. No rental registry or county-auditor filing, but a house rented to three or more lodgers may need a rooming house license.
  • Water. A tenant account requires the owner’s Direct Billing Agreement, may require a deposit and activation fee, and still sends shutoff notices to the owner.
  • Trash. Arrange private trash service and say in the lease who pays.
  • Short stays. Check zoning first, since the posted code fits owner-occupied stays for one or two guests; ask the Treasurer whether the 3% hotel tax applies.
  • Vouchers. No local source-of-income protection found; apply consistent written screening either way.
  • Transit. No COTA; LCT routes are free but run weekdays and limited Saturday hours only.

Frequently asked questions

Is Newark, Ohio part of Columbus?

No. Newark is a separate city and the county seat of Licking County, about 40 miles east of Columbus, with a 2020 census population of 49,934. It has its own council, income tax, utilities, and zoning code, so Columbus-only rules such as Renter’s Choice, the Columbus rental registry, and Columbus source-of-income protections do not apply. Newark’s ZIP codes also reach into Heath and surrounding townships, so confirm the parcel is inside the city.

What is the Newark, Ohio income tax rate?

The City of Newark’s income tax is 1.75%, in effect since January 1, 2002, and administered by the city’s own Income Tax Office rather than RITA. Residents who work elsewhere get a credit of only 1% of the income taxed by the other city, so a resident earning $60,000 in Columbus pays Columbus $1,500 and still owes Newark about $450. Residents must register within 30 days of moving in, and everyone 18 or older must file a Newark return by April 15 regardless of income.

Is there a school district income tax in Newark?

Yes. The Ohio Department of Taxation lists Newark City School District (district number 4507) at 1.00% on a traditional base, which starts from Ohio adjusted gross income and so reaches interest, dividends, and pensions as well as wages. It is filed with the state on Form SD 100 and gets no credit for tax paid elsewhere. NCES reports Newark City Schools had 5,789 students in 13 schools in 2024–2025. Heath City Schools next door is at 0.00%.

Do Newark landlords have to report their tenants?

Yes, in one case. Section 890.22 of Newark’s income tax ordinance requires owners of rental property to file an annual listing with the Tax Administrator of every tenant who is not responsible for their own municipal water bill, with each tenant’s name, address, phone number, dates of occupancy, and forwarding address if available. The listing is due on or before October 1 each year.

Does Newark have a rental registry?

We did not find a City of Newark rental registration, rental license, or proactive inspection program for ordinary long-term rentals. Ohio’s county-auditor rental filing in Revised Code 5323 applies only in counties with more than 200,000 residents, and Licking County had 178,519 at the 2020 census. Newark does license rooming houses, defined as buildings offering sleeping accommodations for hire to three or more people, for at least $50 a year for up to five rooms plus $5 per additional room.

Can tenants put water in their own name in Newark?

Yes, with the owner’s signature. A tenant must bring the Newark Water Office a Direct Billing Agreement completed by the owner or managing agent with the tenant’s name on it, plus photo ID, and an activation fee and deposit may be required. Even then, the owner still receives any disconnection notices. The city’s 2026 notice lists a $31.77 minimum monthly bill for water and sewer and stormwater at $8.05 per ERU per month.

Can I run an Airbnb in Newark, Ohio?

Newark has no separate short-term rental ordinance that we could find, so zoning controls. The zoning code the city posts, adopted in 2009, defines a bed and breakfast as an owner-occupied dwelling providing lodging to transient persons and lists a boarding house or bed and breakfast for up to two lodgers as a permitted use in the single-family and two-family districts. Five or more transient guests make a building a hotel or motel. A whole-house rental by a non-resident owner is not clearly addressed, so ask the Division of Zoning at 740-670-7729 first.

Is there a lodging tax on short-term rentals in Newark?

Newark’s Chapter 892 levies a 3% hotel lodging excise tax on stays of fewer than 30 consecutive days at hotels (five or more guest rooms) and transient accommodations (four or more rooms), in effect since July 1, 2010. A single home with fewer than four guest rooms may fall outside those definitions, so ask the City Treasurer. The state’s 2018 lodging tax table also lists a 3% Licking County rate.

Can Newark landlords refuse housing vouchers?

Newark is not on AHACO’s list of ten central Ohio communities that have adopted source-of-income protections, and we found no local rule requiring landlords to accept vouchers. Federal and Ohio fair housing laws still apply, and the city runs a HUD-funded fair housing program with Southeastern Ohio Legal Services (740-670-7532). The Licking Metropolitan Housing Authority at 144 W. Main Street (740-349-8069) runs the county’s Housing Choice Voucher program.

Does COTA serve Newark, Ohio?

No. We found no COTA service in Newark. Licking County Transit runs free fixed routes Monday through Friday from 5 a.m. to 7 p.m., with three routes on Saturdays from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m., and Demand Response rides for $4 ($2 elderly and disabled, free for 60 and older). Voters rejected a 0.15% sales tax for transit expansion on May 5, 2026, so planned routes toward New Albany and Pataskala are unfunded for now.

What is the voucher payment standard in Newark, Ohio?

Licking County is part of the Columbus, OH HUD Metro FMR Area, where HUD sets Small Area Fair Market Rents by ZIP code. For ZIP 43055, HUD’s FY2027 SAFMRs are $980 for a studio, $1,050 for one bedroom, $1,250 for two, $1,490 for three, and $1,690 for four bedrooms; ZIP 43056 is higher at $1,440 for a two-bedroom. The Licking Metropolitan Housing Authority bases its payment standards on HUD’s Fair Market Rents, so confirm the current figure with the agency that issued the voucher.
