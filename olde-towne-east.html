Olde Towne East: a Columbus renting and living guide

What landlords and tenants should know about Olde Towne East—one of Columbus’s oldest streetcar suburbs on the Near East Side, its large late-19th-century houses and converted apartments, the Bryden Road and East Broad historic districts and the Historic Resources Commission’s exterior review, the Near East Area Commission, COTA service on Main Street, Bryden Road, and Broad Street, Franklin Park next door, and ZIP-level voucher figures—without inventing market rents.

Last updated October 24, 2026. Neighborhood facts below come from the City of Columbus, the Olde Towne East Neighborhood Association, the Near East Area Commission, Columbus Recreation and Parks, COTA timetables, local history sources, and CMHA tables cited in Sources. Asking-rent medians are not invented here; see Rent Pulse and Fair Market Rents for verified figures.

Where Olde Towne East sits

Olde Towne East is a neighborhood inside the City of Columbus on the historic Near East Side, just east of downtown across Interstate 71. Its edges have long been disputed. According to the Olde Towne East Neighborhood Association (OTENA), as summarized by Wikipedia, the neighborhood runs from Interstate 71 on the west to Interstate 70 on the south, with East Long Street on the north and a staggered eastern line around Wilson Avenue, Miller Avenue, and the alleys east of Latta Avenue and Kendall Place. Other maps use East Broad Street as the northern edge, and the Franklin Park Civic Association treats Wilson Avenue as the eastern border. That means a listing labeled “Olde Towne East” may also be described as King-Lincoln Bronzeville or Franklin Park, so check the exact address rather than the label.

Because it is part of Columbus itself, Columbus city services, the Columbus income tax, and Columbus City Code rules for landlords and tenants apply. OTENA describes the neighborhood as a walkable grid within about a 15-minute walk of the Columbus Metropolitan Library, the Columbus Museum of Art, Franklin University, and the Columbus College of Art and Design. For other neighborhood spotlights, start at the Neighborhoods hub, including German Village, Italian Village, Victorian Village, the Short North, Clintonville, and Dublin, plus the separate city of Bexley just east across Alum Creek.

How Olde Towne East got here

OTENA’s history describes Olde Towne East as one of Columbus’s first suburbs, made possible by the city’s first horse-drawn streetcars starting in 1863. The area was family farms and countryside along the National Road (East Main Street) and was annexed into Columbus in 1870. In 1882 trolley tracks were laid on Oak Street to Kelton Avenue, connecting downtown with the former Ohio State Fairgrounds—today’s Franklin Park—and by 1886 large sections had been subdivided into residential lots. Politicians, businessmen, industrialists, and architects built homes here, and the Bryden Road area became known as the “Silk Stocking District.”

The neighborhood changed after the 1920s as wealthier residents moved to newer suburbs such as neighboring Bexley. OTENA says that after World War II many mansions were stripped of fixtures or partitioned into apartments and nursing homes, the landscaped Broad Street median was removed for traffic lanes, and Interstate 71 cut the neighborhood off from downtown. Wikipedia notes that interstate construction and the Model Cities Program cleared parts of the Near East. Revitalization followed the preservation movement of the 1960s and 1970s: OTENA was founded in 1975, and its Tour of Homes began in 1982. Today, new restaurants and businesses cluster along the Parsons Avenue corridor between Bryden Road and Broad Street and around 18th and Oak streets.

What the housing looks like

OTENA counts more than 1,000 homes, some built as early as the 1830s, representing more than 50 architectural styles, including Federal, Italianate, Queen Anne, Gothic Revival, Tudor, Colonial Revival, American Foursquare, and Craftsman. Many are large brick or frame houses with deep front porches, slate or tile roofs, and carriage houses behind. Because so many large houses were divided into apartments in the mid-20th century, a rental here can be a full restored house, one unit in a converted mansion, half of a double, a carriage-house unit, or an apartment in a newer infill building. Ask which of these you are looking at, what is shared, and how utilities are metered. Older buildings can mean older wiring, windows, and plumbing; Ohio’s duty to keep a unit fit and habitable is covered in our landlord-tenant law guide.

Historic districts and the Historic Resources Commission

Unlike German Village or Victorian Village, Olde Towne East does not have its own commission. Its locally designated areas are on the Columbus Register of Historic Properties and are reviewed by the City’s Historic Resources Commission (HRC). The City’s register lists the Bryden Road District (Ordinance 1968-87, listed March 20, 1990), which lies within the Columbus Near East Side district listed on the National Register in 1978; a nomination summary in the Columbus Metropolitan Library’s collection describes it as 324 properties fronting both sides of Bryden Road from Parsons Avenue east to roughly Rhodes Avenue. The register also lists the 18th & E. Broad Historic District and the 21st & E. Broad Historic Group, both added in 1988 along the East Broad Street corridor.

The City says the HRC was established by Ordinance 2638-80 in 1980, has nine mayoral appointees, and issues Certificates of Appropriateness (COAs) for exterior alterations to properties in Columbus Register districts and individually listed properties under City Code chapters 3116 and 3117. COA applications are filed through the City’s Citizens Access Portal. Some routine items—such as certain door replacements, rear privacy fences, and like-for-like sidewalk repair—are on the City’s staff approval list and still need an application but not a hearing. Hearings are generally on the third Thursday of the month at 4 p.m. at 111 N. Front St. The City’s 2026 schedule lists a November 19 hearing (application deadline October 23) and a December 17 hearing (deadline November 20). The October 15, 2026 agenda included several Bryden Road cases, so review is active. Dates can change; confirm with the Historic Preservation Office at HRC@columbus.gov before planning work.

The Near East Area Commission

OTENA notes that Olde Towne East lies within the Near East Area Commission (NEAC), which Columbus City Council established in 1979. Area commissions are advisory: NEAC reviews rezonings, variances, special permits, and demolition and liquor-permit matters and forwards recommendations to the City. Its published process runs a case through a Planning/Zoning Committee meeting (third Thursday, 6:30 p.m.), a Saturday site visit, and a General Body vote on the second Thursday at 6:30 p.m. at 1393 E. Broad St. Landlords planning a conversion, an added unit, or a use change should expect this review alongside any HRC approval.

Getting around: COTA on Main, Bryden, and Broad

Three COTA lines run east–west through or along the neighborhood toward downtown. Line 2 (East Main/North High), which COTA lists as frequent, runs on East Main Street with a timepoint at Main and Ohio Avenue. Line 10 (E. Broad/W. Broad), also frequent, runs on East Broad Street. Line 11 (Bryden/Maize), a standard line, runs on Bryden Road through the middle of the neighborhood. COTA defines frequent service as departures 15 minutes or less apart and standard service as 16–60 minutes apart, generally from 5 a.m. to midnight Monday through Saturday. Check current timetables for the hours you actually travel. On-street parking rules vary block by block, so read the posted signs and ask whether the unit has an alley or off-street space.

Franklin Park and community life

Franklin Park, at 1755 E. Broad St. just east of Olde Towne East, is listed by Columbus Recreation and Parks as a 59.17-acre regional park open 7 a.m. to 11 p.m., with paved paths, a playground, a stocked pond, and the Espy Adaptive Sports Complex. It hosted the Ohio State Fair from 1874 to 1884, and it surrounds the Franklin Park Conservatory and Botanical Gardens, whose Palm House dates to 1895. The department says the park’s Cascades water feature has been closed since June 23, 2026, because of a leak. In the neighborhood, OTENA runs community meetings, the Summer Tour of Homes, Music in the Garden concerts at the community garden on Oak Street, and holiday events. Olde Towne East is in the Columbus City Schools district; confirm the assigned school for the exact address with the district.

Vouchers, ZIPs, and what the SAFMR tables say

Wikipedia lists 43205 as Olde Towne East’s ZIP code and OTENA’s mailing address is in 43205, while some nearby Near East addresses—Franklin Park’s among them—use 43203. CMHA’s FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents, effective October 1, 2026, at 100% of SAFMR, list $1,160 for a one-bedroom and $1,380 for a two-bedroom in 43205, and $1,090 for a one-bedroom and $1,300 for a two-bedroom in 43203. These are payment-standard benchmarks, not asking rents and not automatic approvals; contract rent still goes through CMHA’s rent-reasonableness review, and utility allowances affect the gross-rent comparison. Our Fair Market Rents explainer covers how they work.

Inside Columbus city limits, Columbus City Code 4551.03 restricts refusing to rent based on lawful source of income, including vouchers. That rule and Ohio’s deposit, notice, and repair rules are covered in our Ohio and Columbus landlord-tenant law guide.

What landlords and tenants should check before signing

  • Historic status. Check whether the address is in the Bryden Road District, an East Broad district, or individually listed before planning exterior work.
  • Unit type. Know whether it is a full house, a unit in a converted house, a carriage house, or a newer building, and what is shared.
  • Boundaries. Confirm the address and ZIP rather than relying on the neighborhood label in a listing.
  • Utilities. Ask which utilities are separately metered and who pays for water, trash, and common-area lighting.
  • Parking and transit. Ask about off-street or alley parking, and check which COTA line is nearest.
  • ZIP for vouchers. Pull the ZIP from the address before doing voucher math.

Frequently asked questions

Where is Olde Towne East in Columbus?

Olde Towne East is a neighborhood inside the City of Columbus on the historic Near East Side, just east of downtown across Interstate 71. Its edges are disputed: the Olde Towne East Neighborhood Association describes Interstate 71 on the west and Interstate 70 on the south, with East Long Street on the north and a staggered line around Wilson Avenue and Miller Avenue on the east, while other maps use East Broad Street as the northern edge. King-Lincoln Bronzeville and Franklin Park border it.

Is Olde Towne East a historic district?

Parts of it are. The Bryden Road District was added to the Columbus Register of Historic Properties by Ordinance 1968-87, listed March 20, 1990, and it sits within the Columbus Near East Side district on the National Register. The 18th & E. Broad Historic District and 21st & E. Broad Historic Group were listed on the Columbus Register in 1988. Exterior work in these areas is reviewed by the City’s Historic Resources Commission.

Do I need approval to change the outside of a rental in Olde Towne East?

If the property is inside a Columbus Register district such as the Bryden Road District, or is individually listed, exterior alterations need a Certificate of Appropriateness from the Historic Resources Commission, applied for through the City’s Citizens Access Portal. Some routine items can be approved by staff without a hearing. The City’s 2026 schedule lists a November 19 hearing and a December 17 hearing, with a November 20 application deadline for December.

Who reviews zoning changes in Olde Towne East?

Olde Towne East is within the Near East Area Commission, which Columbus City Council established in 1979. The commission makes advisory recommendations on rezonings, variances, and special permits. Its general body meets on the second Thursday of the month at 6:30 p.m. at 1393 E. Broad St., after Planning and Zoning committee review.

Which COTA lines serve Olde Towne East?

COTA Line 2 (East Main/North High) runs on East Main Street along the southern part of the neighborhood, and Line 10 (E. Broad/W. Broad) runs on East Broad Street to the north; COTA lists both as frequent lines. Line 11 (Bryden/Maize), a standard line, serves Bryden Road through the middle of the neighborhood. Check COTA’s current timetables for the hours you travel.

What is the voucher payment standard in Olde Towne East?

Most Olde Towne East addresses use ZIP 43205, and some Near East addresses nearby use 43203. CMHA’s FY2027 SAFMR figures effective October 1, 2026, at 100% of SAFMR, list $1,160 for a one-bedroom and $1,380 for a two-bedroom in 43205, and $1,090 for a one-bedroom and $1,300 for a two-bedroom in 43203. Always confirm the unit’s ZIP on CMHA’s current table.
