Neighborhood Guide · Dublin, Ohio

Dublin, Ohio: a renting and living guide

What landlords and tenants should know about Dublin—its history, Historic Dublin and Bridge Park, the Architectural Review Board, short-term rental limits, COTA service, school-district boundaries, and ZIP-level voucher notes—without inventing market rents.

Last updated October 18, 2026. Facts below come from the City of Dublin, the Dublin Code of Ordinances, Dublin City Schools, COTA, the U.S. Census Bureau, the Franklin County Auditor, and CMHA tables cited in Sources. Asking-rent medians are not invented here; see Rent Pulse and Fair Market Rents for verified figures.

A separate city, not a Columbus neighborhood

Dublin sits northwest of Columbus along the Scioto River, but it is its own city with its own charter, council, codes, and income tax. That matters more to renters and owners than it might seem: the rules you read about for Columbus do not automatically carry over the city line. According to rating reports the City posts, Dublin operates in parts of three counties—Franklin, Delaware, and Union—and levies a 2% municipal income tax on income earned by people working in Dublin and on the net profits of businesses operating there.

The U.S. Census Bureau counted 49,328 residents in Dublin in the 2020 Census, up from 41,751 in 2010. Census QuickFacts estimates an owner-occupied housing rate of 77.1% and a median gross rent of $1,686 for 2020–2024 (American Community Survey five-year estimates). That median gross rent is a survey of what households reported paying, including utilities, across all unit ages; it is not a current asking rent for a vacant apartment.

How Dublin got here

The City of Dublin’s history page says the Delaware, Shawnee, and Wyandot peoples were the area’s first inhabitants, and that today’s Historic Dublin includes about 800 acres given to Lieutenant James Holt in the Virginia Military District for Revolutionary War service. Ludwig Sells and his sons chose the site around 1803, on what the City describes as the highest point of land along the Scioto. John Sells platted a village in 1810 and asked the surveyor, John Shields, to name it; Shields chose Dublin after his home in Ireland. Despite the name, the City notes that very few Irish families were among the early settlers.

For most of its history Dublin was a small farming and quarry town. The City credits three 1970s decisions with putting it on a growth path: Ashland Chemical Company locating a building there, an I-270 outerbelt ramp to Dublin, and the development of the Muirfield Village Golf Club and residential community. With rapid business and residential growth, Dublin officially became a city in August 1987. The City notes that many of Historic Dublin’s original buildings are listed on the National Register of Historic Places, and dry-laid limestone fences still line some of its roads.

Historic Dublin, Bridge Park, and the Dublin Link

Dublin’s walkable core straddles the Scioto. On the west bank is the Historic District, the site of the Sells family’s original purchase. On the east bank is Bridge Park, a newer mixed-use neighborhood. The two are joined by the Dublin Link, a pedestrian bridge the City describes as 760 feet long and 14 feet wide, with a 169-foot tower whose “keyhole” the deck passes through—the longest single-tower S-shaped suspension bridge in the world, according to the City. Construction began in 2017; the Link opened quietly on March 17, 2020, and was officially dedicated on March 11, 2022.

For renters, this core is where Dublin feels least suburban: apartments and condos near restaurants and plazas, with the river trail and the Link a short walk away. Elsewhere, much of Dublin’s housing is single-family homes in planned subdivisions. Either way, ask how parking works—garage, surface lot, or street—before you sign, because it varies building to building.

Exterior changes in the Historic District

Owners of rentals in Historic Dublin should plan around the Architectural Review Board (ARB). Per the City’s ARB application page, the Board reviews changes to any site or structure in the Historic District, plus outlying historic properties listed in Appendix G of the Zoning Code. That covers building, altering, demolishing, or changing the exterior of a structure—windows, doors, siding, roofing, exterior paint colors—and site work such as fences, patios, parking, commercial landscaping, and signs. The Board typically meets on the fourth Wednesday of each month at 6:30 p.m. Qualified smaller jobs can go through Minor Project Review; demolition of a building classified as contributing requires the owner to show economic hardship.

In practice, that means a roof, window, or paint job on a Historic District rental needs lead time. Landlords should tell tenants early when exterior work is coming, and tenants should ask whether any approvals are pending before they sign.

Short-term rentals: a two-week cap

Dublin regulates short-term rentals tightly. Under Dublin Code Chapter 122, anyone operating a short-term rental (under 30 consecutive days) must register with the Planning Director each calendar year, for a $225 application fee. Section 122.02 caps rentals to transient guests at two weeks total per calendar year, in up to two stays of up to seven days each. If an owner wants to rent longer than that, it must be to the same tenant on a lease of at least 30 consecutive days. Registration also limits occupancy to two people per bedroom and caps guest vehicles. For investors, the takeaway is simple: Dublin is a long-term-lease market, not a nightly-rental one.

Getting around: COTA changes this fall

Transit got a notable upgrade in September 2026. The City of Dublin announced that beginning September 7, 2026, COTA extended Line 1 Kenny north from Carriage Place to the Ohio State Wexner Medical Center Outpatient Care Dublin, creating a direct ride to Ohio State’s campus, the Short North, and Downtown Columbus without a transfer. Buses run every 30 minutes, seven days a week. The same day, the Dublin Park and Ride moved to the corner of Dale Drive and Bridge Park Avenue; riders, including those taking COTA’s Zoo Bus, can park free in the Theodore Garage on Mooney Street.

Even so, most of Dublin is built around the car, and commutes along I-270 and US-33 are part of daily life for many households. If a tenant relies on transit, map the walk from the unit to the nearest stop before committing.

Schools: the district is not the city

Schools are a big reason households look at Dublin, and boundaries are where mistakes happen. Dublin City Schools says it serves about 42 square miles across Franklin, Delaware, and Union counties, and that the district is not geographically the same as the City of Dublin. It serves families with Dublin, Columbus, Plain City, Powell, Shawnee Hills, Upper Arlington, and Hilliard mailing addresses; the district reports that 46.2% of its students live in the City of Dublin and 33.3% in the City of Columbus. A home’s city, mailing address, or ZIP code does not determine its school district.

The district also completed redistricting in June 2026, so attendance boundaries change starting with the 2027–2028 school year. A tenant signing a lease that runs into fall 2027 should check both years in the district’s official School Locator Tool rather than relying on a listing site, and landlords should avoid promising a specific school in an ad.

Vouchers, ZIPs, and county lines

Most Dublin addresses use ZIP 43016 or 43017. For the Franklin County portion, CMHA’s FY2027 SAFMR payment standards effective October 1, 2026, published at 100% of SAFMR, list:

  • 43016: $1,700 (studio), $1,820 (one bedroom), $2,180 (two bedrooms), $2,600 (three bedrooms), $2,950 (four bedrooms).
  • 43017: $1,540 (studio), $1,650 (one bedroom), $1,970 (two bedrooms), $2,350 (three bedrooms), $2,670 (four bedrooms).

These are payment-standard benchmarks, not asking rents and not automatic approvals; contract rent still faces CMHA’s rent-reasonableness review, and the standard includes an allowance for tenant-paid utilities. CMHA’s table covers Franklin County only. Dublin addresses in Delaware County fall under the Delaware Metropolitan Housing Authority, which says its voucher waitlist has been closed since September 30, 2024. Which rules apply to a Dublin lease

Ohio’s landlord-tenant statute, ORC Chapter 5321, applies statewide, so security-deposit, entry-notice, and repair rules work the same way in Dublin as in Columbus; our Ohio landlord-tenant law guide covers them. City of Columbus ordinances, such as the source-of-income protection in Columbus City Code 4551.03, apply only inside Columbus city limits. Dublin has its own Code of Ordinances, posted on American Legal Publishing, and federal and state fair-housing laws apply everywhere.

One more state rule matters across county lines: ORC 5323.02 requires owners of residential rental property to register with the auditor of the county where the property is located. Because Dublin spans three counties, a Dublin landlord may be filing with the Franklin, Delaware, or Union County auditor depending on the parcel. The Franklin County Auditor says filings are due within 60 days after notice following a transfer, or after any change in the information, with a fine of $150 per property per tax year for failing to comply.

Dublin Irish Festival and other big weekends

The Dublin Irish Festival fills Coffman Park on the first weekend in August; the festival says that weekend has been reserved for it since 1988, and the City’s history describes it growing from a small party on the Coffman Park tennis courts to more than 110,000 visitors. The 2026 festival ran July 31 to August 2, and the festival lists August 6–8, 2027 next. Muirfield Village also hosts the Memorial Tournament, which the City’s history ties to Jack Nicklaus. Tenants near Coffman Park or Muirfield should expect heavier traffic those weekends.

What landlords and tenants should check before signing

  • City limits. Is the parcel actually inside the City of Dublin? Check the county auditor’s record, not just the mailing address.
  • County. Franklin, Delaware, or Union? It decides rental registration and which housing authority handles a voucher.
  • Schools. Verify the assignment in the Dublin City Schools locator for both 2026–27 and 2027–28.
  • Historic District. Any pending ARB approvals or planned exterior work?
  • Lease length. Short-term subletting is capped at two weeks a year; leases of 30 days or more are the norm.
  • Commute. Map the route to the nearest COTA stop or the Dale Drive Park and Ride if you rely on transit.

Frequently asked questions

Is Dublin, Ohio part of Columbus?

No. Dublin is its own city northwest of Columbus. According to the City of Dublin, it was platted as a village in 1810 and officially became a city in August 1987. The city operates in parts of Franklin, Delaware, and Union counties, and its 2020 Census population was 49,328.

Do City of Columbus rental rules apply in Dublin?

Not automatically. Columbus City Code provisions, such as the source-of-income rule in CC 4551.03, apply to property inside Columbus city limits. Dublin has its own Code of Ordinances. Ohio's landlord-tenant law (ORC Chapter 5321) and state and federal fair-housing laws apply in both. Some homes with a Dublin mailing address are outside Dublin city limits, so check the parcel's taxing jurisdiction on the county auditor's site.

Can I rent my Dublin home on Airbnb?

Only within tight limits. Dublin Code Chapter 122 requires annual short-term rental registration with the Planning Director (a $225 application fee) and caps rentals to transient guests at two weeks total per calendar year, in up to two stays of up to seven days each. Renting for longer than that must be to the same tenant on a lease of at least 30 consecutive days.

Do I need approval to change the outside of a rental in Historic Dublin?

Yes. Dublin's Architectural Review Board reviews exterior changes to any site or structure in the Historic District, including windows, doors, siding, roofing, and exterior paint colors, plus fences, patios, parking, and signs. The Board typically meets on the fourth Wednesday of each month at 6:30 p.m. Qualified smaller projects can use Minor Project Review.

Which COTA service reaches Dublin?

Since September 7, 2026, COTA's Line 1 Kenny runs north to Ohio State Outpatient Care Dublin, giving a direct ride to OSU, the Short North, and Downtown every 30 minutes, seven days a week. The same day, the Dublin Park and Ride moved to Dale Drive and Bridge Park Avenue, with free parking in the Theodore Garage on Mooney Street.

What is the voucher payment standard in Dublin?

For the Franklin County part of Dublin, CMHA's FY2027 SAFMR payment standards effective October 1, 2026 (at 100% of SAFMR) list a two-bedroom standard of $2,180 in ZIP 43016 and $1,970 in ZIP 43017. CMHA's table covers Franklin County only; Delaware County addresses are served by the Delaware Metropolitan Housing Authority.
