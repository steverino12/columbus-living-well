Bexley: a renting and living guide

What landlords and tenants should know about Bexley—a small, separate city on Alum Creek east of downtown Columbus with its own income tax, school district, and design review—including its owner-occupied short-term rental rules, how code enforcement and parking work, COTA’s frequent Main Street and Broad Street buses, the November 2026 school ballot issue, and voucher figures for ZIP 43209, without inventing market rents.

Last updated October 27, 2026. Facts below come from the City of Bexley and its codified ordinances, the Bexley Historical Society, Bexley City Schools, the Regional Income Tax Agency, the Ohio Department of Taxation, COTA, the U.S. Census Bureau (via Wikipedia’s summary of the 2020 count), and CMHA tables cited in Sources. Asking-rent medians are not invented here; see Rent Pulse and Fair Market Rents for verified figures.

A separate city, not a Columbus neighborhood

Bexley is its own municipality in Franklin County, an enclave surrounded by Columbus on the east side, on the banks of Alum Creek east of the Near East Side, with the Franklin Park Conservatory adjoining it to the west. It is compact: about 2.44 square miles, home to 13,928 people at the 2020 Census. Its ZIP code is 43209, and Capital University and Trinity Lutheran Seminary are based in the city, as is the Ohio Governor’s Residence. Bexley runs a mayor–council government from City Hall at 2242 E. Main Street.

That independence matters for a rental. Columbus City Code does not apply in Bexley, so Columbus-only rules such as the source-of-income protection in Columbus City Code 4551.03 do not reach Bexley properties; Bexley’s own codified ordinances and Ohio’s statewide landlord-tenant law in Ohio Revised Code Chapter 5321 do. Because ZIP areas and city lines don’t always match, confirm the taxing city and school district for a parcel with the Franklin County Auditor before you sign a lease or quote a rule.

The 2020 Census counted 4,942 housing units in Bexley, 4.8% of them vacant. Of occupied homes, 76.6% were owner-occupied and 23.4% renter-occupied, and the rental vacancy rate was 6.4%. In other words, Bexley is mostly an owner-occupied, single-family town, and rentals are a smaller slice of the stock than in in-town neighborhoods.

How Bexley grew

According to the Bexley Historical Society, the city began as two separate communities. North of Town Street (now Bryden Road), an exclusive residential area led by R.H. Jeffrey had set rigid building restrictions; to the south, residents near Capital University formed the Pleasant Ridge Improvement Association to build roads and cinder sidewalks and install the area’s first gas street lamps. Neither had enough people to incorporate alone, and neither wanted to be absorbed by Columbus. Early in the summer of 1908, 144 representatives of the two communities met on the terrace of Jeffrey’s mansion and agreed to unite. Colonel Lincoln Kilbourne proposed the name Bexley, after a parish near his family’s home in Kent, England, and the first village council met that August in the university’s Recitation Hall.

The Historical Society notes that a Philadelphia developer laid out the Bullitt Park Addition plat east of Alum Creek in the early 1890s, setting the lots for the early mansions on Parkview, Columbia, and Drexel avenues. Wikipedia’s summary adds that in 1898 Camp Bushnell, an assembly site for Spanish–American War volunteers around Broad and Drexel, brought water and sewer lines that eased later development. The 1920 Census counted just over 1,300 residents; by 1930 the count was 7,396, and Bexley became a city, which Wikipedia dates to January 1, 1932. The Historical Society’s review of mid-1920s directories shows streets such as Sheridan, College, Euclaire, Cassingham, and Montrose south of Main Street already lined with houses, and storefronts and gas stations replacing open fields along East Main by the late 1920s.

Short-term rentals: owner-occupied only, with a permit

Bexley allows short-term rentals, but only on tight terms set by Section 1266.19 of the codified ordinances (Ordinance 11-20, passed June 9, 2020). A short-term rental is any dwelling rented wholly or partly for less than 30 consecutive days. The key rules:

  • Principal residence. The owner must keep the dwelling as their principal residence, so investor-owned, whole-home Airbnbs don’t qualify.
  • 60-day cap. The home may be rented short-term no more than a combined 60 days in a 12-month period.
  • Registration and inspection. Hosts register annually with the City for $75, after the Code Enforcement Officer inspects for fire-code and life-safety compliance, including smoke and carbon monoxide detectors on every level and in every sleeping room. The City notifies adjacent properties when a registration is approved.
  • Listings and limits. The permit number must appear on every listing; occupancy is capped at two people per bedroom, gatherings at 15 people, and overnight parking at one registered vehicle per available off-street space.
  • Insurance and tax. Hosts must carry at least $300,000 of liability insurance, keep records for four years, and pay Bexley’s lodging tax under Chapter 882.

A first violation is an unclassified misdemeanor with a fine of up to $250; later convictions are third-degree misdemeanors with fines up to $500 or up to 60 days in jail. Denials or revocations can be appealed to the Board of Zoning and Planning within ten business days. Leases of 30 days or more are ordinary residential tenancies. Compare Upper Arlington, which bans stays under 30 days outright, and Grandview Heights, which also limits STRs to owner-occupied or on-site-managed homes.

Bexley also allows accessory dwelling units (ADUs) in single-family districts under Section 1266.24 (Ordinance 15-25, passed September 9, 2025): one ADU per lot, no more than one bedroom and two permanent occupants, an owner living in either the main house or the ADU, registration for $100 renewed every two years, and a cap of 25 new ADU registrations a year.

Design review, zoning, and code enforcement

Bexley reviews exterior design more broadly than most suburbs. The Architectural Review Board (ARB) hears applications for exterior architectural changes to residential and commercial properties and meets the second Thursday of each month at 6:00 p.m.; the City publishes Residential Design Guidelines and Main Street Guidelines for applicants. The Board of Zoning and Planning (BZAP) decides variance requests, approves conditional uses, and advises City Council on planning and rezoning; it meets the fourth Thursday of each month at 6:00 p.m. Meetings are held in person at City Hall and streamed on the City’s YouTube channel. Under Section 1226.081, a certificate of appropriateness is valid for one year, with a possible one-year extension if work hasn’t started. Landlords planning new windows, porches, siding, or additions should budget time for review before scheduling contractors.

Bexley does not appear to run a Columbus-style long-term rental license; the registrations in its zoning code that we found apply to short-term rentals and ADUs. Its Code Enforcement Department instead audits the city for violations of the property maintenance code and responds to complaints. In most cases the owner gets two violation notices with time to correct the problem, and under certain conditions the Building and Zoning Department issues a citation into Mayor’s Court. The City lists common problems such as peeling paint, broken fences, deteriorated roofs or windows, junk and clutter, and indoor furniture used outdoors. Property owners must clear the sidewalks next to their property after snow, and address numbers must be at least four inches tall. Because notices go to the owner, leases should say who handles snow, yard work, and trash.

Parking rules renters should know

Bexley limits how long cars may sit on the street. Under Section 452.13, a vehicle may not stay parked on a street for more than 24 hours, or 72 hours if it is registered to an owner with a current Bexley address—one more reason for tenants to update their vehicle registration after moving in. Parking in an alley is limited to 30 minutes of loading or unloading, and recreational vehicles and boats may not sit on a street for more than three hours in any 24-hour period without a permit. On streets the City has designated for residential permit parking, the City’s rules say permits are free from the Bexley Police Department at 559 N. Cassingham Road, require proof of residency such as a lease or rent receipt, are limited to three per household, and must be renewed each January. Code Enforcement also notes that cars may not be parked on grass and that vehicles advertised for sale may not be parked on the street.

Getting around: COTA Lines 2 and 10

Two of COTA’s frequent lines—which COTA defines as buses 15 minutes or less apart—cross Bexley. Line 2 (East Main/North High) runs on East Main Street through the center of the city, with timetable timepoints at East Main and Drexel Avenue and at East Main and James Road, and continues downtown and up North High Street. Line 10 (E Broad/W Broad) runs on East Broad Street—the dividing line between North and Central Bexley—toward Whitehall to the east and through downtown to the west. Standard fares are $2, with free two-hour transfers and a $4.50 daily cap when you pay digitally. Check current timetables for the hours you travel; COTA adjusts service several times a year.

Schools and the Issue 5 ballot question

Bexley City Schools is a separate district from Columbus City Schools. Its buildings are Bexley High School, Bexley Middle School, and Cassingham, Maryland, and Montrose elementary schools, plus an online school and preschool. The district says its boundaries lie fully within the City of Bexley and that, by Board policy, only students residing in Bexley may enroll. For landlords this has a practical consequence: enrollment requires a lease or proof of ownership with contact information for verification, and the district says all rental agreements must list the new students as well as the parent or guardian. Kindergarten is all-day, and children must turn 5 by the first day of classes.

On the November 3, 2026 ballot, the district has placed Issue 5 to fund Phase I of its facilities plan, at an estimated total project cost of $115 million. The district says Phase I would be funded by a new 0.5% traditional school district income tax and a 2.05-mill property tax, which it calls a no-new-tax-rate request because existing bond payments totaling 2.05 mills expire in 2027. Ohio law excludes Social Security and military benefits from school district income tax. Because tenants who live in the district pay school district income tax directly, and property taxes often reach tenants through rent, both sides may want to follow the result. This guide takes no position on the issue.

Parks and community life

Jeffrey Park, behind the Jeffrey Mansion, covers nearly 40 acres including the two Commonwealth Parks, with woodland walking trails, a boardwalk along Alum Creek, tennis courts, shelter houses, a playground, the seasonal Bexley Community Pool, and the Bexley Historical Society. The City says the Jeffrey family left the mansion and grounds to Bexley in 1941, and the Recreation and Parks Department has operated from the mansion since 1968. Havenwood Park hosts the summer Jazz in the Park series, and Schneider Park in the southwest corner has a splash pad, playground, athletic fields, community gardens, and a limestone path along Alum Creek. Dogs are prohibited in Jeffrey Park and the Commonwealth Parks. In March 2013 Bexley was accredited as an arboretum by the Morton Register of Arboreta, according to Wikipedia’s summary and is also home to the historic Drexel Theater.

Taxes for tenants who move here

Bexley levies a 2.5% municipal income tax, collected by the Regional Income Tax Agency (RITA); the Ohio Department of Taxation’s rate table shows that rate in effect since January 1, 2012. The detail that surprises newcomers is the credit: Bexley credits residents 65% of the municipal tax they pay to another city, capped at 2.5%. A resident who works in a 2.5% city such as Columbus therefore typically owes Bexley the difference—about 0.875% of those wages—on top of the work-city withholding. RITA requires quarterly estimated payments when the expected amount due is $200 or more, and residents under 18 are exempt.

On top of that, Bexley City Schools levies a 0.75% traditional school district income tax, according to the Ohio Department of Taxation’s list of taxing school districts effective January 1, 2026. That tax is administered by the State of Ohio, not the City, and it applies to residents of the district wherever they work. If Issue 5 passes, the district’s plan adds a further 0.5%. Tenants comparing Bexley with Columbus addresses should include both local taxes in their budget and update their address with their employer.

What landlords and tenants should check before signing

  • Which city and district. Confirm the parcel is in Bexley, not Columbus; taxes, codes, and schools differ.
  • Short-term rental plans. Don’t buy or sublease expecting investor Airbnb income; Bexley STRs must be the owner’s principal residence and are capped at 60 days a year.
  • Exterior changes. Plan for Architectural Review Board approval before exterior work.
  • Everyone on the lease. If school-age children will live there, list them on the lease so they can enroll.
  • Local taxes. Budget for the 2.5% city tax with its 65% credit and the 0.75% school district income tax.
  • Parking. Check off-street parking, the 24/72-hour street limit, and whether the block uses permit parking.
  • Bus access. If you don’t drive, check distance to Line 2 on Main Street or Line 10 on Broad Street.

Frequently asked questions

Is Bexley part of Columbus?

No. Bexley is its own city in Franklin County, surrounded by Columbus on the east side. It incorporated as a village in 1908, when the Bullitt Park and Pleasant Ridge communities merged, and later became a city. It has its own mayor and council, income tax, zoning code, code enforcement, police, and school district, so Columbus City Code rules do not apply to Bexley properties.

What income taxes do Bexley residents pay?

Bexley levies a 2.5% municipal income tax, collected by the Regional Income Tax Agency (RITA). Its resident credit is 65% of tax paid to another city, capped at 2.5%, so a resident who works in a 2.5% city such as Columbus typically still owes Bexley about 0.875% of those wages. Bexley City Schools also has a 0.75% traditional school district income tax, filed with the State of Ohio.

Can I run an Airbnb in Bexley?

Only in your own home. Bexley Code Section 1266.19 requires a short-term rental (under 30 consecutive days) to be the owner’s principal residence, registered with the City for $75 a year after a life-safety inspection, and rented short-term no more than 60 days in a 12-month period. The permit number must appear on every listing, and hosts need at least $300,000 of liability insurance.

Which COTA lines serve Bexley?

Line 2 (East Main/North High) runs on East Main Street through the center of Bexley, with timepoints at Drexel Avenue and James Road, and Line 10 (E Broad/W Broad) runs on East Broad Street across Bexley. COTA classifies both as frequent lines, which it defines as buses 15 minutes or less apart.

What is Issue 5 on the November 3, 2026 ballot in Bexley?

Issue 5 asks voters to fund Phase I of the Bexley City Schools facilities plan, estimated at $115 million. The district says it would be paid for with a new 0.5% traditional school district income tax and a 2.05-mill property tax that it describes as a no-new-tax-rate request because current bond payments of 2.05 mills expire in 2027.

What is the voucher payment standard in Bexley?

Bexley uses ZIP 43209. CMHA’s FY2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents, effective October 1, 2026, at 100% of SAFMR, list $1,190 for a studio, $1,270 for one bedroom, $1,510 for two bedrooms, $1,800 for three bedrooms, and $2,050 for four bedrooms. These are payment-standard benchmarks, not asking rents.
